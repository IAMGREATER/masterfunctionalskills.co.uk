Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (The Basics) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Answering a Challenging Q&A Question Using the Bridge-and-Evidence Method) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
At the end of your presentation on introducing electronic staff rotas, an audience member asks:
'Won't older staff who aren't confident with smartphones struggle to check their shifts?'
Write a respectful, evidence-backed spoken response.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Validate the question ('That is an important point regarding digital accessibility...') [A1]
2. Provide concrete reassurance/remedy ('...which is why we are running drop-in training sessions and keeping a printed rota on the staffroom noticeboard during the 3-month transition.') [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'That is a very important question regarding digital confidence. To ensure nobody is left behind, we will run 1-to-1 smartphone setup clinics next week and continue displaying a printed paper copy on the staffroom board throughout the three-month transition.'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Dismissing the questioner's concern ('Everyone knows how to use a phone nowadays').
🔄 Quick Check: Validates the question + offers a concrete practical safeguard.
Question 2 (Core Skill Application — 3 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
During a team discussion on installing recycling bins in a warehouse depot, a colleague remarks:
'No one will bother sorting rubbish; staff will just throw everything into the nearest bin.'
Write a 45-word spoken response using the PREP method.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Point & Reason: Acknowledge that poorly labelled bins cause confusion, but clear visual signage solves this. [M1]
2. Example: Cite that the Leeds depot achieved 85% sorting accuracy by placing colour-coded picture posters above each bin. [M1]
3. Point: Conclude that clear signage and a 5-minute briefing will make compliance easy. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'Compliance is certainly a valid concern if bins are poorly labelled; however, clear visual design makes sorting effortless. For example, when our Leeds depot installed colour-coded bins with large pictorial posters above each station, recycling accuracy reached 85% within two weeks. Therefore, combining clear signage with a brief morning toolbox talk will ensure our team adapts quickly.'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Brushing off an audience question with 'No, I don't think that will be a problem' fails Criterion L1.4 / L2.3, which requires detailed explanations.
🔄 Quick Check: Citing a successful trial at a sister branch or neighbouring ward is the easiest way to supply the 'E' (Example) in PREP.
Section B: Real-Life & Workplace Practice Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Model 1 (Level 1): Answering a Question After a Talk on Healthy Canteen Meals) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
After your short talk proposing healthier salad and soup options in the staff canteen, a peer asks:
'Healthy food is usually much more expensive—won't staff end up paying £7 or £8 for lunch?'
Deliver a clear 3-sentence response.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Acknowledge the concern about lunch prices: 'Thank you, Ben—keeping meals affordable for everyone is definitely a top priority.' [A1]
2. Explain how costs will be kept low: 'By buying seasonal vegetables in bulk from our local market supplier, the canteen can prepare fresh soup and salad bowls for under £3.50 per portion.' [A1]
3. Reaffirm the benefit and check satisfaction: 'That means the healthy meal deal will actually cost 50p less than the current fried meal deal. Does that answer your question?' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'Thank you, Ben—keeping lunch affordable for all staff is definitely a priority. By buying seasonal vegetables in bulk from our local supplier, the kitchen can serve fresh soup and a roll or a large salad bowl for £3.50. That means the healthy option will actually cost 50p less than the current burger meal deal.'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Always give at least 3 full sentences (Point + Reason + Example) when answering any question in your Q&A.
🔄 Quick Check: Giving a specific, realistic price comparison ('£3.50 vs the current burger deal') makes your answer immediately convincing.
Question 4 (Exam-Style Task — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
At the end of your presentation on upgrading office lighting, an audience member asks:
'What is the exact kilowatt-hour rating of the proposed Philips LED tubes?'
You do not know that technical figure. Write a professional 2-sentence spoken response that handles the unknown detail with authority while offering relevant information you DO know.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. State honestly and professionally that you do not have the exact wattage figure to hand, and offer to confirm it after the meeting. [M1]
2. Pivot to a related energy fact you do know (e.g. the 45% overall reduction in electricity bills). [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'That is a great technical question—I do not have the exact kilowatt-hour specification per tube in front of me today, so I will check the manufacturer's datasheet and email that figure to the group this afternoon. What I can confirm from the estates quote is that the LED units consume 45% less electricity overall than our current fluorescent tubes.'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: If a peer challenges your idea, snapping back ('Well you clearly didn't listen to my slide!') ruins your professional register mark.
🔄 Quick Check: Pivoting from a tiny technical detail you don't know to a headline statistic you DO know shows impressive workplace poise.
Section C: Exam-Style Tasks Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Model 2 (Level 2): Defending a Presentation on Digital Patient Records (PREP Method)) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Following your 4-minute presentation advocating for electronic tablets on hospital rounds, an assessor asks:
'Senior ward staff are already overwhelmed. Won't introducing new software simply increase their stress and cause charting errors?'
Deliver a model PREP response.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Point (Validate & Give Direct Thesis): 'While any system transition carries an initial learning curve, introducing bedside tablets will significantly reduce overall nursing workload once implemented.' [A1]
2. Reason (Explain Functional Mechanism): 'This is because automated drop-down menus and barcode wristband scanning eliminate repetitive handwritten transcription across three different paper folders.' [A1]
3. Example (Cite Measurable Pilot Evidence): 'For instance, during the six-week pilot on Ward 6, pairing two floor super-users with nursing staff cut medication charting time by 35 minutes per shift and reduced transcription errors by 28%.' [A1]
4. Point (Reaffirm Conclusion): 'Therefore, with phased super-user mentoring during the first fortnight, the digital system relieves clinical pressure rather than adding to it.' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'While any system change carries an initial learning curve, digital bedside tablets will actually reduce overall nursing workload once implemented. Automated drop-down menus and barcode scanning eliminate repetitive paper charting, saving an average of 35 minutes per shift. For instance, during the pilot on Ward 6, dedicated super-user floor support ensured charting errors dropped by 28% within the first fortnight. Therefore, with proper hands-on mentoring, the system relieves clinical pressure rather than adding to it.'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Smile, thank the questioner for raising an important angle, and respond with calm facts.
🔄 Quick Check: Notice how the candidate did not deny that learning new software takes effort ('While any system change carries an initial learning curve...') before proving with Ward 6 data why the long-term benefit wins.
Question 6 (Level 2 Mastery Challenge — 4 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
After your presentation proposing that a community library should replace half of its book shelves with computer desks, an elderly resident asks:
'Many older residents rely on large-print books and do not use computers—won't your plan push us out of our own library?'
Write a compassionate, constructive 4-sentence spoken response that validates their concern and adapts your proposal to protect large-print book stocks.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Validate the resident's concern with empathy and respect. [M1]
2. Reassure them that the library must remain inclusive for all age groups. [M1]
3. Adapt your proposal on the spot (e.g. keep 100% of the large-print and ground-floor reading collections, and place the new computer desks on the mezzanine floor). [M1]
4. Check if that reassurance helps. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'Thank you so much for raising that point, and I completely understand why protecting our book collection is vital for older residents. Our library must remain a welcoming space for every generation in the community. To ensure no readers lose out, we will ring-fence 100% of our large-print books and comfortable ground-floor armchairs, and instead install the new computer study desks purely in the upstairs reference room. Would keeping the entire ground floor dedicated to traditional reading address your concern?'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Failing to proofread spelling, punctuation, and grammar after completing the draft.
🔄 Quick Check: Adapting your proposal to protect vulnerable service users shows high emotional intelligence and collaborative problem-solving.
