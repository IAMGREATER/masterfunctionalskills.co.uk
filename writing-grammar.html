Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.26 / L2.26 – Write grammatically correct sentences, ensuring accurate subject-verb agreement and syntactic variety.
  • L2.27 – Use complex sentence structures, fronted subordinate clauses, relative clauses, and modal verbs with sustained accuracy.

Golden Rules for Grammar, Sentence Variety & Syntax

Golden Rule 1: Deliberately Mix Simple, Compound, and Complex Sentences

Examiners award top grammar marks for deliberate syntactic variety: use punchy simple sentences for clear directives, compound sentences (FANBOYS) for balanced ideas, and complex sentences (Although, Because, Whilst) for cause-and-effect analysis.

Golden Rule / Technique: Simple (1 clause) + Compound (and/but/so) + Complex (Although/Because + comma) = Top Band Grammar
Golden Rule 2: Never Join Two Complete Sentences with Just a Comma (Comma Splice)

A comma cannot hold two independent sentences together. Fix every comma splice using either a full stop, a semicolon (;), or a coordinating conjunction (, and / , but / , so).

Golden Rule / Technique: Wrong: 'The delivery arrived late, the stock was warm.' → Right: 'The delivery arrived late, so the stock was warm.'

Core Skill 1: The 3 Sentence Architectures Every Candidate Must Demonstrate

Formula and punctuation rules for Simple, Compound, and Complex sentences.

Sentence Type Grammatical Formula Workplace Model Example
1. Simple Sentence One independent clause (Subject + Main Verb) expressing a single complete thought. "All visitors must sign the security register at reception."
2. Compound Sentence Two independent clauses joined by a comma + coordinating conjunction (For, And, Nor, But, Or, Yet, So). "The clinic heating failed at 08:00, so portable radiators were deployed to every waiting room."
3. Complex Sentence Main clause + subordinate clause starting with Although, Because, Since, Whilst, Unless, If. "Although the initial installation costs £1,200, the solar panels will reduce annual bills by 30%."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Mastering Tricky Subject-Verb Agreement Traps

How to match singular subjects with singular verbs even when plural nouns sit in the middle.

  • The Prepositional Phrase Trap: Match the verb to the true head noun, NOT the plural noun before the verb:
    Wrong: "The box of surgical gloves are empty." → Right: "The box of surgical gloves is empty."
  • Indefinite Singular Pronouns (Each, Everyone, Neither, Either): These always take a singular verb:
    Wrong: "Each of the drivers have a permit." → Right: "Each of the drivers has a permit."
    Wrong: "Neither the manager nor the supervisor were informed." → Right: "Neither the manager nor the supervisor was informed."
  • Standard English 'Was' vs 'Were': Never write "We was waiting" or "You was told". Always write "We were waiting" and "They were informed".
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Embedded Relative Clauses ('Who', 'Which', 'Whose')

How to drop an extra layer of detail into the middle of a sentence using paired commas.

To impress Level 2 examiners, insert a non-defining relative clause framed by two bracketing commas:

• People (who): "Sister Evans, who has led the maternity unit since 2019, will coordinate the training programme."
• Things (which): "The main goods lift, which was installed over twenty years ago, requires urgent cable replacement."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 Level 1 Grammar Accuracy Benchmark (3 Marks)

Vocational Context: Retail Stockroom Handover Note

Correct the two grammatical errors (one subject-verb agreement error and one comma splice) in this stockroom note:
'The delivery of winter coats have arrived in Bay 2, the afternoon team needs to hang them on the shop floor before 16:00.'
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Fix the subject-verb agreement error
The true subject is 'The delivery' (singular), not 'coats'. Change 'have arrived' to 'has arrived'.
Criteria: Subject-Verb Agreement (L1.26)
Step 2 Fix the comma splice between the two independent clauses
Replace the comma after 'Bay 2' with ', so' or ', and' (or use a semicolon/full stop).
Criteria: Eliminates Comma Splice
Step 3 Write out the complete corrected sentence
Verify that both clauses read smoothly and accurately.
Criteria: Final Sentence Check
Final Accredited Response: 'The delivery of winter coats has arrived in Bay 2, so the afternoon team needs to hang them on the shop floor before 16:00.'
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Cross out 'of winter coats' in your head: you would never say 'The delivery have arrived'—you say 'The delivery has arrived'!
Benchmark Exam Model 2 Level 2 Syntactic Variety Benchmark (3 Marks)

Vocational Context: Clinical Discharge Coordinator Report

Combine these three simple sentences into a single, grammatically sophisticated complex sentence using a fronted subordinate clause and an embedded relative clause:
1. Mrs Green completed her six-week course of outpatient physiotherapy.
2. Mrs Green underwent hip replacement surgery in August.
3. The consultant approved her return to independent living.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Embed Sentence 2 as a relative clause inside the subject
'Mrs Green, who underwent hip replacement surgery in August,' (framed with paired commas).
Criteria: Embedded Relative Clause (L2.27)
Step 2 Front Sentence 1 using a subordinating conjunction ('Because' or 'After')
'After Mrs Green, who underwent hip replacement surgery in August, completed her six-week course of outpatient physiotherapy,'
Criteria: Fronted Subordinate Clause
Step 3 Attach the main clause (Sentence 3) after the separating comma
'...the consultant approved her return to independent living.'
Criteria: Complex Sentence Synthesis
Final Accredited Response: 'After Mrs Green, who underwent hip replacement surgery in August, completed her six-week course of outpatient physiotherapy, the consultant approved her return to independent living.'
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Combining a fronted subordinate clause ('After...') with a bracketed relative clause (', who...,') showcases top-tier Level 2 grammatical control.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Writing 'Could Of', 'Should Of', or 'Would Of'
In spoken English, 'could've' sounds like 'could of'. Writing 'We could of prevented the accident' in an exam is a serious grammatical error.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always write the auxiliary verb 'have': 'could have', 'should have', 'would have'.
Examiner Pitfall: Leaving Subordinate Clauses Hanging as Incomplete Fragments
Writing 'Although the staff worked extremely hard all weekend.' with a full stop at the end is a sentence fragment because there is no main clause!
How to Avoid This Pitfall: If you start a sentence with 'Although', 'Because', or 'Whereas', make sure you follow the comma with a complete main clause.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Fix the comma splice in this sentence using two different grammatical methods (Method 1: using a coordinating conjunction; Method 2: using a semicolon):
'The fire alarm sounded at 14:00, all staff evacuated to Car Park B within three minutes.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Method 1: Insert ', and' after '14:00'.
2. Method 2: Replace the comma after '14:00' with a semicolon (;).
Final Model Answer: Method 1: 'The fire alarm sounded at 14:00, and all staff evacuated to Car Park B within three minutes.'
Method 2: 'The fire alarm sounded at 14:00; all staff evacuated to Car Park B within three minutes.'
Examiner Tip: Both clauses could stand alone as complete sentences, so a bare comma between them is always wrong.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Identify and correct the three grammatical errors in this workplace paragraph:
'Neither the supervisor nor the operatives was aware that the forklift brakes was faulty. The manager should of inspected the vehicle logbook before the shift started.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Error 1 ('neither... nor...'): With 'neither/nor', the verb agrees with the closer plural noun 'operatives' → change 'was aware' to 'were aware'.
2. Error 2 (plural subject 'brakes'): Change 'brakes was faulty' to 'brakes were faulty'.
3. Error 3 ('should of'): Change 'should of inspected' to 'should have inspected'.
Final Model Answer: 1. 'was aware' → 'were aware' | 2. 'brakes was' → 'brakes were' | 3. 'should of' → 'should have'.
Corrected text: 'Neither the supervisor nor the operatives were aware that the forklift brakes were faulty. The manager should have inspected the vehicle logbook before the shift started.'
Examiner Tip: Watch out for 'was/were' agreement with plural nouns like 'brakes' and never write 'should of'.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
Rewrite the two simple sentences below as a single complex sentence starting with the subordinating conjunction 'Whilst':
Sentence 1: Installing solar panels on the community centre roof requires an upfront investment of £6,000.
Sentence 2: The scheme will reduce annual electricity bills by £1,500 every year.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Start the dependent clause with 'Whilst installing solar panels on the community centre roof requires an upfront investment of £6,000,'.
2. Place a mandatory comma after '£6,000'.
3. Follow with the main clause.
Final Model Answer: 'Whilst installing solar panels on the community centre roof requires an upfront investment of £6,000, the scheme will reduce annual electricity bills by £1,500 every year.'
Examiner Tip: Starting a persuasive paragraph with 'Whilst [counter-argument], [your main benefit]' is the cleanest way to knock down objections in Level 2 writing.
