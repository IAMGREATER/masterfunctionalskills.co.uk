Step-by-Step Practical Guide

1
1. Before You Panic: 3 Checks After a Failed Login

Most login errors are small typing slips. After one wrong attempt, stop and check these three things BEFORE guessing again (five wrong tries can temporarily lock an account):

  • Tap the Eye Icon inside the password box so dots (••••••) reveal the real letters you typed.
  • Check Caps Lock is OFF and check you didn't put a stray space at the end if you copied/pasted.
  • Confirm your Username / Email: Did you sign up with .co.uk or .com, or an older email address?
Tutor Tip: Most sites allow 3–5 attempts before triggering a 15-minute cooldown lock — stop after 2 failed guesses and use Forgot Password.
2
2. How to Reset a Forgotten Password Step-by-Step

Look just underneath the sign-in box for 'Forgotten password?', 'Need help signing in?', or 'Reset password':

  1. Single-click Forgotten password? and type your registered email address (or mobile number), then click Send reset link / code.
  2. Open your email inbox (or Messages app on your phone) and wait up to 2 minutes. If missing, look inside your Junk / Spam folder.
  3. Check the sender is the real company you JUST asked for a reset from, then open the link or type the 6-digit code back onto the login page.
  4. Type your new passphrase twice identically to confirm, then tick Show password briefly to check both boxes match before saving.
Tutor Tip: On a Windows login screen itself, click 'I forgot my PIN' or 'Reset password' under the sign-in box if connected to Wi-Fi and Microsoft account.
3
3. Building Strong, Memorable Passwords (3 Random Words)

Modern UK National Cyber Security Centre advice recommends three random everyday words joined together plus a number or symbol (e.g. KettleCloudGarden42!).

  • Long (14+ characters) makes it far harder for automated software to guess than P@ssw0rd!.
  • Never reuse the exact same password for your Main Email, Online Banking, and shopping sites — your Email is the master key used to reset everything else!
  • If you write hints in a paper notebook, keep the notebook hidden safely at home — never on a sticky note stuck to the laptop screen.
Tutor Tip: Turn on Two-Factor Authentication (2FA SMS or app code) on email and banking so a stolen password alone still cannot let anyone in.

Three Common Beginner Traps

Avoid Trap: Using the exact same password on every website

If one minor shopping website suffers a data breach, criminals test that email + password on your main email and bank.

Avoid Trap: Clicking unexpected 'Your password expired' emails

Genuine password reset emails only arrive moments after YOU press the reset link on a website.

Avoid Trap: Forgetting to update saved browser passwords after a reset

When Chrome or Edge asks 'Update saved password?', click Update so your browser doesn't autofill your old password next week.

Worked Benchmark Steps

1. Check Caps Lock is OFF and click the Eye icon to inspect the typed password.
2. Confirm the email address spelling (.co.uk vs .com).
3. Click 'Forgotten password?' and request a reset email/code.
4. Check Inbox and Junk/Spam, open the verified link, and set a fresh 3-random-word passphrase.
