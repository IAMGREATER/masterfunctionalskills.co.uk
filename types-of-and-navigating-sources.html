Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.14 / L2.16 – Understand organisational and structural features and use them to locate and interpret information efficiently.
  • L1.16 / L2.17 – Recognise different types of texts, sources, and presentational devices and explain how they support the writer's purpose.

Golden Rules for Types of Sources, Layout & Organizational Devices

Golden Rule 1: Distinguish Primary from Secondary Sources

Primary sources are raw, firsthand records created at the time of an event (witness logs, clinical blood pressure charts, original survey responses). Secondary sources analyse, summarise, or comment on primary data (newspaper articles, textbook chapters, review reports).

Golden Rule / Technique: Primary = Firsthand Raw Record | Secondary = Secondhand Analysis / Article
Golden Rule 2: Link Every Layout Device to Reader Navigation

When asked why an author used subheadings, tables, sidebars, or numbered lists, always explain how that structural feature helps a busy reader locate or process specific information without reading every word.

Golden Rule / Technique: Structural Feature -> Cognitive Shortcut (Skimming, Scanning, Sequencing)

Core Skill 1: Organisational Layout Devices & Their Navigational Functions

How structural features organize complex workplace documents.

Structural Device Visual Appearance Exact Navigational Purpose
Subheadings Bold section titles above thematic paragraphs. Divides long texts into clear topic sections so readers can scan straight to the section they need.
Numbered Lists 1, 2, 3 sequential points. Shows chronological order or priority ranking that must be followed step-by-step (e.g. emergency drills).
Callout / Shaded Boxes Bordered or tinted rectangular panel. Separates urgent warnings, emergency phone numbers, or key takeaways from dense body text.
Glossary / Footnotes Alphabetical definition list or superscript notes (¹). Explains technical jargon or acronyms (e.g. COSHH, RIDDOR) for non-specialist readers.
Data Tables Grid of labelled rows and columns. Aligns multi-variable figures (e.g. dosage by patient weight) for rapid cross-referencing without misreading.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Skimming vs Scanning vs Close Reading

The three reading speeds every candidate must switch between during an exam.

  • Skimming (Gist Reading — 30 seconds): Reading only the main headline, subheadings, opening topic sentences, and caption boxes to identify the text type, audience, and overall purpose.
  • Scanning (Keyword Hunting — 15 seconds): Moving your eyes rapidly across the page looking for a specific visual target (e.g. a capitalised name, a date like 2026, a £ sign, or a percentage) without reading every sentence.
  • Close Intensive Reading: Once scanning locates the target paragraph, reading the sentence before, the target sentence, and the sentence after word-for-word to verify nuances like "except", "unless", or "minimum".
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Evaluating Source Credibility & Authority (Level 2)

How to judge whether a source is reliable, official, or anecdotal.

In Level 2 exams, you may be asked why one source is more reliable than another:

High-Credibility Statutory/Peer-Reviewed Sources: Official public health guidelines, Health and Safety Executive (HSE) codes of practice, Office for National Statistics (ONS) datasets. Backed by large sample sizes and legal accountability.
Lower-Credibility Anecdotal Sources: Anonymous public comment threads, personal blogs, or unsourced social media posts based on a single person's unverified experience.

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 2 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Reading Level 1)

Vocational Context: Sports Centre Membership Leaflet

A leisure centre leaflet includes the following subheadings: 1. Swimming Pool Timetable | 2. Concessionary Pricing & Discounts | 3. Gym Induction Rules.
Under Section 2, prices are presented in a three-column table (Peak, Off-Peak, Student/Senior).
(a) Which section should a retired resident read to find out if they qualify for a cheaper pass? [1 Mark]
(b) Explain why a table is used in Section 2 instead of a paragraph. [1 Mark]
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Match reader query to the exact subheading
Section 2: 'Concessionary Pricing & Discounts' covers reduced rates for retired/senior residents.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Navigation]
Step 2 Explain the functional benefit of the table
The table aligns Peak, Off-Peak, and Senior prices in clear columns so readers can compare costs at a glance without reading dense sentences.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Layout Function]
Final Accredited Response: (a) Section 2: Concessionary Pricing & Discounts. | (b) The table organises different membership tiers and prices into clear rows and columns for quick comparison.
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
When asked 'which section', copy the exact subheading title from the prompt.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Reading Level 2)

Vocational Context: Healthcare Compliance Procedure (SOP)

A hospital blood transfusion protocol uses:
• Bold numbered steps (1 to 8) for bedside patient ID checks
• An amber shaded warning box at the top reading: 'STOP: Never transfuse blood without two registered nurses verifying the wristband barcode.'
• An alphabetical glossary at the bottom defining terms like 'Haemolytic Reaction'.
Explain how each of these three structural devices assists clinical staff during an emergency. [3 Marks]
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Analyze Bold Numbered Steps
Bold numbered steps enforce a strict chronological sequence (1 to 8), ensuring nurses do not skip or muddle vital patient ID checks under time pressure.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Sequence Analysis]
Step 2 Analyze Amber Warning Box
The amber shaded box visually isolates the critical two-nurse safety rule at the very top of the page so staff see the life-saving warning before starting.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Visual Alert Analysis]
Step 3 Analyze Alphabetical Glossary
The alphabetical glossary allows newly qualified staff to quickly look up complex medical terms like 'Haemolytic Reaction' without cluttering the step-by-step instructions.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Terminology Verification]
Final Accredited Response: 1. Bold numbered steps ensure staff follow the mandatory chronological order without skipping steps. 2. The amber warning box draws immediate attention to the critical two-nurse check rule. 3. The glossary defines technical medical terms quickly without interrupting the main checklist.
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Always link each layout device to the reader's practical workplace need (e.g., 'ensures nurses do not skip steps under pressure').

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Stating That Layout Features Are 'Just to Make It Look Nice'
Writing 'It looks attractive' or 'It breaks up the page so it isn't boring' earns zero marks. Presentational devices in Functional English exist for rapid navigation and safety.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Explain how the feature assists the user functionally: rapid scanning, chronological ordering, or highlighting hazards.
Examiner Pitfall: Confusing Bullet Points with Numbered Lists
Unordered bullet points group non-sequential items (e.g. a kit list), whereas numbered lists mandate a strict chronological sequence (Step 1 before Step 2).
How to Avoid This Pitfall: When analysing a numbered list, always use words like 'chronological sequence' or 'step-by-step order'.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A logistics driver handbook contains a bordered grid showing maximum vehicle weights (3.5t, 7.5t, 18t) alongside low-bridge height clearances. Explain two reasons why a table is more effective than continuous prose for this information.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Reason 1: Organises vehicle weights and bridge clearances into aligned rows and columns so drivers can cross-reference their specific lorry size in seconds [1 Mark].
2. Reason 2: Prevents drivers from misreading critical height figures buried inside long paragraphs, reducing bridge-strike accidents [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: 1. Aligns weights and clearances in rows/columns for instant lookup. | 2. Reduces the risk of misreading vital height limits in dense paragraphs.
Examiner Tip: Focus on speed of lookup and prevention of reading errors.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
An appliance repair guide includes a footnote mark (*) next to the word 'Capacitor*' and prints at the bottom of the page: '*Capacitor: an electronic component that stores high-voltage charge even after the plug is removed.'
Identify this organisational feature and explain why the writer included it.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Feature: Footnote / asterisked glossary note [1 Mark].
2. Purpose: Explains a specialist electrical term and warns non-expert readers of the hidden high-voltage shock risk without interrupting the main sentence [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Feature: Footnote / glossary definition. | Purpose: Defines the technical term 'Capacitor' and explains its stored voltage hazard for non-specialist readers.
Examiner Tip: Always refer to the specific word ('Capacitor') when explaining why the footnote is helpful.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
You are researching workplace stress and find two sources:
Source A: An official Health and Safety Executive (HSE) annual statistical report based on 45,000 UK employees.
Source B: An anonymous comment posted on an internet chat forum reading: 'Nobody in my office ever takes sick leave for stress — it's all made up by lazy workers!'
Identify which source is more reliable and give two reasons why. [3 Marks]
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Selection: Source A (the HSE report) is far more reliable [1 Mark].
2. Reason 1: It is compiled by an official national regulatory body using a massive sample size of 45,000 employees [1 Mark].
3. Reason 2: Source B is an unverified, biased personal opinion from an anonymous forum user based on a single office [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Source A is more reliable because: 1. It is official statistical data from the HSE covering 45,000 employees. 2. Source B is an anonymous, biased anecdote ('lazy workers') with no verifiable evidence.
Examiner Tip: Contrast the sample size and official authority of Source A against the anonymity and bias of Source B.
