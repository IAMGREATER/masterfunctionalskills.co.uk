Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.31 / L2.31 – Write with a clear understanding of purpose, adapting language and structure to persuade, inform, explain, or advise.
  • L2.32 – Deploy rhetorical devices, counter-arguments, and advisory modal verbs to achieve specific communicative outcomes.

Golden Rules for Purpose in Writing: Persuade, Inform, Explain, Advise

Golden Rule 1: Match Your Toolkit to the Command Verb in the Prompt

Highlight the command verb in the exam question: 'Persuade' requires rhetorical questions, statistics, and counter-arguments; 'Inform/Explain' requires clear facts, dates, and cause-and-effect; 'Advise' requires supportive modal verbs ('you should', 'we recommend').

Golden Rule / Technique: Persuade = DAFOREST & ROI | Inform = Objective Facts | Explain = Cause & Why | Advise = Supportive Guidance
Golden Rule 2: Close With a Purpose-Aligned Call to Action

Your final paragraph must seal your purpose: a persuasive proposal ends by urging approval of a trial; an informational notice ends with contact details for queries; a complaint ends with the specific remedy requested.

Golden Rule / Technique: Every final sentence must directly fulfil the core purpose of the prompt.

Core Skill 1: The 4 Functional Writing Purposes & Linguistic Toolkit

How to adapt your sentence starters and techniques to each of the four exam purposes.

Primary Purpose Key Linguistic Features High-Scoring Exam Sentence
1. To Persuade / Argue Direct address, Rhetorical questions, Rule of Three, credible statistics, knocking down counter-arguments. "Can we truly afford to ignore a measure that is safe, affordable, and proven to cut emissions by 35%?"
2. To Inform Precise dates, times, locations, neutral tone, subheadings, bulleted facts (Who, What, When, Where). "The staff car park resurfacing will commence at 07:00 on Monday 16 November and conclude on Friday 20 November."
3. To Explain Causal conjunctions (because, as a result, therefore, in order to), step-by-step rationale. "The new two-factor login is required because external cyber-attacks on healthcare databases rose by 40% last year."
4. To Advise / Guide Supportive modal verbs (should, ought to, could), empathetic reassurance, practical tips. "To avoid peak commuter traffic, delegates are strongly advised to use the Park & Ride shuttle bus from Junction 4."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Persuasive Writing at Level 2: The Counter-Argument Rebuttal Technique

Why anticipating and defeating an objection is the fastest way to reach Band 3 Content.

In Level 2 persuasive letters, speeches, and articles, include a 2-sentence counter-argument rebuttal in Paragraph 3:

Formula: "Some critics may argue that [Objection]; however, [Evidence that proves them wrong]."

Model Example: "Some committee members may argue that installing water coolers on every floor is an unnecessary expense; however, the £400 annual rental cost is less than a single day's agency cover for dehydration-related sickness."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Balancing Dual-Purpose Tasks (e.g. 'Inform and Persuade')

Many exam prompts combine two purposes—learn how to satisfy both.

  • Example Prompt: "Write an email to staff informing them of the charity fun run and persuading them to sign up."
  • How to Structure It: Use Paragraph 1 & 2 to Inform (exact date, 5km route, start time, registration link), and Paragraph 3 to Persuade (team spirit, free medal, raising £2,000 for the local children's hospice).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 Level 1 Purpose (Advise & Inform) Benchmark (3 Marks)

Vocational Context: Workplace Onboarding & Mentoring

Write a supportive 3-paragraph email to a new administrative apprentice, Leo, advising him on what to bring on his first day (Monday 9 November at 09:00), dress code, and lunch arrangements.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Warm welcome and clear arrival information (Inform)
State start time (09:00 on Monday 9 November) and who to ask for at reception.
Criteria: Informative Clarity (L1.31)
Step 2 Practical advice on dress code and ID documents (Advise)
Use supportive modal verbs ('you should wear', 'please remember to bring') for smart-casual attire and passport/ID.
Criteria: Advisory Tone
Step 3 Reassuring guidance on lunch and first-day buddy support
Explain kitchen facilities and reassure Leo that his mentor will guide him through the day.
Criteria: Encouraging Close
Final Accredited Response: To: leo.davies@email.co.uk
Subject: Welcome to Oakfield Medical Practice – First Day Advice (Monday 9 November)

Dear Leo,

We are delighted that you are joining our administration team next Monday, 9 November. Please arrive at the main reception desk at 09:00, where I will meet you and issue your security pass.

For your first day, we advise wearing smart-casual office clothing, such as tailored trousers and a collared shirt. You should also bring your passport or driving licence so that HR can complete your identification check.

Finally, our staff kitchen has a fridge and microwave if you wish to bring a packed lunch, or we can visit the café across the road together during your break. Please reply if you have any questions at all!

Best wishes,
Hannah Reed, Senior Administrator
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Notice how the tone is warm, practical, and reassuring—perfectly matching the 'Advise and Inform' purpose.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 Level 2 Dual-Purpose Benchmark (4 Marks)

Vocational Context: Public Health Promotion Campaign

Write the concluding two paragraphs of a public health leaflet intended to ADVISE and PERSUADE long-term smokers to enrol in a free local NHS stop-smoking clinic.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Combine empathetic advisory guidance with clinical evidence
Acknowledge how challenging quitting is alone, then cite the 400% higher success rate with specialist support.
Criteria: Dual Purpose Alignment (L2.31)
Step 2 Deploy Rule of Three and financial/health incentives
Highlight saving £2,000 a year and improving breathing within 72 hours.
Criteria: Persuasive Rhetoric (L2.32)
Step 3 Deliver a clear, motivating call to action
Provide the free helpline number and drop-in clinic hours.
Criteria: Actionable Close
Final Accredited Response: 'Quitting smoking is undeniably challenging, but you do not have to face cravings through willpower alone. We strongly recommend booking a free 15-minute consultation with our friendly NHS Stop Smoking advisers, who can prescribe tailored nicotine replacement patches and gum free of charge. Clinical studies prove that combining one-to-one support with dual nicotine therapy makes you four times more likely to quit permanently.

Within just 72 hours of your final cigarette, your lung capacity begins to recover—and quitting a twenty-a-day habit puts over £3,500 back into your pocket every year. Take the first step toward a healthier, wealthier, and smoke-free future by calling our confidential helpline on 0800 012 345 today.'
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Blending supportive advice ('we strongly recommend') with hard persuasive figures ('four times more likely', '£3,500 a year') hits every Level 2 marking descriptor.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Writing an Aggressive Sales Pitch When Asked to 'Inform' or 'Advise'
If a prompt asks you to write an informational guide explaining a new recycling system, filling it with emotional exaggeration and exclamation marks misjudges the purpose.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Match your emotional temperature to the prompt verb: 'Inform' and 'Explain' require calm, factual authority.
Examiner Pitfall: Using Made-Up Surveys That Sound Ridiculous ('99.9% of People Agree')
Writing 'According to a survey, 99.9% of people hate buses' sounds childish and unconvincing.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Use realistic, believable figures in persuasive writing (e.g. 'According to our recent staff survey, 68% of commuters...').

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Identify the primary purpose (To Persuade / To Inform / To Instruct) of each sentence below:
1. 'Isolate the mains electricity switch before removing the inspection cover.'
2. 'Can our community afford to lose its only youth sports field to another luxury housing development?'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Sentence 1 uses an imperative command verb ('Isolate') for a safety step → To Instruct.
2. Sentence 2 uses an emotive rhetorical question to rally opposition → To Persuade.
Final Model Answer: 1. To Instruct | 2. To Persuade
Examiner Tip: Imperative verbs ('Isolate', 'Wash', 'Press') always signal instructional writing; rhetorical questions signal persuasive writing.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Write a 2-sentence counter-argument and rebuttal for a persuasive letter to your manager asking to allow administrative staff to work from home one day a week.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Sentence 1 (Acknowledge the manager's likely objection): Mention concern about telephone cover or productivity.
2. Sentence 2 (Rebut with evidence): Explain how cloud phone forwarding and rotating rota days maintain 100% office cover while boosting output.
Final Model Answer: 'Some managers may be concerned that allowing remote working one day per week could leave the main reception telephone lines understaffed; however, rotating home-working days across the five-person team ensures four administrators remain on site at all times. Furthermore, our cloud telephone system routes calls seamlessly to staff laptops, guaranteeing uninterrupted client service.'
Examiner Tip: Using 'Some may be concerned that...; however,...' shows the examiner you can evaluate multiple viewpoints at Level 2.
Exam Practice Question 3 4 Marks
A prompt instructs: 'Write an article for your college website explaining why apprenticeships offer a valuable alternative to university and advising school leavers on how to apply.'
Draft a 4-sentence paragraph that fulfils both the Explain and Advise purposes.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Sentences 1–2 (Explain): Explain why apprenticeships are valuable (earning a salary while gaining an accredited qualification without £40,000 of student debt).
2. Sentences 3–4 (Advise): Advise school leavers on practical application steps (registering on the government apprenticeship portal and tailoring their CV).
Final Model Answer: 'Higher and degree apprenticeships offer a compelling alternative to traditional university degrees because learners earn a full-time salary while gaining practical industry experience and a recognized qualification, graduating with zero student loan debt. As a result, over 85% of apprentices secure permanent employment immediately upon completing their programme. If you are considering this route, we strongly advise registering on the official Find an Apprenticeship portal before the spring recruitment window opens. Additionally, you should ask your college careers adviser to review your CV and arrange a mock interview.'
Examiner Tip: Notice how 'because' and 'as a result' drive the explanation, while 'we strongly advise' and 'you should' deliver the advice.
