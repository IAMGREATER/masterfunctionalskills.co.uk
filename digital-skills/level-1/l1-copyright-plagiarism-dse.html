Step-by-Step Teaching & Key Concepts

Core Knowledge
1
Copyright Law and Creative Commons

Just because an image or article appears on Google does NOT mean it is free to use!

  • Copyright: Under the Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988, the original creator automatically owns the legal rights to their text, images, videos, and software. Copying, publishing, or selling someone else's work without permission or a licence is copyright infringement.
  • Public Domain: Material whose copyright has expired or has been donated for free public use.
  • Creative Commons (CC): Licences that permit public reuse under specific conditions (e.g. CC-BY requires giving credit/attribution to the author; CC-NC forbids commercial use).
2
Plagiarism & Citing Online Sources

Plagiarism is the practice of taking someone else's work, ideas, or words and passing them off as your own.

  • To avoid plagiarism: Always use quotation marks when quoting word-for-word, and provide a clear citation (Author Name, Title, Year, and Website URL).
  • In workplace reports, referencing reputable sources enhances your credibility and professional integrity.
3
Display Screen Equipment (DSE) & Environmental Responsibility

UK law protects your physical health and the environment:

  • DSE Regulations: Employers must provide workstation risk assessments, adjustable chairs with lumbar support, anti-glare screen filters, and free regular eye tests for screen workers.
  • Repetitive Strain Injury (RSI): Prevent wrist pain by using ergonomic mouse mats and keeping forearms horizontal.
  • WEEE (Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment): Old laptops, monitors, and printer cartridges contain hazardous toxic chemicals (lead, mercury). Never throw electronic hardware into household general rubbish — dispose of them through certified WEEE recycling schemes!

Authentic Pearson Edexcel Exam Model

Accredited Model

Here is how this skill is tested in the real Pearson Edexcel onscreen digital assessment, broken down with official examiner mark codes ([B1] knowledge, [M1] process, [A1] accuracy).

Exam Scenario Prompt: A medical training coordinator is preparing a printed trainee handbook. They download medical diagrams from a commercial medical website and paste them into the handbook without contacting the creator. Explain whether this is permitted, and state one legal way the coordinator could find images they are allowed to use.
Step 1: Explain copyright violation [B1]
It is NOT permitted; it is a breach of copyright law to copy and distribute someone else's intellectual property without permission or a licence.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Correctly applies copyright restrictions to online media.
Step 2: Provide legal alternative [B1]
Search for images under Creative Commons licences (or public domain / royalty-free stock libraries) that permit reuse with attribution.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Identifies Creative Commons as a legitimate licensing solution.
Tutor Abdul's Exam Golden Rule: In Level 1 exams, never assume images online are free. Always mention 'Copyright permission' or 'Creative Commons licences'.

Onscreen Assessment Task:
An office worker is experiencing persistent wrist and finger pain after typing for long hours each day. Name the condition they may be developing, and describe two ergonomic adjustments that can prevent this workplace health issue.
Your Working & Answer Scratchpad: Auto-saves in browser

Pearson Edexcel Benchmark Solution

Condition: Repetitive Strain Injury (RSI). Adjustment 1: Use an ergonomic keyboard and gel wrist rest to keep wrists straight while typing. Adjustment 2: Ensure forearms are horizontal with elbows at a 90-degree angle, and take frequent micro-breaks from typing to stretch fingers and wrists.
Official Marking Criteria:
  • [1 Mark]: Naming Repetitive Strain Injury (RSI).
  • [1 Mark]: Describing wrist rests, forearm angles, or typing micro-breaks.
