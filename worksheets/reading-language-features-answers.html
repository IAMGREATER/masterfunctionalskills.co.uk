Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (The Basics) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Level 1 Identifying Persuasive Devices) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Match the excerpt with the correct language technique:
A. 'Over 85% of healthcare workers surveyed support flexible shift scheduling.'
B. 'Can you afford to ignore the warning signs of burnout?'

Techniques: Statistic / Fact  |  Rhetorical Question  |  Emotive Language
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Excerpt A matches: Statistic / Numerical Fact (85%) [A1]
2. Excerpt B matches: Rhetorical Question (question requiring self-reflection, not direct answer) [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: A: Statistic / Fact; B: Rhetorical Question
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Confusing a direct question with a rhetorical question.
🔄 Quick Check: A has numbers (statistic); B has a question mark engaging the reader without expecting reply.
Question 2 (Level 2 Rule of Three & Emotive Language) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Read this campaign slogan: 'Together we can build a safer, cleaner, and healthier clinic for every patient.'

Identify the language technique used and explain its effect on the reader.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Technique: Rule of three (triad) / three complementary adjectives [A1]
2. Effect: Creates a memorable rhythm, reinforces key priorities, and persuades the reader that improvement is comprehensive [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Technique: Rule of three; Effect: Makes the vision memorable, rhythmic, and persuasive.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Simply naming the technique without explaining how it influences the reader's feelings or thoughts.
🔄 Quick Check: 'Safer, cleaner, healthier' are three parallel comparative adjectives.
Section B: Real-Life & Workplace Practice Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Level 1 Direct Address & Call to Action) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
An infection prevention poster states:
'Clean hands save lives. Every time you wash your hands, you protect a vulnerable patient. Join our ward safety champion team today!'

Identify two language features used in this text and quote an example of each.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Feature 1: Direct address ('you', 'your hands') - personalises the responsibility [A1]
2. Feature 2: Imperative / Command / Call to action ('Join our ward safety champion team today!') [A1]
3. Alternative Feature: Alliteration or punchy slogan ('Clean hands save lives') [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Direct address ('you') and Imperative / Call to action ('Join our ward safety champion team today!')
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Quoting text without naming the language feature.
🔄 Quick Check: 'You' directly engages reader; 'Join' is an imperative verb.
Question 4 (Level 2 Hyperbole and Emotive Tone) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A patient charity leaflet writes:
'Waiting four agonizing hours in a freezing corridor is an absolute nightmare that tears families apart.'

Identify two emotive words and one example of exaggeration (hyperbole) in the sentence.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Emotive word 1: 'agonizing' (or 'freezing') [A1]
2. Emotive word 2: 'nightmare' [A1]
3. Hyperbole: 'tears families apart' (or 'absolute nightmare') [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Emotive words: 'agonizing', 'nightmare'; Hyperbole: 'tears families apart'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Choosing 'four hours' as hyperbole when that is a literal measurement of time.
🔄 Quick Check: 'Tears families apart' exaggerates the effect of a corridor wait for emotional impact.
Section C: Exam-Style Tasks Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Level 2 Text Comparison - Persuasive Impact) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Text A (Formal Trust Notice):
'Flu vaccination clinics operate weekdays in Seminar Room 2. Evidence demonstrates vaccination reduces seasonal staff absenteeism by 32%.'

Text B (Staff Union Poster):
'Don't bring the flu home to your loved ones! Protect your family, shield your patients, and safeguard your colleagues. Get jabbed this week!'

Compare the different language techniques used in Text A and Text B to encourage staff vaccination.
Give at least two specific language features from each text.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Text A Device 1: Statistical evidence ('reduces absenteeism by 32%') [A1]
2. Text A Device 2: Formal, neutral informative tone / factual statements without emotional bias [A1]
3. Text B Device 1: Rule of three with parallel verbs ('Protect your family, shield your patients, safeguard your colleagues') [A1]
4. Text B Device 2: Emotive appeal / personal direct address ('your loved ones') and imperative command ('Get jabbed this week!') [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Text A uses statistics and neutral informative tone; Text B uses rule of three, emotional appeal to family, and imperatives.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Describing what the texts are about rather than analysing the language devices used.
🔄 Quick Check: Text A appeals to logic/data; Text B appeals to emotion, duty, and protective instincts.
Question 6 (Level 2 Writer's Perspective & Technique Evaluation) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
An apprentice blogger writes:
'Is it fair that apprentice nurses shoulder enormous clinical responsibilities while surviving on poverty wages? We are the beating heart of our hospitals, yet we are cast aside like disposable instruments.'

Analyse how the writer uses language to position the reader to support higher apprentice wages.
Identify two distinct techniques, quote evidence, and explain their persuasive effect.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Technique 1: Rhetorical question with emotive vocabulary ('Is it fair that... poverty wages?'). Effect: Provokes reader indignation and moral discomfort, forcing the reader to agree that current pay is unjust. [M1]
2. Quote 1 provided accurately and linked to effect [A1]
3. Technique 2: Metaphor and Simile ('beating heart of our hospitals', 'cast aside like disposable instruments'). Effect: Emphasizes vital clinical necessity while contrasting it sharply with feeling undervalued and exploited. [M1]
4. Quote 2 provided accurately and linked to effect [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Rhetorical question provokes moral indignation; Metaphor ('beating heart') and Simile ('like disposable instruments') dramatise mistreatment.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Vague comments like 'it makes the reader feel bad' without identifying metaphor, rhetorical question, or emotive diction.
🔄 Quick Check: The rhetorical devices create a contrast between noble clinical work and unfair compensation.
