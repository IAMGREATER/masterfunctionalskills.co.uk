UK GDPR, Privacy and Digital Footprints

Understand legal compliance and privacy rights: apply the UK Data Protection Act 2018 and UK GDPR principles, manage browser cookies and tracking, and manage your professional digital footprint.

Explain core UK GDPR principles (lawfulness, purpose limitation, data minimisation, accuracy, storage limitation) Identify individual data rights (Right to Access / Subject Access Request, Right to Erasure) Manage website cookie consent banners (Essential vs Marketing cookies) and browser privacy modes

Step-by-Step Teaching & Key Concepts

Core Knowledge
1
The UK Data Protection Act 2018 & UK GDPR

In the UK, the Data Protection Act 2018 (incorporating UK GDPR) governs how businesses and public bodies handle personal data. Organisations must obey six core principles:

  1. Lawful, Fair & Transparent: Data must be collected honestly with clear notice to individuals.
  2. Purpose Limitation: Data collected for one reason (e.g. appointment booking) cannot be secretly used for another (e.g. marketing).
  3. Data Minimisation: Only collect what is strictly necessary — do not ask for irrelevant personal history.
  4. Accuracy: Keep records up to date and correct mistakes promptly.
  5. Storage Limitation: Do not keep personal data longer than needed. Delete securely when no longer required.
  6. Integrity & Confidentiality: Keep data secure from theft or loss using strong passwords and encryption.
2
Individual Rights Under UK GDPR

Every person (data subject) has statutory legal rights:

  • Right to be Informed: Plain-English privacy policies explaining how data is used.
  • Right of Access (Subject Access Request - SAR): You can request a free copy of all personal records an organisation holds about you.
  • Right to Erasure ('Right to be Forgotten'): Request deletion of your data when there is no longer a legal reason to hold it.
Workplace Confidentiality Mandate: Never discuss patients or clients on public social media, and never take photos of medical records. Disclosing personal data without consent is a serious criminal offence under UK law.
3
Cookies and Browser Privacy Modes

When visiting websites:

  • Cookies: Small text files saved on your computer by websites to remember login states or track browsing behavior. Essential cookies are required for sites to function; marketing cookies track you across other sites for adverts. You can decline marketing cookies on consent banners.
  • Incognito / InPrivate Browsing: Opens a temporary browser window that deletes your search history, cookies, and temporary cache as soon as you close it. (Note: It does NOT hide your activity from your employer or internet provider!).

Step-by-Step Exam Worked Model

Accredited Model

Here is how this skill is tested in the real Pearson Edexcel onscreen digital assessment, broken down with official examiner mark codes ([B1] knowledge, [M1] process, [A1] accuracy).

Exam Scenario Prompt: A patient moves to a different city and contacts their local health clinic requesting that all their non-essential medical marketing survey data be deleted from the clinic mailing system. State the specific UK GDPR right the patient is exercising, and state one key responsibility the clinic has under the Data Protection Act.
Step 1: Identify the GDPR right [B1]
The Right to Erasure (or 'Right to be Forgotten' / Right to Object).
Tutor Marking Rationale: Correctly names the statutory individual right.
Step 2: State clinic responsibility [B1]
The clinic must comply without undue delay (within one month) and delete the data, provided there is no conflicting legal obligation to retain it.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Demonstrates understanding of statutory response deadlines.
Tutor Abdul's Exam Golden Rule: In Level 1 exams, whenever questions ask about data protection legislation in the UK, the legislation is the 'Data Protection Act 2018' (incorporating UK GDPR).

Onscreen Assessment Task:
A company is reviewing its compliance with the Data Protection Act 2018. Describe two key principles of UK GDPR that the company must follow when collecting customer names and addresses.
Your Working & Answer Scratchpad: Auto-saves in browser

Accredited Exam Benchmark Solution

Principle 1: Purpose Limitation and Data Minimisation: The company must only collect customer data that is strictly necessary for the transaction, and must not use it for unrelated marketing without consent. Principle 2: Storage Limitation and Security: Data must be kept securely using encryption and strong access controls, and must not be retained longer than legally necessary.
Official Marking Criteria:
  • [1 Mark]: Explaining Purpose Limitation or Data Minimisation.
  • [1 Mark]: Explaining Storage Limitation, Accuracy, or Security / Confidentiality.
