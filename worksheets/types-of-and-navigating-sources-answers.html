Types of Sources, Layout & Organizational Devices Pearson Edexcel: Pearson Edexcel Functional Skills English (READING)
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Foundational Concept Check Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Using Organisational Features to Locate Information Fast) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A 12-page Staff Handbook contains the following structural sections:
• 1.0 Annual Leave Entitlements | 2.0 Sickness Absence Reporting | 3.0 Uniform & Dress Code | Appendix A: Glossary of Clinical Abbreviations

Which section should a new healthcare assistant consult to find out who to telephone at 06:30 if they are unwell, and which section explains the meaning of 'PRN'?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Sickness call procedure: Section 2.0 (Sickness Absence Reporting) [A1]
2. Meaning of abbreviation 'PRN': Appendix A (Glossary of Clinical Abbreviations) [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Section 2.0 (Sickness Absence Reporting) and Appendix A (Glossary of Clinical Abbreviations).
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Guessing content without matching the subheading keywords ('Sickness Reporting' and 'Glossary').
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: A glossary always defines specialist terms and abbreviations.
Question 2 (Core Skill Application — 2 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A logistics driver handbook contains a bordered grid showing maximum vehicle weights (3.5t, 7.5t, 18t) alongside low-bridge height clearances. Explain two reasons why a table is more effective than continuous prose for this information.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Reason 1: Organises vehicle weights and bridge clearances into aligned rows and columns so drivers can cross-reference their specific lorry size in seconds [1 Mark]. [M1]
2. Reason 2: Prevents drivers from misreading critical height figures buried inside long paragraphs, reducing bridge-strike accidents [1 Mark]. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 1. Aligns weights and clearances in rows/columns for instant lookup. | 2. Reduces the risk of misreading vital height limits in dense paragraphs.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Writing 'It looks attractive' or 'It breaks up the page so it isn't boring' earns zero marks. Presentational devices in Functional English exist for rapid navigation and safety.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Focus on speed of lookup and prevention of reading errors.
Section B: Functional Workplace Application Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Using Subheadings and Tables to Locate Information (Level 1)) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A leisure centre leaflet includes the following subheadings: 1. Swimming Pool Timetable | 2. Concessionary Pricing & Discounts | 3. Gym Induction Rules.
Under Section 2, prices are presented in a three-column table (Peak, Off-Peak, Student/Senior).
(a) Which section should a retired resident read to find out if they qualify for a cheaper pass? [1 Mark]
(b) Explain why a table is used in Section 2 instead of a paragraph. [1 Mark]
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Match reader query to the exact subheading: Section 2: 'Concessionary Pricing & Discounts' covers reduced rates for retired/senior residents. [A1]
2. Explain the functional benefit of the table: The table aligns Peak, Off-Peak, and Senior prices in clear columns so readers can compare costs at a glance without reading dense sentences. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) Section 2: Concessionary Pricing & Discounts. | (b) The table organises different membership tiers and prices into clear rows and columns for quick comparison.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Explain how the feature assists the user functionally: rapid scanning, chronological ordering, or highlighting hazards.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: When asked 'which section', copy the exact subheading title from the prompt.
Question 4 (Exam-Style Task — 2 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
An appliance repair guide includes a footnote mark (*) next to the word 'Capacitor*' and prints at the bottom of the page: '*Capacitor: an electronic component that stores high-voltage charge even after the plug is removed.'
Identify this organisational feature and explain why the writer included it.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Feature: Footnote / asterisked glossary note [1 Mark]. [M1]
2. Purpose: Explains a specialist electrical term and warns non-expert readers of the hidden high-voltage shock risk without interrupting the main sentence [1 Mark]. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Feature: Footnote / glossary definition. | Purpose: Defines the technical term 'Capacitor' and explains its stored voltage hazard for non-specialist readers.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Unordered bullet points group non-sequential items (e.g. a kit list), whereas numbered lists mandate a strict chronological sequence (Step 1 before Step 2).
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Always refer to the specific word ('Capacitor') when explaining why the footnote is helpful.
Section C: Pearson Edexcel Exam-Style Analysis & Evaluation Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Evaluating Organizational Features in a Clinical SOP (Level 2)) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A hospital blood transfusion protocol uses:
• Bold numbered steps (1 to 8) for bedside patient ID checks
• An amber shaded warning box at the top reading: 'STOP: Never transfuse blood without two registered nurses verifying the wristband barcode.'
• An alphabetical glossary at the bottom defining terms like 'Haemolytic Reaction'.
Explain how each of these three structural devices assists clinical staff during an emergency. [3 Marks]
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Analyze Bold Numbered Steps: Bold numbered steps enforce a strict chronological sequence (1 to 8), ensuring nurses do not skip or muddle vital patient ID checks under time pressure. [A1]
2. Analyze Amber Warning Box: The amber shaded box visually isolates the critical two-nurse safety rule at the very top of the page so staff see the life-saving warning before starting. [A1]
3. Analyze Alphabetical Glossary: The alphabetical glossary allows newly qualified staff to quickly look up complex medical terms like 'Haemolytic Reaction' without cluttering the step-by-step instructions. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 1. Bold numbered steps ensure staff follow the mandatory chronological order without skipping steps. 2. The amber warning box draws immediate attention to the critical two-nurse check rule. 3. The glossary defines technical medical terms quickly without interrupting the main checklist.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: When analysing a numbered list, always use words like 'chronological sequence' or 'step-by-step order'.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Always link each layout device to the reader's practical workplace need (e.g., 'ensures nurses do not skip steps under pressure').
Question 6 (Level 2 Mastery Challenge — 3 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
You are researching workplace stress and find two sources:
Source A: An official Health and Safety Executive (HSE) annual statistical report based on 45,000 UK employees.
Source B: An anonymous comment posted on an internet chat forum reading: 'Nobody in my office ever takes sick leave for stress — it's all made up by lazy workers!'
Identify which source is more reliable and give two reasons why. [3 Marks]
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Selection: Source A (the HSE report) is far more reliable [1 Mark]. [M1]
2. Reason 1: It is compiled by an official national regulatory body using a massive sample size of 45,000 employees [1 Mark]. [M1]
3. Reason 2: Source B is an unverified, biased personal opinion from an anonymous forum user based on a single office [1 Mark]. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Source A is more reliable because: 1. It is official statistical data from the HSE covering 45,000 employees. 2. Source B is an anonymous, biased anecdote ('lazy workers') with no verifiable evidence.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Failing to proofread spelling, punctuation, and grammar after completing the draft.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Contrast the sample size and official authority of Source A against the anonymity and bias of Source B.
