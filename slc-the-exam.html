Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.1–L1.6 & L2.1–L2.7 – Full mastery of regulated Ofqual Speaking, Listening and Communicating subject content criteria.
  • Witness Testimony & Recording – Assessor observation records, camera positioning rules, and internal quality assurance (IQA) standards.

Golden Rules for Speaking, Listening & Communicating Exam Strategy & Grading Criteria

Golden Rule 1: Every Single Criterion Must Be Evidenced (100% Mastery Rule)

Unlike Reading and Writing where marks are added up to reach a pass boundary, SLC is graded on an 'All Criteria Met' basis. You must demonstrate every single criterion (L2.1 to L2.7) at least once across your tasks to achieve a Pass.

Golden Rule / Technique: All-or-Nothing Mastery: Hit Every Tick-Box on the Assessor Witness Sheet
Golden Rule 2: Speak Directly to Your Peers—Ignore the Camera and Assessor

The video camera is only there so an external moderator can verify the assessor's marking, and the assessor is a silent observer. Look exclusively at your group members with warm, natural eye contact.

Golden Rule / Technique: Look at Your Group Peers, Never at the Examiner or Camera Lens

See the exact 7 criteria boxes sitting on your examiner's clipboard and how to trigger every tick.

Code Official Criterion Descriptor Exact Action That Guarantees the Tick
L2.1 Identify relevant information and lines of argument Take brief notes during peer talks and refer accurately to a specific figure or argument they made.
L2.2 Make requests and ask detailed questions Ask at least 2 open probing questions ("How will...?", "What evidence...?") during Q&A and discussion.
L2.3 Communicate ideas clearly and effectively with detail Deliver a 4-minute structured presentation and answer Q&A questions using the 4-step PREP method.
L2.4 Support arguments with relevant, persuasive evidence Quote at least 2 statistics, financial figures, or regulatory standards (HSE, CQC, NHS) in your talk and discussion.
L2.5 Follow and analyse lines of argument using spoken techniques Use clear signposts, vocal emphasis, controlled pauses, and synthesise competing views in the discussion.
L2.6 Use appropriate language and register for audience Maintain composed professional Standard English throughout; zero slang or aggressive remarks.
L2.7 Respect turn-taking rights and move discussion forward Never interrupt; use verbal bridges ("Building on..."); invite a quiet peer by name; summarise consensus.

Core Skill 2: Online Remote Assessment (Teams / Zoom) vs In-Person Room Rules

How to pass smoothly if your SLC exam is conducted remotely via Microsoft Teams or Zoom.

  • Camera & Lighting Check: Position your webcam at eye level with a light source in front of you so the assessor and moderator can clearly see your face and non-verbal listening cues.
  • Handling Online Audio Lag: On video calls, slight audio delays can cause accidental overlapping speech. Always use the speaker's first name when handing over ("Priya, what do you think?") so turn-taking is crystal clear.
  • Screen-Sharing Your Slides: Test screen-sharing 10 minutes before the assessment starts, and keep your physical cue cards in your hand so you never read off a hidden Word document on screen.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Conquering Exam Nerves & Vocal Anxiety in 3 Minutes

Simple physiological techniques used by professional speakers to steady a shaky voice.

  1. Box Breathing (4–4–4): Inhale quietly through the nose for 4 seconds, hold for 4 seconds, exhale slowly for 4 seconds. This immediately lowers heart rate and stops vocal tremor.
  2. Memorise Your First Sentence Word-for-Word: Nerves peak in the first 15 seconds. Knowing your opening sentence by heart gets you over the starting line smoothly.
  3. Keep a Glass of Water Next to Your Notepad: Taking a small sip of water before the Q&A begins relieves dry mouth and gives you a natural 3-second pause.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Benchmark Exam Model 2 Level 2 Assessor Marking Audit (L2.1–L2.7)

Vocational Context: Examiner Standardization Review

During a 12-minute Level 2 group discussion, Candidate Jordan spoke four times, cited two statistics, and did not interrupt anyone, yet the assessor flagged a borderline concern on Criterion L2.2. Explain why Jordan was at risk on L2.2 and how to fix it in the final two minutes.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Check the exact requirement of Criterion L2.2
Criterion L2.2 requires the candidate to 'make requests and ask detailed questions using appropriate language'.
Criteria: Identifies Criterion L2.2
Step 2 Diagnose why Jordan's performance was incomplete
Although Jordan made strong statements and cited statistics (meeting L2.3, L2.4, and L2.6), Jordan never asked a question to another participant!
Criteria: Spots Missing Question Evidence
Step 3 Deliver the immediate fix before the timer ends
In the final two minutes, Jordan must ask an open probing or collaborative question to a peer (e.g. 'Elena, how would we measure the success of this trial after three months?').
Criteria: Secures 100% Criteria Pass
Final Accredited Response: Diagnosis: Jordan met L2.3 and L2.4 by making evidenced statements, but failed to ask any detailed questions (L2.2). Fix: Before the discussion ends, Jordan must ask at least two open probing questions to peers or during the presentation Q&A.
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Never finish a discussion having only made statements! You MUST ask at least two open questions so the assessor can tick box L1.2 / L2.2.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Forgetting That You Cannot Pass Overall English Without Passing SLC
Even if you score 90% in your Reading and Writing exams, Ofqual regulations state that your Functional Skills English certificate cannot be issued until your SLC Pass is logged.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Prepare your 4-minute presentation cue cards and discussion phrases with the same care as a job interview.
Examiner Pitfall: Looking at the Assessor for Approval After Every Sentence
Turning to look at the examiner to see if they are nodding breaks the natural group dynamic and makes you look self-conscious.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Pretend the assessor is not in the room—keep 100% of your eye contact on your fellow group participants.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 3 Marks
State the three observable actions you must complete during the Task 2 Group Discussion to ensure Criterion L2.7 (respecting turn-taking rights and moving discussion forward) is ticked off by your assessor.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Action 1: Never interrupt or talk over a peer while they are speaking.
2. Action 2: Use a verbal bridge referencing the previous speaker's name and point.
3. Action 3: Actively invite a quieter participant into the conversation with an open question (and help summarise agreement).
Final Model Answer: 1. Allow peers to finish their sentences without interrupting.
2. Use a verbal bridge that references a peer's point by name ('Building on Marcus's point...').
3. Actively invite a quieter group member to contribute with an open question and help summarise consensus.
Examiner Tip: Memorise these three actions as your non-negotiable discussion checklist.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
A candidate brings a 600-word typed essay into their Level 2 Formal Presentation and reads it aloud word-for-word without looking up. Explain which two SLC assessment criteria this candidate is at high risk of failing and how they should prepare their notes instead.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Identify Criterion L2.3 (Communicate clearly and effectively in speech) and L2.5 (Use spoken techniques such as pitch, pace, eye contact, and natural delivery).
2. Explain the fix: Convert the essay into 4 small index cue cards with 3–4 bold bullet-point keywords per card.
Final Model Answer: At-risk criteria: L2.3 (effective spoken communication) and L2.5 (using spoken techniques and audience engagement), because reading verbatim produces a flat monotone and zero eye contact.
Fix: Condense the script onto four numbered index cue cards containing only short bullet-point keywords and statistics.
Examiner Tip: Assessors are grading your SPEAKING skills, not your ability to read an essay aloud!
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
Your SLC assessment is taking place online via Microsoft Teams tomorrow morning. List three practical technical and presentation checks you should perform 15 minutes before the call begins.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Check 1 (Camera & Lighting): Position camera at eye level in a well-lit, quiet room so your face and non-verbal nodding are clearly visible on the recording.
2. Check 2 (Audio & Screen-Share): Test microphone clarity and verify that your 3–4 presentation slides share cleanly.
3. Check 3 (Cue Cards & Notepad): Place physical cue cards and a blank notepad + pen next to your keyboard so your eyes stay near the webcam.
Final Model Answer: 1. Position the webcam at eye level in a quiet, well-lit room so the assessor can see your eye contact and non-verbal listening cues.
2. Test microphone audio and practise screen-sharing your presentation slides in advance.
3. Place physical bullet-point cue cards and a 2-column listening notepad on your desk so you do not read off the screen.
Examiner Tip: On video calls, sticking your cue card right beside your webcam lens makes it look like you are maintaining direct eye contact with everyone!
