Section A: Core Skills (Warm-Up) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (3D Solid Properties & Euler's Rule) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
An architectural glass roof lantern is shaped as a square-based pyramid.
Calculate the sum of its faces, edges, and vertices.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify F = 5 faces (1 square base + 4 triangles), E = 8 edges, V = 5 vertices [M1]
2. Sum properties: 5 + 8 + 5 = 18 [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 18 (5 faces, 8 edges, 5 vertices)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Counting only the 4 triangular faces and forgetting the square base.
🔄 Quick Check: F + V - E = 5 + 5 - 8 = 2.
Question 2 (Level 1 Foundation — 2 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A ridge tent is shaped as a triangular prism.
(a) How many vertices (corners) does it have?
(b) How many edges does it have?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Vertices: 3 at front triangle + 3 at back triangle = 6 vertices. [M1]
2. Edges: 3 at front + 3 at back + 3 connecting ridge/ground poles = 9 edges. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) 6 vertices | (b) 9 edges
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: A triangular prism has 5 faces, 9 edges, and 6 vertices. A square-based pyramid has 5 faces, 8 edges, and 5 vertices.
🔄 Quick Check: Verify with Euler’s formula: Faces (5) + Vertices (6) − Edges (9) = 2.
Section B: Everyday & Workplace Maths Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Clinical Sharps Bin 3D Geometry Specification (Level 1 Foundation)) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A clinical sharps disposal bin is shaped as a hexagonal prism.
(a) State the 2D shape of its two end faces.
(b) How many faces does the hexagonal prism have in total?
(c) How many edges and vertices does it have?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify the cross-sectional end faces: A hexagonal prism has two identical end faces shaped as hexagons (6-sided polygons). [A1]
2. Calculate total faces (n + 2): 2 hexagonal end faces + 6 rectangular side faces = 8 faces in total. [A1]
3. Calculate total edges (3n) and vertices (2n): Edges: 6 top + 6 bottom + 6 vertical = 18 edges. | Vertices: 6 top + 6 bottom = 12 vertices. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) Hexagons | (b) 8 faces | (c) 18 edges and 12 vertices
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Look for the uniform cross-section: prisms have matching parallel ends; pyramids come to a single point.
🔄 Quick Check: For any prism with an n-sided base: Faces = n + 2, Edges = 3n, Vertices = 2n.
Question 4 (Workplace Application — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A glass paperweight is shaped as a square-based pyramid. Each side of the square base measures 9 cm, and each of the four sloping edges measures 11 cm.
(a) State the number of faces, edges, and vertices.
(b) Calculate the total length of all edges of the pyramid.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Square-based pyramid has 5 faces (1 square + 4 triangles), 8 edges, and 5 vertices. [M1]
2. Base edges = 4 × 9 cm = 36 cm. [P1]
3. Sloping edges = 4 × 11 cm = 44 cm. [P1]
4. Total edge length = 36 + 44 = 80 cm. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) 5 faces, 8 edges, 5 vertices | (b) 80 cm
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Adding 1.5 + 0.8 + 0.9 = 3.2 m only counts 3 edges instead of all 12 edges.
🔄 Quick Check: A square-based pyramid has 4 base edges and 4 sloping edges meeting at the apex.
Section C: Exam-Style Problem Solving Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Exhibition Display Case Aluminium Edge Framing Cost (Level 2 Multi-Step)) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A fabricator builds the complete 12-edge aluminium frame for a cuboid glass display case measuring 1.5 m long, 0.8 m wide, and 0.9 m high.
• Aluminium angle bar is sold in 3.5 m lengths at £14.50 per length.
Calculate the minimum cost of the aluminium lengths required to build the frame.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Calculate total length of all 12 edges of the cuboid: Total Edge Length = (4 × 1.5 m) + (4 × 0.8 m) + (4 × 0.9 m) = 6.0 + 3.2 + 3.6 = 12.8 metres. [M1]
2. Divide by 3.5 m stock bar length and round UP: 12.8 m ÷ 3.5 m = 3.657... → Round UP to 4 lengths. [M1]
3. Calculate total cost: 4 lengths × £14.50 = £58.00. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: £58.00 (4 lengths required for 12.8 m total edge framing)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Multiply the sum of (L + W + H) by 4 to account for all 12 edges.
🔄 Quick Check: Remember a cuboid has 12 edges in 3 sets of 4: 4 lengths + 4 widths + 4 heights!
Question 6 (Level 2 Multi-Step Problem Solving — 3 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A heavy-duty steel cage is shaped as a cube of side length 1.4 metres. Calculate the total length of steel bar required to weld all 12 edges of the cube.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. A cube has 12 equal edges. [M1]
2. Total steel length = 12 × 1.4 m = 16.8 metres. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 16.8 m
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Stating a conclusion without showing the full numerical comparison values.
🔄 Quick Check: Multiply the side length by 12 for a cube wireframe.
