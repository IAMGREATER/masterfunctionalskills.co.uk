Asking Questions Effectively Pearson Edexcel: Pearson Edexcel Functional Skills English (SLC)
Total Marks Available: 10 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~6/10)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (The Basics) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Converting Closed Yes/No Questions into Open Probing Questions) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
During a group discussion on staff wellbeing, a candidate asks:
'Do you think a longer lunch break is a good idea?' (Closed question).
Rewrite this as an open, probing question that invites detailed explanation and evidence.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Replace closed opener ('Do you think...') with an open interrogative ('How would...', 'What impact would...') [A1]
2. Prompt the listener to consider practical effects on shift cover and morale [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'How would extending the staff lunch break to forty-five minutes affect afternoon clinic cover and overall team morale?'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Asking a question that can still be answered with a single word ('Yes' or 'No').
🔄 Quick Check: Starts with 'How', 'Why', or 'What impact' so a full explanation is required.
Question 2 (Core Skill Application — 2 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Convert both of these closed Yes/No questions into detailed open probing questions:
1. 'Do you think moving the staffroom upstairs is a good idea?'
2. 'Will the new computer system save time?'
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Rewrite Question 1 starting with 'How might...' or 'What impact...' to explore accessibility and break time. [M1]
2. Rewrite Question 2 starting with 'In what ways will...' or 'How much time...'. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 1. 'How might relocating the staffroom to the second floor affect staff who only have a fifteen-minute break or colleagues with mobility needs?'
2. 'In what specific ways will the upgraded booking software reduce administrative processing times each day?'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Questions framed as attacks ('Why would anyone waste money on that?') make speakers defensive and fail criteria for collaborative professional register.
🔄 Quick Check: Starting questions with 'How might...' or 'In what specific ways...' makes a one-word answer impossible.
Section B: Real-Life & Workplace Practice Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Model 1 (Level 1): Asking Questions After a Peer's Presentation on Cycling to Work) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A fellow candidate has just finished a 3-minute talk encouraging college students to cycle to campus instead of taking the bus. Formulate one clarifying question and one open question to ask them during the Q&A.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Acknowledge the speaker's talk politely: 'Thank you for that interesting talk, Sam.' [A1]
2. Ask a specific Clarifying Question about campus facilities: 'Could you clarify where the locked bike sheds are located on the main campus and whether students need to pay for a key fob?' [A1]
3. Ask an Open Question about wet weather / winter safety: 'What advice would you give to beginners who feel nervous about cycling in heavy rain or dark winter evenings?' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Question 1 (Clarifying): 'Thank you for your talk, Sam. Could you clarify where the secure cycle shelters are located on campus and how students apply for a key fob?'
Question 2 (Open): 'What practical advice would you give to students who feel nervous about cycling on busy roads during dark winter afternoons?'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Frame challenging questions neutrally around risk and feasibility: 'How might we overcome the upfront cost of that option?'
🔄 Quick Check: Asking a question that starts with 'What practical advice would you give...' gives the presenter a great chance to elaborate, earning marks for both of you!
Question 4 (Exam-Style Task — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A colleague proposes: 'We should replace all annual staff appraisals with monthly 15-minute informal check-ins.'
Write two open probing questions to test the practical feasibility of this proposal in a busy workplace.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Probe 1 (Time/Workload): Ask how line managers will schedule 12 meetings per employee alongside daily operational duties. [M1]
2. Probe 2 (Record-Keeping): Ask how long-term training and promotion targets will be documented consistently. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 1. 'With line managers supervising up to fifteen staff each, how will they find capacity to conduct fifteen separate check-ins every month during busy clinical shifts?'
2. 'What standardised form will we use during these informal chats to ensure staff training requests and pay-progression targets are still formally documented for HR?'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: To pass Criterion L1.2, the assessor must record evidence of you asking detailed questions. If you never raise your hand in Q&A, you risk failing that criterion.
🔄 Quick Check: Good probing questions always test the two main workplace pressures: Time capacity and Documentation/Cost.
