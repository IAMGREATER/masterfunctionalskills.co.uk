Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.6 / L2.6 – Use appropriate language and register suitable for purpose, subject, and audience in formal and informal situations.
  • L2.3 – Adapt verbal communication effectively to suit varying levels of technical understanding and emotional need.

Golden Rules for Adapting Talk to Audience and Situation

Golden Rule 1: Calibrate Formality Along the Workplace Register Continuum

Match your spoken vocabulary to the room. Casual street slang ('mate', 'wrecked', 'innit') is unacceptable in an assessed SLC task, while cold technical jargon alienates worried customers or patients.

Golden Rule / Technique: Supportive & Accessible (Public/Patients) ↔ Collaborative & Clear (Peers) ↔ Formal & Evidenced (Boardroom)
Golden Rule 2: Translate Specialist Jargon Immediately into Plain English

When speaking to non-specialists (patients, parents, customers, or new apprentices), always pair any technical term or acronym with a clear plain-English explanation.

Golden Rule / Technique: Formula: Technical Term + 'which means...' (e.g. 'hypertension—which means high blood pressure')

Core Skill 1: The Register Adaptation Matrix Across 3 Workplace Settings

Compare how the exact same operational issue is communicated to three different audiences.

Situation Inappropriate / Too Casual Adapted Functional English
1. Requesting Leave (To Manager) "I need Friday off cos my mates are going away." "I would like to request annual leave for this Friday; I have already arranged shift cover with Michael."
2. Explaining Delay (To Customer) "Our IT server is totally wrecked so we can't check your order." "We are experiencing a temporary system update this morning; I will take your telephone number and call you back by 11:00 with your tracking details."
3. Challenging a Proposal (In Committee) "That idea makes zero sense and costs way too much." "While I appreciate the rationale behind the proposal, have we evaluated the impact on our quarterly training budget?"
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: De-Escalating Frustrated Customers or Anxious Patients (The LAST Method)

How to adapt your tone when speaking to someone who is upset or stressed.

  1. L — Listen Without Interrupting: Let the client finish explaining their problem while maintaining calm, empathetic eye contact.
  2. A — Apologise & Acknowledge Empathy: "I completely understand how frustrating it is to wait in all morning for a missed delivery, and I sincerely apologise."
  3. S — Solve with a Concrete Action Plan: State exactly what you will do right now and give a realistic timeframe.
  4. T — Thank & Check Satisfaction: "Thank you for your patience, Mr Davies—does that Saturday morning slot work for you?"
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Specialist Technical Register vs Public Plain-English Register

How to switch smoothly between professional peers and members of the public.

  • Speaking to a Mechanic Colleague (Technical Peer): "The diagnostic scan flagged a faulty DPF pressure sensor and worn front brake callipers."
  • Speaking to the Car Owner (Public Customer): "Our diagnostic check showed a faulty exhaust filter sensor and worn front brake parts. Replacing both today will cost £185 and ensure your car passes its MOT safely."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 Level 1 Audience Adaptation Benchmark (L1.6)

Vocational Context: Automotive Service Reception

A mechanic tells you: 'Tell Mr Robinson his van won't be ready today because the supplier sent the wrong alternator bracket.'
Deliver a polite, 3-sentence spoken message to Mr Robinson that apologises, explains the delay clearly without blaming people, and confirms tomorrow's collection time.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Courteous greeting & sincere apology
'Good afternoon Mr Robinson, I am calling from Metro Garage with an update on your transit van, and I want to apologise for a slight delay.'
Criteria: Polite Customer Register (L1.6)
Step 2 Clear explanation & new completion time
'Unfortunately, the replacement alternator bracket delivered this morning did not match your engine model, so we have ordered an express replacement arriving at 08:30 tomorrow.'
Criteria: Clear Factual Explanation
Step 3 Reassurance & customer check
'Our senior technician will fit and road-test it first thing, meaning your van will be ready for collection by 11:00 tomorrow morning—does that arrangement work for you?'
Criteria: Solution-Focused Close
Final Accredited Response: 'Good afternoon Mr Robinson, I am calling from Metro Garage with an update on your van, and I sincerely apologise for a slight delay. Unfortunately, the alternator bracket delivered this morning did not match your engine model, so we have arranged an express replacement arriving at 08:30 tomorrow. Our technician will fit and test it immediately, so your van will be ready for collection by 11:00 tomorrow morning.'
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Always pair bad news (a delay) with a definite solution and an exact updated time ('ready by 11:00 tomorrow').
Benchmark Exam Model 2 Level 2 Register & Sensitivity Benchmark (L2.3, L2.6)

Vocational Context: Health and Social Care Discharge Consultation

A clinical handover note reads: 'Patient ambulates with Zimmer frame. Requires BID domiciliary care package post-discharge and PRN analgesia.'
How should the care coordinator communicate this verbally to the elderly patient (Mrs Davies) and her daughter (Claire)?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Establish warm, respectful rapport with both listeners
'Good afternoon Mrs Davies, hello Claire. I would like to talk through the support package we have arranged so you feel safe and comfortable returning home tomorrow.'
Criteria: Empathetic Tone (L2.6)
Step 2 Translate clinical terminology into accessible plain English
'To help you walk steadily around your bungalow, our physiotherapist has provided this lightweight walking frame. We have also arranged for friendly home-care assistants to visit twice a day—once at 08:30 to help you get washed and dressed, and once at 19:00.'
Criteria: Translates 'ambulates' & 'BID domiciliary' (L2.3)
Step 3 Explain medication clearly and invite questions
'Finally, the pharmacy has packed your pain-relief tablets, which you can take whenever your knee feels sore, up to four times a day. What questions do you both have about the visiting times?'
Criteria: Translates 'PRN analgesia' & Checks Understanding
Final Accredited Response: 'Good afternoon Mrs Davies, hello Claire. I would like to run through the support we have arranged for your return home tomorrow. To help you walk steadily indoors, you will have this lightweight walking frame. We have also arranged for home carers to visit twice a day—at 08:30 and 19:00—and packed pain-relief tablets that you can take whenever your knee aches. What questions do you both have about the routine?'
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Functional Skills examiners award high marks when candidates replace clinical acronyms ('BID domiciliary', 'PRN analgesia') with warm, transparent explanations.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Using Unexplained Acronyms or Jargon with Lay Audiences
Throwing around terms like 'SOP', 'KPI', 'DSE', or 'dyspnoea' when speaking to customers or patients creates confusion and fails audience adaptation criteria.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Either use plain English from the start or immediately define the term.
Examiner Pitfall: Over-Formality That Sounds Robotic or Patronising
Reciting memorised dictionary words that you wouldn't naturally use makes your delivery sound stiff and insincere.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Aim for natural, composed professional Standard English—clear, courteous, and direct.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Rewrite this casual spoken remark so it is appropriate for a formal presentation to your College Board of Governors:
'Loads of students are quitting the evening maths class cos the buses stop running at 8pm and they can't get home.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Replace informal slang ('Loads of', 'quitting', 'cos') with formal educational register.
2. State the transport issue clearly and professionally.
Final Model Answer: 'A significant number of adult learners have withdrawn from the evening Mathematics programme because local public bus services cease operating at 20:00, leaving students without reliable transport home.'
Examiner Tip: Replacing 'Loads of students are quitting cos...' with 'A significant number of adult learners have withdrawn because...' instantly elevates your spoken register.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
An IT technician needs to tell a non-technical receptionist: 'Don't click links in phishing emails because malware will encrypt the local LAN server.'
Rewrite this spoken advice in 2–3 clear, jargon-free sentences that explain what to look out for and why it matters.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Explain 'phishing emails' in plain terms (suspicious emails pretending to be from banks or couriers).
2. Explain 'malware encrypting the LAN server' in plain terms (harmful software locking all our patient appointment files).
Final Model Answer: 'Please be extra cautious if you receive unexpected emails asking you to click a link or enter your password, even if they look official. Clicking a fake link can install harmful software that locks everyone out of our shared patient booking computer system. If an email ever looks unusual, please forward it straight to the IT desk before clicking anything.'
Examiner Tip: Explaining the practical consequence ('locks everyone out of our booking system') helps non-technical colleagues understand why the rule matters.
Exam Practice Question 3 4 Marks
A parent arrives at a nursery reception desk upset because their child's favourite jacket has gone missing from the cloakroom. Using the LAST method (Listen/Acknowledge, Apologise, Solve, Thank/Check), write a calm, empathetic 4-sentence spoken response.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Acknowledge & Apologise: Validate the parent's frustration warmly.
2. Solve (Immediate Action): Offer to check the classroom lost-property box and garden area immediately.
3. Solve (Follow-up): Promise to put a notice on the parents' app if not found within 10 minutes.
4. Check satisfaction.
Final Model Answer: 'I completely understand how upsetting it is to find Leo's jacket missing at pickup time, Mrs Green, and I am really sorry for the worry this has caused. I am going to check the Blue Room lost-property basket and the outdoor play area with his key worker right now. If we do not locate it within the next five minutes, I will send an urgent photo alert to all parents on the nursery app so anyone who picked it up by mistake can return it tomorrow morning. Does that sound helpful while you wait in reception?'
Examiner Tip: Staying calm, using the child's name, and taking immediate practical action de-escalates upset parents or customers every time.
