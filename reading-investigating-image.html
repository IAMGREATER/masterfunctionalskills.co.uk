Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.14 / L2.16 – Analyse how images, diagrams, and graphical representations support or extend written content.
  • L2.17 – Evaluate the persuasive, emotional, and informational impact of visual imagery and captions in functional texts.

Golden Rules for Images, Diagrams, Charts & Captions

Golden Rule 1: Connect Specific Image Details to the Text's Core Message

Images are never merely decorative in Functional Skills exams. Describe the specific visual detail shown in the image (e.g. a smiling apprentice in a hard hat, or a flooded high street) and explain how it proves or reinforces the writer's point.

Golden Rule / Technique: Formula: [Specific Visual Detail] + [Emotion or Fact Conveyed] + [Link to Text Purpose]
Golden Rule 2: Always Read the Caption and Chart Labels First

Captions, axis labels, and legends anchor the meaning of an image or graph. Examiners often hide a 1-mark retrieval fact inside an image caption or chart key that does not appear anywhere in the main body paragraphs!

Golden Rule / Technique: Never skip captions! They frequently contain unique exam-scoring facts.

Core Skill 1: Four Categories of Visual Media in Functional Skills Papers

How photographs, diagrams, charts, and icons function differently.

Visual Type Typical Exam Context Primary Function & Effect on Reader
Emotive Photograph Charity appeals, environmental campaigns, community petitions. Humanises statistics and triggers empathy, sympathy, or outrage by showing real people/animals affected.
Instructional Diagram First-aid guides, flat-pack assembly, lifting posture manuals. Demonstrates exact physical positioning (e.g. straight back, bent knees) far more clearly than words alone.
Bar Chart / Pie Chart Financial reports, climate audits, staff survey summaries. Converts complex percentages and trends into instant visual proportions so readers spot peaks and drops immediately.
Hazard / Warning Symbols COSHH chemical labels, workshop signs, road safety notices. Communicates danger universally across language barriers at a single glance (e.g. skull and crossbones, high voltage).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: How Captions Anchor and Extend Image Meaning

Why the text directly underneath a photo is critical for comprehension.

A photograph on its own can be ambiguous. The caption (the short text printed directly beneath or beside an image) performs three vital jobs:

  1. Identifies Who, Where, and When: Names the specific person, location, and date (e.g., "Apprentice electrician Maya Patel testing solar panels at Kettering Depot, May 2026").
  2. Links the Image to the Argument: Explains how the photo proves the article's claim (e.g., "The new panels cut depot electricity bills by 38%").
  3. Provides Extra Retrieval Facts: In Pearson Edexcel exams, always check captions when a retrieval question asks for a date, name, or cost that you cannot find in the main paragraphs!
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Evaluating Whether an Image Is Effective (Level 2)

How to structure a 2-mark or 3-mark evaluation of an image's suitability.

Question Pattern: "Explain how the image and caption support the writer's purpose in Text B. [2 Marks]"
Step 1 (Visual Impact): Describe what the image shows and the feeling/impression it creates ("The photograph of overflowing litter bins next to a children's swingset creates disgust and concern for child safety...").
Step 2 (Link to Purpose): Connect it to the writer's goal ("...which reinforces the writer's campaign to persuade the council to fund twice-weekly park collections.").

Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 2 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Reading Level 1)

Vocational Context: Warehouse Lifting Safety Guide

A safety leaflet includes a diagram showing a worker lifting a heavy box with a straight back and bent knees, accompanied by a green tick icon and the caption: 'Figure 1: Hold the load close to your waist (maximum solo lift: 20 kg).'
(a) According to the caption, what is the heaviest weight one worker should lift alone? [1 Mark]
(b) Why has the writer included this diagram alongside the written rules? [1 Mark]
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Retrieve the exact weight limit from the caption
The caption states 'maximum solo lift: 20 kg'.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Caption Retrieval]
Step 2 Explain the functional purpose of the diagram
The diagram visually shows staff the exact safe body posture (straight back, bent knees, load close to waist) so they can copy it easily and avoid back injury.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Diagram Purpose]
Final Accredited Response: (a) 20 kg. | (b) Visually demonstrates the correct lifting posture (bent knees, straight back) so workers can easily see and copy the safe technique.
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Notice how the 20 kg figure was inside the caption — always read every word around a diagram!
Benchmark Exam Model 2 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Reading Level 2)

Vocational Context: Charitable Healthcare Fundraising

Text A (a fundraising appeal for the County Air Ambulance) includes a photograph of a paramedic kneeling beside a red helicopter trauma pack, smiling warmly alongside a young boy who survived a road accident.
(a) What emotional response does this photograph evoke in potential donors? [1 Mark]
(b) Explain how this specific image supports the text's headline: 'Every Second Counts, Every Pound Saves Lives'. [2 Marks]
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Identify the emotional response evoked
Evokes warmth, relief, hope, gratitude, and empathy for the recovered child.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Emotion]
Step 2 Link the helicopter/trauma pack to 'Every Second Counts'
The emergency helicopter and trauma pack highlight the rapid, time-critical medical response ('Every Second Counts').
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Visual Detail 1]
Step 3 Link the smiling child to 'Every Pound Saves Lives'
The smiling, healthy boy provides tangible photographic proof that public donations directly save real children's lives, persuading readers to donate.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Visual Detail 2]
Final Accredited Response: (a) Evokes warmth, hope, relief, and empathy. | (b) The helicopter and trauma kit illustrate the speed of emergency care ('Every Second Counts'), while the smiling recovered boy gives tangible visual proof that donations save real lives ('Every Pound Saves Lives').
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Always link the specific visual details in the photograph directly to the exact words in the headline or text purpose.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Stating the Obvious Without Analysing Significance
Writing 'The picture shows a nurse so you know the text is about nurses' scores 0 at Level 2. You must explain WHY that specific depiction was chosen (e.g. to project frontline professionalism, reassurance, or urgency).
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always state what the image proves, clarifies, or makes the reader feel.
Examiner Pitfall: Saying 'It Helps People Who Cannot Read'
Writing 'The picture is there for people who cannot read English' is a generic guess that examiners reject unless the text is specifically about international safety symbols.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Explain how the image complements the written text for the target audience.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A marine conservation poster includes a close-up photograph of a sea turtle entangled in a discarded plastic six-pack ring, alongside the caption: 'Over 100,000 marine animals die from plastic entanglement every year.'
Explain why the author used this photograph and caption together rather than just printing waste statistics.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Effect of photograph: Provokes immediate shock, sympathy, and guilt by showing an innocent animal physically trapped in everyday litter [1 Mark].
2. Combined effect with caption: Combines emotional impact with the stark 100,000 annual death toll to prove it is a widespread crisis and persuade readers to cut plastic use [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: The photo provokes shock and sympathy by showing a real turtle trapped in plastic, while the caption's 100,000 statistic proves the massive scale of the crisis to persuade readers to stop littering.
Examiner Tip: Mention both the emotional impact of the photo and the factual scale added by the caption.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
An engineering apprenticeship brochure features a photograph of a young female technician wearing safety goggles and operating a CNC laser cutter in a bright, modern workshop.
Give two reasons why the college selected this specific photograph for the brochure.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Reason 1: Challenges gender stereotypes by showing that engineering apprenticeships are welcoming and accessible to women [1 Mark].
2. Reason 2: Portrays the workshop as modern, high-tech, and safety-conscious (safety goggles, CNC laser), making the course look attractive and professional [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: 1. Encourages female applicants by showing women succeeding in engineering. | 2. Makes the course look modern, hands-on, and safe (high-tech laser cutter and safety goggles).
Examiner Tip: Look closely at who is in the photo and what equipment they are using to deduce how it appeals to the target audience.
Exam Practice Question 3 2 Marks
A company energy report includes a bar chart titled 'Quarterly Gas Consumption (kWh)' showing a tall red bar for Winter (45,000 kWh) and a short green bar for Summer (8,000 kWh).
Explain how this bar chart helps managers reading the report.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Visual comparison: Allows managers to see the dramatic contrast between winter peak usage (45,000 kWh) and summer usage (8,000 kWh) instantly through bar height and colour coding [1 Mark].
2. Decision support: Makes seasonal energy trends clear at a glance without having to calculate figures from a paragraph [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Visually contrasts the sharp winter spike (45,000 kWh in red) against low summer usage (8,000 kWh in green) so managers can spot seasonal energy trends at a glance.
Examiner Tip: Refer to the visual contrast (tall red bar vs short green bar) and the exact figures shown.
