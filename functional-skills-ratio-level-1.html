Official Level 1 Specification & Scope (L1.N.17)

Regulated Ofqual Functional Skills Mathematics Level 1 learning outcomes (Open Awards, Pearson Edexcel, City & Guilds, NCFE & AQA):

  • Work with simple ratios in everyday workplace recipes, paint/concrete mixes, and staff-to-client rules.
  • Share a total quantity into a 2-part or 3-part ratio using the ADAM method (Add parts, Divide to find 1 part, And Multiply).
  • Scale up a ratio when told the size of one specific share rather than the total.

Core Level 1 Golden Rules: Simple Ratio and Sharing in a Given Ratio (Level 1)

Golden Rule 1: Order of Words = Order of Numbers

In 'screenwash to water = 1 : 4', screenwash is 1 part and water is 4 parts—never swap them around!

Level 1 Key Rule: 1 part screenwash + 4 parts water = 5 total parts
Golden Rule 2: Sharing a Total? Use ADAM

Add the ratio parts → Divide total by total parts to find 1 box → Multiply by each share!

Level 1 Key Rule: Share £60 in 2 : 3 → 5 parts → 1 part = £12 → £24 and £36
Golden Rule 3: Given ONE Part Instead of Total? Divide by That Part Only!

If mortar is 1 cement : 3 sand and you have 15 kg of sand, divide 15 by 3 (not 4!) so 1 part = 5 kg cement.

Level 1 Key Rule: Check whether the question gives the TOTAL or ONE PART!
Core Skill 1: Simplifying Ratios & Converting to Unitary Form (1 : n) Level 1 & 2 Standard

In Functional Skills exams, ratios must often be simplified to their lowest whole terms or expressed in unitary form (1 : n or n : 1):

Simplifying to Lowest Whole Terms

Divide both sides by the Highest Common Factor (HCF):

Original Divide By HCF Simplest Form
24 : 36 ÷ 12 both sides 2 : 3
15 : 45 ÷ 15 both sides 1 : 3
18 : 24 : 30 ÷ 6 all sides 3 : 4 : 5
45 cm : 1.5 m 45 cm : 150 cm (÷ 15) 3 : 10
Unitary Form (1 : n and n : 1)

To make the leading term 1, divide both numbers by the first number:

Express 4 : 18 in the form 1 : n
(4 ÷ 4) : (18 ÷ 4) → 1 : 4.5 ✓
In unitary form, decimals are fully allowed and expected!
Express 35 : 7 in the form n : 1
(35 ÷ 7) : (7 ÷ 7) → 5 : 1 ✓
Divide both terms by 7 so the second number becomes 1.

Core Skill 3: Scaling from ONE Known Part or the Difference Between Parts High-Mark Exam Differentiator

In Level 1 problem-solving questions, the examiner rarely gives you the grand total. Instead, they tell you what ONE person receives or the DIFFERENCE between shares:

Given Scenario Golden Rule for 1 Part Worked Model (Ratio 3 : 7)
Case A: Known Single Share
e.g. "Person A gets £60" 		Divide value by its OWN parts only:
Do NOT add ratio terms together!
1 Part = £60 ÷ 3 = £20
Person B (7 parts) = 7 × £20 = £140
Grand Total = 10 × £20 = £200
Case B: Known Difference
e.g. "Person B gets £80 MORE than Person A" 		Divide difference by DIFFERENCE in parts:
Parts Difference = 7 − 3 = 4 parts
1 Part = £80 ÷ (7 − 3) = £80 ÷ 4 = £20
Person A = 3 × £20 = £60
Person B = 7 × £20 = £140
Diff check: £140 − £60 = £80 ✓
Lead Tutor Trap Alert: If the question says "Liam gets £60" in ratio 3 : 7, dividing £60 by 10 (= £6) is an instant fail! £60 is Liam's 3 parts, not the 10-part total. Always read whether you have a share, a difference, or a total.

Vocational Context: Decorating Paint Tint Mix (Sharing a Total)

A decorator mixes blue paint and white paint in the ratio 2 : 5 to make 21 litres of sky-blue paint. How many litres of blue paint and how many litres of white paint does she need?
Step
Level 1 Method & Working
Marking Focus
Step 1 Add the ratio parts
2 + 5 = 7 equal parts
→ 7 parts
[M1] Correct total parts
Step 2 Divide total volume by 7 to find 1 part
21 litres ÷ 7 = 3 litres per part
→ 1 part = 3 L
[M1] Value of 1 share found
Step 3 Multiply each part by 3 L
Blue: 2 × 3 = 6 L | White: 5 × 3 = 15 L
→ 6 L blue and 15 L white
[A1] Both volumes accurate (6 + 15 = 21 check)
Final Exam Answer: 6 litres of blue paint and 15 litres of white paint

Common Level 1 Examiner Traps to Avoid

Level 1 Examiner Trap: Dividing Total by One Number Instead of Total Parts
Doing 21 ÷ 2 or 21 ÷ 5 instead of adding 2 + 5 = 7 parts first.
Level 1 Examiner Trap: Dividing by Total Parts When Given Only One Ingredient
If given 15 L of white paint (5 parts), divide by 5 (1 part = 3 L), NOT by 7!

Interactive Level 1 Exam Practice Questions

Level 1 Practice 1: Tip Jar Split 2 Marks
Two baristas share £72 in tips in the ratio of hours worked, 4 : 5. How much does each person receive?
Level 1 Worked Solution & Mark Breakdown:
4 + 5 = 9 parts; £72 ÷ 9 = £8 per part (M1): 1 part = £8
4 × £8 and 5 × £8 (A1): £32 and £40
Final Answer: £32 and £40
Level 1 Practice 2: Concrete Sand & Cement 2 Marks
Concrete uses cement to sand in the ratio 1 : 4. A builder uses 3 bags of cement. How many bags of sand does he need?
Level 1 Worked Solution & Mark Breakdown:
Scale 1 part = 3 bags (M1): 4 × 3
Sand bags needed (A1): 12 bags of sand
Final Answer: 12 bags of sand
