Composing, Replying and Forwarding Emails

Master everyday workplace email: write clear subject lines, compose polite messages with appropriate greetings and sign-offs, reply to incoming messages, and forward emails to colleagues.

Compose a new email with recipient address, clear subject line, and polite body text Differentiate between Reply (to sender only) and Reply All (to everyone on the thread) Forward an existing email to a new contact with an explanatory message
On This Page:
1. Key Concepts 2. Worked Exam Model 3. Interactive Quiz 4. Practice Exam Task

Step-by-Step Teaching & Key Concepts

Core Knowledge
1
Composing a New Email

Click New Mail (or Compose) to open an email window. Every message needs three core parts:

  1. To: Type the recipient's exact email address (e.g. sarah.jones@example.co.uk). Addresses must have no spaces and contain an @ symbol.
  2. Subject Line: A short, informative summary of your message (e.g. 'Staff Meeting Agenda - 14th November'). Never leave this blank!
  3. Email Body: The message itself. Always start with a polite greeting ('Dear Sarah' or 'Good morning Team') and finish with a sign-off ('Kind regards, Abdul').
Workplace Email Compose Window
To: amanda.clark@healthclinic.org
Subject: First Aid Training Rota - October 2026
Good morning Amanda,

Please find attached the updated first aid rota for next week.

Kind regards,
Abdul
New Email Shortcut: Ctrl + N • Send Email: Ctrl + Enter
2
Replying: Reply vs Reply All

When responding to an email you received:

  • Reply: Sends your response ONLY to the person who sent the email. Use this 90% of the time.
  • Reply All: Sends your response to the sender AND everyone else who was copied into the email. Only use this when everyone on the team genuinely needs to read your reply.
Workplace Etiquette Alert: Do not click 'Reply All' just to say 'Thank you'! That clutters up everyone's inbox across the whole department.
3
Forwarding an Email

Forward sends a copy of an email you received to a completely new person who wasn't on the original thread. When forwarding, always type a brief note at the top explaining why you are sharing it (e.g. 'Hi David, please see the update from estates below for your information').

Step-by-Step Exam Worked Model

Accredited Model

Here is how this skill is tested in the real Pearson Edexcel onscreen digital assessment, broken down with official examiner mark codes ([B1] knowledge, [M1] process, [A1] accuracy).

Exam Scenario Prompt: A support worker receives an email from their manager with the subject 'Next Week Shift Cover'. The worker needs to confirm they can work Saturday. Explain which button they should click (Reply, Reply All, or Forward) and write a suitable two-sentence email response.
Step 1: Select the correct action [B1]
Click 'Reply'
Tutor Marking Rationale: Reply sends the answer directly back to the manager who asked.
Step 2: Compose response with proper etiquette [B1]
Dear Manager, Thank you for your email. I can confirm that I am available to cover the Saturday shift. Kind regards, Abdul
Tutor Marking Rationale: Includes greeting, polite confirmation, and professional sign-off.
Tutor Abdul's Exam Golden Rule: In Entry 3 exams, marks are given for: 1. Subject line filled in; 2. Polite greeting; 3. Clear message; 4. Professional sign-off.
