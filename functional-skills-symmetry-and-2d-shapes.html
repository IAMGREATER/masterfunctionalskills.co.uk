  • E3.8 / L1.21 – Identify lines of reflective symmetry and order of rotational symmetry in 2D shapes and workplace signage.

Golden Rules for Symmetry & 2D Shapes

Golden Rule 1: A Line of Symmetry is an Exact Mirror Fold Line

If you fold a shape along a line of symmetry, both halves must match up edge-to-edge and corner-to-corner. In a non-square rectangle, the diagonals are NOT lines of symmetry!

Golden Rule / Method: Reflective Symmetry = Both Halves Match When Folded
Golden Rule 2: Minimum Rotational Symmetry Order is ALWAYS 1 (Never 0!)

Rotational symmetry order is how many times a shape looks identical during a full 360° spin. Because every shape matches itself at 360°, the lowest possible order of rotational symmetry is 1 (never write 0).

Golden Rule / Method: Rotational Order = Matches in 360° (Minimum Order = 1)

Core Skill 2: Reflecting a Shape Across a Mirror Line on a Grid

Counting perpendicular grid squares to the mirror line.

  1. Pick one vertex (corner) of the original shape and count how many grid squares it is from the mirror line.
  2. Count that exact same number of grid squares on the opposite side of the mirror line and plot a dot.
  3. Repeat for every corner and join the dots with a ruler.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Symmetry in Capital Letters, Logos & Road Signs

Identifying horizontal, vertical, and rotational symmetry in symbols.

  • Vertical Line of Symmetry Only: A, M, T, U, V, W, Y.
  • Horizontal Line of Symmetry Only: B, C, D, E, K.
  • Both Vertical & Horizontal (2 Lines, Order 2): H, I, O, X.
  • Zero Lines of Symmetry, Rotational Order 2: N, S, Z.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 2 Marks (Entry Level 3 / Level 1)

Vocational Context: Health and Safety Wayfinding

A blank hazard warning sign is shaped as an equilateral triangle, while a first-aid cross has 4 equal arms.
(a) How many lines of reflective symmetry does the equilateral triangle have?
(b) What is the order of rotational symmetry of the 4-armed first-aid cross?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 State reflective symmetry of an equilateral triangle
An equilateral triangle has 3 equal sides and 3 lines of symmetry.
Criteria: A1: 3 lines of symmetry
Step 2 State rotational symmetry order of the 4-armed equal cross
A 4-armed equal cross matches itself every 90° (4 times in 360°) → Order 4.
Criteria: A1: Order 4
Final Accredited Answer: (a) 3 lines of symmetry | (b) Rotational symmetry of order 4
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
For any regular shape with n equal sides or arms, lines of symmetry = rotational order = n.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1/2)

Vocational Context: Architectural Floor Tiling Design

A designer creates a square floor mosaic that is symmetrical across both the vertical midline and the horizontal midline (4 quadrants).
• In the top-left quadrant, the pattern uses 6 navy tiles and 9 cream tiles.
• Navy tiles cost £2.50 each and cream tiles cost £1.80 each.
Calculate the total number of navy and cream tiles needed for the entire 4-quadrant mosaic, and work out the total tile cost.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Multiply top-left quadrant counts by 4 for the full symmetrical pattern
Total Navy tiles = 4 × 6 = 24 tiles.
Total Cream tiles = 4 × 9 = 36 tiles.
Criteria: M1 / A1: 24 navy and 36 cream tiles
Step 2 Calculate cost of navy tiles and cream tiles
Navy cost = 24 × £2.50 = £60.00.
Cream cost = 36 × £1.80 = £64.80.
Criteria: M1: Individual tile costs
Step 3 Sum for total mosaic cost
£60.00 + £64.80 = £124.80.
Criteria: A1: Accurate total cost (£124.80)
Final Accredited Answer: 24 navy tiles, 36 cream tiles — Total Cost = £124.80
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Symmetry across both horizontal and vertical midlines means all 4 quarters of the design have identical tile counts!

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Writing 'Order 0' for Rotational Symmetry
There is no such thing as rotational symmetry of order 0! Every shape looks identical after a full 360° turn, so the minimum order is 1.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Reflective lines of symmetry can be 0, but Rotational Order is ALWAYS at least 1.
Examiner Pitfall: Drawing Diagonal Lines of Symmetry on a Non-Square Rectangle
A rectangle has only 2 lines of symmetry (horizontal and vertical).
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Only a square or rhombus has diagonal lines of symmetry.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
State the number of lines of reflective symmetry and the order of rotational symmetry for a standard parallelogram.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Lines of reflective symmetry = 0.
2. Order of rotational symmetry = 2 (matches at 180° and 360°).
Final Correct Answer: 0 lines of symmetry; Rotational symmetry of order 2
Examiner Tip: A parallelogram is the classic shape with 0 mirror lines but rotational symmetry of order 2!
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
A regular polygon has an interior angle of 120° and 6 equal sides. How many lines of symmetry does it have, and through how many degrees do you rotate it before it first matches its outline?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. A 6-sided regular hexagon has 6 lines of symmetry.
2. Angle of rotation = 360° ÷ 6 = 60°.
Final Correct Answer: 6 lines of symmetry; 60° rotation
Examiner Tip: Divide 360° by the rotational order (6) to find the smallest rotation angle (60°).
Exam Practice Question 3 2 Marks
On a coordinate grid, point P is at (2, 5). What are the coordinates of the reflected point P′ when reflected across the vertical Y-axis (x = 0)?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Point P(2, 5) is 2 units to the right of the Y-axis.
2. Reflecting across the Y-axis places P′ 2 units to the left at the same height: (−2, 5).
Final Correct Answer: (−2, 5)
Examiner Tip: Reflecting across the vertical Y-axis flips the sign of the x-coordinate while keeping y unchanged.
