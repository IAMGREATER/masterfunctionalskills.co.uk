Specification & Learning Outcomes

Regulated Standards

Candidates are assessed on the following regulated Functional Skills criteria:

  • L1.23: Calculate the volume of cubes and cuboids in everyday workplace and practical contexts.
  • L2.17: Calculate the volume of 3D shapes including cuboids, triangular prisms, and cylinders (using π = 3.142 or given formulas for complex shapes like spheres and cones).
  • L2.18: Solve practical multi-step problems involving capacity and liquid volume conversions (1 cm³ = 1 ml, 1,000 cm³ = 1 litre, 1 m³ = 1,000 litres).
  • State correct 3D volumetric units in final solutions (e.g. cm³, m³, litres).

Golden Rules for Volume of 3D Shapes

Golden Rule 1: Volume is 3D Space — Always State Cubic Units

Volume measures total internal 3D capacity. Answers must always include cubic units (e.g. cm³, m³, mm³). Writing cm or cm² loses accuracy marks on Functional Skills papers!

Examiner Check: Linear = cm • Area = cm² • Volume = cm³
Golden Rule 2: Volume of ANY Uniform Prism = Area of Cross-Section × Length

A prism has identical faces at each end. To find its volume, simply calculate the area of the 2D end face (cross-section) and multiply by how far it extends (length or depth):

General Prism Formula: Volume = Area of Cross-Section × Length (V = A × L)
Golden Rule 3: For Cylinders, Always Use the Radius (r), Never the Diameter

The cylinder formula is V = π r² h. Exam questions frequently provide the diameter (the distance all the way across). You must halve the diameter to find the radius before squaring:

Trap Warning: Diameter = 1.2 m → Radius = 1.2 ÷ 2 = 0.6 m → Area = π × (0.6)²
Golden Rule 4: Master the Liquid Capacity Conversions

Exam questions often ask for capacity in litres:
• 1 cm³ = 1 ml
• 1,000 cm³ = 1 litre
• 1 m³ = 1,000 litres (1 cubic metre holds exactly one thousand litres of water).

Example 1: Volume of a Cuboid (Workplace Storage Container)

An NHS pharmacy distribution crate measures 18 cm in length, 12 cm in width, and 15 cm in height. Calculate the total volume of the crate in cubic centimetres (cm³).

[2 Marks]
Volume of Cuboid = Length × Width × Height = l × w × h
Step 1: Identify the three linear dimensions: Length = 18 cm, Width = 12 cm, Height = 15 cm.
Step 2: Multiply all three dimensions: 18 × 12 × 15 = 3,240.
Step 3: State correct units of volume: cm³.
Final Volume: 3,240 cm³ Full 2 Marks
FS Level 1 FS Level 2 Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Example 2: Volume of a Triangular Prism (Ventilation Duct)

A ventilation duct in an industrial facility has the shape of a triangular prism. The front triangular cross-section has a horizontal base of 6 m and a perpendicular height of 4 m. The prism extends along a length of 14 m. Calculate the volume of air inside the duct.

[3 Marks]
Volume = Cross-Section Area × Length = (½ × b × h) × L
Step 1: Calculate the triangular front face area:
Area = (6 × 4) ÷ 2 = 24 ÷ 2 = 12 m².
Step 2: Multiply cross-sectional area by the length of the duct:
Volume = 12 m² × 14 m = 168 m³.
Step 3: State cubic units: 168 m³.
Final Volume: 168 m³ Full 3 Marks
Level 2 Focus Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Example 3: Volume & Capacity of a Cylinder (Water Storage Tank)

A vertical water storage cylinder has a circular base with diameter 1.2 m and a height of 2.5 m. Using π = 3.142, calculate the total volume of the cylinder in cubic metres (m³), rounded to 2 decimal places. Then state its capacity in litres (1 m³ = 1,000 litres).

[4 Marks]
Volume of Cylinder = π × r² × h  (π = 3.142)
Step 1: Find the radius by halving the diameter:
Radius (r) = 1.2 m ÷ 2 = 0.6 m.
Step 2: Calculate circular base area (π r²):
Base Area = 3.142 × 0.6² = 3.142 × 0.36 = 1.13112 m².
Step 3: Multiply by cylinder height:
Volume = 1.13112 × 2.5 = 2.8278 m³ ≈ 2.83 m³ (to 2 d.p.).
Step 4: Convert to litres (multiply by 1,000):
Capacity = 2.8278 × 1,000 = 2,828 litres (or 2,830 litres).
Volume: 2.83 m³  |  Capacity: 2,828 Litres Full 4 Marks
Level 2 Focus Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Example 4: Compound 3D Solids (Splitting Stepped Shapes)

A warehouse loading dock extension consists of an L-shaped concrete ramp. The structure can be split into two separate cuboids: Block A measures 8 m long, 5 m wide, and 4 m high. Block B measures 6 m long, 3 m wide, and 4 m high. Calculate the total volume of concrete required.

[3 Marks]
Total Volume = Volume of Block A + Volume of Block B
Step 1: Calculate Block A volume:
Volume(A) = 8 m × 5 m × 4 m = 160 m³.
Step 2: Calculate Block B volume:
Volume(B) = 6 m × 3 m × 4 m = 72 m³.
Step 3: Add both volumes together:
Total Volume = 160 m³ + 72 m³ = 232 m³.
Total Volume: 232 m³ Full 3 Marks
Level 2 Focus Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Worked Exam Models

Benchmark Solutions

Exam Model 1: Water Tank Capacity & Filling Rate (Level 2)

A healthcare hydrotherapy tank measures 3.5 m in length, 2 m in width, and 1.8 m in depth. Water is pumped into the empty tank at a steady rate of 420 litres per minute. Given that 1 m³ = 1,000 litres, determine how many minutes it will take to fill the tank completely.

[4 Marks]
Step 1: Calculate total tank volume in cubic metres:
Volume = 3.5 × 2 × 1.8 = 12.6 m³ [M1 Method Mark].
Step 2: Convert cubic metres to litres (multiply by 1,000):
Capacity = 12.6 × 1,000 = 12,600 litres [M1 Method Mark].
Step 3: Divide total litres by pump flow rate:
Time = 12,600 ÷ 420 = 30 minutes [M1 Method Mark].
Step 4: State clear final answer with units: 30 minutes [A1 Accuracy Mark].
Total Filling Time: 30 Minutes Full 4 Marks
Level 2 Focus Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Common Examiner Traps & How to Avoid Them

Trap 1: Forgetting to Divide by 2 on Triangular Prisms

The most frequent calculation loss on Paper 2 is multiplying Base × Height × Length without halving for the triangular cross-section face. Always write (b × h ÷ 2) × L first.

Trap 2: Mixing Centimetres and Metres

Examiners love giving two dimensions in metres and one in centimetres (e.g. Length = 4 m, Width = 2.5 m, Depth = 60 cm). Convert 60 cm = 0.6 m before multiplying. Multiplying mixed units produces a catastrophic answer that forfeits method marks!

Practice Task 1: Medical Sample Insulated Dispatch Box [2 Marks]

An insulated courier box used to transport refrigerated vaccine vials has external dimensions of: length 25 cm, width 16 cm, and height 14 cm.

Calculate the total volume of the box in cubic centimetres (cm³).

Official Tutor Step-by-Step Mark Scheme
2 / 2 Marks Awarded
Step 1 (Multiply Dimensions) M1 Method Mark
25 × 16 × 14 = 5,600
Multiply length by width by height.
Step 2 (Units & Accuracy) A1 Accuracy Mark
5,600 cm³
State correct numerical answer with cubic units (cm³).
Correct Final Answer:
5,600 cm³
Examiner Marking Tip: In non-calculator papers, break the multiplication into easy stages: 25 × 16 = 400; then 400 × 14 = 5,600.

Practice Task 2: Horticultural Glasshouse Roof Air Space [3 Marks]

A community greenhouse has a roof triangular prism section. The front triangular gable end has a base of 8 m and a perpendicular height of 3.5 m. The greenhouse is 12 m long.

Work out the total volume of the roof section in cubic metres (m³).

Official Tutor Step-by-Step Mark Scheme
3 / 3 Marks Awarded
Step 1 (Triangular Face Area) M1 Method Mark
Area = (8 × 3.5) ÷ 2 = 28 ÷ 2 = 14 m²
Calculate the area of the triangular cross-section by dividing the product of base and height by 2.
Step 2 (Prism Volume Calculation) M1 Method Mark
Volume = 14 m² × 12 m = 168 m³
Multiply cross-sectional area by the length of the prism.
Step 3 (Accuracy & Units) A1 Accuracy Mark
168 m³
Correct final answer with cubic metres units.
Correct Final Answer:
168 m³
Examiner Marking Tip: Be sure to write the formula Area = (b × h) / 2 in your exam script. Even if you make an arithmetic error in the final multiplication, writing the formula guarantees M1 method credit.
FS Level 1 FS Level 2 Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE Open Awards Highfield
Practice Task 3: Chemical Storage Cylindrical Drum (Level 2) [4 Marks]

An industrial cleaning supplier manufactures a cylindrical storage drum with: radius 0.4 m and height 1.5 m.

(a) Using π = 3.142, calculate the volume of the drum in cubic metres, rounded to 2 decimal places.
(b) Given that 1 m³ = 1,000 litres, calculate how many whole 25-litre containers can be filled from one completely full drum.

Official Tutor Step-by-Step Mark Scheme
4 / 4 Marks Awarded
Step 1 (Calculate Cylinder Volume) M1 Method Mark
V = 3.142 × 0.4² × 1.5 = 3.142 × 0.16 × 1.5 = 0.75408 m³
Apply cylinder volume formula π × r² × h.
Step 2 (Round Volume to 2 d.p.) A1 Accuracy Mark
0.75 m³
Round correctly: 0.75408 rounds to 0.75 m³.
Step 3 (Convert to Litres) M1 Method Mark
Capacity = 0.75408 × 1,000 = 754.08 litres (or 0.75 × 1,000 = 750 litres)
Multiply cubic metres by 1,000 to convert to capacity in litres.
Step 4 (Whole Containers Filled) A1 Accuracy Mark
754.08 ÷ 25 = 30.16 → Exactly 30 whole containers
Divide capacity by 25. Round DOWN to nearest integer because the remaining 0.16 does not fill a whole container.
Correct Final Answer:
Volume: 0.75 m³  |  30 whole containers
Examiner Marking Tip: On "How many whole containers?" questions, always truncate/round DOWN to the nearest integer. You cannot sell or use a partial container as a whole one!
FS Level 1 FS Level 2 Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE Open Awards Highfield
