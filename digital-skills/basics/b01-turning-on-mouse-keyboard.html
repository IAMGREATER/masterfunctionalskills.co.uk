Step-by-Step Practical Guide

1
1. Switching On, Waking Up & Shutting Down

Open the laptop lid and press the round or marked Power Button once firmly (never hold it down to turn on). Wait 15–30 seconds for the welcome picture or login screen to appear.

When you finish for the day, never slam the lid shut with unsaved work or hold the power button unless the laptop is completely frozen. Click Start → Power Icon → Shut down.

Tutor Tip: Use Sleep for short tea breaks; use Shut down at the end of the day; use Restart when a programme freezes or asks to update.
2
2. Mouse & Laptop Touchpad Buttons

Every mouse and laptop touchpad has two sides: the Left side makes things happen; the Right side shows an extra menu of options.

  • Single Left-Click (1 tap): Clicks buttons, ticks boxes, opens website links, and highlights text or files.
  • Double Left-Click (2 quick taps): Opens folders, programmes, or documents sitting on your Desktop or File Explorer.
  • Single Right-Click (1 right tap): Opens a popup menu with choices like Rename, New Folder, Copy, or Delete.
  • Scroll: Roll the middle wheel (or slide two fingers together on a touchpad) up and down.
Tutor Tip: Most beginners double-click blue website links by mistake — websites and menu buttons only ever need ONE single left-click!
3
3. Essential UK Keyboard Keys (Plus the #1 Beginner Trap)

Before you type an email address or password, know what these five keys do:

  • Shift (hold while tapping a letter): Types ONE capital letter, or types the top symbol shown on number/punctuation keys. On a UK keyboard, Shift + ' (near Enter) gives @.
  • Caps Lock: Locks ALL LETTERS into capitals until pressed again — check the tiny Caps Lock light is OFF before entering passwords!
  • Backspace vs Delete: Backspace (top-right above Enter) erases characters backwards to the LEFT of your flashing line. Delete erases forward to the RIGHT.
  • Enter: Submits a search/login form, or drops down to a new paragraph.
  • Spacebar: Puts a blank space between words (never press Space inside a password or email address!).
Tutor Tip: No extra tip needed — practise on a blank Notepad or sticky note for 2 minutes first if typing feels unfamiliar.

Three Common Beginner Traps

Avoid Trap: Leaving Caps Lock switched ON when signing in

Passphrases and passwords are case-sensitive. Always look for the small Caps Lock indicator light before typing your password.

Avoid Trap: Using Shift + 2 instead of Shift + ' for @

US keyboards put @ on Shift + 2, but standard UK laptops put speech marks " on 2 and @ beside the Enter key.

Avoid Trap: Holding the power button to switch off

Forcing power off can corrupt open documents and stall Windows updates. Only hold for 5 seconds as a last resort if frozen.
