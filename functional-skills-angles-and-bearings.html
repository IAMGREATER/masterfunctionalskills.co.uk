Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • L1.21 / L2.23 – Measure and calculate angles in degrees on straight lines (180°) and around a point (360°).
  • L2.23 – Use 3-figure bearings measured clockwise from North and combine bearings with scale drawings.

Golden Rules for Angles, Bearings & Turn Navigation

Golden Rule 1: Three Rules of Three-Figure Bearings (North • Clockwise • 3 Digits)

1. Always start pointing NORTH (000°). 2. Always measure CLOCKWISE. 3. Always write as THREE FIGURES (e.g. 045°, 008°, 230°).

Golden Rule / Method: From North (000°) • Clockwise • 3 Figures (e.g. 075°)
Golden Rule 2: Reverse (Back) Bearing = Add or Subtract 180°

To find the return bearing from B back to A: if the outward bearing is less than 180°, ADD 180°. If the outward bearing is greater than 180°, SUBTRACT 180° (and pad with a leading zero if under 100°).

Golden Rule / Method: Outward < 180° → +180° • Outward > 180° → −180°

Core Skill 1: Angles on a Straight Line (180°), Around a Point (360°) & Vertically Opposite

Core deductive angle laws for workplace junctions and security sweeps.

  • Angles on a Straight Line: Sum to 180°.
  • Angles Around a Full Point: Sum to 360°.
  • Right Angle Split: Angles inside a perpendicular corner sum to 90°.
  • Vertically Opposite Angles: Where two straight lines cross, opposite angles are equal.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Measuring Bearings Clockwise from North (Including Westward Reflex Angles)

Converting anti-clockwise angles West of North into 3-figure clockwise bearings.

If a diagram shows an angle of 55° anti-clockwise (West) from North:

  • Because bearings MUST be measured clockwise from North, subtract 55° from a full 360° circle: 360° − 55° = 305°.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Combining Bearings with Map Scale Ratios

Plotting a target location using a protractor bearing and a ruler scale distance.

  1. Step 1 — Angle: Place your protractor center on the starting town with 000° aligned on the North arrow, and mark the bearing clockwise.
  2. Step 2 — Distance: Convert the real distance into cm using the map scale (e.g. 12 km at 1 cm : 2 km = 6 cm) and rule the line to that exact length.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1/2)

Vocational Context: Emergency Medical Helicopter Dispatch

An emergency transfer helicopter flies from Helipad H on a bearing of 074° to an accident site A.
(a) State whether 074° is an acute, right, obtuse, or reflex angle.
(b) Calculate the 3-figure back bearing required to fly from accident site A back to Helipad H.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Classify 074°
Since 074° is between 0° and 90°, it is an ACUTE angle.
Criteria: A1: Acute angle
Step 2 Add 180° for the reverse bearing
074° + 180° = 254°.
Criteria: M1 / A1: Return bearing = 254°
Final Accredited Answer: (a) Acute angle | (b) 254°
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Always write bearings with 3 figures (074° and 254°).
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2)

Vocational Context: Maritime Engineering Navigation

A maintenance vessel sails from Harbour P to Turbine T. The angle measured anti-clockwise (West) from North is 65°, and the real-world distance is 28 km.
(a) Calculate the 3-figure clockwise bearing of Turbine T from Harbour P.
(b) Calculate the 3-figure back bearing of Harbour P from Turbine T.
(c) On a sea chart with scale 1 : 400,000 (where 1 cm = 4 km), how many cm from P should Turbine T be plotted?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Subtract 65° from 360° for the clockwise bearing from P
360° − 65° = 295°.
Criteria: M1 / A1: Outward bearing = 295°
Step 2 Subtract 180° for the back bearing from T to P
295° − 180° = 115°.
Criteria: A1: Back bearing = 115°
Step 3 Divide 28 km by 4 km/cm for the chart distance
28 km ÷ 4 km = 7.0 cm.
Criteria: A1: Chart distance = 7.0 cm
Final Accredited Answer: (a) 295° | (b) 115° | (c) 7.0 cm
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Whenever an angle is given to the West (left) of North, subtract it from 360° to get the clockwise bearing.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Omitting the Leading Zero on Bearings Under 100°
Writing 65° instead of 065° loses the bearing accuracy mark.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always write 3 digits for every bearing.
Examiner Pitfall: Measuring from the Wrong End ("Bearing of A FROM B")
Measuring the angle at A instead of at B when asked for "the bearing of A from B".
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Circle the letter after the word "FROM"—that is where your North line and protractor must sit!

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A straight hospital corridor is met by an angled fire-exit passage. One angle at the wall is 128°. Calculate the acute angle next to it on the straight wall.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Angles on a straight line sum to 180°.
2. 180° − 128° = 52°.
Final Correct Answer: 52°
Examiner Tip: 180° − 128° = 52°.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
A delivery drone flies from Depot D to Drop-Zone Z on a bearing of 242°. Calculate the 3-figure back bearing from Drop-Zone Z to Depot D.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Since 242° > 180°, subtract 180°: 242° − 180° = 62°.
2. Pad to 3 digits: 062°.
Final Correct Answer: 062°
Examiner Tip: Remember the leading zero: 062°!
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
Three cables meet at a central mast point. The angles between the first two pairs of cables are 115° and 140°. Calculate the third angle around the point.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Sum of known angles = 115° + 140° = 255°.
2. Third angle = 360° − 255° = 105°.
Final Correct Answer: 105°
Examiner Tip: Angles around a full point always add up to 360°.
