Part 1: Quick-Check Answer Key
Quick answers for practice questions
Q1 Question 1: Cutting Wordy Filler Phrases
(a) 'Due to the fact that' → Because
(b) 'At this moment in time' → Currently / Now
(c) 'In the near future' → Soon / Shortly
(d) 'In order to' → To
(e) 'Has the ability to' → Can
(f) 'Despite the fact that' → Although
Q2 Question 2: Active vs Passive Voice
(a) The nurse administered the medication. → Active (nurse performs action)
(b) The medication was administered by the nurse. → Passive
(c) The committee approved the budget proposal. → Active
(d) The fire alarm was tested by the safety officer. → Passive
(e) Security inspected all visitor badges at reception. → Active
Q3 Question 3: Clarity, Conciseness, and Eliminating Redundancy
(a) 'At this moment in time, we are currently experiencing a slight shortage of supplies.' → 'We currently have a shortage of supplies.' (eliminates 'at this moment in time', 'slight')
(b) 'The manager made a final decision that was completely unanimous among all team members.' → 'The team unanimously decided.' (removes 'final decision', 'completely unanimous among all')
(c) 'Please refer back to the previous prior email that was sent to you yesterday.' → 'Please refer to yesterday's email.' (removes 'back', 'previous prior', 'that was sent to you')
(d) 'In order to successfully achieve our goals, it is essential and necessary that we collaborate together.' → 'To achieve our goals, we must collaborate.' (removes 'in order to', 'successfully', 'essential and necessary', 'together')
(e) 'During the month of November, we will hold a training workshop on the date of November 14th.' → 'We will hold a training workshop on 14th November.' (removes redundant 'month of', 'on the date of')
(f) 'The fire doors must be kept completely shut and closed at all times throughout the day.' → 'Fire doors must remain closed at all times.' (removes 'completely shut and', 'throughout the day')
(g) 'I am writing this email to let you know that I am writing in regard to your query.' → 'I am writing in response to your query.' (removes circular throat-clearing)
(h) 'He was an unexpected surprise visitor who arrived without any prior warning.' → 'He was a surprise visitor who arrived without warning.' (removes 'unexpected', 'prior')
Q4 Question 4: Structuring Workplace Emails for Maximum Actionability
(a) 'URGENT: Water Isolation Valve Shutdown - Building 3 - Friday 18th Oct' → Subject line (clear, specific, and prioritised)
(b) 'Due to scheduled maintenance on the main pressure main, water will be turned off.' → Context / Background rationale
(c) 'Please ensure all clinical autoclaves and water coolers are disconnected by 16:00.' → Action item (specific directive for recipients)
(d) 'All departmental responses confirming shutdown must be received by 12:00 tomorrow.' → Deadline (clear time boundary for compliance)
(e) 'Kind regards, Sarah Jenkins, Facilities & Maintenance Lead' → Professional sign-off with job title and contact identity
(f) 'If you have critical clinical equipment requiring continuous supply, contact extension 404.' → Contingency / Exception handling contact
(g) 'Attached is the floor-by-floor shutdown timetable for your reference.' → Reference to supplementary documentation / attachments
(h) 'Summary: All staff to vacate laboratory 2 between 14:00 and 16:00 on Wednesday.' → Executive summary / Key takeaway highlight
Q5 Question 5: Effective Written De-escalation in Customer Service
(a) 'I completely understand how frustrating it must have been to wait 45 minutes for your order.' → Validation (acknowledges and respects customer feelings)
(b) 'Our delivery driver took an incorrect route, and we take full responsibility for this delay.' → Accountability (owns the mistake without making excuses)
(c) 'I have immediately processed a full refund and dispatched a complimentary replacement via courier.' → Solution (takes immediate, tangible remedial action)
(d) 'I will personally track this package and text you as soon as the courier is 10 minutes away.' → Reassurance / Personal ownership (restores customer confidence)
(e) 'Thank you for bringing this hygiene issue to our direct attention so we could address it with staff.' → Appreciation (reframes a complaint as constructive feedback)
(f) 'You have been a loyal trade client for six years, and your continued trust is our highest priority.' → Relationship affirmation (validates customer value and loyalty)
(g) 'Let us review the invoice line by line together right now to correct any billing errors.' → Collaborative problem solving
(h) 'I assure you that all our catering staff have been retrained on allergen protocols this morning.' → Systemic reassurance (confirms future prevention)
Part 2: Real-Life Problem Solutions
Step-by-step methods and mark breakdown
Question 1 Worked Solution Estates Safety Briefing Memo
[3 Marks Available]
Read this poorly structured sentence from a workplace maintenance briefing:
'It has come to our attention that people are leaving doors open because of the fact that they don't know the code, which creates a huge security problem for the whole building at this moment in time.'

Rewrite this message so that it is concise, authoritative, and professionally phrased in two clear sentences.
Cut filler phrases ('because of the fact that', 'at this moment in time')
[W1]
Split into two distinct, punchy sentences: Issue statement + Action/Risk statement
[W1]
Maintain professional authority and clarity
[A1]
Correct Final Answer: 'Security fire doors are frequently being left propped open due to staff unfamiliarity with the access codes. This practice poses a serious security hazard and must cease immediately.'
Quick Check Method: Concise, eliminates redundancy, clear instruction.
Common Mistake to Avoid: Leaving the sentence as one long, breathless clause.
