Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate complete mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes across Pearson Edexcel, City & Guilds, and NCFE:

  • Level 1 L1.9 • Level 2 L2.8: Find fractions of whole number quantities, measurements, or currency amounts under non-calculator and calculator conditions.
  • Level 2 L2.9: Add, subtract, multiply, and divide proper fractions, improper fractions, and mixed numbers, finding common denominators where required.
  • Level 2 Problem Solving: Express one quantity as a simplified fraction of another, calculate original totals from fractional parts (reverse fractions), and solve multi-step workforce budget problems.

Golden Rules for Fractions & Mixed Numbers

Golden Rule 1: Fraction of an Amount: Divide by Bottom, Multiply by Top

To find any fractional part of a workplace budget or measurement, divide the total amount by the denominator (bottom number) to find 1 part, then multiply by the numerator (top number) to scale up.

Golden Rule / Method: Amount ÷ Denominator (Bottom) × Numerator (Top)
Golden Rule 2: The Common Denominator Law: Never Add Denominators Together!

You can only add or subtract fractions when the slice sizes are identical! Find the Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) of the denominators first. Never write 1/4 + 1/4 = 2/8; denominators tell you slice size and stay unchanged: 1/4 + 1/4 = 2/4 = 1/2.

Golden Rule / Method: Equal Denominators Required • Add / Subtract Numerators ONLY
Golden Rule 3: Multiply Straight Across • Divide with Keep-Change-Flip (KCF)

For multiplication: multiply top × top and bottom × bottom directly. For division: Keep the first fraction, Change ÷ into ×, and Flip the second fraction upside down (reciprocal). Always convert mixed numbers to improper fractions before multiplying or dividing!

Golden Rule / Method: Multiplication = Direct Top × Top / Bottom × Bottom • Division = KCF

Core Skill 2: Adding & Subtracting Fractions & Mixed Numbers

Converting mixed numbers, finding the Lowest Common Multiple (LCM), and combining numerators.

WORKED METHOD: 4-STAGE FRACTION SUBTRACTION
Worked Calculation: 234 − 125
Stage 1: Convert to Top-Heavy (Improper) Fractions
234 = (2×4)+34
= 114
125 = (1×5)+25
= 75
Stage 2 & 3: Lowest Common Denominator (LCM = 20)
11 × 54 × 5 − 7 × 45 × 4
= 5520 − 2820 = 2720
= 1720 ✓
STEP 1 Make Fractions Top-Heavy
2 3/4 → 11/4  |  1 2/5 → 7/5
Multiply whole number by denominator, add the numerator: 2 × 4 + 3 = 11. Keeps calculations foolproof!
STEP 2 Find Common Denominator (LCM)
LCM of 4 and 5 is 20
Multiply 11/4 by 5 top and bottom (55/20). Multiply 7/5 by 4 top and bottom (28/20). Denominators are now matched!
STEP 3 Subtract Numerators Only
55 − 28 = 27 (over 20)
The denominator stays 20 — never subtract denominators! Result: 27/20.
STEP 4 Convert to Mixed Number
27 ÷ 20 = 1 remainder 7 → 1 7/20
20 goes into 27 once with 7 left over. Final accredited answer: 1 7/20 ✓
Subi's Assessor Golden Rule: Never Subtract Denominators!

"A classic examiner trap is writing 55/20 − 28/20 = 27/0! Denominators simply tell you the size of each slice. When the slice sizes match, only subtract the top numbers."

Core Skill 3: Multiplying & Dividing Fractions (Keep • Change • Flip)

Fast rules for direct multiplication, reciprocal division (KCF), and cross-simplifying.

MULTIPLICATION: DIRECT
Worked: 34 × 25
34 × 25 = 3 × 24 × 5 = 620
Simplify (÷ 2): 310 ✓
Rule: Multiply top × top and bottom × bottom directly. No common denominators needed! Always simplify by dividing top and bottom by their common factor (2).
DIVISION: KEEP-CHANGE-FLIP
Worked: 34 ÷ 25
1. KEEP 3/4 2. CHANGE to × 3. FLIP to 5/2
34 × 52 = 3 × 54 × 2 = 158
Mixed Number: 178 ✓
Rule: Keep first fraction intact. Turn division into multiplication. Flip second fraction upside down (reciprocal). Multiply across and convert to mixed number.
Sonya's Fraction Rule: Mixed Numbers MUST Become Improper First!

"If an exam question asks you to multiply or divide mixed numbers (e.g. 2 1/2 × 1 3/4), NEVER try to multiply the whole numbers and fractions separately! Always turn both into top-heavy fractions (5/2 × 7/4 = 35/8 = 4 3/8) to guarantee all method marks [M1]."

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1 & Level 2)

Vocational Context: Residential Care Workforce Scheduling

A residential care provider employs a total of 72 care workers.
• On night duty, 38 of the workforce are deployed.
• Of the remaining staff, 23 work the day shift.
• All other staff members are on scheduled annual leave.

How many staff members are on scheduled annual leave?
Step & Focus
Visual Calculation Board & Method
Mark Criteria
Step 1 Calculate staff on night duty (3/8 of 72) Divide by 8, multiply by 3
72 ÷ 8 = 9 staff (Value of 1/8)
9 × 3 = 27 staff on night duty
27 care workers are allocated to cover overnight duty.
[M1] 72 ÷ 8 × 3 = 27 staff
Step 2 Find remaining staff & day shift (2/3 of 45) Crucial "Remaining" subtraction
Remaining staff: 72 − 27 = 45 staff
Day shift: 45 ÷ 3 = 15  →  15 × 2 = 30 staff on day shift
The fraction 2/3 must be taken from the 45 remaining staff, NOT the initial 72!
[M1] 45 ÷ 3 × 2 = 30 staff
Step 3 Calculate staff on scheduled leave Final subtraction
45 − 30 = 15 staff on leave
(Check: 27 night + 30 day + 15 leave = 72 total ✓)
[A1] 15 staff on leave
Final Accredited Answer: 15 care workers are on scheduled annual leave.
Assessor Method Checklist: Spotting the 'Remaining' Keyword
The single most common mistake in workforce fraction questions is calculating 2/3 of 72 (= 48), which loses both subsequent marks. Always highlight words like "of the remainder" or "remaining staff" in the exam question stem!
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2 Multi-Step Problem Solving)

Vocational Context: Healthcare Facility Estates & Parking Allocation

A healthcare campus redevelops its main parking facility containing 240 vehicle spaces:
• 16 of total spaces are reserved for emergency on-call clinicians.
• 38 of total spaces are reserved for clinical nursing staff.
• 15 of total spaces are designated as electric vehicle (EV) charging bays.
• All remaining spaces are open for general outpatient visitors.

The estates manager requires at least 65 spaces available for outpatient visitors. Does this layout plan meet the requirement?
Show all calculations and support your answer with numerical evidence.
Step & Focus
Visual Calculation Board & Method
Mark Criteria
Step 1 Calculate clinician and nursing allocations 1/6 and 3/8 of 240
Emergency Clinicians: 240 ÷ 6 = 40 spaces
Nursing Staff: (240 ÷ 8) × 3 = 30 × 3 = 90 spaces
[M1] 40 and 90 spaces calculated
Step 2 Calculate electric vehicle (EV) bays 1/5 of 240
EV Charging Bays: 240 ÷ 5 = 48 spaces
[M1] 240 ÷ 5 = 48 spaces
Step 3 Total reserved spaces and find remaining visitor spaces Sum & subtract from 240
Total Reserved: 40 + 90 + 48 = 178 spaces
Visitor Spaces: 240 − 178 = 62 spaces
[M1] 240 − 178 = 62 visitor spaces
Step 4 Compare with 65-space requirement & conclude Formal statement with evidence
62 spaces available < 65 spaces required
Shortfall: Exactly 3 spaces short of the target.
[A1] Clear "No" supported by 62 < 65
Final Accredited Answer: NO, the redevelopment plan does not meet the requirement.
(Only 62 spaces remain for outpatient visitors, which is 3 spaces below the required 65).
Assessor Check: Fraction Common Denominator Alternative
Notice that all three fractions were taken from the total 240 spaces (unlike Model 1 where the second fraction was of the remainder). You can also add the fractions: 1/6 (20/120) + 3/8 (45/120) + 1/5 (24/120) = 89/120 allocated. Remaining = 31/120 of 240 = 62 spaces!

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Trap 1: Adding Denominators Together
Writing 1/4 + 1/4 = 2/8 loses all accuracy marks. Denominators describe the slice size! When denominators are identical, only add the top numbers: 1/4 + 1/4 = 2/4 = 1/2.
How to Avoid: Never add or subtract denominators; only add or subtract numerators over a common denominator.
Trap 2: Ignoring 'Of the Remaining'
In multi-step questions, if 3/8 of 72 are on night duty, the second fraction (2/3) is applied to the remaining 45 staff, NOT the original 72!
How to Avoid: Highlight the word 'remaining' and perform the subtraction BEFORE calculating the next fraction.
Trap 3: Multiplying Whole Numbers & Fractions Separately
Writing 2 1/2 × 1 1/4 = 2 1/8 is completely wrong! You must convert both into improper fractions first (5/2 × 5/4 = 25/8 = 3 1/8).
How to Avoid: Always convert mixed numbers to improper fractions BEFORE multiplying or dividing!

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Calculate 135 + 214 without a calculator. Give your answer as a mixed number in its simplest form.
Marking Scheme Steps:
Step 1: Common denominator for 5 and 4 is 20 [M1]
3/5 = 12/20  |  1/4 = 5/20
Step 2: Add whole numbers: 1 + 2 = 3
Step 3: Add fractions: 12/20 + 5/20 = 17/20
Combined = 3 17/20 ✓ [A1]
Final Correct Answer: 3 17/20
Examiner Tip: Keeping whole numbers and fractions separate avoids huge improper numerators when adding!
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
Work out 214 ÷ 35 without a calculator. Give your answer as a mixed number in its simplest form.
Marking Scheme Steps:
Step 1: 2 1/4 = 9/4 (Improper fraction)
Step 2: Keep-Change-Flip → 9/4 × 5/3 = 45/12 [M1]
Step 3: Simplify by 3 → 15/4
Step 4: Mixed number → 15 ÷ 4 = 3 3/4 [A1]
Final Correct Answer: 3 3/4
Examiner Tip: You can also cross-cancel 9 and 3 before multiplying: (3/4) × (5/1) = 15/4 = 3 3/4.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A charity development grant of £1,800 is split between three community projects:
• Project A receives 49 of the total grant.
• Project B receives 13 of the total grant.
• Project C receives all remaining funds.

How much money does Project C receive?
Marking Scheme Steps:
Project A: (1,800 ÷ 9) × 4 = 200 × 4 = £800 [M1]
Project B: 1,800 ÷ 3 = £600 [M1]
Total A + B: £800 + £600 = £1,400
Project C: £1,800 − £1,400 = £400 [A1]
Final Correct Answer: £400
Examiner Check: Fractions check: 4/9 + 3/9 = 7/9, leaving 2/9 for Project C. (1,800 ÷ 9) × 2 = £400 ✓.
