Golden Rules for Coordinates: Positive & Four Quadrants

Golden Rule 1: 'Along the Corridor (X), Then Up or Down the Stairs (Y)'

Coordinates are always written as an ordered pair (x, y). The first number is the horizontal X-axis (right is +, left is −). The second number is the vertical Y-axis (up is +, down is −).

Golden Rule / Method: (x, y) = (Horizontal Left/Right, Vertical Up/Down)
Golden Rule 2: Midpoint Formula = Average the X-Values, Average the Y-Values

To find the exact halfway point (midpoint) between two coordinates (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂): add the two x-coordinates and divide by 2, then add the two y-coordinates and divide by 2.

Golden Rule / Method: Midpoint = ((x₁ + x₂) ÷ 2 , (y₁ + y₂) ÷ 2)

Core Skill 1: Navigating the Four Quadrants from the Origin (0, 0)

Understanding positive and negative signs in each of the four Cartesian quadrants.

  • Origin (0, 0): The exact intersection where the X-axis and Y-axis cross.
  • Quadrant 1 (Top-Right): (+x, +y) e.g. (4, 3) — Right 4, Up 3.
  • Quadrant 2 (Top-Left): (−x, +y) e.g. (−4, 3) — Left 4, Up 3.
  • Quadrant 3 (Bottom-Left): (−x, −y) e.g. (−4, −3) — Left 4, Down 3.
  • Quadrant 4 (Bottom-Right): (+x, −y) e.g. (4, −3) — Right 4, Down 3.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Finding Missing Vertices of Rectangles, Squares & Parallelograms

Using coordinate alignment rules to deduce the 4th corner without guessing.

For an axis-aligned rectangle, the four corners always share two pairs of x-coordinates and two pairs of y-coordinates:

  • If three corners are A(−2, 5), B(6, 5), and C(6, −1):
  • X-values are −2, 6, 6 → missing x-coordinate must be −2.
  • Y-values are 5, 5, −1 → missing y-coordinate must be −1.
  • Therefore, the fourth corner D is (−2, −1).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Calculating Midpoints & Grid Distances Across Zero

Finding horizontal/vertical side lengths and central sensor coordinates.

  • Distance Across Zero: From x = −3 to x = 5 is a total distance of 3 + 5 = 8 grid units.
  • Midpoint Formula Example: Midpoint of (−4, 2) and (6, −8) = ((−4 + 6) ÷ 2, (2 + −8) ÷ 2) = (2 ÷ 2, −6 ÷ 2) = (1, −3).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1/2)

Vocational Context: Automated Warehouse Robotics Grid

An automated warehouse dispatch floor is mapped on a Cartesian coordinate grid. Three charging bays form three corners of a rectangle:
• Bay A: (−3, 2)
• Bay B: (5, 2)
• Bay C: (5, −4)
(a) State the coordinates of the fourth corner, Bay D.
(b) Calculate the area of the rectangle in square grid units.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Align x and y coordinate pairs for the 4th corner D
X-coordinates pair as −3 and 5; Y-coordinates pair as 2 and −4. Bay D = (−3, −4).
Criteria: A1: Accurate 4th vertex coordinates (−3, −4)
Step 2 Calculate horizontal length and vertical width across axes
Length (from −3 to 5) = 3 + 5 = 8 units.
Width (from −4 to 2) = 4 + 2 = 6 units.
Criteria: M1: Accurate side lengths (8 and 6 units)
Step 3 Multiply length by width for area
Area = 8 × 6 = 48 square units.
Criteria: A1: Accurate area (48 square units)
Final Accredited Answer: (a) Bay D = (−3, −4) | (b) Area = 48 square units
Sonya’s Examiner Tip Examiner Method
Notice how the x-coordinates pair up (−3, −3 and 5, 5) and the y-coordinates pair up (2, 2 and −4, −4).
Benchmark Exam Model 2 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2)

Vocational Context: Healthcare IT Network Infrastructure

On a clinical facility floor plan grid where 1 grid unit = 2.5 metres, Nursing Station P is at (−6, 4) and Pharmacy Terminal Q is at (2, −2).
(a) A relay router is installed at the exact midpoint M between P and Q. Find the coordinates of M.
(b) The straight-line distance from P to Q is 10 grid units. Calculate the real-world cabling length if an extra 15% is added for vertical drops.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate midpoint M by averaging x and y coordinates
x = (−6 + 2) ÷ 2 = −4 ÷ 2 = −2.
y = (4 + −2) ÷ 2 = 2 ÷ 2 = 1.
Midpoint M = (−2, 1).
Criteria: M1 / A1: Accurate midpoint coordinates (−2, 1)
Step 2 Convert 10 grid units to real metres and add 15%
Real distance = 10 × 2.5 m = 25 metres.
With 15% extra = 25 m × 1.15 = 28.75 metres.
Criteria: M1 / A1: Accurate real cable length (28.75 m)
Final Accredited Answer: (a) Midpoint M = (−2, 1) | (b) Cable length = 28.75 metres
Subi’s Assessor Pro-Tip Assessor Guidance
When averaging a negative and positive number, be careful with signs: −6 + 2 = −4, and −4 ÷ 2 = −2.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Swapping X and Y Coordinates
Writing (2, −3) instead of (−3, 2) plots the point in Quadrant 4 instead of Quadrant 2.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always write (x, y) above your answer box and check Horizontal first, Vertical second.
Examiner Pitfall: Confusing Points on the Axes: (0, 4) vs (4, 0)
(4, 0) lies on the horizontal X-axis; (0, 4) lies on the vertical Y-axis.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: For (0, 4): move 0 along the corridor, then go 4 straight up the stairs.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Calculate the coordinates of the midpoint between point P(−4, 6) and point Q(8, −2).
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Midpoint x = (−4 + 8) ÷ 2 = 4 ÷ 2 = 2.
2. Midpoint y = (6 + −2) ÷ 2 = 4 ÷ 2 = 2.
3. Midpoint = (2, 2).
Final Correct Answer: (2, 2)
Examiner Tip: Add the x-coordinates and divide by 2; add the y-coordinates and divide by 2.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Three vertices of a square on a coordinate grid are (−1, −1), (4, −1), and (4, 4).
(a) State the coordinates of the fourth vertex.
(b) Calculate the perimeter of the square in grid units.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Missing fourth vertex aligns with x = −1 and y = 4 → (−1, 4).
2. Side length = 4 − (−1) = 5 units.
3. Perimeter = 4 × 5 = 20 grid units.
Final Correct Answer: (a) (−1, 4) | (b) 20 grid units
Examiner Tip: From −1 to 4 is 5 units (1 unit to zero plus 4 units past zero).
Exam Practice Question 3 2 Marks
A drone starts at coordinates (−2, 3). It flies 7 units East (right) and 5 units South (down). What are its new coordinates?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. New x-coordinate = −2 + 7 = 5.
2. New y-coordinate = 3 − 5 = −2.
3. New coordinates = (5, −2).
Final Correct Answer: (5, −2)
Examiner Tip: East increases X (+7); South decreases Y (−5).
