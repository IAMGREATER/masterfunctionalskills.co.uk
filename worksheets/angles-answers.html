Angles, Bearings & Turn Navigation Pearson Edexcel: Pearson Edexcel L1 & L2 Mathematics
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (Warm-Up) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Angles on a Straight Line & Around a Point) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A security camera mounted on a flat wall rotates through three adjacent angles along a straight 180° arc.
Sector A is 64° and Sector B is 78°. Calculate the angle of Sector C.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Sum known angles on the straight line: 64° + 78° = 142° [M1]
2. Subtract from 180°: 180° - 142° = 38° [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 38°
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Subtracting from 360° instead of 180° for angles on a straight line.
🔄 Quick Check: 64° + 78° + 38° = 180°.
Question 2 (Level 1 Foundation — 2 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Two straight conveyor tracks cross each other. One of the four angles formed at the intersection is 64°. Calculate the size of the obtuse angle next to it on the straight line.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Angles on a straight line sum to 180°. [M1]
2. 180° − 64° = 116°. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 116°
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Writing "74°" instead of "074°" forfeits the bearing accuracy mark.
🔄 Quick Check: Check: 116° + 64° = 180°.
Section B: Everyday & Workplace Maths Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (CCTV Security Camera Sweep Angle Calculation (Level 1 Foundation)) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A 360° panoramic security dome camera is divided into four monitoring zones around a central point. Zone A covers 95°, Zone B covers 110°, and Zone C covers 82°.
(a) Calculate the angle covered by Zone D.
(b) State whether Zone D is an acute, obtuse, or reflex angle.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Sum the three known zone angles: 95° + 110° + 82° = 287°. [M1]
2. Subtract from 360° (full turn around a point): 360° − 287° = 73°. [A1]
3. Classify 73°: Since 73° is less than 90°, it is an ACUTE angle. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) Zone D = 73° | (b) Acute angle
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Always pad bearings under 100° with a leading zero (e.g. 009°, 045°, 074°).
🔄 Quick Check: Angles around a central point always add up to a full circle of 360°.
Question 4 (Workplace Application — 2 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A coastguard lifeboat sails from Harbour P to a fishing vessel Q on a bearing of 218°. Calculate the 3-figure bearing of Harbour P from vessel Q.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Since 218° > 180°, subtract 180°: 218° − 180° = 38°. [M1]
2. Write in mandatory 3-figure format: 038°. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 038°
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Measuring 60° West of North and writing 060° instead of 360° − 60° = 300°.
🔄 Quick Check: Do not forget the leading zero on 038°!
Section C: Exam-Style Problem Solving Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Air Ambulance Flight Navigation & Back Bearing (Level 2 Multi-Step)) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
An emergency air ambulance takes off from Hospital Helipad H and flies 45 km on a bearing of 074° to an incident site A.
(a) Calculate the 3-figure back bearing required to fly directly from incident site A back to Hospital Helipad H.
(b) On a radar map with scale 1 cm : 6 km, what is the plotted distance between H and A in cm?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Apply the back-bearing rule for 074° (< 180°): Since 074° is less than 180°, add 180°: 074° + 180° = 254°. [M1]
2. State return bearing in 3-figure format: Return bearing from A to H = 254°. [A1]
3. Calculate radar map distance using scale 1 cm : 6 km: 45 km ÷ 6 km = 7.5 cm. [M1 / A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) Return bearing = 254° | (b) Map distance = 7.5 cm
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: If given an angle West of North (anti-clockwise), subtract it from 360° to get the clockwise bearing.
🔄 Quick Check: Always check which location comes after the word "FROM": "bearing of H FROM A" means stand at A, face North, and turn clockwise.
Question 6 (Level 2 Multi-Step Problem Solving — 3 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A radar dish points due North (000°). It rotates 135° clockwise, then rotates 48° anti-clockwise. What is its final 3-figure bearing?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Clockwise turn from 000° = 135°. [M1]
2. Anti-clockwise turn subtracts angle: 135° − 48° = 87°. [P1]
3. Write as 3-figure bearing: 087°. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 087°
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Stating a conclusion without showing the full numerical comparison values.
🔄 Quick Check: Clockwise = Add (+); Anti-clockwise = Subtract (−).
