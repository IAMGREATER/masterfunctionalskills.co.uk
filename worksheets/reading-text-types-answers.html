Text Types, Genre Conventions & Target Audience Pearson Edexcel: L1.16 • L2.16 • L2.17
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (The Basics) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Level 1 Identifying Text Genres from Structural Fingerprints) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Identify the exact text type (genre) of each extract below from its formatting clues:
Extract A: 'Thread: Best cordless combi drill under £120? | Posted by @ChippyPete (Yesterday at 19:14) [Reply] [Like (12)]'
Extract B: '14 Oak Lane, Bristol | 12 October 2026 | Dear Mr Henderson, Re: Boundary Wall Repair Quote... Yours sincerely, A. Kowalski'
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Extract A: Online discussion forum / message board thread (confirmed by 'Thread:', '@ChippyPete', timestamp, and '[Reply] [Like]' buttons) [A1]
2. Extract B: Formal business letter (confirmed by postal address, date, 'Dear Mr Henderson', 'Re:' line, and 'Yours sincerely') [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Extract A: Online discussion forum thread; Extract B: Formal business letter.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Calling Extract A 'an email' or 'a blog' when it has a thread title, username handle, and Reply/Like buttons.
🔄 Quick Check: Forum = Thread + @username + Reply; Formal letter = Address + Dear Mr X + Yours sincerely.
Question 2 (Level 1 Pinpointing the Specific Target Audience) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Read this extract from a community leaflet:
'Are you aged 60 or over and finding your garden lawn too heavy to mow? Our vetted volunteer gardeners offer free fortnightly grass cutting for retired householders across the borough.'

Identify the specific target audience for this leaflet and give two clues from the text that prove it.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Define specific target audience: Retired householders / older residents aged 60 or over who have a garden and struggle with mowing [A1]
2. Extract two textual clues: 'aged 60 or over' and 'retired householders across the borough' (or 'finding your garden lawn too heavy to mow') [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Target audience: Retired homeowners/residents aged 60+ who struggle to mow their lawn. Clues: 'aged 60 or over' and 'retired householders'.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Writing a vague answer like 'everyone in the borough' or 'people who like gardening'.
🔄 Quick Check: Always include age/role specifics ('retired householders aged 60+') when given in the text.
Section B: Real-Life & Workplace Practice Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Level 1/2 Distinguishing Formal Reports from Magazine Articles) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Look at the structural features of two workplace documents:
Document 1: Titled 'Q3 Warehouse Forklift Incident Investigation', divided into 1.0 Executive Summary, 2.0 Audit Findings, 3.0 Cost Breakdown Table, 4.0 Mandatory Recommendations.
Document 2: Headlined 'Forklift Futures: Why AI Sensors Are Taking Over the Aisle!' by Sarah Jenkins, featuring a catchy strapline, two-column prose, and a boxed pull quote.

Identify the genre of Document 1 and Document 2, and explain how their purposes differ.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Document 1 genre: Formal workplace investigation report (numbered sections 1.0–4.0, Findings, Recommendations) [A1]
2. Document 2 genre: Trade magazine / newspaper feature article (punny headline, byline, columns, pull quote) [A1]
3. Contrast purposes: Document 1 aims to objectively investigate warehouse accidents and mandate safety actions for management, whereas Document 2 aims to engage and inform industry readers about new AI forklift trends [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Doc 1: Formal workplace report (to investigate incidents and recommend actions for managers). Doc 2: Magazine article (to engage and inform readers about new AI forklift technology).
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Calling both documents 'articles' when Document 1 has numbered Executive Summary, Findings, and Recommendations.
🔄 Quick Check: Findings + Recommendations = Report; Headline + Byline + Pull Quote = Article.
Question 4 (Level 2 Analysing Standard Operating Procedures / Instructional Texts) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Read this extract from a dental surgery wall card:
AUTOCLAVE STERILISATION PROTOCOL (SOP-12)
Warning: Wear heat-resistant gauntlets.
1. Load unwrapped steel instruments onto perforated trays.
2. Select Cycle B (134°C at 2.1 bar pressure for 3 minutes).
3. Sign and staple the printout strip to the daily clinical logbook.

Identify the text type, state its exact purpose, and explain why it is unsuitable for a general patient waiting room.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify text type: Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) / technical instruction manual [A1]
2. State exact purpose: To instruct dental staff step-by-step on how to sterilise surgical instruments safely and legally in the autoclave [A1]
3. Explain audience unsuitability: Uses specialist technical specifications ('134°C at 2.1 bar', 'Cycle B') and imperative staff duties that only trained dental nurses/technicians perform, not patients [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Text Type: Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) / instruction card. Purpose: To instruct dental staff on sterilising instruments safely. Unsuitable for patients because it contains technical operating steps ('134°C at 2.1 bar') meant only for trained clinical staff.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Stating the purpose as simply 'to instruct' without mentioning sterilising dental instruments.
🔄 Quick Check: Check that all three parts (Genre, Purpose with topic, Audience reason) are answered.
Section C: Exam-Style Tasks Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Level 2 Evaluating Dual-Purpose Texts) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Read this extract from a leaflet sent to householders by 'GreenHome Insulation Ltd':
'Did you know that 35% of your home's heat escapes straight through an uninsulated loft? Under the 2026 Great British Insulation Scheme, homes in Council Tax Bands A–D qualify for up to £1,500 towards mineral wool loft lagging. Do not shiver through another expensive winter — book your free 20-minute roof survey at greenhome.co.uk before 30th November!'

Analyse how this text operates as a dual-purpose text, quoting evidence for both purposes.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify Purpose 1 (To Inform): Informs householders about heat loss facts and government grant eligibility rules [M1]
2. Quote evidence for Purpose 1: '35% of your home's heat escapes' and 'Council Tax Bands A–D qualify for up to £1,500' [A1]
3. Identify Purpose 2 (To Persuade / Promote commercially): Persuades homeowners to book a survey with GreenHome Insulation Ltd before the deadline [M1]
4. Quote evidence for Purpose 2: Rhetorical question ('Did you know...?'), emotive command ('Do not shiver through another expensive winter'), and call to action ('book your free 20-minute roof survey... before 30th November!') [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Dual purpose: 1. To inform householders about loft heat loss ('35%') and £1,500 grant eligibility ('Council Tax Bands A–D'). 2. To persuade them to book a commercial survey with GreenHome Insulation Ltd ('Do not shiver... book your free 20-minute roof survey').
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Identifying only the informative grant details and missing the commercial persuasive pitch at the end.
🔄 Quick Check: Both purposes (Inform + Persuade) must be named and backed by direct quotes.
Question 6 (Level 2 Comparing Genre Conventions Across Three Extracts) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Read three short texts about a train strike:
Text A (Rail Operator SMS Alert): 'ALERT: Strike action Weds 18 Nov. Zero trains on North Line. Claim full refund at rail.co.uk/refunds.'
Text B (Union Press Release): 'Signallers have been forced into industrial action after management refused to address dangerous lone-working rosters.'
Text C (Travel Advice Guide): 'If you must travel on Wednesday, consider booking an intercity coach early or car-sharing with colleagues.'

Identify the text type and primary purpose of Text A, Text B, and Text C, and explain why Text A uses fragmented sentences.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Text A genre & purpose: Automated SMS/text alert — to inform passengers urgently of the 18 Nov line closure and how to claim a refund [A1]
2. Text B genre & purpose: Union press release / statement — to justify the strike and persuade the public to blame management's safety record [A1]
3. Text C genre & purpose: Commuter travel advice guide — to advise passengers on practical alternative travel options (coaches/car-sharing) [A1]
4. Explain Text A's fragmented style: Mobile SMS alerts have strict character limits and must deliver critical cancellation facts instantly on a phone screen [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Text A: SMS alert to inform urgently of line closure/refunds (uses short fragments for instant mobile reading). Text B: Union press release to justify the strike and persuade the public. Text C: Travel guide to advise commuters on alternative transport.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Forgetting to answer the final part explaining why Text A uses short fragmented sentences.
🔄 Quick Check: Text A = Inform (SMS); Text B = Persuade/Justify (Press release); Text C = Advise (Guide).
