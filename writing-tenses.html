Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.27 / L2.27 – Use correct and consistent tense across extended narratives, letters, and formal reports.
  • L2.28 – Accurately apply present perfect, past continuous, past perfect, and modal conditional constructions in workplace documentation.

Golden Rules for Consistency of Tense

Golden Rule 1: Choose Your Anchor Tense for Each Paragraph and Stick to It

An incident report or witness statement is anchored in the PAST tense ('The alarm sounded, staff evacuated, and the manager arrived'). Never slip mid-sentence into the present tense ('then the manager arrives and says').

Golden Rule / Technique: Past Events = Past Tense | Current Policy = Present Tense | Recommendations = Future / Modal Verbs
Golden Rule 2: Use Present Perfect ('Have/Has + Past Participle') to Link Past Actions to Now

When describing recent workplace improvements or ongoing problems that started in the past and still affect today, use the present perfect: 'We have installed four cameras' or 'Waiting times have increased by 20% since May'.

Golden Rule / Technique: Simple Past = 'We updated the rota on Monday.' | Present Perfect = 'We have updated the rota to improve cover.'

Core Skill 1: The Workplace Tense Selection Matrix

Which verb tenses to deploy for each section of a Functional Skills writing response.

Workplace Purpose Target Tense Form Model Sentence Example
Recounting an Incident / History Past Simple & Past Continuous: (walked, was inspecting) "While the technician was inspecting the boiler, the pressure valve failed."
Describing Current Rules / Facts Present Simple: (requires, operates, ensures) "Our current clinic policy requires two nurses to verify controlled dosages."
Recent Changes Affecting Now Present Perfect: (has/have + past participle) "Over the past six months, staff turnover has fallen by 18%."
Proposing Future Solutions Future & Modal Conditional: (will reduce, would enable) "Introducing staggered breaks will eliminate lunchtime queue bottlenecks."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Irregular Past Participles & Standard English Verb Forms

Eliminate non-standard spoken verb errors ('I seen', 'I done', 'it had broke') that cost SPaG marks.

  • See / Saw / Have Seen: Never write "I seen the spill". Write "I saw the spill" (past simple) or "I have seen several incidents" (present perfect).
  • Do / Did / Have Done: Never write "The team done the audit". Write "The team completed / did the audit" or "The team has done the audit".
  • Break / Broke / Had Broken: Never write "The lock was broke". Write "The lock was broken".
  • Come / Came / Have Come: Never write "The parcel come yesterday". Write "The parcel came / arrived yesterday".
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: How to Shift Tenses Legitimately Across a 4-Paragraph Letter

Signposting time changes clearly using temporal adverbials.

You CAN change tense between paragraphs—provided you signal the time shift with a clear time marker:

• Paragraph 2 (Past Event): "Last Tuesday at 14:30, a delivery lorry struck the car park barrier and damaged the sensor."
• Paragraph 3 (Present Impact): "Currently, the barrier remains stuck in the open position, allowing unauthorised commuters to park on site."
• Paragraph 4 (Future Action): "Next week, our estates contractor will install a reinforced automatic gate."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

'We arrived at the residential address at 03:15 and find the patient conscious on the hallway floor. The lead paramedic administers oxygen while the technician checked the ECG monitor, and then we takes the patient to St Jude’s Hospital.'

Read your incident log aloud in your head: shifting from 'arrived' to 'find' and 'administers' immediately sounds jarring.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 Level 2 Advanced Tense Control Benchmark (4 Marks)

Vocational Context: Warehouse Health & Safety Audit Report

Write a 3-sentence executive summary for a safety report that:
1. Uses the past perfect (had + past participle) to state what had gone wrong prior to last month's inspection.
2. Uses the present perfect (have/has + past participle) to state what remedial steps have been completed since then.
3. Uses a future modal verb to state the projected outcome for next quarter.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Sentence 1: Past Perfect for pre-inspection background
Use 'had deteriorated' and 'had not received' to show conditions before the October audit.
Criteria: Past Perfect Mastery (L2.28)
Step 2 Sentence 2: Present Perfect for recent completed actions
Use 'has repainted' and 'have completed' to connect recent fixes to the current state.
Criteria: Present Perfect Mastery

Use 'will eliminate' and 'should ensure' to project next quarter's compliance.

Consequently, these interventions will reduce vehicle-pedestrian near-misses to zero during the upcoming peak winter quarter.'

Moving logically from Past Perfect ('had faded') → Present Perfect ('has repainted') → Future ('will reduce') demonstrates effortless Level 2 grammatical command.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Slipping into the Conversational Present When Recounting Past Events
In casual speech, people often say: 'So I walked up to the counter and the manager says to me...' In Functional Skills writing, mixing 'walked' and 'says' is penalised as a tense error.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always keep past narratives strictly in the past tense: 'I walked up to the counter and the manager said to me...'
Examiner Pitfall: Confusing Past Simple with Past Participles ('I Seen' / 'I Done')
Writing 'I seen the broken window' or 'We done the risk assessment' is non-standard grammar and loses immediate SPaG marks.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Use 'I saw' / 'We did' for simple past, or add 'have/had': 'I have seen' / 'We have done'.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Correct the two tense errors in this sentence from an audit report:
'Yesterday morning, the pharmacy auditor inspected the controlled drugs cabinet and finds that two signature entries are missing from Tuesday's log.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. The time marker 'Yesterday morning' and verb 'inspected' establish the past tense.
2. Change 'finds' to 'found' and 'are missing' to 'were missing'.
Final Model Answer: 'Yesterday morning, the pharmacy auditor inspected the controlled drugs cabinet and found that two signature entries were missing from Tuesday's log.'
Examiner Tip: When a sentence begins with a past time marker like 'Yesterday morning' or 'Last week', every verb that follows in that recount must stay in the past tense.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Replace the three non-standard verb forms in this witness statement with correct Standard English past-tense or past-participle verbs:
'When I come onto the ward at 07:00, I seen that the medication fridge door had broke during the night.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Change 'come' (present) to 'came' (or 'arrived').
2. Change 'I seen' to 'I saw' (or 'I noticed').
3. Change 'had broke' to 'had broken'.
Final Model Answer: 'When I came onto the ward at 07:00, I saw that the medication fridge door had broken during the night.'
Examiner Tip: After 'had', 'have', or 'has', always use the past participle ('broken', 'taken', 'written', 'seen'), never the simple past ('broke', 'took', 'wrote').
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
Rewrite this paragraph so that it maintains consistent present tense appropriate for a permanent Staff Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) manual:
'All laboratory technicians must wear safety goggles before entering Room 4. You checked the fume cupboard airflow gauge, and then you will record the pressure reading in the yellow logbook.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Identify the anchor tense for an SOP manual: Present tense (or imperative instruction).
2. Change past tense 'checked' and future tense 'will record' into present simple verbs ('check' and 'record') to match the routine procedure.
Final Model Answer: 'All laboratory technicians must wear safety goggles before entering Room 4. Technicians check the fume cupboard airflow gauge and then record the pressure reading in the yellow logbook.'
Examiner Tip: Standard Operating Procedures describe permanent rules, so keep all verbs in the present simple tense.
