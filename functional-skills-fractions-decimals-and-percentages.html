Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate complete mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes across Pearson Edexcel, City & Guilds, and NCFE:

  • Level 1 L1.12 • Level 2 L2.11: Recognise and use equivalences between common fractions, decimals, and percentages in non-calculator and calculator contexts.
  • Level 2 L2.12: Order, compare, and rank fractions, decimals, and percentages across mixed representations by converting into a unified benchmark format.
  • Level 2 Problem Solving: Solve multi-step workplace decision problems comparing promotional discounts, departmental KPIs, and procurement contracts.
Subi's Assessor Blueprint: FDP is Your Secret Exam Superpower!

"Fractions, decimals, and percentages are just three different languages describing the exact same thing: a portion of a whole! When examiners give you a tricky comparison like 3/8 vs. 36% vs. 0.35, don't try to compare them as they are. Translate everything into percentages or 3-digit decimals. The entire problem immediately becomes effortless!"

Golden Rules for FDP Equivalence & Conversions

Golden Rule 1: The FDP Conversion Triangle

To move between forms: Fraction → Decimal (divide top by bottom: numerator ÷ denominator); Decimal → Percentage (multiply by 100, move decimal 2 spots right); Percentage → Decimal (divide by 100, move decimal 2 spots left); Percentage / Decimal → Fraction (write over 100 or 1,000 and simplify).

Golden Rule / Method: Fraction → (÷) → Decimal → (× 100) → Percentage • % → (÷ 100) → Decimal → (/100) → Fraction
Golden Rule 2: Convert ALL Values to Percentages Before Ordering or Comparing

Never guess which fraction is bigger than a decimal! Convert every single value in the question to a percentage or a 3-digit decimal (e.g. pad 0.62 to 0.620). Once all values are in the same currency, ranking them in ascending or descending order takes seconds.

Golden Rule / Method: 3/8 = 37.5% • 0.35 = 35.0% • 36% = 36.0% → Clear Ranking: 0.35 < 36% < 3/8
Golden Rule 3: Instant Recall of Key Family Equivalences

Memorizing the key families eliminates time-consuming division in the non-calculator section: Halves (50%), Quarters (25%, 75%), Fifths (20%, 40%, 60%, 80%), and Eighths (12.5%, 37.5%, 62.5%, 87.5%).

Golden Rule / Method: 1/8 = 12.5% (0.125) • 1/5 = 20% (0.2) • 1/4 = 25% (0.25) • 3/8 = 37.5% (0.375)

Core Skill 2: Converting Any Fraction to a Percentage (Scaling vs. Bus-Stop)

Two failsafe non-calculator methods for converting unmemorized fractions into percentages.

METHOD A: SCALE TO 100
For Denominators That Divide Into 100
Use when denominator is 2, 4, 5, 10, 20, 25, or 50. Multiply top and bottom by the multiplier that makes the bottom 100!
Worked: Convert 1725 to a percentage
17 × 425 × 4 = 68100 = 68% ✓
Multiplier Key: 20 × 5 = 100 • 25 × 4 = 100 • 50 × 2 = 100.
METHOD B: SHORT DIVISION
For Denominators Like 8, 16, or Odd Numbers
Divide numerator by denominator under the bus stop with decimals (top ÷ bottom), then multiply by 100.
Worked: Convert 58 to a percentage
8 into 5.000 = 0.625
0.625 × 100 = 62.5% ✓
Step: Add trailing decimal zeros (5.000) under the bus stop and shift decimal point 2 places right for %.

Core Skill 3: Converting Decimals & Percentages to Simplest Fractions

Writing over 10, 100, or 1,000 and cancelling down using Highest Common Factors (HCF).

WORKED METHOD: CANCELLING COMMON FACTORS
Worked Conversions: 36% and 0.375 into Simplest Fractions
Example A: 2-Digit Decimal / Percentage (36% or 0.36)
Step 1: Write over 100 → 36100
Step 2: Both divide by 4 (HCF = 4):
36 ÷ 4100 ÷ 4 = 925 ✓
Example B: 3-Digit Decimal (0.375)
Step 1: 3 decimal places = over 1,000 → 3751000
Step 2: Cancel in steps (÷ 25, then ÷ 5):
375 ÷ 1251000 ÷ 125 = 38 ✓
Subi's Assessor Golden Rule: Always Cancel to 'Simplest Form'

"Writing 36/100 or 375/1000 will bank the method mark [M1], but to get the accuracy mark [A1], you must divide down until no common factors remain. If you are stuck, keep dividing by 2 or 5 in steps until you can't divide anymore!"

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Trap 1: Confusing 0.05 with 50%
A very frequent error under exam pressure is assuming 0.05 is 50% because both feature the single digit 5. In reality:
0.05 = 5100 = 5% 0.5 = 0.50 = 50100 = 50%
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always multiply the decimal by 100 explicitly and shift the digits two places to the left: 0.05 × 100 = 5%. Write a placeholder zero if uncertain!
Trap 2: "Discount Off" vs "Amount Paid" Multipliers
Exams frequently mix phrases: "Save 25%", "Get 15 off", and "Pay 0.85 of the total".
• Discount: The money you DO NOT pay.
• Amount Paid: The money that leaves your bank account (100% − Discount%).
If you pay 0.85, the discount is 15% (100% − 85% = 15%), NOT 85%!
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Highlight the verb in the question stem: "save", "discount", "off" vs "pay", "cost", "charge". Align all comparisons to either 100% discount or 100% paid.
Trap 3: Forgetting to Revert to Original Question Numbers in Final Ordering
When asked to order numbers such as 0.62, 35, 65%, 58 from smallest to largest, learners correctly convert them to decimals: 0.600, 0.620, 0.625, 0.650. But then they write the converted decimals on the answer line!
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Pearson Edexcel criteria award the final mark [A1] only if the final answer is stated using the exact original representations given in the question: 3/5, 0.62, 5/8, 65%.

Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
(a) Convert 44% into a fraction in its simplest form.
(b) Convert 1320 into a percentage.
Pearson Edexcel Marking Scheme Steps:
Part (a): 44% to Simplest Fraction
44% = 44 ÷ 4100 ÷ 4 = 1125
[B1] Correct simplified fraction 11/25
Part (b): 13/20 to Percentage
13 × 520 × 5 = 65100 = 65%
[B1] Correct percentage 65%
Final Answer: (a) 1125   |   (b) 65%
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
In a staff travel survey of 160 employees at an organisation:
• 35% commute by train.
• 38 commute by car.
• The remaining employees cycle to work.

What decimal proportion of staff cycle, and how many employees does this represent?
Pearson Edexcel Marking Scheme Steps:
Step 1: Convert Known Groups to Decimals
Train: 35% = 0.350
Car: 38 = 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375
Combined Train + Car: 0.350 + 0.375 = 0.725
[M1] Converting to common decimal format and combining (0.725)
Step 2: Calculate Cycling Remainder & Headcount
Decimal proportion who cycle: 1.000 − 0.725 = 0.275 (or 27.5%)
Number of cycling employees: 0.275 × 160 = 44 employees
[M1] Subtracting combined proportion from 1 (or 100%)
Final Answer: Decimal Proportion = 0.275   |   Staff Cycling = 44 employees
[A1] Both decimal proportion (0.275) and headcount (44) correct
