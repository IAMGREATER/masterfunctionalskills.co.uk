Golden Rules for The Writer's Purpose & Target Audience

Golden Rule 1: Always Pair the Purpose Verb with the Specific Topic

Never write 'To inform' or 'To persuade' on its own! Pearson Edexcel mark schemes require the full formula: 'To [Purpose Verb] the reader about [Specific Topic and Action]'.

Golden Rule / Technique: Formula: To [persuade / inform / instruct / advise] [audience] about [specific topic].
Golden Rule 2: Look at the Final Sentence (Call to Action) to Confirm Purpose

A text may spend three paragraphs giving factual statistics, but if the final sentence says 'Sign our petition before Friday' or 'Book your free eye test today', its overarching main purpose is to persuade/promote!

Golden Rule / Technique: Informative body + Urgent Call to Action at the end = Persuasive / Promotional Purpose

Core Skill 1: The Six Core Writer's Purposes in Functional Skills

How to distinguish between Inform, Persuade, Instruct, Advise, Explain, and Review.

Purpose Verb Key Linguistic Clues Full-Mark Exam Answer Example
To Persuade / Campaign Rhetorical questions, emotive language, rule of three, call to action ("Donate now!"). "To persuade local residents to sign the petition against closing the town post office."
To Inform Neutral tone, dates, times, prices, facts without trying to sell an opinion. "To inform hospital staff about the new car park barrier operating hours and permit fees."
To Instruct Numbered steps (1, 2, 3), imperative command verbs ("Switch off", "Attach"), warnings. "To instruct laboratory technicians on how to calibrate the centrifuge safely."
To Advise / Guide Modal verbs ("you should", "try to", "it is a good idea to"), supportive tone. "To advise first-year apprentices on how to manage exam stress and sleep well."
To Review / Evaluate Weighs pros and cons, star ratings, evaluative adjectives, final verdict. "To review the strengths and weaknesses of the EcoWash dishwasher for family kitchens."
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Distinguishing Instruct vs Advise (High-Frequency Distinction)

Learn the simple modal verb test that separates mandatory instructions from helpful advice.

To Instruct (Mandatory Rules)

Leaves zero choice. Uses imperative commands and "must / do not":
"1. Isolate the mains switch. 2. Lock the fuse box with your safety padlock."

To Advise (Optional Recommendations)

Offers helpful suggestions the reader can choose to follow. Uses "could / should / consider":
"You might find it helpful to prepare your interview outfit the evening before."

Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: How Writers Adapt Language to Suit Their Target Audience

Explaining why a text's vocabulary and examples fit a specific demographic.

When asked "How has the writer adapted this text to suit its target audience?", look at three elements:

  1. Register & Slang: Uses casual, upbeat language for teenagers/students vs formal clinical language for senior managers.
  2. Relatable Examples & Scenarios: Mentions student loans and campus halls for university students, or pension tax relief and mortgage rates for older professionals.
  3. Visual & Layout Aids: Uses step-by-step diagrams and plain-English glossaries for beginners vs dense data tables for experienced analysts.
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 2 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Reading Level 1)

Vocational Context: Apprentice Induction Advice Sheet

Read this extract from a college booklet:
'Starting your first carpentry apprenticeship can feel overwhelming! To make a great impression in week one, you should pack two sharp pencils, arrive 10 minutes before your site supervisor, and never hesitate to ask questions if a power tool feels unfamiliar.'
(a) What is the main purpose of this extract? [1 Mark]
(b) Who is the specific target audience? [1 Mark]
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Identify the purpose verb + specific topic
The text uses supportive suggestions ('you should', 'never hesitate to') to advise and reassure new apprentices on how to succeed in their first week on site.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Purpose + Topic]
Step 2 Identify the specific target audience
New carpentry apprentices (or trainees starting their first construction apprenticeship).
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Specific Audience]
Final Accredited Response: (a) To advise and reassure new carpentry apprentices on how to prepare and succeed during their first week at work. | (b) First-year carpentry apprentices.
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Notice how the answer states 'To advise new carpentry apprentices on how to succeed in their first week' — including the topic secures the mark!

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Treating Persuasive Texts as Solely Informative
A text may contain plenty of facts and statistics, but if those facts are selected to convince you to buy a product, donate money, or sign a petition, its main purpose is to persuade.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always check the conclusion and final call-to-action before deciding the main purpose.
Examiner Pitfall: Giving a One-Word Answer ('To Inform')
Writing 'To inform' without explaining what the text informs the reader about scores 0 marks on Pearson Edexcel Level 1 papers.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always write a full sentence: 'To inform [audience] about [specific topic].'

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A hospice director publishes a newspaper article titled: 'Dignity at the End: Why Community Palliative Care Deserves Equal Healthcare Funding.'
State the writer's main purpose and target audience.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Main purpose: To persuade/campaign for equal public healthcare funding for community palliative care services by highlighting patient dignity [1 Mark].
2. Target audience: Newspaper readers, voters, and healthcare policymakers/commissioners [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Main Purpose: To persuade the public and policymakers that community palliative care deserves equal healthcare funding. | Target Audience: Adult newspaper readers and healthcare decision-makers.
Examiner Tip: Look at the word 'Why... Deserves' in the headline — that confirms the writer is arguing/persuading for a cause.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
Read this extract from a home energy guide:
'If you want to cut £150 off your winter heating bill without spending a penny, try bleeding your radiators in October and moving sofas at least 30 cm away from wall heaters.'
Is the main purpose of this extract to instruct or to advise? Explain your answer.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Identification: To advise [1 Mark].
2. Explanation: It offers optional, money-saving suggestions ('If you want to...', 'try bleeding...') to help householders lower their bills, rather than mandatory safety commands [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: To advise — it offers helpful, optional money-saving tips ('If you want to...', 'try bleeding...') rather than compulsory rules.
Examiner Tip: Words like 'try' and 'if you want to' always signal advice rather than strict instruction.
Exam Practice Question 3 2 Marks
A leaflet distributed at a GP surgery is titled: 'Understanding Type 2 Diabetes: Symptoms, Blood Sugar Checks, and Local Support Groups'.
Explain the main purpose of this leaflet and identify who it is written for.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Main purpose: To inform and educate readers about Type 2 diabetes symptoms, blood sugar monitoring, and where to access local support [1 Mark].
2. Target audience: Newly diagnosed Type 2 diabetes patients, their families, or GP patients worried about symptoms [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Main Purpose: To inform and guide readers about Type 2 diabetes symptoms, monitoring, and local support groups. | Target Audience: GP patients diagnosed with or at risk of Type 2 diabetes and their families.
Examiner Tip: Include all key elements from the title when stating what the leaflet informs patients about.
