Welcome to the Continuous Line Graphs, Trends & Linear Interpolation blueprint. Line graphs depict continuous variables changing over time. In Pearson Edexcel assessments, you must describe trends with start and end numerical values, interpolate values between plotted points, and determine exact time intervals when a monitored parameter crosses a critical safety threshold.

Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • L1.25 – Draw and interpret time-series line graphs, describe trends supported by numerical data, and interpolate values between points.

Golden Rules for Line Graphs & Trend Analysis

Golden Rule 1: Time Belongs on the Horizontal X-Axis; Join Points with Ruled Segments

When plotting time-series data (hours, days, months, years), always place Time along the bottom horizontal X-axis and the measured variable up the vertical Y-axis. Plot points as neat crosses (×) and join consecutive points with straight ruler lines.

Golden Rule / Method: Time = Horizontal X-Axis • Plot with × • Join Consecutive Points
Golden Rule 2: Describing a Trend Requires Direction + Specific Numbers

Never write just 'it went up' or 'it went down'. Always state the overall direction AND quote starting/ending/peak figures from the graph (e.g. 'Overall downward trend, falling from 38.8°C at 08:00 to 37.0°C at 18:00').

Golden Rule / Method: Trend Mark = Direction (Rise/Fall/Fluctuate) + Start & End Figures

Core Skill 1: Interpolation vs Extrapolation on Time-Series Graphs

Reading values halfway between plotted time intervals vs projecting into the future.

  • Interpolation (Between Plotted Points): To estimate the reading at 11:00 when points are plotted at 10:00 (38.4°C) and 12:00 (37.8°C), read up from 11:00 to the straight line joining them: (38.4 + 37.8) ÷ 2 = 38.1°C.
  • Extrapolation (Beyond the Graph): Continuing a trend line into future months assumes current conditions remain unchanged.
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Finding the Steepest Rate of Change

Identifying which time period had the fastest increase or decrease.

The steepest line segment represents the fastest rate of change per hour or per month:

  • Calculate the vertical drop or rise across each equal time step to confirm which interval changed by the largest amount.
  • A horizontal flat line means the value remained constant (zero change) during that period.
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Comparing Dual Line Graphs (Two Lines on the Same Axes)

Finding crossover points and maximum differences between two competing series.

  • Intersection Point (Crossover): Where the two lines cross, both categories had the exact same value at that time.
  • Vertical Gap: To find the difference between Branch A and Branch B in March, read both values at March and subtract: Top Line − Bottom Line.
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

(a) Describe the overall trend in the patient's temperature over the 10-hour period.

Sonya’s Examiner Tip Trend Description Rule
In Functional Skills, stating only 'it went down' gets 0 marks! You must provide three components: direction (downward/falling), start value with time (38.8°C at 08:00), and finish value with time (37.0°C at 18:00).

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Writing Simply 'It Went Down' Without Supporting Numbers
Vague trend statements without figures from the axes score 0 marks.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always write: "Decreased from [Start Value] in [Start Time] to [End Value] in [End Time]."
Examiner Pitfall: Drawing Bars Instead of Plotted Points Joined by Lines
When asked to draw a line graph, drawing vertical bars loses the format mark.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Plot neat crosses (×) at each coordinate and connect them point-to-point with a ruler.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A depot diesel tank line graph shows 420 litres at 08:00 and 180 litres at 16:00, decreasing at a steady linear rate. How many litres were in the tank at 12:00?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. 12:00 is halfway between 08:00 and 16:00.
2. Midpoint = (420 + 180) ÷ 2 = 600 ÷ 2 = 300 litres.
Final Correct Answer: 300 litres
Examiner Tip: Average the 08:00 and 16:00 readings (drops by 30 litres per hour).
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
A dual line graph compares quarterly sales (£000s) for Store A and Store B:
• Store A: Q1 = 40, Q2 = 55, Q3 = 50, Q4 = 75
• Store B: Q1 = 50, Q2 = 45, Q3 = 65, Q4 = 60
In which quarter was the difference in sales between the two stores greatest, and what was that difference in £?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Differences: Q1 = 10k; Q2 = 10k; Q3 = 65 − 50 = 15k; Q4 = 75 − 60 = 15k.
2. Greatest difference is in Q3 and Q4, with a difference of £15,000.
Final Correct Answer: Q3 and Q4 (£15,000 difference)
Examiner Tip: Remember the axis is in £000s (thousands), so 15 represents £15,000!
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
Using the Store A sales figures from Question 2 (Q1: £40k, Q2: £55k, Q3: £50k, Q4: £75k), calculate the mean quarterly sales for Store A in £.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Sum = 40 + 55 + 50 + 75 = 220 (£000s) = £220,000.
2. Mean = £220,000 ÷ 4 = £55,000.
Final Correct Answer: £55,000
Examiner Tip: 220 ÷ 4 = 55 thousand pounds (£55,000).
