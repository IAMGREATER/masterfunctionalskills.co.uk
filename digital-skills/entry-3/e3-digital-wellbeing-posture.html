Digital Wellbeing, Screen Breaks and Online Footprints

Protect your physical health and mental wellbeing while working on digital devices: apply healthy workstation ergonomics, take regular screen breaks, and understand your permanent digital footprint.

Apply healthy posture and ergonomic workstation guidelines to avoid eye and back strain Use the 20-20-20 rule and take regular breaks from display screens Understand that content shared online creates a lasting digital footprint

Step-by-Step Teaching & Key Concepts

Core Knowledge
1
Healthy Posture and Ergonomics

Sitting at a computer for hours can cause neck pain, backache, and repetitive strain injury (RSI) unless you set up your workstation correctly:

  • Screen Position: The top of your monitor should be at eye level, roughly an arm's length (50-70 cm) away from your face.
  • Chair & Back: Adjust chair height so your feet rest flat on the floor with your knees at a 90-degree angle. Your lower back should be fully supported.
  • Wrists & Hands: Keep your wrists straight when typing, not bent up or resting on sharp desk edges.
Ideal Ergonomic Workstation Layout
Eyes & Screen
• Top of monitor at eye level
• Arm's length away
• Avoid window glare
Posture & Arms
• Back upright & supported
• Elbows at 90 degrees
• Feet flat on floor
2
The 20-20-20 Rule for Eye Strain

Staring at brightly lit screens causes eye fatigue, headaches, and dry eyes. Prevent this using the 20-20-20 rule:

Every 20 minutes, look away from your screen at an object 20 feet away for at least 20 seconds.

Also, stand up, stretch your legs, and walk around every 45 to 60 minutes.

Tutor Abdul's Health Tip: Regular screen breaks make you more productive and alert. In exams, questions will ask how to prevent eye strain: 'Take regular screen breaks' is always an accredited answer.
3
Understanding Your Digital Footprint

Your Digital Footprint is the trail of data you leave behind whenever you use the internet — including social media posts, comments, photos, search history, and website visits.

  • Permanent Nature: Even if you delete a photo or angry comment, others may have saved a screenshot.
  • Workplace Impact: Employers frequently check candidates' public online presence before hiring. Always keep public profiles polite, respectful, and professional.

Accredited Model

Here is how this skill is tested in the real Pearson Edexcel onscreen digital assessment, broken down with official examiner mark codes ([B1] knowledge, [M1] process, [A1] accuracy).

Exam Scenario Prompt: A medical records administrator spends seven hours a day typing on a computer. By mid-afternoon, they suffer from headaches and lower back pain. State two adjustments they can make to their workstation or daily routine to relieve these symptoms.
Step 1: First health recommendation [B1]
Take regular screen breaks (e.g. following the 20-20-20 rule or standing up every hour).
Tutor Marking Rationale: Screen breaks relieve eye muscle tension and prevent digital fatigue.
Step 2: Second ergonomic recommendation [B1]
Adjust chair height and back support so feet are flat on the floor and screen is at eye level.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Correct ergonomic setup aligns the spine and reduces lower back strain.
Tutor Abdul's Exam Golden Rule: In Entry 3 exams, health and safety questions always reward practical, common-sense advice: screen breaks, chair adjustments, and monitor eye-level alignment.

Pearson Edexcel Benchmark Solution

Advice 1: Be careful what you post publicly; never post confidential work details, inappropriate photos, or offensive comments, as digital content can be permanent. Advice 2: Check your privacy settings so personal posts are visible only to trusted friends and family, and maintain a professional online image.
Official Marking Criteria:
