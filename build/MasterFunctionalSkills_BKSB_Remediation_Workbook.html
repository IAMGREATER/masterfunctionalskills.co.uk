Diagnostic Error Classification

Categorizing your mistake is the first step toward permanent mastery.
▶The 4 Failure Root Causes
Type A • Misconception (Process): Choosing an incorrect formula or reversing an operation (e.g. multiplying by 0.15 instead of dividing by 0.85 for reverse discount).
Type B • Algorithmic (Format): Correct numerical calculation, but syntax, rounding, or unit formatting triggers an automated red X.
Type C • Reading / Comprehension: Overlooking qualifying conditions in the prompt (e.g. 'rounded to 1 decimal place' or 'excluding VAT').
Type D • Arithmetic Slips: Simple addition or subtraction slips under timed pressure.
★The Golden Rule of Error Logging

For every red X on your screen, determine whether it was Type A, B, C, or D. If Type A, review the worked model fix. If Type B, write down the formatting rule. If Type C, highlight the prompt text.

Mode 1 • Place Value, Ordering Decimals & Significant Figures

Diagnostic Misconception Analysis: Place Value, Ordering Decimals & Significant Figures
▶Diagnostic Autopsy • Root Cause Deconstruction
On online BKSB diagnostic screens, learners frequently confuse the number of decimal places with significant figures, or incorrectly believe that longer decimal numbers (e.g. 0.125) are larger than shorter decimals (e.g. 0.4). Another widespread error is failing to maintain trailing place-value zeros when rounding to specific significant figures.
★Golden Rule • Mode 1

Rule: Pad decimals with trailing zeros to make them the same length before comparing (e.g. 0.400 is larger than 0.125). For significant figures, start counting from the FIRST non-zero digit on the left!

▶Tutor Worked Model Fix
Faulty Student Attempt: Round 0.04085 to 2 significant figures. Student writes: 0.04 (incorrectly stopped at 2 decimal places).
Correct Model Method: First non-zero digit is 4 (1st sig fig). Next digit is 0 (2nd sig fig). The next digit is 8 (rounds up).
Answer: 0.041. Check: Has exactly 2 significant figures.

Mode 2 • Negative Numbers, Overdrafts & Temperature Drops

Diagnostic Misconception Analysis: Negative Numbers, Overdrafts & Temperature Drops
▶Diagnostic Autopsy • Root Cause Deconstruction
Learners consistently stumble on directional arithmetic with negative numbers, often adding when calculating temperature differences or misunderstanding overdraft repayments (e.g. treating -£180 + £250 as -£430 or +£430 instead of +£70).
★Golden Rule • Mode 2

Rule: Draw a vertical temperature line or money ladder! Going warmer/paying in moves UP (+); going colder/spending moves DOWN (-). Difference between a positive and negative number is always found by ADDING their absolute distances to zero!

▶Tutor Worked Model Fix
Faulty Student Attempt: A commercial freezer is at -18°C. A malfunction causes the temperature to rise by 7°C. Student writes: -25°C.
Correct Model Method: Rising temperature moves UP toward zero. -18 + 7 = -11°C. Check: -11 is 7 degrees warmer than -18.

Diagnostic Misconception Analysis: Order of Operations (BIDMAS / BODMAS)
▶Diagnostic Autopsy • Root Cause Deconstruction
Candidates read calculations strictly from left to right, performing addition or subtraction before multiplication or division. This is heavily penalised in BKSB multi-step questions where operations are deliberately placed in mixed order.
★Golden Rule • Mode 3

Rule: Follow BIDMAS strictly: Brackets → Indices (powers/roots) → Division & Multiplication → Addition & Subtraction. Highlight multiplication and division blocks first!

Diagnostic Misconception Analysis: Fractions of Quantities & Mixed Number Arithmetic
▶Diagnostic Autopsy • Root Cause Deconstruction
Learners struggle when finding non-unit fractions (e.g. 3/8 of £480), often dividing by the numerator instead of denominator. When adding mixed numbers, learners frequently add numerators and denominators straight across (e.g. 1/2 + 1/3 = 2/5).
★Golden Rule • Mode 4

Rule: Divide by the bottom (denominator), multiply by the top (numerator)! For adding/subtracting fractions, you MUST find a common denominator first!

▶Tutor Worked Model Fix
Faulty Student Attempt: Calculate 3/5 of £250. Student does 250 × 5 ÷ 3 = 416.66.
Correct Model Method: Divide by 5: 250 ÷ 5 = £50 (1 part). Multiply by 3: 50 × 3 = £150.

Mode 5 • Decimal Multiplication & Dividing by Decimals

Diagnostic Misconception Analysis: Decimal Multiplication & Dividing by Decimals
▶Diagnostic Autopsy • Root Cause Deconstruction
When multiplying decimals (e.g. 0.4 × 0.03), learners miscount decimal places and give answers like 0.12 instead of 0.012. When dividing by a decimal (e.g. 24 ÷ 0.6), learners get confused and try to divide without clearing the decimal divisor.
★Golden Rule • Mode 5

Rule: Multiply without decimals as whole numbers, then count TOTAL decimal places in both numbers and count back from the right! For division by a decimal: Multiply BOTH numbers by 10, 100, or 1000 to make the divisor a whole number before dividing!

▶Tutor Worked Model Fix
Faulty Student Attempt: Work out 0.6 × 0.04. Student writes: 0.24 (only counted 2 decimal places).
Correct Model Method: Whole numbers: 6 × 4 = 24. Total decimal places: 1 + 2 = 3 decimal places. Answer: 0.024.

Mode 6 • Reverse Percentages: Original Value Before Discount or VAT

Diagnostic Misconception Analysis: Reverse Percentages: Original Value Before Discount or VAT
▶Diagnostic Autopsy • Root Cause Deconstruction
This is the #1 most failed maths question on BKSB Level 2. When a price is discounted by 20% to £80, learners incorrectly calculate 20% of £80 (£16) and ADD it back to get £96. This is fundamentally wrong because the 20% was deducted from the ORIGINAL (larger) amount, not the sale price!
★Golden Rule • Mode 6

Rule: The original value is ALWAYS 100%. If discounted by 20%, the sale price represents 80% (0.80). To find the original: Original = New Value ÷ Percentage Multiplier!

▶Tutor Worked Model Fix
Faulty Student Attempt: A coat costs £120 after a 20% discount. Student calculates 20% of 120 = £24, so original = 120 + 24 = £144. (0 marks!)
Correct Model Method: Original = 100%. Sale price = 100% - 20% = 80% = 0.80.
Original = 120 ÷ 0.80 = £150.00. Check: 20% of £150 = £30 → £150 - £30 = £120. (Matches!)

Mode 7 • Percentage Increase, Decrease & Percentage Change

Diagnostic Misconception Analysis: Percentage Increase, Decrease & Percentage Change
▶Diagnostic Autopsy • Root Cause Deconstruction
Learners get confused between calculating an increased amount vs calculating the percentage change between two figures. When calculating % change, learners frequently divide by the NEW value instead of the ORIGINAL value.
★Golden Rule • Mode 7

Rule: % Change = (Difference ÷ Original Value) × 100. Always divide by the starting (original) figure!

▶Tutor Worked Model Fix
Faulty Student Attempt: A business revenue grew from £40,000 to £50,000. Student writes: (10,000 ÷ 50,000) × 100 = 20%.
Correct Model Method: Difference = £10,000. Original = £40,000.
% Increase = (10,000 ÷ 40,000) × 100 = 25%.

Mode 8 • Ratio Sharing: Part-to-Part vs Part-to-Whole Traps

Diagnostic Misconception Analysis: Ratio Sharing: Part-to-Part vs Part-to-Whole Traps
▶Diagnostic Autopsy • Root Cause Deconstruction
On BKSB, questions often state: 'A and B share money in the ratio 3 : 2. Person A receives £60. How much does Person B receive?' Learners habitually add 3 + 2 = 5 and divide £60 by 5! This fails because £60 is Person A's share (3 parts), NOT the total money!
★Golden Rule • Mode 8

Rule: Read carefully: Is the given number the TOTAL amount, or ONE PERSON'S SHARE? If it is one person's share, divide by THEIR ratio parts to find the value of 1 part!

▶Tutor Worked Model Fix
Faulty Student Attempt: Sand and cement are mixed 4 : 1. A builder uses 20 kg of cement. Total mortar? Student does 20 ÷ 5 = 4 kg. (0 marks!)
Correct Model Method: Cement is 1 part = 20 kg. Sand is 4 parts = 4 × 20 = 80 kg. Total mortar = 80 + 20 = 100 kg.

Mode 10 • Compound Area & Perimeter of L-Shapes

Diagnostic Misconception Analysis: Compound Area & Perimeter of L-Shapes
▶Diagnostic Autopsy • Root Cause Deconstruction
When given an L-shape with missing side lengths, learners fail to calculate the missing sides before finding perimeter, or multiply outer dimensions together without splitting the shape into separate rectangles.
★Golden Rule • Mode 10

Rule: For L-shapes, vertical sides balance (Top vertical = Bottom vertical + Step). Horizontal sides balance (Total width = Left width + Right width). Split the shape into two rectangles, find each area, and add them!

▶Tutor Worked Model Fix
Faulty Student Attempt: L-shape outer dimensions 8 m by 6 m with cutout 4 m by 3 m. Student writes: Area = 8 × 6 = 48 m².
Correct Model Method: Outer box = 8 × 6 = 48 m². Cutout = 4 × 3 = 12 m². Net Area = 48 − 12 = 36 m².

Mode 11 • Circle Geometry: Area (πr²) vs Circumference (πd)

Diagnostic Misconception Analysis: Circle Geometry: Area (πr²) vs Circumference (πd)
▶Diagnostic Autopsy • Root Cause Deconstruction
Learners constantly mix up radius and diameter, or confuse Area (πr²) with Circumference (πd). When given a diameter of 6m, learners frequently calculate π × 6² without halving the diameter first!
★Golden Rule • Mode 11

Rule: Circumference is distance around = π × diameter. Area is space inside = π × radius². Always check: Did the question give RADIUS or DIAMETER? If diameter, halve it first to get radius!

▶Tutor Worked Model Fix
Faulty Student Attempt: Find the area of a circular rug of diameter 4 m. Student does: 3.14 × 4² = 50.24 m².
Correct Model Method: Diameter = 4 m → Radius = 2 m. Area = πr² = 3.14 × 2² = 3.14 × 4 = 12.56 m².

Master Diagnostic Golden Rules Directory

Master Diagnostic Golden Rules (Maths & English):
• Rule 1 (Reverse Percentage): Never multiply the final discounted price by the discount percent. Always divide by the decimal multiplier (1.00 − discount rate).
• Rule 2 (Time in Calculations): Minutes are not decimals! 45 minutes is 45/60 = 0.75 hours, NOT 0.45 hours.
• Rule 3 (Units & Precision): Money answers must always have 2 decimal places (£14.50, never £14.5). State units on area (m²) and volume (m³).
• Rule 4 (Circle Area vs Circumference): Area = 3.142 × Radius² (squares the radius first). Circumference = 3.142 × Diameter. Never mix them up.
• Rule 5 (Grouped Mean): Always multiply interval midpoints by frequency before summing. Divide by the total frequency, never by the number of intervals.
• Rule 6 (Fact vs Opinion): A fact can be proved objectively with data or dates. An opinion contains value words ('best', 'should', 'terrible').
• Rule 7 (Apostrophes of Possession): Singular noun takes 's (the manager's car). Plural noun ending in 's' takes trailing apostrophe (the workers' canteen).
• Rule 8 (Formal Tone & Contractions): In formal letters and reports, never use contractions (write 'do not', never 'don't').

Diagnostic & Algorithmic Glossary

Algorithmic Marking
Automated computer grading that marks an answer entirely incorrect if syntax, units, or decimal formatting deviate from the expected string.
Class Midpoint
The central numerical value of a grouped class interval: (Lower bound + Upper bound) / 2.
Compound Fraction
A numerical expression combining whole numbers and fractions (3 1/4), requiring conversion to improper fractions for multiplication.
Diagnostic Assessment
An initial online baseline test determining a learner's current working level and identifying specific skill gaps.
Independent Event
A probability scenario where the outcome of the first trial has zero influence on the outcome of the second trial.
Inverse Operation
The opposite mathematical operation used to verify a calculation (e.g. division verifies multiplication).
Process Error
An error where an incorrect formula or strategy was selected, resulting in loss of method marks.
Rounding Prematurity
A fatal calculation error where intermediate numbers are rounded early, corrupting the final answer.
Subject-Verb Agreement
The grammatical rule requiring singular subjects to take singular verbs and plural subjects to take plural verbs.
