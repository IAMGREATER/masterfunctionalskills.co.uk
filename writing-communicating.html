Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.20 / L2.20 – Communicate information, ideas, and opinions clearly, coherently, and effectively.
  • L2.21 – Write with sustained clarity and appropriate detail, ensuring logical progression and precise vocabulary.

Golden Rules for Communicating Clearly & Coherently

Golden Rule 1: One Core Idea Per Sentence (Eliminate Rambling Run-Ons)

Avoid breathless 50-word sentences strung together with 'and then... and so...'. Break ideas down into crisp clauses connected by precise conjunctions so your reader understands the exact sequence on the first read.

Golden Rule / Technique: Clear Subject → Strong Active Verb → Exact Operational Consequence
Golden Rule 2: Progress Logically from Situation to Impact to Action

Guide your reader step-by-step. State clearly what happened (Situation), explain why it matters (Impact on safety, cost, or service), and conclude with what must happen next (Remedial Action).

Golden Rule / Technique: Sequence: Situation → Operational Impact → Remedial Action

Core Skill 1: Three Techniques for Crystal-Clear Professional Prose

Eliminating wordiness, vague pronouns, and passive ambiguity in workplace writing.

  • Prefer Active Voice for Accountability: Write "Senior Nurse Taylor completed the controlled drug audit at 18:00" rather than "The audit was done by someone later on."
  • Trim Bloated Wordiness: Replace "Due to the fact that" with "Because"; replace "At this present moment in time" with "Currently"; replace "In the event that" with "If".
  • Anchor Every Pronoun ('This', 'It', 'They'): Never write "They didn't fix it, which caused a problem." Specify the exact nouns: "The external contractors failed to repair the boiler valve, which left Ward 3 without hot water."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Before & After: Upgrading Vague Sentences into Precise Level 2 Prose

Side-by-side comparison showing how specific nouns, times, and verbs earn Band 3 Content marks.

Vague / Ambiguous Draft (Band 1) Clear, Precise Professional Upgrade (Band 3) Why It Scores Higher
"Some stuff was left in the corridor and somebody nearly tripped over it earlier." "Three unpacked cardboard delivery boxes were left obstructing Fire Exit B at 11:30, causing a visiting outpatient to trip." Replaces 'some stuff' and 'earlier' with exact quantities, location, time, and hazard.
"We need better computers because the ones we have are really slow and annoying." "Upgrading the reception desktop terminals will reduce patient check-in times from five minutes to under sixty seconds." Replaces emotional complaint ('annoying') with measurable operational benefit.
"People aren't washing their hands properly so germs are spreading." "Recent infection-control audits revealed a 15% drop in hand-hygiene compliance prior to patient contact." Uses authoritative workplace register and concrete audit evidence.

Core Skill 3: Level 1 vs Level 2 Clarity & Detail Standards

How examiners judge 'sufficient detail' and 'effective communication' across both levels.

  • Level 1 Standard (L1.20): Statements must be unambiguous and directly relevant to the task. Dates, times, prices, and locations must be stated clearly so the recipient knows exactly what to do.
  • Level 2 Standard (L2.21): Ideas must be developed with persuasive or analytical depth. Instead of merely stating a rule, explain the rationale behind the rule and the consequence of non-compliance.

Benchmark Exam Model 2 Level 2 Precision & Register Benchmark (4 Marks)

Vocational Context: Healthcare Incident Reporting

Rewrite this confusing, colloquial incident note for a formal clinical governance log:
'There was medication which was left out because someone didn't put it back when they finished their shift and then someone else almost gave it to the wrong patient on bed 4.'
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Identify key factual components and remove vague pronouns
Replace 'someone' and 'they' with specific clinical roles and exact handover timing.
Criteria: Eliminates Ambiguity (L2.21)
Step 2 Apply formal clinical incident terminology
Use precise terms: 'unsecured medication', 'shift changeover', 'near-miss administration', 'remedial lock-check'.

Step 3 Structure as Situation → Risk → Immediate Containment
Draft three clear sentences stating the security breach, the near-miss event, and the corrective action taken.

Consequently, a near-miss occurred when the medication was almost administered to the resident in Bed 4. The medication was immediately returned to the locked controlled-drugs cabinet, and all staff were briefed on mandatory end-of-shift counter checks.'
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Using Vague Pronouns like 'It', 'This', and 'They'
Writing 'They told them about it, which caused delays' leaves the examiner guessing who spoke, who listened, and what was delayed.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always name the exact role and object: 'The Warehouse Supervisor informed the delivery drivers of the road closure.'
Examiner Pitfall: Contradictory Statements Within the Same Response
Saying in Paragraph 1 that a training course is 'completely free for staff' and in Paragraph 3 that 'staff only pay £25 each' destroys coherence.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Check that figures, dates, and policies remain 100% consistent from your opening sentence to your conclusion.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Rewrite this vague workplace sentence so it communicates clearly and professionally:
'The machines are broken so workers are sitting around and delivery times are going down the drain.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Replace colloquialisms ('sitting around', 'going down the drain') with formal operational terms.
2. State cause and effect clearly in a well-punctuated sentence.
Final Model Answer: 'Due to an unexpected conveyor belt malfunction on Line 2, packing operations have halted and customer delivery schedules are experiencing significant delays.'
Examiner Tip: Always replace idioms like 'down the drain' with concrete workplace impacts like 'experiencing significant delays'.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Eliminate the wordy filler phrases and vague pronouns in this draft email sentence:
'At this moment in time, due to the fact that they haven't sent the stuff we ordered last week, we are basically unable to finish doing their kitchen job on time.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Replace 'At this moment in time' with 'Currently' and 'due to the fact that' with 'because'.
2. Replace 'they', 'the stuff', and 'their kitchen job' with specific nouns (e.g. the supplier, the ceramic wall tiles, the kitchen installation).
Final Model Answer: 'Currently, because our supplier has not yet delivered the ceramic wall tiles ordered last week, we are unable to complete the kitchen installation on schedule.'
Examiner Tip: Cutting bloated phrases makes your writing sharper, faster to read, and far more authoritative.
Exam Practice Question 3 4 Marks
You are a Care Home Team Leader. Write a clear, 3-sentence handover note for the night shift explaining that Resident Mr Arthur Evans (Room 12) had his blood pressure medication adjusted by Dr Patel at 15:00 today, requires his blood pressure checked at 21:00, and must be monitored for dizziness when standing.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Sentence 1 (Situation): State resident name, room number, and exact medication change by Dr Patel at 15:00.
2. Sentence 2 (Required Action): Instruct night staff to record his blood pressure at 21:00.
3. Sentence 3 (Safety Monitoring): Specify observation for postural dizziness during mobility transfers.
Final Model Answer: 'At 15:00 today, Dr Patel adjusted the blood pressure medication for Mr Arthur Evans in Room 12. Night-shift staff must check and record Mr Evans's blood pressure on his observation chart at 21:00. Additionally, please supervise him closely when standing or transferring, as the revised dosage may cause temporary dizziness.'
Examiner Tip: Notice how every clinical detail (Who, Where, When, What action, Why) is stated with zero ambiguity.
