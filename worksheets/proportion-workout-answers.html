Question 1 Worked Solution Building & Mortar Mixing
[3 Marks Available]
A bricklayer mixes sand and cement in the ratio 4 : 1 by weight. He needs to produce 350 kg of mortar mix.
Work out the weight of sand and the weight of cement required.
Total parts: 4 + 1 = 5 parts
[M1]
Value of 1 part (cement): 350 ÷ 5 = 70 kg
[M1]
Value of 4 parts (sand): 70 × 4 = 280 kg
[A1]
Correct Final Answer: Sand: 280 kg, Cement: 70 kg
Pearson Reverse Check Method: 280 + 70 = 350 kg; 280 : 70 = 4 : 1.
Common Exam Trap / Pitfall: Multiplying 350 by 4 and 1 without dividing by the total parts first.
Question 2 Worked Solution Workplace Business Profits
[3 Marks Available]
Three partners, Alex, Brian and Clara, invest in a catering business in the ratio 2 : 3 : 5. At the end of the year, the business generates a net profit of £48,000.
How much profit does Clara receive?
Calculate total ratio parts: 2 + 3 + 5 = 10 parts
[M1]
Find value of 1 part: £48,000 ÷ 10 = £4,800
[M1]
Multiply by Clara's share (5 parts): £4,800 × 5 = £24,000
[A1]
Correct Final Answer: £24,000.00
Pearson Reverse Check Method: Alex = £9,600, Brian = £14,400, Clara = £24,000. Total = £48,000.
Common Exam Trap / Pitfall: Dividing profit equally by 3 rather than using ratio shares.
Question 3 Worked Solution Care Home Staff Ratios
[2 Marks Available]
A specialist residential care home operates with a required staffing ratio of 1 healthcare assistant for every 5 residents.
On Thursday night, there are 42 residents in the facility.
What is the minimum number of healthcare assistants that must be on duty?
Divide residents by ratio factor: 42 ÷ 5 = 8.4
[M1]
Round UP to nearest whole person to maintain mandatory compliance: 9 assistants
[A1]
Correct Final Answer: 9 assistants
Pearson Reverse Check Method: 8 assistants × 5 = 40 (insufficient for 42). 9 assistants × 5 = 45 (compliant).
Common Exam Trap / Pitfall: Rounding down to 8 assistants (leaving 2 residents unattended).
Question 4 Worked Solution Chemical Cleaning Dilution
[3 Marks Available]
An industrial disinfectant concentrate must be diluted with water in the ratio 1 : 25 (1 part concentrate to 25 parts water).
A cleaning supervisor needs to make 13 litres of ready-to-use cleaning solution.
How many millilitres of concentrate does she need?
Convert 13 litres to millilitres: 13 × 1,000 = 13,000 ml
[M1]
Total ratio parts: 1 + 25 = 26 parts
[M1]
Concentrate required (1 part): 13,000 ÷ 26 = 500 ml
[A1]
Correct Final Answer: 500 ml
Pearson Reverse Check Method: 500 ml concentrate + (25 × 500 = 12,500 ml water) = 13,000 ml = 13 litres.
Common Exam Trap / Pitfall: Dividing by 25 instead of total parts (26).
Question 5 Worked Solution Architectural Scale Plan
[3 Marks Available]
A warehouse building plan is drawn to a scale of 1 : 50.
On the plan, the width of the loading bay is 16.4 cm.
Work out the actual real-life width of the loading bay in metres.
Multiply plan length by scale factor: 16.4 cm × 50 = 820 cm
[M1]
Convert centimetres to metres (divide by 100): 820 ÷ 100 = 8.2 m
[M1]
State real-life width: 8.2 metres
[A1]
Correct Final Answer: 8.2 metres
Pearson Reverse Check Method: 8.2 m = 820 cm; 820 ÷ 50 = 16.4 cm.
Common Exam Trap / Pitfall: Dividing by 50 instead of multiplying, or forgetting to convert cm to m.
Question 6 Worked Solution Vehicle Fleet Fuel Efficiency
[3 Marks Available]
A courier van travels 270 miles on 36 litres of diesel fuel.
At this rate of fuel consumption, how many litres of diesel will the van need to complete a route of 450 miles?
Find miles per litre (unit rate): 270 ÷ 36 = 7.5 miles per litre
[M1]
Calculate fuel needed for 450 miles: 450 ÷ 7.5 = 60 litres (or (36/270) × 450)
[M1]
State total fuel required: 60 litres
[A1]
Correct Final Answer: 60 litres
Pearson Reverse Check Method: 60 litres × 7.5 mpg = 450 miles.
Common Exam Trap / Pitfall: Multiplying 450 by 7.5 instead of dividing.
