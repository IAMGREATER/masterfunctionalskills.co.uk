Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • L1.22 / L2.24 – Calculate the total surface area of cubes and cuboids in practical wrapping and painting contexts.
  • L2.24 – Calculate the surface area of triangular prisms, cylinders, and open-top containers.

Golden Rules for Surface Area of Prisms & Cylinders

Golden Rule 1: Surface Area is the Total Area of All Outside Faces

Surface area is 2D area (measured in cm² or m²), NOT 3D volume. For a closed cuboid: calculate the area of each pair of matching faces and add all 6 faces together: (2 × Top/Bottom) + (2 × Front/Back) + (2 × Left/Right).

Golden Rule / Method: Cuboid Surface Area = 2(lw) + 2(lh) + 2(wh)
Golden Rule 2: Cylinder Surface Area = Two Circles Plus One Unwrapped Rectangle

The curved body unrolls into a flat rectangle (length = π × Diameter, height = cylinder height). Add the two circular end caps: Total Surface Area = (2 × π × r²) + (π × d × h).

Golden Rule / Method: Cylinder SA = 2πr² + πdh

Core Skill 1: Cuboid 6-Face Method vs Open-Top Containers

Step-by-step breakdown of cuboid faces and how to adjust for 5-face open tanks or rooms.

A closed rectangular box has 6 rectangular faces arranged in 3 identical pairs:

  • Top & Bottom Pair: 2 × (Length × Width)
  • Front & Back Pair: 2 × (Length × Height)
  • Left & Right Ends: 2 × (Width × Height)
  • Open-Top / No-Base Containers (5 Faces): Only include 1 × (Length × Width) plus the 4 vertical walls.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Surface Area of a Triangular Prism (5 Faces)

Adding two triangular end cross-sections to three rectangular side panels.

A triangular prism (such as a roof tent or Toblerone-style package) has 5 faces:

  1. Two Triangular Ends: Each triangle is (Base × Vertical Height) ÷ 2. Together, the two ends equal Base × Vertical Height.
  2. Base Rectangle: Base Width × Prism Length.
  3. Two Sloping Roof Rectangles: Slant Edge × Prism Length (use the slanted edge length, NOT the vertical height!).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Curved & Total Surface Area of a Cylinder

Unrolling the curved wall into a rectangle and adding circular end caps.

Component Formula (using π = 3.14) When to Use
Curved Wall Only3.14 × Diameter × HeightPipe insulation, tin can paper labels, paint roller coverage
One Circular End3.14 × Radius × RadiusOpen-top cylindrical bin or water butt (Curved Wall + 1 Circle)
Closed Cylinder (All 3 Faces)(2 × 3.14 × r²) + (3.14 × d × h)Sealed oil drum, closed medical gas tank

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1)

Vocational Context: Healthcare Supply Chain Packaging

A sterile equipment crate is a closed cuboid measuring 50 cm long, 40 cm wide, and 30 cm high.
Calculate the total external surface area of the crate in cm².
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate Top & Bottom faces
2 × (50 cm × 40 cm) = 2 × 2,000 = 4,000 cm².
Criteria: M1: Top and bottom pair
Step 2 Calculate Front/Back and Left/Right faces
Front & Back = 2 × (50 cm × 30 cm) = 2 × 1,500 = 3,000 cm².
Left & Right = 2 × (40 cm × 30 cm) = 2 × 1,200 = 2,400 cm².
Criteria: M1: Remaining four faces
Step 3 Sum all 6 faces
Total Surface Area = 4,000 + 3,000 + 2,400 = 9,400 cm².
Criteria: A1: Accurate total surface area (9,400 cm²)
Final Accredited Answer: 9,400 cm²
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Write down the three pairs clearly so the examiner can award method marks even if one multiplication slips.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2)

Vocational Context: Facility Maintenance Painting & Costing

A facilities maintenance team must apply anti-rust primer to the 5 external faces (four vertical sides and the top roof; NOT the base on the ground) of a steel container measuring 3.5 m long, 2.0 m wide, and 1.8 m high.
• Anti-rust primer covers 6 m² per tin and costs £18.50 per tin.
Calculate the total cost of the primer tins required.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate the single top roof face (no base)
Top = 3.5 m × 2.0 m = 7.0 m².
Criteria: M1: Single top face area
Step 2 Calculate the 4 vertical side walls
Front & Back = 2 × (3.5 m × 1.8 m) = 2 × 6.3 = 12.6 m².
Left & Right = 2 × (2.0 m × 1.8 m) = 2 × 3.6 = 7.2 m².
Total 5-face Surface Area = 7.0 + 12.6 + 7.2 = 26.8 m².
Criteria: M1: Total 5-face surface area (26.8 m²)
Step 3 Calculate whole tins of primer needed
Tins = 26.8 m² ÷ 6 m² = 4.466... → Round UP to 5 whole tins.
Criteria: M1: Division by coverage and rounding up
Step 4 Calculate total purchase cost
5 tins × £18.50 = £92.50.
Criteria: A1: Accurate total cost (£92.50)
Final Accredited Answer: £92.50 (5 tins required for 26.8 m²)
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Notice the question specifies 5 EXTERNAL FACES (no base). Always round paint tins UP so you do not run out with 2.8 m² left unpainted.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Calculating Volume Instead of Surface Area
Multiplying Length × Width × Height (e.g. 50 × 40 × 30 = 60,000 cm³) calculates 3D volume, scoring 0 marks on a surface area question.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Highlight the words "SURFACE AREA" and write down the 3 pairs of rectangles before touching your calculator.
Examiner Pitfall: Using Diameter Instead of Radius for Cylinder Ends
When calculating the two circular ends of a cylinder, learners often forget to halve the diameter first.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always write "Radius = Diameter ÷ 2" at the very top of your working box.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Calculate the total surface area of a closed cardboard shipping box measuring 40 cm long, 30 cm wide, and 20 cm high.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Top & Bottom: 2 × (40 × 30) = 2 × 1,200 = 2,400 cm².
2. Front & Back: 2 × (40 × 20) = 2 × 800 = 1,600 cm².
3. Left & Right: 2 × (30 × 20) = 2 × 600 = 1,200 cm².
4. Total Surface Area = 2,400 + 1,600 + 1,200 = 5,200 cm².
Final Correct Answer: 5,200 cm²
Examiner Tip: Sum the three pairs of matching rectangular faces.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
A closed cylindrical chemical storage drum has a radius of 0.5 m and a height of 1.2 m. Using π = 3.14, calculate the total external surface area of the drum in m².
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Two circular ends: 2 × 3.14 × 0.5 × 0.5 = 1.57 m².
2. Curved wall (Diameter = 1.0 m): 3.14 × 1.0 × 1.2 = 3.768 m².
3. Total Surface Area = 1.57 + 3.768 = 5.338 m².
Final Correct Answer: 5.338 m² (or 5.34 m²)
Examiner Tip: Unroll the cylinder into 2 circles (2 × 3.14 × r²) plus 1 rectangle (3.14 × d × h).
Exam Practice Question 3 4 Marks
A waterproof canvas tent is shaped as a triangular prism of length 2.5 m. The triangular front and back ends each have a base of 1.6 m, a vertical height of 1.5 m, and two sloping roof edges of 1.7 m each.
Calculate the total surface area of canvas needed for all 5 faces (including the groundsheet base).
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Two triangular ends: 2 × ((1.6 × 1.5) ÷ 2) = 2.4 m².
2. Groundsheet base rectangle: 1.6 m × 2.5 m = 4.0 m².
3. Two sloping roof rectangles: 2 × (1.7 m × 2.5 m) = 2 × 4.25 = 8.5 m².
4. Total Canvas Surface Area = 2.4 + 4.0 + 8.5 = 14.9 m².
Final Correct Answer: 14.9 m²
Examiner Tip: Use the vertical height (1.5 m) for the triangles, and the slant edge (1.7 m) for the sloping rectangular roof panels.
