Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • L1.15 / L2.17 – Compare costs and unit pricing (e.g. price per 100g, per litre) to determine the best value option.

Golden Rules for Best Buys & Unit Pricing

Golden Rule 1: Unit Price = Cost ÷ Quantity

Always divide the PRICE by the QUANTITY to find the cost of 1 unit (1 gram, 100g, 1 kg, 1 litre). The option with the LOWEST unit price is the best value.

Golden Rule / Method: Unit Price = £ Price ÷ Quantity
Golden Rule 2: Convert to Common Units Before Dividing

Never compare a 750g tub with a 2 kg tub directly. Convert 2 kg to 2,000g, or compare both per 100g or per 1 kg.

Golden Rule / Method: Match units first: grams to grams, ml to ml

Core Skill 1: Skill 1: The Unit Cost Method (Cost per Litre, per kg, or per Item)

The universal calculator method for any pack size comparison.

  • Cost per 1 Litre / 1 kg: Divide price by litres or kilograms (e.g. 2.5 L for £4.50 → £4.50 ÷ 2.5 = £1.80 per litre).
  • Cost per 100g / 100ml: Divide price by the number of 100g units (e.g. 600g for £2.70 → £2.70 ÷ 6 = £0.45 (45p) per 100g).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Skill 2: Scaling to a Common Quantity (Non-Calculator Best Method)

Multiplying up to a common weight or volume without doing awkward divisions.

When comparing 400 g for £1.65 and 600 g for £2.40, scale both to their Lowest Common Multiple (1,200 g):

  • 400 g pack × 3 (1,200 g): 3 × £1.65 = £4.95.
  • 600 g pack × 2 (1,200 g): 2 × £2.40 = £4.80.
  • Since £4.80 < £4.95, the 600 g pack is better value by 15p per 1.2 kg.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Skill 3: Evaluating Multi-Buy Offers ("3 for 2" & "Buy One Get One Half Price")

How to find the true effective unit price of promotional bundles.

Promotional Offer How to Calculate Bundle Cost Example (Base Price £6.00)
3 for the Price of 2 Pay for 2 items, receive 3 items 2 × £6 = £12 for 3 → £4.00 each
Buy 1, Get 2nd Half Price Pay 1.5× single price for 2 items £6 + £3 = £9 for 2 → £4.50 each
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1/2)

Vocational Context: Care Facility Commercial Purchasing

A care home procurement manager must purchase commercial laundry detergent. Two brands are available:
• Brand A: 4.5 litres for £14.40
• Brand B: 6 litres for £18.60
(a) Calculate the cost per litre for each brand.
(b) Which brand offers the best value for money?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate cost per litre for Brand A
£14.40 ÷ 4.5 litres = £3.20 per litre.
Criteria: M1: Brand A unit division
Step 2 Calculate cost per litre for Brand B
£18.60 ÷ 6 litres = £3.10 per litre.
Criteria: M1: Brand B unit division
Step 3 Compare unit prices and conclude
Brand B is £3.10 per litre, which is 10p cheaper per litre than Brand A (£3.20). Therefore, Brand B offers the best value.
Criteria: A1: Correct comparison and conclusion
Final Accredited Answer: (a) Brand A = £3.20/L, Brand B = £3.10/L | (b) Brand B is the best value (10p cheaper per litre).
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Always conclude with a written sentence naming the winning option and stating both unit prices.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2 Problem Solving)

Vocational Context: Clinical Supplies Procurement

A dental practice needs to buy 24 rolls of clinical couch roll. Three wholesalers offer the following deals:
• Wholesaler A: Packs of 6 rolls for £19.50, with a "Buy 3 packs, get 4th pack free" promotion.
• Wholesaler B: Packs of 8 rolls for £24.00, with a 15% trade discount on all orders.
• Wholesaler C: A bulk box of 24 rolls for £59.50.

Which wholesaler is the cheapest way to buy 24 rolls, and what is the lowest total cost?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate cost for 24 rolls with Wholesaler A
24 rolls ÷ 6 rolls/pack = 4 packs needed.
With "Buy 3, get 4th free", the practice pays for 3 packs: 3 × £19.50 = £58.50.
Criteria: M1: Wholesaler A cost (£58.50)
Step 2 Calculate cost for 24 rolls with Wholesaler B
24 rolls ÷ 8 rolls/pack = 3 packs needed.
List price = 3 × £24.00 = £72.00.
After 15% discount: £72.00 × 0.85 = £61.20.
Criteria: M1: Wholesaler B discounted cost (£61.20)
Step 3 Compare all three totals for 24 rolls
Wholesaler A = £58.50 | Wholesaler B = £61.20 | Wholesaler C = £59.50.
Criteria: M1: Direct comparison of all 3 options
Step 4 State final decision and lowest cost
Wholesaler A is the cheapest option at £58.50 (£1.00 cheaper than Wholesaler C and £2.70 cheaper than Wholesaler B).
Criteria: A1: Correct wholesaler (A) and £58.50 total
Final Accredited Answer: Wholesaler A is cheapest at £58.50 (vs £61.20 for B and £59.50 for C).
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
When a target quantity is given (24 rolls), comparing the total cost for all 24 rolls is much faster and more accurate than finding the price of 1 single roll!

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Dividing Quantity by Price Instead of Price by Quantity
Calculating 4.5 ÷ 14.40 = 0.3125 gives litres per pound, not cost per litre. While mathematically valid, learners frequently misinterpret whether the higher or lower number is better.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always do Money ÷ Amount: Cost per Litre = £ ÷ Litres (lower cost is better).

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A supermarket sells olive oil in two bottle sizes: 250 ml for £2.80, and 750 ml for £7.20. Which size is better value for money? Show working to support your answer.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Scale 250 ml up to 750 ml (multiply by 3): 3 × £2.80 = £8.40.
2. Compare: £7.20 for 750 ml is £1.20 cheaper than £8.40.
3. Conclusion: The 750 ml bottle is better value.
Final Correct Answer: 750 ml bottle is better value (£7.20 vs £8.40 for 750 ml).
Examiner Tip: Multiplying 250 ml by 3 is faster and cleaner than dividing.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Coffee granules are sold in three jar sizes:
• Small: 200 g for £3.60
• Medium: 500 g for £8.50
• Large: 1.2 kg for £21.00
Work out the cost per 100 g for each jar to determine which jar offers the best value.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Small (200 g): £3.60 ÷ 2 = £1.80 per 100 g.
2. Medium (500 g): £8.50 ÷ 5 = £1.70 per 100 g.
3. Large (1.2 kg = 1,200 g): £21.00 ÷ 12 = £1.75 per 100 g.
4. Best value: The Medium (500 g) jar is cheapest at £1.70 per 100 g.
Final Correct Answer: Medium 500 g jar is best value (£1.70 per 100g vs £1.75 for Large and £1.80 for Small).
Examiner Tip: Never assume the largest pack is automatically the cheapest! Examiners love designing questions where the middle size wins.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
An office manager needs 30 A4 notebooks. Shop X sells them at £2.40 each with a "3 for the price of 2" offer. Shop Y sells packs of 10 notebooks for £16.50 with a 10% discount. Which shop is cheaper for 30 notebooks?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Shop X: 30 notebooks = 10 groups of 3 → pay for 20 notebooks: 20 × £2.40 = £48.00.
2. Shop Y: 3 packs of 10 = 3 × £16.50 = £49.50. Less 10% (£4.95) = £44.55.
3. Conclusion: Shop Y is cheaper (£44.55 vs £48.00).
Final Correct Answer: Shop Y is cheaper (£44.55 vs £48.00 at Shop X).
Examiner Tip: In a "3 for 2" deal on 30 items, you pay for 2/3 of the items (20 items).
