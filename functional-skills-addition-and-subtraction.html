Golden Rules for Addition & Subtraction

Golden Rule 1: Vertical Column Alignment

Always align units over units, tens over tens, and decimal points over decimal points. Misaligned columns cause 80% of manual arithmetic errors. Draw a vertical guideline right down the decimal points before adding any numbers.

Golden Rule / Method: Line up decimal points vertically • Pad empty spots with zeros (.00)
Golden Rule 2: Mandatory Reverse Operation Check

In Pearson Edexcel Non-Calculator exams, 1 mark is often explicitly awarded for showing a reverse operation check (e.g. if A - B = C, prove that C + B = A). Always write the full equation with the answer after the equals sign.

Golden Rule / Method: Check: Result + Subtrahend = Starting Float / Total
Golden Rule 3: The 2 Decimal Places Currency Rule

In UK Functional Skills examinations, monetary answers MUST always be written to exactly 2 decimal places. Writing £55.4 or £8 will cost you the accuracy mark [A1]!

Accredited Format: £55.40 ✓ (Never £55.4 ✗) • £8.00 ✓ (Never £8 ✗)

Step 1 Trigger the Cascading Borrow (Th → H)
You cannot compute 0 - 8. The units, tens, and hundreds all have zeros! Go all the way to Thousands (5). Cross out 5 → write 4. The Hundreds column now has 10.
Step 2 Borrow from Hundreds into Tens
Borrow 1 hundred from the 10 hundreds. Cross out 10 → write 9. The Tens column now has 10.
Step 3 Borrow from Tens into Units
Borrow 1 ten from the 10 tens. Cross out 10 → write 9. The Units column now has 10!
Step 4 Subtract Column-by-Column (Right to Left)
U: 10 − 8 = 2  |  T: 9 − 6 = 3  |  H: 9 − 4 = 5  |  Th: 4 − 1 = 3
Every single column works out smoothly without negative numbers! Result: 3,532.
Subi's Shortcut: The "Nines Domino Rule"

"Notice the golden pattern: whenever you borrow across several zeros from a lead digit, the first digit goes down by 1 (5 → 4), all middle zeros turn directly into 9, and only the very last zero becomes 10! Remembering this will save you 2 minutes of messy scribbles in your exam."

What Gets 1 Full Mark [B1]
  • Full equation with answer: e.g. 551 + 299 = 850.
  • Using the opposite operation: (+ reversed into −, or − reversed into +).
  • Exact numerical agreement: The check confirms the starting figure.
What Gets ZERO Marks [0 / 1]
  • Incomplete calculation: Writing 551 + 299 without = 850!
  • Writing words instead of math: "I checked my work", "I did it again on paper".
  • Repeating the original calculation: Writing 850 - 299 = 551 again.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Trap 1: Flipping Digits Upside Down (e.g. 0 − 6 = 6)
When calculating column subtraction (e.g. 50 − 26), learners often see 0 on top and 6 on the bottom, and mistakenly think: "6 take away 0 is 6!", giving an answer of 34 or 36. This is the single most common subtraction error across the UK.
WRONG METHOD (0 Marks):
0 − 6 → writes 6 (Flipped subtraction!)
CORRECT METHOD (Full Marks):
Borrow 1 from tens → 10 − 6 = 4 ✓
How to Avoid: If the top digit is smaller than the bottom digit, you MUST borrow from the column immediately to the left. Top number ALWAYS has priority.
Trap 2: Right-Aligning Decimals Like Whole Numbers
When adding £12.50 + £3.40 + £8, learners sometimes shove £8 all the way to the right under the zero, turning 8 pounds into 8 pence (0.08)!
How to Avoid: Never line numbers up by the right-hand edge when decimals are present. Line them up strictly by the decimal point and pad empty spaces with .00.
