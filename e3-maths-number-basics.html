3 Simple Entry 3 Golden Rules: Number Basics

Golden Rule 1: Use H | T | U Columns for Every 3-Digit Number

Every 3-digit number has three columns from left to right: Hundreds (H), Tens (T), and Units (U).

Entry 3 Example: In 468: 4 = 400, 6 = 60, and 8 = 8 units.
Golden Rule 2: Keep Zero (0) as a Column Holder

When a number has no tens or no units, always write 0 so the other digits stay in their proper columns.

Entry 3 Example: Four hundred and seven = 407 (NOT 47!).
Golden Rule 3: Check the Last Digit for Odd or Even

Only look at the Units digit on the right: 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 are Even; 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 are Odd.

Entry 3 Example: 843 ends in 3 → Odd | 596 ends in 6 → Even.

Core Skill 1: Reading & Writing Numbers up to 1,000

Say the Hundreds first, then read the Tens and Units together.

345 → Three hundred and forty-five
Notice 'forty' is spelt f-o-r-t-y (no 'u').
708 → Seven hundred and eight
Zero tens means we jump straight from 'seven hundred' to 'and eight'.
1,000 → One thousand
Ten hundreds make 1,000 — the top number tested at Entry Level 3.

Core Skill 2: Comparing & Ordering 3-Digit Numbers

Compare from left to right: check Hundreds first, then Tens, then Units.

Order smallest to largest: 482, 428, 824
Compare Hundreds: 4, 4, 8 → 824 is the biggest.
Tie-breaker on Tens: 428 vs 482
2 tens (20) is smaller than 8 tens (80), so 428 comes before 482.
Final order: 428, 482, 824
Always re-read the question to check 'smallest first' vs 'largest first'!
Sonya’s Entry 3 Exam Tip
Whenever you hear a number like 'five hundred and four', draw three quick dashes _ _ _ on your rough paper first. Fill in 5 on the left and 4 on the right — then pop a 0 in the empty middle gap to get 504 every single time!

Entry Level 3 Step-by-Step Worked Exam Question

Entry Level 3 Exam Model 3 Marks (Non-Calculator)

Everyday Scenario: Community Library Ticket Numbers

Four visitors take numbered queue tickets at a community hub: 390, 309, 903, and 349.
(a) Write these ticket numbers in order starting with the smallest.
(b) Which ticket number is an even number?
Step
Clear Working Out
Mark Focus
Step 1 Compare the Hundreds digits first
390 (3H), 309 (3H), 903 (9H), 349 (3H) → 903 is largest
[M1] Correct left-to-right comparison
Step 2 Order the three 300-numbers using Tens
309 (0 tens), 349 (4 tens), 390 (9 tens)
[A1] Exact order: 309, 349, 390, 903
Step 3 Spot the even Units digit (0, 2, 4, 6, 8)
390 ends in 0 (the others end in 9 or 3)
[B1] Even ticket = 390
Final Exam Answer: (a) 309, 349, 390, 903 | (b) 390
Sonya’s Check Reminder: Always include units (£, p, cm, g, ml, or °C) and double-check you answered the exact question asked!

Common Entry 3 Mistakes to Avoid

Common Entry 3 Mistake: Leaving Out the Placeholder Zero
Writing 'three hundred and six' as 36 instead of 306. Always check that a hundred-number has 3 digits!
Common Entry 3 Mistake: Looking at the First Digit for Odd/Even
Saying 841 is even because 8 is even — ONLY the last digit (1) decides odd or even, so 841 is odd!

Interactive Entry Level 3 Practice Questions

Entry 3 Practice 1: Warehouse Crate Labels 2 Marks
Write the number eight hundred and fifty-two in figures, and write down the value of the digit 5.
Entry Level 3 Worked Solution:
Figures (1 Mark): 852
Value of 5 (1 Mark): 50 (or 5 tens)
Final Answer: 852; value of 5 is 50
Entry 3 Practice 2: Ticket Order Check 2 Marks
Put these parcel weights (in grams) in order from largest to smallest: 615, 651, 561, 605.
Entry Level 3 Worked Solution:
Compare Hundreds & Tens (1 Mark): 651 > 615 > 605 > 561
Largest first (1 Mark): 651, 615, 605, 561
Final Answer: 651, 615, 605, 561
