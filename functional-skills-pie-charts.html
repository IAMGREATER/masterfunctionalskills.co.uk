Golden Rules for Pie Charts & Proportional Angles

Golden Rule 1: The Magic Multiplier: Degrees per Item = 360° ÷ Total Frequency

Divide 360° by the total frequency to find how many degrees represent ONE item. Multiply each category's frequency by this magic multiplier, and check that all your angles add up to exactly 360°!

Golden Rule / Method: Sector Angle = (Frequency ÷ Total) × 360° • Check Sum = 360°
Golden Rule 2: Working Backwards from an Angle: (Angle ÷ 360°) × Total

If given a sector angle (e.g. 90° or 108°) and the total number of items (e.g. 200 people): Frequency = (Angle ÷ 360°) × Total.

Golden Rule / Method: Frequency = (Sector Angle ÷ 360°) × Total Frequency

Core Skill 2: Extracting Frequencies When Given One Sector’s Value (Without the Total)

Using proportional scaling per degree when the grand total is not stated.

Sometimes a question tells you: "The 60° sector represents 30 patients. How many patients are in the 150° sector?"

  • Find Degrees per Patient: 60° ÷ 30 patients = 2° per patient (or 0.5 patients per degree).
  • Scale the Target Sector: 150° ÷ 2° = 75 patients (and Total = 360° ÷ 2° = 180 patients).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Comparing Two Pie Charts with Different Total Frequencies

Why a larger angle does NOT automatically mean a larger number of people!

Classic Level 2 Exam Trap:

  • Clinic A (Total = 100 patients) has a 90° sector → (90 ÷ 360) × 100 = 25 patients.
  • Clinic B (Total = 400 patients) has a 72° sector → (72 ÷ 360) × 400 = 80 patients.
  • Even though Clinic B has a smaller angle (72° vs 90°), it has more patients (80 vs 25) because its total population is four times larger!
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1/2)

Vocational Context: Facility Environmental Sustainability

A hospital records its annual energy consumption of 1,000 MWh by source:
• Gas: 500 MWh | Electricity: 300 MWh | Solar: 120 MWh | Biomass: 80 MWh
Calculate the sector angle in degrees for each of the four energy sources on a pie chart.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate degrees per MWh (360° ÷ 1,000)
360° ÷ 1,000 MWh = 0.36° per MWh.
Criteria: M1: Angle multiplier (0.36° per MWh)
Step 2 Multiply each energy quantity by 0.36°
Gas = 500 × 0.36° = 180°
Electricity = 300 × 0.36° = 108°
Solar = 120 × 0.36° = 43.2°
Biomass = 80 × 0.36° = 28.8°
Criteria: M1: Sector multiplications
Step 3 Check that all four angles sum to 360°
180° + 108° + 43.2° + 28.8° = 360°. Confirmed.
Criteria: A1: All 4 angles accurate
Final Accredited Answer: Gas = 180°, Electricity = 108°, Solar = 43.2°, Biomass = 28.8°
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Always add your final angles together to verify they sum to 360°.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2)

Vocational Context: Logistics Fleet Decarbonisation Audit

Two regional delivery depots display pie charts of their vehicle fleets:
• North Depot (Total = 180 vehicles): The Electric Van sector angle is 100°.
• South Depot (Total = 300 vehicles): The Electric Van sector angle is 72°.
The fleet director claims: "North Depot has a greater number of Electric Vans than South Depot because its pie chart sector angle is larger (100° vs 72°)."
Is the fleet director correct? Show full calculations for both depots.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate number of Electric Vans at North Depot
North = (100° ÷ 360°) × 180 = 50 electric vans.
Criteria: M1 / A1: North Depot count (50 vans)
Step 2 Calculate number of Electric Vans at South Depot
South = (72° ÷ 360°) × 300 = 0.2 × 300 = 60 electric vans.
Criteria: M1: South Depot count (60 vans)
Step 3 Compare counts and state conclusion
No, the director is incorrect. North Depot has a higher proportion, but South Depot has 60 electric vans compared to 50 at North Depot (10 more vans).
Criteria: A1: Valid comparison and No conclusion
Final Accredited Answer: Incorrect — South Depot has 60 electric vans vs 50 at North Depot
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Pie chart angles only show PROPORTIONS. You must multiply by each depot’s total fleet size to compare actual vehicle counts!

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Using 100 Instead of 360° for Pie Chart Angles
Calculating percentages (out of 100) and plotting those directly as degrees leaves two-thirds of the circle empty!
How to Avoid This Pitfall: A full circle is 360°, not 100°.
Examiner Pitfall: Measuring Each New Sector from the Original Top Vertical Line
When drawing a pie chart with a protractor, you must measure each new sector starting from the line of the PREVIOUS sector you just drew.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Rotate your protractor so its zero line sits on the edge of the sector you just completed.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
In a pie chart representing 120 staff members, the "Full-Time" sector has an angle of 210°. How many staff members work full-time?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Fraction of circle = 210° ÷ 360° = 7/12.
2. Number of staff = (210 ÷ 360) × 120 = 70 staff members.
Final Correct Answer: 70 staff members
Examiner Tip: (210 ÷ 360) × 120 = 70.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
A care home survey of 90 residents shows: 38 chose Gardening, 27 chose Music, and 25 chose Bingo. Calculate the pie chart sector angle for each activity.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Multiplier = 360° ÷ 90 = 4° per resident.
2. Gardening = 38 × 4° = 152°.
3. Music = 27 × 4° = 108°.
4. Bingo = 25 × 4° = 100°.
5. Check: 152° + 108° + 100° = 360°.
Final Correct Answer: Gardening = 152°, Music = 108°, Bingo = 100°
Examiner Tip: 360° ÷ 90 = 4° per resident makes the multiplications super fast!
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
On a budget pie chart, the "IT Software" sector has an angle of 54° and represents £13,500. Calculate the total budget represented by the entire 360° pie chart.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Value per degree = £13,500 ÷ 54° = £250 per degree.
2. Total 360° budget = £250 × 360 = £90,000.
Final Correct Answer: £90,000
Examiner Tip: Divide £13,500 by 54° to find the value of 1° (£250), then multiply by 360°.
