Section A: Core Skills (Warm-Up) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Identifying Correlation & Using a Line of Best Fit) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A scatter graph plots Outdoor Temperature (°C) on the horizontal axis against Daily Hospital Heating Gas Usage (m³) on the vertical axis.
As outdoor temperature increases, gas usage decreases steadily.
State the type of correlation shown, and use the line of best fit equation y = 400 - 12x to estimate gas usage when the outdoor temperature (x) is 15°C.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify correlation: Negative correlation (as x increases, y decreases) [M1]
2. Substitute x = 15: y = 400 - (12 × 15) = 400 - 180 = 220 m³ [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Negative correlation; 220 m³
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Calling a downward-sloping relationship 'weak positive' instead of 'negative correlation'.
🔄 Quick Check: At x = 0°C, usage = 400 m³; dropping 12 m³ per degree over 15°C drops by 180 m³ to 220 m³.
Question 2 (Level 1 Foundation — 2 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A scatter graph plots the age of second-hand delivery vans (years) against their resale value (£).
(a) State the type of correlation you would expect.
(b) Describe the relationship in words.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Type: Negative correlation. [M1]
2. Relationship: As the age of the van increases, its resale value decreases. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) Negative correlation | (b) As van age increases, resale value decreases
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Connecting scatter points with a zigzag line instead of drawing ONE straight line with a ruler scores 0 marks for the line of best fit.
🔄 Quick Check: State both the statistical name (Negative) and the real-world meaning.
Section B: Everyday & Workplace Maths Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Logistics Van Fuel Consumption vs Cargo Payload Scatter Analysis (Level 2 Core)) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A fleet manager plots cargo payload (kg) on the X-axis against fuel consumption (litres per 100 km) on the Y-axis for 10 delivery vans.
(a) What type of correlation is shown if fuel consumption rises as payload increases?
(b) Using the line of best fit, a 500 kg payload corresponds to 8.2 litres per 100 km. Estimate the total fuel required for a 600 km round trip carrying a 500 kg load.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. State correlation type: Positive correlation (as payload increases, fuel consumption increases). [A1]
2. Extract fuel rate at 500 kg payload: Rate = 8.2 litres per 100 km. [M1]
3. Scale up to 600 km (× 6): 6 × 8.2 litres = 49.2 litres. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) Positive correlation | (b) 49.2 litres
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Use a ruler to draw a single straight line through the middle of the cluster.
🔄 Quick Check: 600 km is 6 lots of 100 km, so multiply the 100 km rate (8.2 L) by 6.
Question 4 (Workplace Application — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
On a scatter graph of Patient Age (X-axis) against Blood Pressure (Y-axis), the line of best fit passes through (30 years, 118 mmHg) and (60 years, 136 mmHg). Use linear interpolation to estimate the blood pressure for a 45-year-old patient.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. 45 years is the exact midpoint between 30 and 60 years. [M1]
2. Midpoint Y = (118 + 136) ÷ 2 = 254 ÷ 2 = 127 mmHg. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 127 mmHg
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: A negative correlation line (or a positive line with a baseline offset) does NOT pass through the origin (0, 0).
🔄 Quick Check: Since 45 is halfway between 30 and 60, the reading on the straight line of best fit is halfway between 118 and 136.
Section C: Exam-Style Problem Solving Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Hospital Solar Panel Output vs Sunshine Hours Outlier & Budget Check (Level 2 Multi-Step)) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
An energy manager records daily sunshine hours and solar electricity generated (kWh) across 7 days:
• (2 hrs, 30 kWh), (3 hrs, 45 kWh), (4 hrs, 60 kWh), (5 hrs, 12 kWh), (6 hrs, 90 kWh), (7 hrs, 105 kWh), (8 hrs, 120 kWh).
(a) Identify the coordinates of the outlier day (when an inverter fault occurred).
(b) Ignoring the outlier, use the linear trend to estimate the electricity generated on a day with 5.5 hours of sunshine, and calculate the financial saving if electricity costs £0.24 per kWh.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify the outlier point that breaks the upward trend: At 5 hours, output dropped to 12 kWh instead of 75 kWh. Outlier = (5, 12). [A1]
2. Determine the trend rate and interpolate at 5.5 hours: Trend increases by 15 kWh per hour of sunshine (e.g. 2×15=30, 4×15=60, 6×15=90). | At 5.5 hours: 5.5 × 15 = 82.5 kWh. [M1 / A1]
3. Multiply 82.5 kWh by £0.24 per kWh: 82.5 × £0.24 = £19.80. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) Outlier = (5 hrs, 12 kWh) | (b) 82.5 kWh generated, saving £19.80
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Only pass through (0, 0) if the plotted points clearly aim at the origin.
🔄 Quick Check: Always exclude the outlier before finding the line of best fit!
Question 6 (Level 2 Multi-Step Problem Solving — 2 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A scatter diagram plots staff height (cm) against their monthly overtime hours for 25 nurses. The points are scattered randomly across the grid. State the type of correlation and whether height can be used to predict overtime.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Zero (or no) correlation. [M1]
2. No, height cannot be used to predict overtime hours because there is no relationship between the variables. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: No (zero) correlation; height cannot predict overtime
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Stating a conclusion without showing the full numerical comparison values.
🔄 Quick Check: When points form a random cloud with no upward or downward slope, state "No correlation".
