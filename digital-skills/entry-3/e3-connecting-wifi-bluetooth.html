Connecting to Wi-Fi and Bluetooth Networks

Learn how to find and connect your device to wireless internet (Wi-Fi), enter network security keys safely, and pair Bluetooth devices like wireless mice and headsets.

Locate available Wi-Fi networks using the network icon Connect to a secure Wi-Fi network using the correct password / security key Pair and connect Bluetooth peripherals (wireless mouse, headphones)
On This Page:
1. Key Concepts 2. Worked Exam Model 3. Interactive Quiz 4. Practice Exam Task

Step-by-Step Teaching & Key Concepts

Core Knowledge
1
Connecting to a Wi-Fi Network

Wi-Fi allows your device to connect to the internet without plugging in a physical cable:

  1. Click the Network / Wi-Fi icon in the bottom right taskbar (or top right on mobile devices).
  2. A list of available network names (SSIDs) will appear. Click on your organisation or home network name.
  3. Ensure 'Connect automatically' is ticked if it is a trusted network.
  4. Click Connect and type the exact Network Security Key (password). Passwords are case-sensitive.

2
Recognising Secure vs Open Wi-Fi

When viewing Wi-Fi networks, look closely at the symbol:

  • Padlock Icon: Indicates a Secured Network. You must enter a password. Data transmitted between your device and the router is encrypted.
  • No Padlock / Shield Warning: Indicates an Open Public Network (e.g. at a café or train station). Anyone can connect without a password, but your data could be monitored by others.
Security Golden Rule: Never carry out online banking, input credit card numbers, or view confidential medical records on an unsecured public Wi-Fi network without encryption.
3
Connecting Bluetooth Peripherals

Bluetooth is a short-range wireless technology used to connect nearby accessories (typically within 10 metres) such as wireless mice, keyboards, and headsets.

To pair a device: Turn on Bluetooth on your computer, switch your accessory into pairing mode, click Add Bluetooth Device in Settings, and choose your accessory from the list.
