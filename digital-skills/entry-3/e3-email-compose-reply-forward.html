Three Non-Negotiable Exam Golden Rules

Golden Rule 1: Verify '@' + Zero Spaces in Recipient Addresses & Fill Subject

Every email address follows localname@domain (e.g. sarah.jones@clinic.org) with zero spaces. Always write a concise Subject summarising why you are emailing.

✗ Sending email with '(no subject)' or 'sarah jones @gmail.com'
✓ To: sarah.jones@clinic.org | Subject: Shift Cover Confirmation - 14 Nov
Golden Rule 2: Match Reply vs Reply All vs Forward Precisely

Reply = back ONLY to sender. Reply All = everyone in To & CC. Forward = share thread onward to someone NEW who wasn't copied.

✗ Hitting Reply All to thank the manager when 45 staff are in CC
✓ Click Reply for 1-to-1 answers; Forward with a brief intro note for new colleagues
Golden Rule 3: Include Polite Greeting, Clear Message & Sign-Off (No CAPS)

Workplace emails expect 'Good morning [Name],' clear respectful body sentences (no text-speak like 'u'/'pls' or ALL CAPS shouting), and 'Kind regards, [Name]'.

✗ HI NEED SHIFT CHANGE ASAP THX!!
✓ Good morning Amanda, ... Kind regards, Jordan

Step-by-Step Teaching & Key Concepts

1
Composing a New Email

Click New Mail (or Compose) to open an email window. Every message needs three core parts:

  1. To: Type the recipient's exact email address (e.g. sarah.jones@example.co.uk). Addresses must have no spaces and contain an @ symbol.
  2. Subject Line: A short, informative summary of your message (e.g. 'Staff Meeting Agenda - 14th November'). Never leave this blank!
  3. Email Body: The message itself. Always start with a polite greeting ('Dear Sarah' or 'Good morning Team') and finish with a sign-off ('Kind regards, Abdul').
Workplace Email Compose Window
To: amanda.clark@healthclinic.org
Subject: First Aid Training Rota - October 2026
Good morning Amanda,

Please find attached the updated first aid rota for next week.

Kind regards,
Abdul
New Email Shortcut: Ctrl + N • Send Email: Ctrl + Enter
2
Replying: Reply vs Reply All

When responding to an email you received:

  • Reply: Sends your response ONLY to the person who sent the email. Use this 90% of the time.
  • Reply All: Sends your response to the sender AND everyone else who was copied into the email. Only use this when everyone on the team genuinely needs to read your reply.
Workplace Etiquette Alert: Do not click 'Reply All' just to say 'Thank you'! That clutters up everyone's inbox across the whole department.
3
Forwarding an Email

Forward sends a copy of an email you received to a completely new person who wasn't on the original thread. When forwarding, always type a brief note at the top explaining why you are sharing it (e.g. 'Hi David, please see the update from estates below for your information').

Worked Model 1 • Core StandardStep-by-Step Accredited Guide
Exam Scenario Prompt: A support worker receives an email from their manager with the subject 'Next Week Shift Cover'. The worker needs to confirm they can work Saturday. Explain which button they should click (Reply, Reply All, or Forward) and write a suitable two-sentence email response.
Step 1: Select the correct action [B1]
Click 'Reply'
Tutor Marking Rationale: Reply sends the answer directly back to the manager who asked.
Step 2: Compose response with proper etiquette [B1]
Dear Manager, Thank you for your email. I can confirm that I am available to cover the Saturday shift. Kind regards, Abdul
Tutor Marking Rationale: Includes greeting, polite confirmation, and professional sign-off.
Tutor Abdul's Exam Golden Rule: In Entry 3 exams, marks are given for: 1. Subject line filled in; 2. Polite greeting; 3. Clear message; 4. Professional sign-off.
Worked Model 2 • Applied Workplace Scenario Exam Scenario Model 2 • Forwarding Schedule Update With Explanation
Exam Scenario Prompt 2: You receive an email from Training Coordinator Priya containing next month's fire-marshal workshop dates. A new colleague, Tariq (tariq.ali@clinic.org), wasn't copied in and needs the details. Write the forwarding steps and cover note.
Step 1: Select Forward and enter new recipient cleanly [B1]
Click 'Forward' (keeps Priya's original email below), type 'tariq.ali@clinic.org' cleanly into the To box (check Subject keeps 'Fwd: Fire Marshal Dates').
Tutor Marking Rationale: Uses Forward properly rather than re-typing an entire third-party thread.
Step 2: Type a contextual cover message above the thread [B1][M1]
Hi Tariq, Please see the upcoming fire-marshal workshop dates forwarded below from Priya so you can choose a slot. Kind regards, Jordan
Tutor Marking Rationale: Exam briefs penalise blank forward bodies — always explain WHY you forwarded it.
Tutor Abdul's Distinction Tip: Never reply to an old irrelevant thread with a brand-new topic! Start a New Email with a fresh Subject line whenever the subject changes.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Trap 1: Confusing Email Field 'Subject' With Main Body
Typing the entire 3-sentence letter into the tiny one-line Subject box.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Keep Subject to 4–8 summary words (`Request for Spreadsheet Practice`); put greetings and sentences inside the large body pane.
Trap 2: Clicking 'Reply All' to Answer One Person
Interrupting dozens of colleagues' inboxes unnecessarily.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Check who needs your reply — default to single `Reply` unless a team-wide decision requires all recipients.
Trap 3: Text-Speak Abbreviations & Missing Sign-Off
Using `cu`, `thx`, or `brb` in workplace assessment drafts.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Write complete words with capital `I`, periods, and a polite sign-off (`Kind regards, ...`).
