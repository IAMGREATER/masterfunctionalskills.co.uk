Identifying Phishing and Social Engineering

Recognise sophisticated social engineering attacks: analyse spoofed email headers, inspect hyperlinked URLs without clicking, identify SMS scams (smishing) and telephone impersonation (vishing), and follow reporting procedures.

Inspect sender domain names and hover over hyperlinks to reveal destination URLs Differentiate between Phishing (email), Smishing (SMS text), and Vishing (phone voice) Follow official workplace incident reporting protocols when suspicious communications arrive

Step-by-Step Teaching & Key Concepts

Core Knowledge
1
How Social Engineering Works

Social Engineering manipulates human emotions (fear, urgency, greed, or curiosity) rather than hacking hardware directly. Criminals know that tricking a human into clicking a link is often easier than breaking computer encryption.

Key variations include:

  • Phishing (Email): Fake emails appearing to come from banks, delivery couriers, or senior managers.
  • Smishing (SMS / Text Messages): Scam texts about missed parcel deliveries or banking blocks containing shortened links.
  • Vishing (Voice / Phone Calls): Scammers impersonating the police, Microsoft technical support, or your bank fraud team on the telephone.
2
Inspecting Sender Domains and Hyperlinks

Never trust the display name alone! Anyone can set their display name to 'Healthcare IT Support' or 'HM Revenue & Customs'.

  1. Check the actual email address: Look at the characters after the @ symbol. If it says support@hmrc-tax-gov-service99.net, it is fake. Official HMRC emails end in @hmrc.gov.uk.
  2. Hover to Reveal (URL Snooping): Hover your mouse cursor over any hyperlink without clicking. A small preview box will appear showing the real web address. If the text says 'www.bank.co.uk' but the hover link points to 'www.scam-login-portal.ru', it is fraudulent!
Hyperlink Hover Preview Simulation
Fake IT Support Notification

Your cloud storage is full. Click here to upgrade your storage

Real URL: http://phish-login-steal.xyz/account
3
Workplace Incident Reporting

If you receive a phishing email at work:

  • Do NOT click any links, do NOT open any attachments, and do NOT reply.
  • Click the Report Phishing button in Outlook, or forward the email as an attachment to your organisation's official IT security helpdesk.
  • Delete the message from your Deleted Items folder.

Authentic Pearson Edexcel Exam Model

Accredited Model

Here is how this skill is tested in the real Pearson Edexcel onscreen digital assessment, broken down with official examiner mark codes ([B1] knowledge, [M1] process, [A1] accuracy).

Exam Scenario Prompt: A clinic worker receives an email that appears to come from 'Payroll Department' with the subject 'URGENT: Payroll Verification Required'. The email states that wages will be stopped unless the employee clicks a link and enters their bank login details. State two clues that indicate this is a social engineering attack, and describe the action the employee should take.
Step 1: Identify two phishing clues [B1]
1. Uses high psychological pressure / urgency (threatening to stop wages). 2. Requests private banking login credentials via an emailed link (legitimate payroll departments never ask for bank passwords).
Tutor Marking Rationale: Identifies psychological manipulation and credential harvesting.
Step 2: Describe correct workplace action [B1]
Do not click the link; report the email immediately to the internal IT security department and delete it.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Correctly follows organisational reporting and remediation protocol.
Tutor Abdul's Exam Golden Rule: In Level 1 exams, never say 'Reply to ask if it's real'. The accredited action is ALWAYS: 'Do not click links; report to IT and delete'.

Onscreen Assessment Task:
A staff member accidentally clicked a link in a phishing email and entered their workplace network password before realising it was a scam. List the immediate three steps the employee must take to protect the organisation.
Your Working & Answer Scratchpad: Auto-saves in browser

Pearson Edexcel Benchmark Solution

Step 1: Change their network password immediately from a clean device. Step 2: Disconnect the computer from the network (unplug network cable / turn off Wi-Fi) to stop malware spreading. Step 3: Contact the workplace IT security team immediately to report the compromise so they can reset sessions and check account logs.
Official Marking Criteria:
  • [1 Mark]: Immediately changing the compromised password.
  • [1 Mark]: Reporting to IT security and isolating device from the network.
