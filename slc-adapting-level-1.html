Official Level 1 Specification & Scope (L1.SLC.6)

Regulated Ofqual Functional Skills English Level 1 learning outcomes (Open Awards, Pearson Edexcel, City & Guilds, NCFE & AQA):

  • L1.SLC.6 – Adapt contributions to discussion to suit audience, purpose and medium.
Included at Level 1
Using polite, clear workplace/group register in a formal discussion or 3–4 minute short talk without slang, interruptions, or reading word-for-word off a page.
Saved for Level 2 (Not Tested Here)
Chairing complex multi-stakeholder debates or adapting technical jargon across contrasting specialist audiences. View Level 2 Guide →

Core Level 1 Golden Rules: Adapting Speech to Audience & Purpose (Level 1 SLC)

Golden Rule 1: Step Up One Notch from Everyday Chat

In your Level 1 SLC assessment, speak clearly in complete sentences using polite standard English—avoid filler slang while still sounding natural.

Level 1 Key Rule: Swap 'That idea's rubbish, mate' → 'I see a potential issue with cost there—could we look at a cheaper option?'
Golden Rule 2: Use Bullet-Point Cue Cards—Never Read a Script Word-for-Word

Speaking, Listening & Communicating assesses spoken communication, eye contact, and natural pace, not reading aloud.

Level 1 Key Rule: Put 4–5 bold keywords per card and talk to the group.
Golden Rule 3: Adjust smoothly for Online Video Calls vs In-Person Rooms

On Teams/Zoom, look towards the camera lens, pause briefly so you don't talk over lags, and signal clearly before speaking.

Level 1 Key Rule: 'Can I just build on what Sam said there?'

Core Skill 1: The Register Adaptation Matrix Across 3 Workplace Settings

Compare how the exact same operational issue is communicated to three different audiences.

Situation Inappropriate / Too Casual Adapted Functional English
1. Requesting Leave (To Manager) "I need Friday off cos my mates are going away." "I would like to request annual leave for this Friday; I have already arranged shift cover with Michael."
2. Explaining Delay (To Customer) "Our IT server is totally wrecked so we can't check your order." "We are experiencing a temporary system update this morning; I will take your telephone number and call you back by 11:00 with your tracking details."
3. Challenging a Proposal (In Committee) "That idea makes zero sense and costs way too much." "While I appreciate the rationale behind the proposal, have we evaluated the impact on our quarterly training budget?"
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: De-Escalating Frustrated Customers or Anxious Patients (The LAST Method)

How to adapt your tone when speaking to someone who is upset or stressed.

  1. L — Listen Without Interrupting: Let the client finish explaining their problem while maintaining calm, empathetic eye contact.
  2. A — Apologise & Acknowledge Empathy: "I completely understand how frustrating it is to wait in all morning for a missed delivery, and I sincerely apologise."
  3. S — Solve with a Concrete Action Plan: State exactly what you will do right now and give a realistic timeframe.
  4. T — Thank & Check Satisfaction: "Thank you for your patience, Mr Davies—does that Saturday morning slot work for you?"
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Specialist Technical Register vs Public Plain-English Register

How to switch smoothly between professional peers and members of the public.

  • Speaking to a Mechanic Colleague (Technical Peer): "The diagnostic scan flagged a faulty DPF pressure sensor and worn front brake callipers."
  • Speaking to the Car Owner (Public Customer): "Our diagnostic check showed a faulty exhaust filter sensor and worn front brake parts. Replacing both today will cost £185 and ensure your car passes its MOT safely."
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Level 1 Step-by-Step Worked Exam Model

Level 1 Benchmark Exam Model 3 Marks (SLC Assessment Criteria)

Vocational Context: Level 1 Group Discussion on Staff Wellbeing Ideas

During a formal Level 1 group discussion about spending a £500 workplace wellbeing budget, another learner suggests buying an expensive coffee machine. Show how to disagree politely and adapt your tone appropriately.
Step
Level 1 Method & Working
Marking Focus
Step 1 Acknowledge the speaker politely
Show active listening first
→ 'That's an interesting suggestion, Jordan, because lots of staff drink coffee...'
Pass Criterion — Respectful register
Step 2 Introduce alternative constructively
Give clear workplace reason without bluntness
→ '...however, a commercial machine might use the whole £500 budget and wouldn't benefit tea or water drinkers.'
Pass Criterion — Clear reasoned point
Step 3 Invite group response
Keep tone collaborative
→ 'What do people think about spending half on quieter breakroom seating instead?'
Pass Criterion — Adapts to collaborative purpose
Final Exam Answer: 'That's a good thought, Jordan; however, it might take the whole £500 budget. Could we split the fund so everyone benefits?'
Emma’s Level 1 Exam Tip: Polite disagreement is one of the fastest ways to prove L1.SLC.6—acknowledge first, then offer your counter-reason calmly.

Common Level 1 Examiner Traps to Avoid

Level 1 Examiner Trap: Being Too Blunt or Too Silent
Saying 'Nope' shuts discussion down; staying silent for 5 minutes gives the assessor no evidence to pass you.
Level 1 Examiner Trap: Staring Down at Full Written Paragraphs
Reading head-down from an A4 sheet prevents eye contact and natural spoken tone.

Interactive Level 1 Exam Practice Questions

Level 1 Practice 1: Upgrading Informal Speech 2 Marks
Rewrite this overly casual remark for a formal Level 1 SLC discussion:
'Nah, nobody cares about posters, stick it on WhatsApp.'
Level 1 Worked Solution & Mark Breakdown:
Polite disagreement (1 Mark): Acknowledge point politely without 'Nah / nobody cares'
Constructive suggestion (1 Mark): Propose digital messaging in clear standard English
Final Answer: 'I'm not sure printed posters get noticed by busy staff—sending updates via our team messaging group might reach everyone faster.'
Level 1 Practice 2: Cue Card Check 1 Marks
Why are 5 short bullet headings on an index card better than a full 400-word script for your 3–4 minute talk?
Level 1 Worked Solution & Mark Breakdown:
Reason (1 Mark): Prompts your memory while letting you maintain eye contact and natural spoken tone.
Final Answer: Bullets keep your talk structured while allowing natural speech and eye contact with listeners.
