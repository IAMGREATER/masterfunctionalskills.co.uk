Golden Rules for 3D Solids: Faces, Edges & Vertices

Golden Rule 1: The Three Properties: Faces, Edges & Vertices

Faces = Flat or curved 2D surfaces. Edges = Straight or curved lines where two faces meet. Vertices = Sharp corners (points) where three or more edges meet.

Golden Rule / Method: Faces = Surfaces • Edges = Lines • Vertices = Corners
Golden Rule 2: Prism vs Pyramid Rule & Euler's Formula (F + V - E = 2)

For an n-sided Prism: Faces = n + 2, Edges = 3n, Vertices = 2n. For an n-sided Pyramid: Faces = n + 1, Edges = 2n, Vertices = n + 1. For any flat-faced polyhedron: Faces + Vertices − Edges = 2.

Golden Rule / Method: Prism: F=n+2, E=3n, V=2n • Pyramid: F=n+1, E=2n, V=n+1

Core Skill 2: Prisms vs Pyramids: Cross-Sections & Naming

How to distinguish a uniform prism from an apex-converging pyramid.

  • Prisms Have Uniform Cross-Sections: A prism has two identical parallel end polygons connected by rectangular sides. Slicing it parallel to the end always reveals the same 2D shape.
  • Pyramids Converge to a Single Apex: A pyramid has one base polygon and triangular sides that meet at a single top vertex (apex).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Calculating Total Wireframe / Edge Lengths of 3D Solids

Summing all 12 edges of a cuboid or 9 edges of a triangular prism for structural steelwork.

When building a 3D metal frame or taping all edges of a box:

  • Cube (12 equal edges): Total Edge Length = 12 × Side Length.
  • Cuboid (4 lengths, 4 widths, 4 heights): Total Edge Length = 4L + 4W + 4H.
  • Square-Based Pyramid (8 edges): Total Edge Length = (4 × Base Edge) + (4 × Slant Edge).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Vocational Context: Healthcare Infection Control Equipment

A clinical sharps disposal bin is shaped as a hexagonal prism.
(a) State the 2D shape of its two end faces.
(b) How many faces does the hexagonal prism have in total?
(c) How many edges and vertices does it have?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Identify the cross-sectional end faces
A hexagonal prism has two identical end faces shaped as hexagons (6-sided polygons).
Criteria: A1: Correct cross-sectional face shape
Step 2 Calculate total faces (n + 2)
2 hexagonal end faces + 6 rectangular side faces = 8 faces in total.
Criteria: A1: Accurate total faces (8)
Step 3 Calculate total edges (3n) and vertices (2n)
Edges: 6 top + 6 bottom + 6 vertical = 18 edges.
Vertices: 6 top + 6 bottom = 12 vertices.
Criteria: A1: Accurate edges (18) and vertices (12)
Final Accredited Answer: (a) Hexagons | (b) 8 faces | (c) 18 edges and 12 vertices
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
For any prism with an n-sided base: Faces = n + 2, Edges = 3n, Vertices = 2n.

Calculate the minimum cost of the aluminium lengths required to build the frame.

Criteria
Step 1 Calculate total length of all 12 edges of the cuboid

Remember a cuboid has 12 edges in 3 sets of 4: 4 lengths + 4 widths + 4 heights!

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Confusing a Triangular Prism with a Pyramid
A triangular prism has 5 faces, 9 edges, and 6 vertices. A square-based pyramid has 5 faces, 8 edges, and 5 vertices.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Look for the uniform cross-section: prisms have matching parallel ends; pyramids come to a single point.
Examiner Pitfall: Counting Only 4 Edges Instead of 12 on a Cuboid
Adding 1.5 + 0.8 + 0.9 = 3.2 m only counts 3 edges instead of all 12 edges.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Multiply the sum of (L + W + H) by 4 to account for all 12 edges.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A ridge tent is shaped as a triangular prism.
(a) How many vertices (corners) does it have?
(b) How many edges does it have?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Vertices: 3 at front triangle + 3 at back triangle = 6 vertices.
2. Edges: 3 at front + 3 at back + 3 connecting ridge/ground poles = 9 edges.
Final Correct Answer: (a) 6 vertices | (b) 9 edges
Examiner Tip: Verify with Euler’s formula: Faces (5) + Vertices (6) − Edges (9) = 2.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
A glass paperweight is shaped as a square-based pyramid. Each side of the square base measures 9 cm, and each of the four sloping edges measures 11 cm.
(a) State the number of faces, edges, and vertices.
(b) Calculate the total length of all edges of the pyramid.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Square-based pyramid has 5 faces (1 square + 4 triangles), 8 edges, and 5 vertices.
2. Base edges = 4 × 9 cm = 36 cm.
3. Sloping edges = 4 × 11 cm = 44 cm.
4. Total edge length = 36 + 44 = 80 cm.
Final Correct Answer: (a) 5 faces, 8 edges, 5 vertices | (b) 80 cm
Examiner Tip: A square-based pyramid has 4 base edges and 4 sloping edges meeting at the apex.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A heavy-duty steel cage is shaped as a cube of side length 1.4 metres. Calculate the total length of steel bar required to weld all 12 edges of the cube.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. A cube has 12 equal edges.
2. Total steel length = 12 × 1.4 m = 16.8 metres.
Final Correct Answer: 16.8 m
Examiner Tip: Multiply the side length by 12 for a cube wireframe.
