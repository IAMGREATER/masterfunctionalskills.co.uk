Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.28 / L2.28 – Use a wide range of punctuation accurately, including capital letters, full stops, question marks, commas, and apostrophes.
  • L2.29 – Deploy colons, semicolons, inverted commas, brackets, and dashes accurately to enhance clarity in complex professional documents.

Golden Rules for Punctuation Mastery: Apostrophes, Colons & Commas

Golden Rule 1: The Singular vs Plural Possessive Apostrophe Rule

Ask yourself: WHO owns the item? If ONE person/thing owns it, add 's (the nurse's locker). If MULTIPLE people ending in s own it, place the apostrophe AFTER the s (the nurses' staffroom). If an irregular plural owns it (children, men, women, people), add 's (the children's ward).

Golden Rule / Technique: 1 Owner = nurse's | 2+ Owners ending in s = nurses' | Irregular Plural = children's / people's
Golden Rule 2: Colons Introduce Lists or Explanations; Semicolons Link Equal Clauses

Place a COLON (:) after a complete clause to introduce a list, explanation, or verdict. Place a SEMICOLON (;) between two complete, closely related sentences without using 'and' or 'but'.

Golden Rule / Technique: Colon (:) = 'Here is what you need: ...' | Semicolon (;) = 'Clause 1 is true; Clause 2 is closely linked.'

Core Skill 1: The Complete Level 2 Punctuation Arsenal

How and where to use all 6 high-value punctuation marks in your Writing exam.

Punctuation Mark Exact Grammatical Rule High-Scoring Exam Example
Introductory Comma (,) Placed immediately after a fronted adverbial, discourse marker, or subordinate clause. "Following last week's safety audit, all ladders were replaced."
Possessive Apostrophe (') Shows ownership. Never use in ordinary plurals (cameras) or possessive pronoun its. "The manager's office is next to the drivers' rest area."
Colon (:) Follows a complete main clause to introduce a list, key reason, or quotation. "The audit identified three priorities: lighting, heating, and security."
Semicolon (;) Joins two related independent clauses OR separates complex items in a detailed list. "The main car park is full; however, overflow spaces remain open."
Brackets ( ) / Dashes (–) Encloses extra parenthetical statistics, dates, or clarifications mid-sentence. "Staff turnover (currently 24% per annum) must be reduced."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Conquering the #1 Exam Trap: It's vs Its

Why possessive 'its' NEVER takes an apostrophe.

  • It's (with an apostrophe) = "It is" or "It has": Only use an apostrophe if you can expand the word to "it is". (Note: in formal letters and reports, write "it is" in full anyway!).
  • Its (NO apostrophe) = "belonging to it": Just like possessive his and hers don't have apostrophes, possessive its never has an apostrophe:
    Correct: "The hospital celebrated its 50th anniversary, and the charity published its annual accounts."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Using Semicolons in Complex Multi-Word Lists

When list items already contain commas, use semicolons to separate the items cleanly.

Look how a colon and semicolons organise a complex workplace list with 100% clarity:

"The safety committee comprises three regional leads: Sarah Jenkins, Head of Nursing; David Patel, Facilities Engineer; and Marcus Vance, Health and Safety Representative."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Rewrite this unpunctuated reception notice, inserting all missing capital letters, commas, apostrophes, and full stops:
'on monday 2 november dr patels morning clinic will start at 09:30 before booking an appointment please check that the receptionists computer has your current mobile number address and postcode'

Check days of the week (Monday), months (November), and titles/surnames (Dr Patel)—missing a capital letter on a proper noun is an avoidable SPaG slip.

Vocational Context: Clinical Governance Directive

Insert all missing punctuation (including two possessive apostrophes, a colon, a semicolon, and commas) into this clinical directive:
'All nurses must check each patients wristband before administering medication specifically verify the full name date of birth and allergy status the wards electronic scanner must also be used'
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Insert singular possessive apostrophes
'each patient's wristband' and 'the ward's electronic scanner'.
Criteria: Possessive Accuracy (L2.28)
Step 2 Insert colon and list commas
Place a colon after 'medication:' to introduce the specific checks, and commas between 'full name, date of birth, and allergy status'.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: The 'Greengrocer's Apostrophe' in Ordinary Plurals
Putting apostrophes into simple plural nouns (e.g. 'Two nurse's were on duty', 'We bought new laptop's', 'CCTV camera's') is penalised heavily.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Never use an apostrophe just because a word ends in 's'. Only use an apostrophe for ownership or contraction.
Examiner Pitfall: Placing a Colon After an Incomplete Verb Phrase
Writing 'The three main problems are: cost, time, and staffing' is technically incorrect because 'The three main problems are' is not a complete sentence.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Make the clause before the colon complete: 'The audit identified three main problems: cost, time, and staffing.'

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Insert the apostrophe in the exact correct position for each phrase below:
1. the childrens play area (belonging to the children)
2. the six delivery drivers vans (belonging to six drivers)
3. the company updated its / it's health policy (select the correct form)
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. 'children' is already an irregular plural, so add 's → children's.
2. 'drivers' is a regular plural ending in s, so place the apostrophe after the s → drivers'.
3. Possessive pronoun never takes an apostrophe → its.
Final Model Answer: 1. the children's play area | 2. the six delivery drivers' vans | 3. its (no apostrophe)
Examiner Tip: Test irregular plurals carefully: children's, women's, men's, and people's always put the apostrophe BEFORE the s.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Punctuate this sentence accurately using one colon, commas, and one semicolon:
'The leisure centre refurbishment includes three major upgrades a heated indoor pool a 40-station fitness suite and a healthy cafe membership prices will remain frozen until January.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Place a colon after 'three major upgrades:' to introduce the list.
2. Place commas between the list items ('a heated indoor pool, a 40-station fitness suite, and a healthy cafe').
3. Place a semicolon after 'cafe;' to join the final independent clause.
Final Model Answer: 'The leisure centre refurbishment includes three major upgrades: a heated indoor pool, a 40-station fitness suite, and a healthy cafe; membership prices will remain frozen until January.'
Examiner Tip: Read the clause before your colon ('The leisure centre refurbishment includes three major upgrades')—because it makes complete sense on its own, the colon is 100% grammatical.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
Explain how adding bracketing commas changes the meaning of Sentence A compared to Sentence B:
Sentence A: The care assistants who completed the dementia course received a £100 bonus.
Sentence B: The care assistants, who completed the dementia course, received a £100 bonus.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Analyse Sentence A (no commas = defining relative clause): Only those specific care assistants who completed the course got the bonus (others did not).
2. Analyse Sentence B (paired commas = non-defining parenthetical clause): ALL the care assistants completed the course and ALL of them received the £100 bonus.
Final Model Answer: Sentence A (no commas) means ONLY the subset of care assistants who completed the course received the £100 bonus. Sentence B (with bracketing commas) means ALL care assistants completed the course and received the bonus.
Examiner Tip: Bracketing commas act like handles: if you lift out the words between the two commas, the main sentence still applies to the whole group.
