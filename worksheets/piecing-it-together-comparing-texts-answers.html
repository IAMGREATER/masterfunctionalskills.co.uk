Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (The Basics) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Linking Two Texts with Comparative Connectives) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Read these two views on paperless hospital records:
Text A (IT Director): 'Digital tablets eliminate lost paper charts and allow doctors to view blood results instantly.'
Text B (Ward Sister): 'Instant blood results are helpful, but Wi-Fi blackspots on the ground floor often freeze the tablets mid-round.'
State one point of agreement and one difference between Text A and Text B.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Point of agreement: Both agree that instant access to blood results is beneficial [A1]
2. Point of difference: Text A is wholly positive, whereas Text B warns that Wi-Fi blackspots freeze tablets mid-round [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Both agree instant blood results are useful, whereas Text B highlights that Wi-Fi blackspots freeze the tablets.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Summarising Text A without contrasting it directly with Text B.
🔄 Quick Check: Check that both similarity and difference are clearly stated.
Question 2 (Core Skill Application — 3 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Read the two extracts on town centre parking:
Text 1 (Council Press Release): 'Increasing high-street parking tariffs by £1.20 per hour will relieve severe daytime congestion and cut town centre exhaust emissions by 20%.'
Text 2 (Shopkeepers' Petition): 'Although town centre traffic is undeniably congested, this greedy £1.20 parking hike will drive loyal shoppers straight to out-of-town retail parks!'
Identify one similarity and two differences in how the texts present the parking issue.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Similarity: Both Text 1 and Text 2 agree that town centre parking/traffic is heavily congested [1 Mark]. [M1]
2. Difference 1 (Impact): Text 1 argues the £1.20 increase will cut emissions by 20%, whereas Text 2 warns it will drive shoppers away to out-of-town retail parks [1 Mark]. [M1]
3. Difference 2 (Tone/Language): Text 1 uses formal, objective statistics ('20%'), whereas Text 2 uses emotive, critical language ('greedy', exclamation mark) [1 Mark]. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Similarity: Both agree the town centre is congested. | Difference 1: Text 1 claims the £1.20 rise cuts emissions by 20%, whereas Text 2 claims it will drive shoppers to retail parks. | Difference 2: Text 1 uses a formal statistical tone, while Text 2 uses emotive language ('greedy').
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Writing a summary of Text A followed by an unrelated summary of Text B prevents you from demonstrating synthesis and caps your mark at 1 out of 4.
🔄 Quick Check: Ensure you clearly label which text is which and use comparative connectives like 'whereas' and 'both'.
Section B: Real-Life & Workplace Practice Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Comparing Two Staff Emails on Canteen Changes (Level 1)) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Read the two extracts:
Text A (Facilities Manager): 'Replacing our staffed hot canteen with smart vending machines will save £22,000 a year and provide 24-hour food access for night-shift workers.'
Text B (Warehouse Supervisor): 'While 24-hour access for night staff is welcome, vending machines only sell processed snacks rather than the hot, nutritious meals our manual workers need.'
(a) Identify one point of agreement between Text A and Text B. [1 Mark]
(b) Identify one point of disagreement about the food provided. [1 Mark]
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify shared agreement across both extracts: Both Text A and Text B agree that providing 24-hour food access for night-shift staff is a positive benefit. [A1]
2. Contrast their views on the food using a connective: Whereas Text A views smart vending machines as a cost-saving upgrade (£22,000 saved), Text B argues they only provide 'processed snacks' instead of the hot, nutritious meals workers need. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) Agreement: Both agree that 24-hour food access for night-shift workers is beneficial. | (b) Disagreement: Text A promotes vending machines to save £22,000, whereas Text B criticises them for serving processed snacks instead of hot, nutritious meals.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Use comparative sentence frames: 'While Text A argues [X], Text B contends that [Y].'
🔄 Quick Check: Even in a 2-mark Level 1 question, always mention both texts explicitly when explaining a disagreement.
Question 4 (Exam-Style Task — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Read the two extracts about open-plan offices:
Text A (Office Architect): 'Open-plan workspaces foster instant team collaboration, spark creative ideas, and reduce partitioning costs by 35%.'
Text B (Software Developer): 'Working in a noisy fishbowl of constant chatter destroys deep concentration and leaves neurodivergent staff exhausted by 3 p.m.'
Compare how the two writers view open-plan offices and identify one language technique used in Text B.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Viewpoint Comparison: Text A views open-plan offices positively as collaborative and cost-effective ('reduce partitioning costs by 35%'), whereas Text B views them negatively as distracting and exhausting ('destroys deep concentration') [2 Marks]. [M1]
2. Language Technique in Text B: Metaphor ('noisy fishbowl') or emotive verb ('destroys'), emphasizing lack of privacy and sensory overload [1 Mark]. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Text A praises open-plan offices for boosting collaboration and cutting costs by 35%, whereas Text B criticises them for ruining concentration and exhausting staff. Text B uses the metaphor 'noisy fishbowl' to highlight the lack of privacy.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Saying 'Text A talks about parking costs whereas Text B talks about uniform rules' is not a valid comparison because the two points are not about the same sub-topic.
🔄 Quick Check: Quote short phrases from both extracts to anchor your comparison.
Section C: Exam-Style Tasks Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Comparing Perspectives and Language on 12-Hour Hospital Shifts (Level 2)) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Read the two extracts:
Text A (Medical Organisation Circular): 'Transitioning to 12-hour nursing rosters has reduced clinical handover errors by 15% and guarantees seamless operational continuity for acute patients.'
Text B (Nursing Union Blog): 'Exhausted nurses are being pushed to breaking point! Working gruelling 12-hour marathons doubles medication errors after the tenth hour — how can exhausted staff keep patients safe?'
Compare the writers' views on 12-hour shifts and how they use language to convey their perspectives. [4 Marks]
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Compare central viewpoints on patient safety with quotes: Both texts focus on patient safety during 12-hour shifts; however, Text A supports the shifts by claiming they 'reduced clinical handover errors by 15%', whereas Text B opposes them, claiming they 'double medication errors after the tenth hour'. [A1]
2. Compare tone and language devices with quotes: Text A uses a formal, reassuring corporate tone with professional vocabulary ('seamless operational continuity'). In contrast, Text B uses an urgent, emotive tone featuring metaphors ('gruelling 12-hour marathons') and a rhetorical question ('how can exhausted staff keep patients safe?') to provoke concern. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Text A supports 12-hour shifts, citing a '15%' drop in handover errors in a formal, objective tone ('seamless operational continuity'). Conversely, Text B strongly opposes 12-hour shifts, warning that 'medication errors' double and using emotive language ('pushed to breaking point') and a rhetorical question ('how can exhausted staff keep patients safe?') to highlight staff burnout.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Always match the exact same theme across both texts (e.g. how both view costs, or how both view staff morale).
🔄 Quick Check: Always mention BOTH texts in EVERY comparative paragraph. If you write only about Text A in paragraph 1 and Text B in paragraph 2 without cross-referencing, examiners cap your mark.
Question 6 (Level 2 Mastery Challenge — 4 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Read these two extracts on AI customer service chatbots:
Text A (Tech Vendor): 'Our AI Assistant resolves 82% of routine billing queries in under 10 seconds, freeing human agents to handle complex cases.'
Text B (Consumer Watchdog): 'Frustrated customers are trapped in endless automated loops when trying to report urgent banking fraud. Why replace empathetic human advisors with robotic scripts?'
Compare how Text A and Text B present AI chatbots, referring to both content and language. [4 Marks]
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Content comparison: Text A presents AI chatbots as fast and helpful ('resolves 82% of routine billing queries in under 10 seconds'), whereas Text B presents them as obstructive during urgent emergencies ('trapped in endless automated loops') [2 Marks]. [M1]
2. Language comparison: Text A relies on persuasive statistics ('82%', '10 seconds') and a professional tone, whereas Text B uses emotive imagery ('trapped in endless... loops') and a rhetorical question ('Why replace empathetic human advisors with robotic scripts?') [2 Marks]. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Text A portrays AI chatbots as efficient support tools that free up staff ('resolves 82%... in under 10 seconds'), using factual statistics. In contrast, Text B portrays them as frustrating barriers ('trapped in endless automated loops'), using emotive language and a rhetorical question to defend human advisors.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Failing to proofread spelling, punctuation, and grammar after completing the draft.
🔄 Quick Check: Four-mark comparison questions require two well-developed comparisons supported by direct quotes from both texts.
