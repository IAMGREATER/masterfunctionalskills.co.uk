Curriculum Specification & Formula Guide

Level 1 & Level 2 Criteria

Area measures the two-dimensional surface inside a boundary, always recorded in square units (mm², cm², m², or km²).

Shape Formula Key Dimension Check Exam Level
Rectangle / Square Area = Length × Width Multiply adjacent perpendicular sides Level 1 Level 2
Parallelogram Area = Base × Perpendicular Height Never use the slanted side Level 2
Triangle Area = (Base × Height) ÷ 2 Always remember to divide by 2 Level 1 Level 2
Trapezium Area = ((a + b) ÷ 2) × Height Find mean of parallel sides first Level 2
Compound Shapes Total Area = Area A + Area B Split with a clean dotted line Level 1 Level 2
Circle Area = π r² = 3.142 × r² (where r² = Radius × Radius) Halve the diameter if diameter is given Level 2

Golden Rules & Mental Checklists

Keep these four rules in mind to avoid the most common calculation mistakes in exam papers.

Golden Rule 1: Never Confuse Perimeter and Area

Perimeter is the one-dimensional distance around the boundary (measured in cm, m).
Area is the flat two-dimensional surface inside (always measured in square units: cm², m²).

Always state area answers with a square exponent: cm² or m²
Golden Rule 2: Convert All Dimensions to the SAME Unit First

Examiners deliberately mix units (e.g. length = 4.2 m, width = 70 cm). You must convert both to metres (70 cm = 0.7 m) before multiplying. Multiplying 4.2 × 70 gives 294 m², which is completely wrong!

Check: 1 m = 100 cm • 70 cm ÷ 100 = 0.7 m • 4.2 × 0.7 = 2.94 m²
Golden Rule 3: Use Perpendicular Height (Never the Slant)

For triangles and parallelograms, the height must form a strict 90° right angle with the base. Never use the slanted edge!

Triangle Area = (Base × Vertical Height) ÷ 2
Golden Rule 4: Average the Parallel Sides First for Trapeziums

Add the two parallel boundary sides (a and b), divide by 2 to find their average, then multiply by the vertical separation (h) between them.

Trapezium Area = ((a + b) ÷ 2) × Height

Example 1: Area of a Rectangle

Calculate the area of the rectangle shown to the right.

[2 marks]
Area of rectangle = 14 × 6 = 84 m²
Step 1: Identify dimensions: Length = 14 m, Width = 6 m.
Step 2: Multiply length by width: 14 × 6 = 84.
Step 3: Write the correct square units: m².
Final Answer: 84 m² Full 2 Marks

Example 2: Area of a Parallelogram

Calculate the area of the parallelogram shown to the right.

[2 marks]
Area of parallelogram = 11 × 6 = 66 cm²
Step 1: Identify base = 11 cm and perpendicular vertical height = 6 cm.
Trap Check: Ignore the 8 cm slanted side — slant heights are never used for area!
Step 2: Multiply: 11 × 6 = 66 cm².
Final Answer: 66 cm² Full 2 Marks

Example 3: Area of a Triangle

Calculate the area of the triangle shown to the right.

[2 marks]
Area of triangle = ½ × 14 × 7 = 49 cm²
Step 1: Multiply base by perpendicular vertical height: 14 × 7 = 98.
Step 2: Divide by 2: 98 ÷ 2 = 49.
Step 3: State the correct metric square units: 49 cm².
Final Answer: 49 cm² Full 2 Marks

Example 4: Area of a Trapezium

Calculate the area of the trapezium shown to the right.

[2 marks]
Area = ((8 + 14) ÷ 2) × 6 = 11 × 6 = 66 cm²
Step 1: Add parallel sides a and b: 8 + 14 = 22.
Step 2: Divide by 2 to find mean parallel width: 22 ÷ 2 = 11 cm.
Step 3: Multiply by perpendicular height: 11 × 6 = 66 cm².
Final Answer: 66 cm² Full 2 Marks
FS Level 2 Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE Open Awards Highfield

Example 5: Area of a Compound Shape (L-Shape)

Calculate the total floor area of the L-shaped workshop shown to the right.

[3 marks]
Total Area = Area A + Area B = 42 + 48 = 90 m²
Step 1: Split horizontally: Block A (top) and Block B (bottom).
Step 2: Area of Block A: 7 m × 6 m = 42 m².
Step 3: Area of Block B: 12 m × 4 m = 48 m².
Step 4: Add both areas together: 42 + 48 = 90 m².
Final Answer: 90 m² Full 3 Marks
FS Level 1 FS Level 2 Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE Open Awards Highfield

Example 6: Area of a Circle

Calculate the area of the circular hospital helipad shown to the right. Give your answer to 1 decimal place.

[2 marks]
Area = π r² = 3.142 × r² = 3.142 × 7² = 3.142 × (7 × 7) ≈ 153.96 m²
Step 1: Identify radius: Radius = 7 m (if given diameter, halve it first).
Step 2: Square the radius (r² = r × r): 7² = 7 × 7 = 49.
Step 3: Multiply by 3.142: 49 × 3.142 = 153.958 ≈ 154.0 m² (or 153.9 m² using π).
Final Answer: 154 m² (or 153.9 m²) Full 2 Marks
FS Level 2 Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE Open Awards Highfield

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Official Mark Schemes

These realistic healthcare and vocational exam questions demonstrate how examiners award Method Marks (M), Process Marks (P), and Accuracy Marks (A).

Worked Exam Model #1 • Level 1 & Level 2 4 Marks Total
Exam Context: An NHS healthcare centre manager needs to retile the floor of a new physiotherapy room measuring 8.5 m long and 4 m wide. Each box of non-slip floor tiles covers 3.2 m² and costs £28.50 per box. Tiles are only sold in full boxes.

Calculate the total cost to buy enough boxes of tiles for the room floor.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate the floor surface area of the room
Area = 8.5 m × 4 m = 34 m²
Criteria: [M1] Correct method for rectangle area (8.5 × 4)
Step 2 Divide room area by pack coverage to find required boxes
34 ÷ 3.2 = 10.625 boxes
Criteria: [M1] Process mark for area ÷ 3.2
Step 3 Round UP to whole boxes (practical real-world rounding)
10.625 boxes → Round UP to 11 full boxes
Criteria: [P1] Correct interpretation to round UP to whole units (11)
Step 4 Calculate total purchase cost
11 × £28.50 = £313.50
Criteria: [A1] Accurate final cost of £313.50
Final Exam Answer: £313.50 (11 Boxes)
Full 4/4 Marks Achieved
Worked Exam Model #2 • Level 2 3 Marks Total
Exam Context: An NHS health centre ambulance bay is shaped as a trapezium. The two parallel boundary walls are 24 m and 36 m long. The perpendicular distance between these walls is 15 m. Tarmac surfacing costs £18 per m². Calculate the total cost to tarmac the bay.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Apply the trapezium area formula
Mean of parallel sides = (24 + 36) ÷ 2 = 60 ÷ 2 = 30 m
Area = 30 m × 15 m = 450 m²
Criteria: [M1] Correct method for trapezium area
Step 2 Multiply surface area by cost per square metre
450 m² × £18 = £8,100
Criteria: [M1] Multiplying area by £18
Step 3 State final total cost
£8,100 (or £8,100.00)
Criteria: [A1] Accuracy mark for £8,100
Final Exam Answer: £8,100

Examiner Traps to Avoid

Official examiner reports from Pearson Edexcel highlight the four most frequent reasons candidates drop marks on area questions.

Trap 1: Forgetting to Divide by 2 on Triangles

Candidates calculate Base × Height and immediately write that down as their answer. That gives the area of a rectangle, not a triangle!

Remedy: Always double check: did I divide by 2? Write “÷ 2” in your working immediately.
Trap 2: Rounding DOWN or Keeping Decimals for Packs/Tins

When a question asks for tins of paint or packs of tiles, writing “4.3 tins” or rounding down to 4 means the job cannot be finished. In the real world, you cannot purchase 0.3 of a tin!

Remedy: Practical questions require whole units. Always round UP to the nearest whole integer.
Trap 3: Mixing Centimetres and Metres

Length given as 2.4 m and width as 75 cm. Multiplying 2.4 × 75 gives 180 m², which is completely incorrect!

Remedy: Convert first: 75 cm ÷ 100 = 0.75 m. Then multiply: 2.4 × 0.75 = 1.8 m².
Trap 4: Using Full Dimensions on Split Compound Shapes

When splitting an L-shape, candidates frequently use the overall total base for both rectangles, accidentally counting sections twice!

Remedy: Label each section Block A and Block B. Clearly write the length and width of each block separately.

Interactive Exam Practice Tasks

Self-Paced Practice

Attempt these realistic Pearson Edexcel style exam questions. Type your working in the scratchpad, then reveal the model mark scheme to evaluate your marks.

Practice Task 1: Triangle Roof Gable [2 Marks]

A builder needs to cover a triangular roof gable with timber cladding. The gable has a horizontal base of 7.2 m and a perpendicular height of 2.8 m.

Calculate the total area of cladding required.

Official Tutor Step-by-Step Mark Scheme
2 / 2 Marks Awarded
Step 1 (Formula & Method) M1 Method Mark
Area = (Base × Height) ÷ 2 = (7.2 × 2.8) ÷ 2
Substitute the base (7.2 m) and perpendicular height (2.8 m) into the standard triangle area formula.
Step 2 (Calculate Final Area) A1 Accuracy Mark
20.16 ÷ 2 = 10.08 m²
Divide the product by 2. Ensure units are explicitly given as square metres (m²).
Correct Final Answer:
10.08 m²
Examiner Marking Tip: The most common lost mark on triangle area is forgetting to divide by 2! Always write out the formula clearly to guarantee method marks even if a minor arithmetic slip occurs.
FS Level 1 FS Level 2 Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE Open Awards Highfield
Practice Task 2: Compound L-Shaped Office Suite [4 Marks]

An office manager wants to carpet an L-shaped meeting room. The outer dimensions are: total length 9 m, total width 8 m, with an indent reducing the width to 5 m over a 4 m stretch.

Carpet tiles cost £16.50 per m². Calculate the total cost to carpet the room.

Official Tutor Step-by-Step Mark Scheme
4 / 4 Marks Awarded
Step 1 (Split Compound Shape) M1 Method Mark
Rectangle A = 9 m × 5 m = 45 m²
Split the compound floor plan into two simple non-overlapping rectangles. Rectangle A measures 9 m by 5 m.
Step 2 (Second Rectangle Area) M1 Method Mark
Rectangle B = (8 − 5) m × 4 m = 3 m × 4 m = 12 m²
Calculate the remaining width: 8 − 5 = 3 m. Multiply by length 4 m.
Step 3 (Calculate Total Floor Area) M1 Method Mark
Total Area = 45 m² + 12 m² = 57 m²
Add the two areas together (or subtract the cutout: 9 × 8 − 5 × 3 = 72 − 15 = 57 m²).
Step 4 (Calculate Total Flooring Cost) A1 Accuracy Mark
Cost = 57 m² × £16.50 = £940.50
Multiply total floor area by the rate per square metre. State currency symbol (£) and two decimal places for pence.
Correct Final Answer:
£940.50
Examiner Marking Tip: In money answers, always include two decimal places for pence (e.g. £940.50, never £940.5). Missing the second zero will cost an accuracy mark in Functional Skills exams.
FS Level 1 FS Level 2 Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE Open Awards Highfield
Practice Task 3: Compound Reception Lobby [3 Marks]

A facilities coordinator needs to carpet an L-shaped reception lobby with dimensions: main section 14.0 m × 3.5 m, connected to a side entrance bay of 9.0 m × 5.5 m.

Calculate the total floor area requiring carpet.

Official Tutor Step-by-Step Mark Scheme
3 / 3 Marks Awarded
Step 1 (Main Section Area) M1 Method Mark
Rectangle A = 14.0 m × 3.5 m = 49.0 m²
Calculate the area of the first rectangular section.
Step 2 (Side Bay Area) P1 Process Mark
Rectangle B = 9.0 m × 5.5 m = 49.5 m²
Calculate the area of the secondary entrance bay.
Step 3 (Combine Total Floor Area) A1 Accuracy Mark
Total Area = 49.0 m² + 49.5 m² = 98.5 m²
Sum both non-overlapping rectangular areas for the full lobby floor.
Correct Final Answer:
98.5 m²
Examiner Marking Tip: Reverse Sanity Check: If treating the area as a bounding box of 14.0 × 9.0 = 126 m², the cutout void is 5.0 × 5.5 = 27.5 m². 126 − 27.5 = 98.5 m².
FS Level 1 FS Level 2 Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE Open Awards Highfield
