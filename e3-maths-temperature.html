3 Simple Entry 3 Golden Rules: Temperature

Golden Rule 1: Know Key Everyday °C Benchmarks

0°C = water freezes • 4°C = safe fridge • 20°C = comfy room • 37°C = normal body • 100°C = water boils.

Entry 3 Example: Always include the unit °C in your answer!
Golden Rule 2: Higher on the Scale = Warmer • Lower = Colder

On any vertical thermometer, moving UP means the temperature gets warmer (higher); moving DOWN means it gets colder (lower).

Entry 3 Example: 24°C is warmer than 15°C; 2°C is colder than 9°C.
Golden Rule 3: Count Step Marks Carefully on Thermometer Scales

Thermometers often go up in 2s, 5s, or 10s — check the gap between two printed numbers before reading the red bar!

Entry 3 Example: 5 spaces between 10°C and 20°C → each tick mark is 2°C!

Visual Celsius (°C) Thermometer & Everyday Benchmarks

Core Skill 1: Finding Temperature Rises and Drops

Subtract the lower temperature from the higher temperature to find how much it changed.

Morning is 8°C; afternoon reaches 19°C
Rise = 19 − 8 = 11°C warmer.
Oven cools from 180°C down by 40°C
180 − 40 = 140°C.

Core Skill 2: Fridge & Food Safety Checks

Workplace catering questions love checking whether a fridge is between 1°C and 5°C!

Fridge thermometer shows 3°C
Safe! 3°C sits between 1°C and 5°C.
Emma’s Entry 3 Exam Tip
If a thermometer shows 0°C and drops by 3 degrees overnight, picture going 3 steps below zero on the scale to −3°C (freezing cold!). Counting steps on the thermometer picture makes it effortless.

Entry Level 3 Step-by-Step Worked Exam Question

Entry Level 3 Exam Model 3 Marks (Non-Calculator)

Everyday Scenario: Hospital Ward Fridge Check

A medicine fridge must stay between 2°C and 8°C. At 9:00 am the thermometer reads 4°C. While the door is left open during restocking, the temperature rises by 6°C.
What is the new temperature, and is it still in the safe range?
Step
Clear Working Out
Mark Focus
Step 1 Add the 6°C rise to 4°C
4°C + 6°C = 10°C
[M1] 10°C calculation
Step 2 Compare 10°C against safe range 2°C–8°C
10°C is higher than 8°C
[M1] Compare with 8°C
Step 3 Clear decision
10°C — No, it is too warm (2°C above the 8°C limit)
[A1] 10°C and No
Final Exam Answer: 10°C — No (above the 8°C maximum)
Emma’s Check Reminder: Always include units (£, p, cm, g, ml, or °C) and double-check you answered the exact question asked!

Common Entry 3 Mistakes to Avoid

Common Entry 3 Mistake: Counting Every Tick as 1°C Without Checking
Always divide the gap by the number of spaces first — many oven dials go up in 10°C or 25°C steps!
Common Entry 3 Mistake: Forgetting the °C Unit Symbol
Write 18°C rather than bare 18.

Interactive Entry Level 3 Practice Questions

Entry 3 Practice 1: Weather Comparison 2 Marks
Four cities record lunchtime temperatures: Cardiff 14°C, Edinburgh 9°C, London 17°C, Belfast 11°C.
(a) Which city is the coldest?
(b) How much warmer is London than Edinburgh?
Entry Level 3 Worked Solution:
(a) Coldest (1 Mark): Edinburgh (9°C)
(b) Difference (1 Mark): 17 − 9 = 8°C
Final Answer: (a) Edinburgh, (b) 8°C
Entry 3 Practice 2: Greenhouse Heater Alert 2 Marks
A greenhouse is at 16°C at sunset and drops by 9°C overnight. What is the overnight temperature?
Entry Level 3 Worked Solution:
Subtract (1 Mark): 16 − 9
Answer (1 Mark): 7°C
Final Answer: 7°C
