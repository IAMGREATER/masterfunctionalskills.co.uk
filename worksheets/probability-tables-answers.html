Section A: Core Skills (Warm-Up) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Sample Space & Combined Independent Events) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
An automated quality scanner tests two independent batches of blood vials. The probability that Batch 1 passes is 0.9 and the probability that Batch 2 passes is 0.8.
Calculate the probability that both batches pass first time.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Apply multiplication rule for independent events: P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B) [M1]
2. Calculate 0.9 × 0.8 = 0.72 (or 72%) [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 0.72 (or 18/25 or 72%)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Adding 0.9 + 0.8 = 1.7 (probabilities can never exceed 1!).
🔄 Quick Check: 0.72 + (0.9×0.2) + (0.1×0.8) + (0.1×0.2) = 0.72 + 0.18 + 0.08 + 0.02 = 1.00.
Question 2 (Level 1 Foundation — 3 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
The probability that a delivery train arrives on time on Monday is 0.8, and the probability it arrives on time on Tuesday is 0.7 (independent events). Calculate:
(a) The probability that it arrives on time on both days.
(b) The probability that it is late on both days.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. P(Both on time) = 0.8 × 0.7 = 0.56. [M1]
2. P(Late Mon) = 1 − 0.8 = 0.2; P(Late Tue) = 1 − 0.7 = 0.3. [M1]
3. P(Both late) = 0.2 × 0.3 = 0.06. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) 0.56 | (b) 0.06
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Computing P(A and B) as 0.05 + 0.04 = 0.09 instead of 0.05 × 0.04 = 0.002.
🔄 Quick Check: Find the individual late probabilities first (0.2 and 0.3), then multiply them together (0.06).
Section B: Everyday & Workplace Maths Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Backup Hospital Generator Independent Reliability (Level 2 Core)) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A hospital intensive care unit has two independent backup generators, Generator A and Generator B.
• The probability that Generator A fails during a storm is 0.05.
• The probability that Generator B fails during a storm is 0.04.
(a) Calculate the probability that both generators fail at the same time.
(b) Calculate the probability that neither generator fails (both work).
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Multiply failure probabilities for P(Both Fail): P(A fails AND B fails) = 0.05 × 0.04 = 0.002. [M1 / A1]
2. Find working probabilities for each generator: P(A works) = 1 − 0.05 = 0.95. | P(B works) = 1 − 0.04 = 0.96. [M1]
3. Multiply working probabilities for P(Both Work): P(Both work) = 0.95 × 0.96 = 0.912 (or 91.2%). [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) P(Both fail) = 0.002 | (b) P(Both work) = 0.912
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Remember: AND = Multiply (×); OR = Add (+).
🔄 Quick Check: Notice that 0.05 × 0.04 = 0.002 (three decimal places), NOT 0.02! Never add probabilities when asked for "BOTH" (AND).
Question 4 (Workplace Application — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Using the train probabilities from Question 1 (On time Mon = 0.8, On time Tue = 0.7), calculate the probability that the train arrives on time on exactly one of the two days.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Way 1 (On time Mon AND Late Tue) = 0.8 × 0.3 = 0.24. [M1]
2. Way 2 (Late Mon AND On time Tue) = 0.2 × 0.7 = 0.14. [P1]
3. Total P(Exactly one day on time) = 0.24 + 0.14 = 0.38. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 0.38
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Writing 0.2 × 0.3 as 0.6 instead of 0.06.
🔄 Quick Check: Check: 0.56 (both on time) + 0.06 (both late) + 0.38 (one of each) = 1.00!
Section C: Exam-Style Problem Solving Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Fundraising Fair Two-Spinner Game Financial Profit Projection (Level 2 Multi-Step)) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
At a hospital charity fair, players pay £2.00 per game to spin two fair spinners:
• Spinner A has 3 equal sections numbered 1, 2, 3.
• Spinner B has 4 equal sections numbered 2, 4, 6, 8.
• The player wins a £5.00 cash prize if the sum of the two spinners is 9 or more.
If 180 people play the game, calculate the expected net profit raised for the hospital charity.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Determine total outcomes in the sample space and winning combinations (Sum ≥ 9): Total outcomes = 3 × 4 = 12 equally likely combinations. | Sums ≥ 9: (1+8=9), (2+8=10), (3+6=9), (3+8=11) → 4 winning outcomes. [M1]
2. Calculate expected number of winners out of 180 players: P(Win) = 4/12 = 1/3. | Expected winners = (1/3) × 180 = 60 winners. [M1]
3. Calculate total entry revenue minus total prize payouts: Total Revenue = 180 × £2.00 = £360.00. | Total Prize Payout = 60 × £5.00 = £300.00. | Expected Net Profit = £360.00 − £300.00 = £60.00. [M1 / A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: £60.00 expected net profit (P(Win) = 4/12 = 1/3; 60 expected winners)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Count total decimal digits: 1 dp + 1 dp = 2 decimal places in the product (0.06).
🔄 Quick Check: List all 12 pairs in a 3×4 grid so you do not miss any winning combination!
Question 6 (Level 2 Multi-Step Problem Solving — 3 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Two fair 6-sided dice are rolled and their scores are added together. Out of the 36 possible outcomes, how many outcomes give a total score of 10 or more, and what is the probability as a simplified fraction?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Sums ≥ 10: (4,6), (5,5), (6,4) [sum 10]; (5,6), (6,5) [sum 11]; (6,6) [sum 12] → 6 outcomes. [M1]
2. Probability = 6 / 36 = 1 / 6. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 6 outcomes; Probability = 1/6
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Stating a conclusion without showing the full numerical comparison values.
🔄 Quick Check: List the pairs systematically: three ways to make 10, two ways to make 11, one way to make 12 = 6/36 = 1/6.
