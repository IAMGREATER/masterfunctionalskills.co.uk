  • Level 1 (L1.25): Interpret nets of simple 3-D shapes (including cubes, cuboids, prisms, and pyramids).

Foundational Theory Level 1 & Level 2 Core Geometry Concept

What is a 2D Net?

A 2D net is a flat, two-dimensional pattern that can be folded along crease lines and assembled to construct a three-dimensional hollow solid.

Imagine taking a cardboard cereal box, cutting carefully along some of its glued seams with scissors, and flattening it completely onto a table. The resulting flat piece of card is the net of that cuboid.

Key Drawing Conventions Used in Exams:

  • Solid Lines (——): Represent cut lines (the outer perimeter of the net).
  • Dashed Lines (- - - -): Represent crease or fold lines where the card must be bent.
  • Flaps / Tabs: Extra narrow margins added in manufacturing so glue or tape can stick faces together. (In basic Functional Skills exam drawings, glue tabs are usually omitted unless explicitly requested).

Shape Properties Level 1 & Level 2 Exam Essential Knowledge

3D Shape Anatomy: Faces, Edges & Vertices

To determine whether a net is valid, you must first know the exact properties of the 3D solid it is supposed to make. Before folding, count the faces and verify their 2D shapes:

Cube
  • Faces: 6 congruent squares
  • Edges: 12 edges (all equal)
  • Vertices (corners): 8 vertices
Net Check: Exactly 6 identical squares. No row of 5 squares. No 2×2 solid blocks.
Cuboid (Rectangular Prism)
  • Faces: 6 rectangles (3 pairs)
  • Edges: 12 edges
  • Vertices (corners): 8 vertices
Net Check: 3 matching pairs of opposite rectangular faces (Top/Bottom, Front/Back, Left/Right).
Triangular Prism
  • Faces: 5 faces (2 Δ + 3 ▭)
  • Edges: 9 edges
  • Vertices (corners): 6 vertices
Net Check: 2 identical triangular end faces and 3 rectangular side walls folding along the prism length.
Square-Based Pyramid
  • Faces: 5 faces (1 □ + 4 Δ)
  • Edges: 8 edges
  • Vertices (corners): 5 (including apex)
Net Check: 1 central square base with 4 identical isosceles triangles folding up towards the apex.
Cylinder
  • Faces: 3 (2 circles + 1 curved)
  • Edges: 2 circular edges
  • Vertices: 0 vertices
Net Check: 2 identical circular lids and 1 unrolled rectangle whose width equals the circle's circumference ($\pi d$).
Cone
  • Faces: 2 (1 circle + 1 curved)
  • Edges: 1 circular edge
  • Vertices: 1 apex point
Net Check: 1 circular base and 1 sector of a larger circle (like a slice of pizza) that rolls into a cone.

The 3 Golden Rules of Nets

Golden Rule 1: Always Count & Verify Face Shapes First

Before doing any mental folding, count the number of faces on the flat pattern. If an exam question asks whether a pattern folds into a cube, but the pattern has 5 squares or 7 squares, you can instantly reject it without wasting time! A cube must have exactly 6 squares. A triangular prism must have exactly 2 triangles and 3 rectangles.

Fast Check: Does the flat net have the exact number and shapes of faces required for the 3D solid?
Golden Rule 2: Touching Edges Must Match Exactly in Length

When a flat net folds into a 3D solid, pairs of edges that meet to form a single seam must be the exact same length. If an edge of $5 cm$ folds against an edge of $3 cm$, the box will not close properly and will leave a gaping hole or tear. Always check adjacent folding sides.

Seam Rule: When two edges fold up to meet in 3D, do their labelled dimensions match 1-to-1?
Golden Rule 3: Opposite Faces Never Touch Along an Edge

In a closed 3D solid like a cube or cuboid, opposite faces (e.g. Top and Bottom, or Front and Back) are parallel to each other. They never share an edge on a flat net! In any straight row or column of faces, opposite faces are always separated by exactly one intervening face. If two opposite faces touch directly on the net, they will collide when folded.

Opposite Rule: Opposite faces are separated by exactly one step on the net grid.

The 11 Valid Nets of a Cube (Full Matrix)

A cube has exactly 11 unique valid nets (excluding rotations and reflections). Every single valid cube net consists of 6 identical squares arranged so that no faces collide or leave gaps when folded.

Mathematicians categorize all 11 nets into four structural families based on their central rows:

1-4-1 Group Net #1
Latin Cross (Classic)
4 in central row. Top and bottom flaps both at column 2.
1-4-1 Group Net #2
Offset Latin Cross
4 in central row. Top flap at column 1, bottom flap at column 2.
1-4-1 Group Net #3
T-Cross Net
4 in central row. Top flap at column 0, bottom flap at column 2.
1-4-1 Group Net #4
Staggered Cross
4 in central row. Top flap at column 3, bottom flap at column 1.
1-4-1 Group Net #5
Wide Flap Net
4 in central row. Top flap at column 0, bottom flap at column 3.
1-4-1 Group Net #6
Col-1 Aligned Cross
4 in central row. Top and bottom flaps both at column 1.
2-3-1 Group Net #7
Z-Pattern Net
2 on top row, 3 in middle row, 1 on bottom row.
2-3-1 Group Net #8
Step-Down Net
2 on top row, 3 in middle row, bottom flap at column 2.
2-3-1 Group Net #9
Compact Step Net
2 on top row, 3 in middle row, bottom flap at column 1.
2-2-2 Group Net #10
Staircase Zigzag
3 pairs of 2 squares descending in a balanced staircase.
3-3 Group Net #11
Twin Offset Bars
Two rows of 3 squares offset by exactly one square.
Tutor Golden Memory Rule: Notice the universal pattern across all 11 valid nets above:
• There are NO solid 2×2 blocks of squares anywhere.
• There are NO lines of 5 squares.
• Memorizing the classic 1-4-1 Latin Cross (Net #1) and the 2-3-1 Z-Pattern (Net #7) covers over 90% of exam questions!
Exam Traps Level 1 & Level 2 Common Misconceptions

How to Spot an Invalid Net in Seconds

In exam multiple choice and identification tasks, examiners deliberately present patterns that appear valid at first glance. Watch out for these three fatal flaws:

Trap 1: 2x2 Square Block
Solid 2x2 Block
A solid 2×2 block of four squares can never form a cube. Two faces fold directly into the exact same position, clashing and leaving an open hole on the opposite side.
Trap 2: 5 Squares in a Row
5-Square Linear Row
A cube only has 4 vertical walls! A line of 5 squares wraps completely around past the 4th side, overlapping face 1 and leaving one side wide open.
Trap 3: Adjacent Same-Side Flaps
Adjacent Same-Side Flaps
Both top flaps fold along the exact same orientation. Instead of forming a top and bottom lid, both flaps collide to form the top lid twice, leaving the base missing.
Examiner Blueprint Level 1 & Level 2 Method Mark (M1) Guide

Step-by-Step Guide: How to Draw Any Net on an Exam Grid

When an exam asks you to "Draw an accurate net of the box on the centimetre grid", follow this systematic 5-step blueprint to guarantee full method and accuracy marks:

Step 1
Identify and list all 3D dimensions: Write down Length ($L$), Width ($W$), and Height ($H$). Count the required faces (e.g. 6 for a cuboid, 5 for a prism) and note the dimensions of each matching pair.
Step 2
Draw the Base (Bottom face) in the center: Use a ruler and count grid squares carefully. Label this face "BASE" or "BOTTOM" lightly with its length and width.
Step 3
Attach the four side walls folding out:
• Attach the Front and Back walls ($L × H$) to the top and bottom edges of the base.
• Attach the Left and Right side walls ($W × H$) to the side edges of the base.
Step 4
Attach the Top Lid: Attach the top face ($L × W$) to the outer edge of either the Front or Back wall so it can fold over to close the box.
Step 5
Check seams & line styles: Verify that dashed lines are used for internal fold lines and solid lines for the perimeter. Ensure adjacent edges have identical measurements!

Model 1: Flat-Pack Delivery Box (Cuboid)

A logistics warehouse manufactures flat-pack delivery cartons measuring 8 cm long, 5 cm wide, and 3 cm high.

1. The 3D Cuboid Box
Dimensions: Length = 8 cm, Width = 5 cm, Height = 3 cm. Total 6 rectangular faces.
2. The Unfolded 2D Net

(3×5) RIGHT
(3×5) Length = 8 cm 5 cm 3 cm
Notice how the side wings ($3 cm × 5 cm$) attach along the $5 cm$ edge of the base so they fold up into vertical walls $3 cm$ high!
Step 1
List the 3 pairs of opposite faces:
• Top & Bottom: 2 faces measuring 8 cm × 5 cm
• Front & Back: 2 faces measuring 8 cm × 3 cm
• Left & Right: 2 faces measuring 5 cm × 3 cm
Step 2
Verify Edge Matching: When the Left flap ($3× 5$) folds up, its $3 cm$ edge joins the $3 cm$ height edge of the Front face. Both edges are exactly $3 cm$, creating a perfect corner seam!
Sonya’s Examiner Tip Edge Seam Matching
When checking nets, trace where adjacent edges meet! Each pair of folding flaps that join to form a corner must be identical in length. If one edge is 3 cm and the other is 5 cm, the net will not close.

In a triangular prism net, the two triangular end flaps must face in consistent orientations so that when folded, their apexes align across the central rectangular spine.

Question 1: Cube Net Verification & Trap Elimination
[3 Marks]

Look at the four 2D nets shown below. Which TWO nets can be folded to form a closed cube? Explain why the other two nets cannot fold into a cube.

Official Tutor Step-by-Step Mark Scheme
[3 Marks] Full Marks
Step 1 (Valid Nets Identification) B1 Mark
Net A and Net C are valid nets. Net A is the classic 1-4-1 Latin cross net and Net C is a valid 2-3-1 Z-pattern net. Both have 6 squares with no overlapping faces.
Step 2 (Net B Diagnostic) M1 Mark
Net B fails because it contains a solid 2×2 block of squares. When folded, two of these squares collide into the exact same face, leaving an open hole on the opposite side.
Step 3 (Net D Diagnostic) M1 Mark
Net D fails because it has 5 squares in a straight row. A cube has only 4 vertical walls, so a row of 5 wraps past the 4th side, overlapping face 1 and leaving one side open.
Correct Final Answer:
Valid: Net A and Net C. Invalid: Net B (2×2 block clashes) and Net D (5 in a row overlaps).

Question 2: Packaging Scenario — Tablet Delivery Box
[3 Marks]

An electronics distributor manufactures cardboard packaging boxes for graphic tablets. Each box is a cuboid measuring 12 cm long, 8 cm wide, and 5 cm high.

Three candidate flat-pack net designs (Net 1, Net 2, and Net 3) are evaluated below:

(a) State which candidate net is mathematically correct.

(b) Explain the specific geometric error in the other two candidate nets.

Official Tutor Step-by-Step Mark Scheme
[3 Marks] Full Marks
Step 1 (Correct Net Selection) A1 Mark
Proposed Net 1 is correct. The central spine alternates between the $12× 5$ and $12× 8$ faces, and the two side flaps are $8 cm × 5 cm$ attached to the $8 cm$ edges of the base.
Step 2 (Net 2 Error Diagnostic) M1 Mark
Net 2 fails because its side flaps measure 8 cm × 8 cm instead of 8 cm × 5 cm. When folded up, they will be 3 cm too tall and collide with the top lid.
Step 3 (Net 3 Error Diagnostic) M1 Mark
Net 3 fails because the central spine has 5 rectangles instead of 4, giving 7 faces in total. A cuboid has only 6 faces, so Net 3 will overlap onto itself.
Correct Final Answer:
Net 1 is correct. Net 2 has oversized side flaps (8×8cm). Net 3 has 7 faces instead of 6.
FS Level 1 FS Level 2 Pearson Edexcel • City & Guilds
Question 3: Triangular Prism Packaging Area
[2 Marks]

A confectionery company packages triangular chocolate bars in a prism box. The end faces are equilateral triangles with sides of 6 cm. The length of the box is 15 cm.

(a) State the total number of faces, edges, and vertices on this box.

(b) Calculate the total cardboard surface area of the three rectangular faces of the net.

Official Tutor Step-by-Step Mark Scheme
[2 Marks] Full Marks
Part (a): Shape Anatomy B1 Mark
Faces = 5 (2 triangles + 3 rectangles), Edges = 9, Vertices = 6.
Part (b): Rectangular Area M1 A1 Mark
Each rectangular face measures $15 cm × 6 cm = 90 cm^2$.
Total area for 3 rectangular faces $= 3 × 90 = \mathbf{270 cm^2}$.
Correct Final Answer:
(a) 5 faces, 9 edges, 6 vertices. (b) 270 cm²
FS Level 1 FS Level 2 Pearson Edexcel • City & Guilds
Question 4: Cylindrical Paint Tin Label
[3 Marks]

A manufacturing plant produces cylindrical paint tins. Each tin has a diameter of 8 cm and a height of 12 cm. A rectangular paper label wraps completely around the tin with an extra 1 cm overlap for glue.

(a) What 2D shape is formed when the curved surface of a cylinder is unrolled flat?

(b) Calculate the minimum total length of the paper label required, including the 1 cm overlap. (Use $\pi = 3.142$ and give your answer to 1 decimal place).

Official Tutor Step-by-Step Mark Scheme
[3 Marks] Full Marks
Part (a): Shape Identification B1 Mark
A Rectangle (or oblong).
Part (b): Circumference Calculation M1 Mark
$Circumference} = \pi × d = 3.142 × 8 = 25.136 cm$.
Part (b): Total Length with Overlap A1 Mark
$Total Length} = 25.136 + 1.0 = 26.136 cm ≈ \mathbf{26.1 cm}$ (to 1 d.p.).
Correct Final Answer:
(a) Rectangle. (b) 26.1 cm (to 1 d.p.).
FS Level 2 Pearson Edexcel • City & Guilds
