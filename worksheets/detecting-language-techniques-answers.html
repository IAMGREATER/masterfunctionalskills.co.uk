Detecting Language Techniques & Rhetorical Devices Pearson Edexcel: Pearson Edexcel Functional Skills English (READING)
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Foundational Concept Check Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Naming & Quoting Persuasive DAFOREST Techniques) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Read this extract from a hospital blood-donation campaign:
'Donating blood takes just forty-five minutes, yet 84% of first-time donors say it is the proudest moment of their year. Will you step forward and save three lives today?'
Identify two distinct language techniques used in this extract and quote an example of each.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Statistic / Numerical Fact: '84% of first-time donors' (or 'forty-five minutes' / 'three lives') [A1]
2. Rhetorical Question / Direct Address: 'Will you step forward and save three lives today?' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 1. Statistic ('84% of first-time donors'); 2. Rhetorical question / Direct address ('Will you step forward and save three lives today?').
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Copying the quotes without naming the specific language techniques (Statistic and Rhetorical Question).
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Both techniques are explicitly named and paired with exact short quotes.
Question 2 (Core Skill Application — 2 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A warehouse safety poster displays the slogan: 'Stop. Look. Listen. Protect your life around forklifts today.'
Name one language device used in the slogan and explain its persuasive effect on warehouse operators.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Device: Rule of three / short imperative verbs ('Stop. Look. Listen.') or direct address ('Protect your life') [1 Mark]. [M1]
2. Effect: Delivers punchy, authoritative commands that cut through workplace distraction and force operators to check for moving forklifts immediately [1 Mark]. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Device: Imperative verbs / Rule of three ('Stop. Look. Listen.'). | Effect: Delivers urgent, memorable commands that prompt workers to pause and check for hazards immediately.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Listing 'bullet points', 'subheadings', or 'bold text' when asked for a language technique scores zero. Those are presentational/layout devices.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Short one-word commands are called imperative verbs or minor sentences.
Section B: Functional Workplace Application Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Identifying Persuasive Devices in a Community Poster (Level 1)) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Read the poster extract:
'Every blood donation can save up to three lives! Book your 45-minute appointment at the Town Hall this Friday and become a local lifesaver.'
Identify two language features used in this extract and give the matching quote for each. [2 Marks]
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify Feature 1 and quote: Numerical fact / statistic + exclamation: 'Every blood donation can save up to three lives!' [A1]
2. Identify Feature 2 and quote: Imperative command / direct address / alliteration: 'Book your 45-minute appointment' or 'local lifesaver'. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 1. Statistic / Fact: 'save up to three lives'. | 2. Direct address / Imperative command: 'Book your 45-minute appointment' (or alliteration: 'local lifesaver').
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Language techniques relate strictly to words, figures of speech, and sentence grammar (metaphors, alliteration, rhetorical questions, rule of three).
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Always pair the name of the language feature with the exact words quoted from the extract.
Question 4 (Exam-Style Task — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Read this extract from a coastal conservation leaflet:
'Every single minute, a rubbish truck of plastic is dumped into our suffocating oceans. Will you help us turn the tide on marine pollution before our beaches become toxic wastelands?'
Identify two language features with quotes [2 Marks] and explain how they persuade the reader [1 Mark].
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Feature 1 & Quote: Emotive personification/metaphor ('suffocating oceans' / 'toxic wastelands') or shocking statistic ('a rubbish truck of plastic every single minute') [1 Mark]. [M1]
2. Feature 2 & Quote: Rhetorical question / direct address ('Will you help us turn the tide...?') [1 Mark]. [M1]
3. Effect: Creates shock and alarm at the scale of ocean damage and directly challenges the individual reader to join the clean-up effort [1 Mark]. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Features: 1. Emotive imagery ('suffocating oceans' / 'toxic wastelands'). 2. Rhetorical question with direct address ('Will you help us turn the tide...?'). Effect: Shocks the reader with vivid imagery of environmental harm and places personal responsibility on them to act.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: If you identify 'alliteration' and quote a 25-word sentence, the examiner cannot tell which two words you think are alliterative.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Always connect the imagery ('suffocating oceans') to the emotional response it triggers (alarm/guilt).
Section C: Pearson Edexcel Exam-Style Analysis & Evaluation Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Analyzing Rhetorical Persuasion in an NHS Staff Wellbeing Campaign (Level 2)) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Read the campaign extract:
'Our dedicated nurses are the beating heart of this hospital, yet they are stretched to breaking point. Are we truly willing to stand by while our caring colleagues collapse under exhaustion?'
Identify two language techniques used by the author, quote an example of each, and explain their combined persuasive effect on hospital management. [3 Marks]
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify Technique 1 & Quotation: Metaphor: 'beating heart of this hospital' (or 'stretched to breaking point'). [A1]
2. Identify Technique 2 & Quotation: Rhetorical question (with inclusive pronoun 'we' and alliteration 'caring colleagues collapse'): 'Are we truly willing to stand by while our caring colleagues collapse under exhaustion?' [A1]
3. Explain Specific Reader Effect: The metaphor emphasises that nurses are essential to keeping the hospital alive, while the rhetorical question provokes moral guilt and urgency, pressuring management to act before staff burn out. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Technique 1: Metaphor ('beating heart of this hospital'). Technique 2: Rhetorical question ('Are we truly willing to stand by while our caring colleagues collapse under exhaustion?'). Effect: Emphasises how vital nurses are while provoking moral guilt and urgency so management improves working conditions immediately.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Quote only the crisp 2–6 words that contain the technique (e.g., 'caring colleagues collapse').
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Avoid writing vague effects like 'it makes the reader want to read on'. Always name the specific emotion: guilt, urgency, pride, empathy, or outrage.
Question 6 (Level 2 Mastery Challenge — 2 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Read this sentence from an apprenticeship recruitment brochure:
'Earn while you learn: gain practical skills, industry qualifications, and debt-free confidence.'
Identify two language techniques used in this sentence.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Technique 1: Internal rhyme / imperative catchphrase ('Earn while you learn') or direct address ('you') [1 Mark]. [M1]
2. Technique 2: Rule of three ('practical skills, industry qualifications, and debt-free confidence') [1 Mark]. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 1. Direct address / internal rhyme ('Earn while you learn'). | 2. Rule of three ('practical skills, industry qualifications, and debt-free confidence').
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Failing to proofread spelling, punctuation, and grammar after completing the draft.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Look out for three noun phrases separated by commas — that is a classic Rule of Three!
