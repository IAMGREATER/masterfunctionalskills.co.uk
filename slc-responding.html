Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.4 / L2.3 – Respond to questions and feedback clearly and effectively, giving detailed explanations and elaboration.
  • L2.5 – Follow and analyse lines of argument, using speech to clarify, defend, and develop points under questioning.

Golden Rules for Responding to Questions & Feedback

Golden Rule 1: Use the PREP Formula to Answer Any Question Under Pressure

Never panic or ramble when asked a tough question after your presentation. Structure your answer in 4 steps: Point (direct answer) → Reason (why) → Example (real-world evidence) → Point (reaffirm conclusion).

Golden Rule / Technique: Point → Reason → Example → Point (PREP)
Golden Rule 2: Validate the Questioner's Concern Before Pivoting with Evidence

When someone challenges your proposal, never react defensively or say 'No, you're wrong'. Acknowledge their point ('That is an understandable concern regarding budget...'), then pivot calmly ('...however, the pilot data shows...').

Golden Rule / Technique: Acknowledge Courtesy → Validate Concern → Pivot with Factual Evidence

Core Skill 1: Three Professional Strategies for Handling Tough or Unexpected Questions

How to stay composed when an audience member asks a difficult or multi-part question.

  1. Buy 3 Seconds of Thinking Time with a Courteous Opening: "Thank you for raising that—staffing cover is a crucial operational aspect of this proposal." This gives your brain 3 seconds to organise your PREP answer while sounding confident.
  2. Unpack Two-Part Questions Explicitly: "There are two important parts to your question: first the upfront cost, and second the training time. Taking the cost first..."
  3. Handle Unknown Figures with Professional Honesty: Never invent absurd numbers on the spot. Say: "I do not have the exact Q4 breakdown in front of me today; however, our last audit showed a 15% saving, and I would be happy to email the full cost table after this meeting."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: The PREP Response Architecture in Action

See how the 4-sentence PREP model turns a challenging question into a Distinction-level answer.

PREP Stage What It Does Spoken Example (Question: 'Won't reusable cups create more washing-up work?')
P — Point Acknowledge and give a clear direct answer. "That is a fair question, but switching to reusable mugs will not add extra workload for our catering staff."
R — Reason Explain the operational mechanism why. "Because our existing commercial dishwasher runs a high-temperature cycle in just ninety seconds per rack."
E — Example Provide a concrete trial statistic or comparison. "For example, when the Bristol office made this switch, staff simply placed their own mugs straight into the dishwasher racks at the end of breaks."
P — Point Seal the conclusion and check if answered. "Therefore, we eliminate 4,000 disposable cups a month with zero extra catering hours. Does that address your concern?"
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Receiving Constructive Criticism & Modifying Your Proposal

Why showing flexibility when a peer spots a flaw scores top marks at Level 2.

You do not have to stubbornly defend every detail! If a questioner points out a genuine gap (e.g. disabled access or night-shift cover), incorporating their feedback proves advanced collaborative listening:

"Thank you for pointing that out, Farah—I had focused on daytime staff and had not factored in night-shift security. We should definitely amend the proposal to keep three floodlit spaces directly outside the main entrance reserved for night workers."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 Level 1 Responding to Questions Benchmark (L1.4)

Vocational Context: Workplace Canteen Improvement Talk

After your short talk proposing healthier salad and soup options in the staff canteen, a peer asks:
'Healthy food is usually much more expensive—won't staff end up paying £7 or £8 for lunch?'
Deliver a clear 3-sentence response.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Acknowledge the concern about lunch prices
'Thank you, Ben—keeping meals affordable for everyone is definitely a top priority.'
Criteria: Polite Acknowledgment (L1.4)
Step 2 Explain how costs will be kept low
'By buying seasonal vegetables in bulk from our local market supplier, the canteen can prepare fresh soup and salad bowls for under £3.50 per portion.'
Criteria: Clear Explanation & Figure
Step 3 Reaffirm the benefit and check satisfaction
'That means the healthy meal deal will actually cost 50p less than the current fried meal deal. Does that answer your question?'
Criteria: Positive Reassurance
Final Accredited Response: 'Thank you, Ben—keeping lunch affordable for all staff is definitely a priority. By buying seasonal vegetables in bulk from our local supplier, the kitchen can serve fresh soup and a roll or a large salad bowl for £3.50. That means the healthy option will actually cost 50p less than the current burger meal deal.'
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Giving a specific, realistic price comparison ('£3.50 vs the current burger deal') makes your answer immediately convincing.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 Level 2 Extended Q&A Response Benchmark (L2.3, L2.5)

Vocational Context: Healthcare Informatics Implementation

Following your 4-minute presentation advocating for electronic tablets on hospital rounds, an assessor asks:
'Senior ward staff are already overwhelmed. Won't introducing new software simply increase their stress and cause charting errors?'
Deliver a model PREP response.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Point (Validate & Give Direct Thesis)
'While any system transition carries an initial learning curve, introducing bedside tablets will significantly reduce overall nursing workload once implemented.'
Criteria: Validates Premise & States Thesis (L2.3)
Step 2 Reason (Explain Functional Mechanism)
'This is because automated drop-down menus and barcode wristband scanning eliminate repetitive handwritten transcription across three different paper folders.'
Criteria: Logical Operational Rationale
Step 3 Example (Cite Measurable Pilot Evidence)
'For instance, during the six-week pilot on Ward 6, pairing two floor super-users with nursing staff cut medication charting time by 35 minutes per shift and reduced transcription errors by 28%.'
Criteria: Persuasive Evidence (L2.4)
Step 4 Point (Reaffirm Conclusion)
'Therefore, with phased super-user mentoring during the first fortnight, the digital system relieves clinical pressure rather than adding to it.'
Criteria: Composed Concluding Synthesis (L2.5)
Final Accredited Response: 'While any system change carries an initial learning curve, digital bedside tablets will actually reduce overall nursing workload once implemented. Automated drop-down menus and barcode scanning eliminate repetitive paper charting, saving an average of 35 minutes per shift. For instance, during the pilot on Ward 6, dedicated super-user floor support ensured charting errors dropped by 28% within the first fortnight. Therefore, with proper hands-on mentoring, the system relieves clinical pressure rather than adding to it.'
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Notice how the candidate did not deny that learning new software takes effort ('While any system change carries an initial learning curve...') before proving with Ward 6 data why the long-term benefit wins.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Giving One-Sentence Dismissive Replies ('No, It Will Be Fine')
Brushing off an audience question with 'No, I don't think that will be a problem' fails Criterion L1.4 / L2.3, which requires detailed explanations.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always give at least 3 full sentences (Point + Reason + Example) when answering any question in your Q&A.
Examiner Pitfall: Getting Flustered or Arguing Back Defensively
If a peer challenges your idea, snapping back ('Well you clearly didn't listen to my slide!') ruins your professional register mark.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Smile, thank the questioner for raising an important angle, and respond with calm facts.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 3 Marks
During a team discussion on installing recycling bins in a warehouse depot, a colleague remarks:
'No one will bother sorting rubbish; staff will just throw everything into the nearest bin.'
Write a 45-word spoken response using the PREP method.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Point & Reason: Acknowledge that poorly labelled bins cause confusion, but clear visual signage solves this.
2. Example: Cite that the Leeds depot achieved 85% sorting accuracy by placing colour-coded picture posters above each bin.
3. Point: Conclude that clear signage and a 5-minute briefing will make compliance easy.
Final Model Answer: 'Compliance is certainly a valid concern if bins are poorly labelled; however, clear visual design makes sorting effortless. For example, when our Leeds depot installed colour-coded bins with large pictorial posters above each station, recycling accuracy reached 85% within two weeks. Therefore, combining clear signage with a brief morning toolbox talk will ensure our team adapts quickly.'
Examiner Tip: Citing a successful trial at a sister branch or neighbouring ward is the easiest way to supply the 'E' (Example) in PREP.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
At the end of your presentation on upgrading office lighting, an audience member asks:
'What is the exact kilowatt-hour rating of the proposed Philips LED tubes?'
You do not know that technical figure. Write a professional 2-sentence spoken response that handles the unknown detail with authority while offering relevant information you DO know.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. State honestly and professionally that you do not have the exact wattage figure to hand, and offer to confirm it after the meeting.
2. Pivot to a related energy fact you do know (e.g. the 45% overall reduction in electricity bills).
Final Model Answer: 'That is a great technical question—I do not have the exact kilowatt-hour specification per tube in front of me today, so I will check the manufacturer's datasheet and email that figure to the group this afternoon. What I can confirm from the estates quote is that the LED units consume 45% less electricity overall than our current fluorescent tubes.'
Examiner Tip: Pivoting from a tiny technical detail you don't know to a headline statistic you DO know shows impressive workplace poise.
Exam Practice Question 3 4 Marks
After your presentation proposing that a community library should replace half of its book shelves with computer desks, an elderly resident asks:
'Many older residents rely on large-print books and do not use computers—won't your plan push us out of our own library?'
Write a compassionate, constructive 4-sentence spoken response that validates their concern and adapts your proposal to protect large-print book stocks.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Validate the resident's concern with empathy and respect.
2. Reassure them that the library must remain inclusive for all age groups.
3. Adapt your proposal on the spot (e.g. keep 100% of the large-print and ground-floor reading collections, and place the new computer desks on the mezzanine floor).
4. Check if that reassurance helps.
Final Model Answer: 'Thank you so much for raising that point, and I completely understand why protecting our book collection is vital for older residents. Our library must remain a welcoming space for every generation in the community. To ensure no readers lose out, we will ring-fence 100% of our large-print books and comfortable ground-floor armchairs, and instead install the new computer study desks purely in the upstairs reference room. Would keeping the entire ground floor dedicated to traditional reading address your concern?'
Examiner Tip: Adapting your proposal to protect vulnerable service users shows high emotional intelligence and collaborative problem-solving.
