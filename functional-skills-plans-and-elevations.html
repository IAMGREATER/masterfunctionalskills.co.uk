Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • L1.20 / L2.22 – Interpret and draw 2D representations of 3D objects including plan views, front elevations, and side elevations on scaled grids.

Golden Rules for Plans, Elevations & Front/Side Views

Golden Rule 1: The Three Orthographic Viewing Directions

Plan View = Looking straight down from above (bird's-eye view: shows Length & Width, NEVER Height). Front Elevation = Looking straight at the front (shows Width & Height). Side Elevation = Looking from the left or right side (shows Depth & Height).

Golden Rule / Method: Plan = Top (L×W) • Front = Ahead (W×H) • Side = Profile (D×H)
Golden Rule 2: Convert Real Dimensions Using the Grid Scale Before Drawing

Always read the grid key first (e.g. "1 square represents 0.5 m" means a 3 m wall requires 6 grid squares). Draw solid lines for visible step changes in surface level.

Golden Rule / Method: Grid Squares = Real Dimension ÷ Scale Value of 1 Square

Core Skill 2: 2D Projections of Cylinders, Cones, Pyramids & Prisms

Recognizing how curved and sloped 3D solids flatten into 2D shapes.

  • Upright Cylinder: Plan View = Circle; Front Elevation = Rectangle (width = diameter, height = cylinder height); Side Elevation = Rectangle.
  • Upright Cone: Plan View = Circle with central dot (apex); Front & Side Elevations = Isosceles Triangle.
  • Square-Based Pyramid: Plan View = Square with two intersecting diagonals; Front & Side Elevations = Isosceles Triangle.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Stepped & L-Shaped Building Elevations

Drawing internal split lines where a surface steps back or changes height.

When looking at a stepped building (e.g. a 2-storey block of height 6 m with a 1-storey porch of height 3 m sticking out at the front):

  • The Front Elevation shows the full outer rectangle PLUS a horizontal line at 3 m height where the porch roof meets the main wall.
  • The Plan View shows the main roof rectangle PLUS the porch roof rectangle separated by a solid line.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1)

Vocational Context: Healthcare Facility Engineering Plans

A backup diesel generator housing is a cuboid measuring 4.0 m long (front), 2.5 m deep (side), and 2.0 m high.
(a) State the 2D shape and dimensions of the Plan View.
(b) State the 2D shape and dimensions of the Front Elevation.
(c) State the 2D shape and dimensions of the Side Elevation.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Determine Plan View (Length × Depth)
Viewed from above: a rectangle measuring 4.0 m by 2.5 m.
Criteria: A1: Accurate plan view (4.0 m × 2.5 m)
Step 2 Determine Front Elevation (Length × Height)
Viewed from the front: a rectangle measuring 4.0 m wide by 2.0 m high.
Criteria: A1: Accurate front elevation (4.0 m × 2.0 m)
Step 3 Determine Side Elevation (Depth × Height)
Viewed from the side: a rectangle measuring 2.5 m deep by 2.0 m high.
Criteria: A1: Accurate side elevation (2.5 m × 2.0 m)
Final Accredited Answer: (a) Plan: 4.0 m × 2.5 m rectangle | (b) Front: 4.0 m × 2.0 m rectangle | (c) Side: 2.5 m × 2.0 m rectangle
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Plan view NEVER includes height; front view NEVER includes depth.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2)

Vocational Context: Modular Healthcare Building Construction

An architectural drawing shows the Front Elevation of a modular pharmacy building on a grid where 1 square = 0.5 m.
• On the grid, the front wall is a rectangle 12 squares wide by 6 squares high, topped by a triangular roof gable that rises an additional 4 squares to a central peak.
• Insulated cladding panels for this front elevation cost £45.00 per m².
Calculate the total cost to clad the entire front elevation.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Convert grid squares into real-world metres (× 0.5 m)
Width = 12 × 0.5 m = 6.0 m.
Wall Height = 6 × 0.5 m = 3.0 m.
Triangle Gable Height = 4 × 0.5 m = 2.0 m.
Criteria: M1: Accurate conversion from grid squares to metres
Step 2 Calculate area of rectangular wall + triangular gable
Rectangle Area = 6.0 m × 3.0 m = 18.0 m².
Triangle Area = (6.0 m × 2.0 m) ÷ 2 = 6.0 m².
Total Front Elevation Area = 18.0 + 6.0 = 24.0 m².
Criteria: M1: Compound elevation area (24.0 m²)
Step 3 Multiply total area by £45.00 per m²
24.0 m² × £45.00 = £1,080.00.
Criteria: A1: Accurate total cladding cost (£1,080.00)
Final Accredited Answer: £1,080.00 (Total front elevation area = 24.0 m²)
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Convert grid squares into metres BEFORE calculating area so you do not get caught out by square grid units!

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Including Height in the Plan View
Drawing the vertical height on a plan view is a classic mistake—looking straight down from the sky only reveals length and width.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Think: Plan = Floorplan (Length and Width only).
Examiner Pitfall: Forgetting the Internal Step Line on Stepped Blocks
Drawing only the outer outline of an L-shaped block in the front elevation without the horizontal step line loses 1 mark.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Any change in depth or height visible from your viewing direction must be drawn as a solid straight line.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
An upright cylindrical water storage tank has a diameter of 3.2 m and a height of 4.5 m.
(a) What 2D shape and measurement is its Plan View?
(b) What 2D shape and dimensions is its Front Elevation?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Plan view from above = Circle of diameter 3.2 m (radius 1.6 m).
2. Front elevation from the side = Rectangle measuring 3.2 m wide by 4.5 m high.
Final Correct Answer: (a) Circle (diameter 3.2 m) | (b) Rectangle (3.2 m wide × 4.5 m high)
Examiner Tip: Looking horizontally at a cylinder from the front or side produces a flat rectangle.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
An architect draws the Side Elevation of a warehouse measuring 15 m deep by 6.5 m high on centimetre squared paper using a scale of 1 cm represents 2.5 m. Calculate the width and height of the rectangle drawn on the grid in cm.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Grid width = 15 m ÷ 2.5 m = 6 cm.
2. Grid height = 6.5 m ÷ 2.5 m = 2.6 cm.
Final Correct Answer: 6 cm wide by 2.6 cm high
Examiner Tip: Divide each real-world dimension by 2.5 m to find the grid length in cm.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A concrete loading ramp is a right-angled triangular prism of width 2.0 m, horizontal base depth 4.8 m, vertical back height 1.4 m, and sloping ramp length 5.0 m. State the exact dimensions of the Plan View when viewed directly from above.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Looking straight down from above, the sloping ramp projects onto its horizontal base footprint.
2. Plan view is a rectangle of width 2.0 m by horizontal depth 4.8 m.
Final Correct Answer: Rectangle measuring 2.0 m by 4.8 m
Examiner Tip: From directly above, a sloped ramp appears as its horizontal footprint (4.8 m), not its 5.0 m slant length!
