Counting, Ordering & Writing Numbers to 1,000,000

Everyday application of Counting, Ordering & Writing Numbers to 1,000,000.
★Key Method & Rules
Numbers up to 1,000,000 are written with digits grouped in threes. Each position has a distinct place value.

Units, Tens, Hundreds, Thousands, Ten Thousands, Hundred Thousands, Millions.
✎Step-by-Step Example
Order these warehouse inventory counts from smallest to largest: 48,210; 40,890; 48,090.
  1. Compare ten thousands first: all 40,000
  2. Compare thousands: 40,890 is smallest
  3. Compare hundreds: 48,090 then 48,210
  4. Correct order: 40,890; 48,090; 48,210
✓Remember!
Always align digits from the right when comparing number size.
!Watch Out!
Don't confuse forty thousand with four hundred thousand.

Negative Numbers in Practical Contexts

Everyday application of Negative Numbers in Practical Contexts.
★Key Method & Rules
Negative numbers show values below zero such as freezer temperatures or bank overdrafts.

Temperature rise: count up through zero. Overdraft: negative balance means money owed to the bank.
✎Step-by-Step Example
A cold storage freezer is at -18°C. A sensor alert triggers if temperature rises by 7°C. What is the new reading?
  1. Start at -18°C
  2. Add 7°C: -18 + 7
  3. New temperature = -11°C
✓Remember!
Remember: moving towards zero increases temperature.
!Watch Out!
A smaller negative number is actually warmer than a larger negative number!

Column Addition & Subtraction with Money

Everyday application of Column Addition & Subtraction with Money.
★Key Method & Rules
Align decimal points vertically in straight columns before adding or subtracting monetary values.

Add or subtract from right to left, carrying or borrowing across columns as needed.
✎Step-by-Step Example
A tradesperson buys supplies: £142.50, £68.75, and £29.99. Find the total cost.
  1. Align decimal points: 142.50 + 68.75 + 29.99
  2. Sum pennies: 0 + 5 + 9 = 14 (carry 1)
  3. Total = £241.24
✓Remember!
Always include the £ sign and exactly two decimal places in money answers.
!Watch Out!
Keep digits in neat vertical columns to avoid alignment slips.

A clinic orders 24 boxes of medical gloves. Each box contains 125 pairs. How many pairs in total?

Everyday application of Division with Remainders in Context.
★Key Method & Rules
Division splits quantities into equal shares. Interpret remainders based on the real-world situation.

Round UP if packing items into boxes; round DOWN if counting complete sets.
✎Step-by-Step Example
An operative has 148 components. Each shipping crate holds 12 components. How many crates are needed?
  1. Divide: 148 / 12 = 12 remainder 4

✓Remember!
Always interpret the remainder: does the scenario need an extra crate or van?
!Watch Out!
Don't leave an exam answer as '12 r 4' when asked for full crates.

Fractions of Quantities: Unit & Non-Unit

Everyday application of Fractions of Quantities: Unit & Non-Unit.
★Key Method & Rules
To find a fraction of an amount: Divide by the bottom (denominator), then multiply by the top (numerator).

Amount / Denominator x Numerator = Fraction of amount.
✎Step-by-Step Example
Find 3/4 of £360 for an equipment deposit payment.
  1. Divide by 4: £360 / 4 = £90 (one quarter)
  2. Multiply by 3: £90 x 3 = £270
✓Remember!
Remember: 'Divide by the bottom, times by the top'.
!Watch Out!
Check your answer is smaller than the original amount for proper fractions.

Decimals: Ordering, Place Value & Rounding

Everyday application of Decimals: Ordering, Place Value & Rounding.
★Key Method & Rules
Decimals represent parts of a whole: tenths (0.1), hundredths (0.01), and thousandths (0.001).

To round to 1 decimal place: look at the hundredths digit. 5 or more rounds up; 4 or less stays.
✎Step-by-Step Example
Round 14.86 metres to 1 decimal place for a carpet fitting measurement.
  1. Look at second decimal place: 6 (hundredths)
  2. Since 6 >= 5, round the tenths up from 8 to 9
  3. Rounded = 14.9 metres
✓Remember!
Add trailing zeros when aligning decimals of different lengths (e.g. 3.40 and 3.25).
!Watch Out!
Money is always rounded to 2 decimal places.

Simple Percentages: 10%, 25%, 50% & 75%

Everyday application of Simple Percentages: 10%, 25%, 50% & 75%.
★Key Method & Rules
Key percentage fractions: 50% = half (/ 2); 25% = quarter (/ 4); 10% = divide by 10; 75% = 3/4.

Build other percentages from 10%: 20% = 10% x 2; 5% = half of 10%; 15% = 10% + 5%.
✎Step-by-Step Example
A sofa costs £480. In a seasonal sale it is reduced by 25%. Find the sale price.
  1. Find 25% (divide by 4): £480 / 4 = £120 discount
  2. Deduct discount: £480 - £120
  3. Sale price = £360.00
✓Remember!
Always read carefully: does the question ask for the DISCOUNT or the SALE PRICE?
!Watch Out!
10% is the master building block — find 10% by shifting digits one place right.

Simple Ratio: Sharing in 2 Parts

Everyday application of Simple Ratio: Sharing in 2 Parts.
★Key Method & Rules
Ratios compare parts of a whole. To share in a ratio, find the value of one part.

Add parts -> Total / Parts = 1 part -> Multiply by each ratio number.
✎Step-by-Step Example
Share £240 between two charity branches in the ratio 3 : 1.
  1. Total parts: 3 + 1 = 4 parts
  2. One part: £240 / 4 = £60
  3. Branch 1 (3 parts): 3 x £60 = £180
  4. Branch 2 (1 part): 1 x £60 = £60
✓Remember!
Always check the two shares add back up to the starting total (£180 + £60 = £240).
!Watch Out!
Keep the numbers in the same order as written in the question.

Everyday application of Simple Proportion & Scaling Recipes.
★Key Method & Rules
Direct proportion means if you double one amount, you double the other.

Find the quantity for 1 portion/item (unitary method), then multiply by the target number.
✎Step-by-Step Example
A soup recipe for 4 people needs 600 ml of stock. How much stock is needed for 6 people?

Everyday application of Estimation & Checking Calculations.
★Key Method & Rules
Estimation checks whether an answer is sensible by rounding numbers to the nearest 10, 100, or whole number.

Round numbers before calculating: 49 x 21 ~ 50 x 20 = 1,000.

12-Hour & 24-Hour Clock Time

Everyday application of 12-Hour & 24-Hour Clock Time.
★Key Method & Rules
24-hour time uses 4 digits from 00:00 to 23:59 without am or pm. For times after 12:00, add 12 to the pm hour.

To convert pm to 24h: add 12 to hour (3:15 pm = 15:15). To convert 24h to pm: subtract 12 (19:40 = 7:40 pm).
✎Step-by-Step Example
A shift starts at 08:30 and finishes at 16:45. How long was the shift?
  1. Count hours from 08:30 to 16:30 = 8 hours
  2. Count remaining minutes from 16:30 to 16:45 = 15 minutes
  3. Total duration = 8 hours 15 minutes
✓Remember!
There are 60 minutes in an hour, NOT 100! 8 hours 15 mins is not 8.15 hours.
!Watch Out!
00:00 is midnight; 12:00 is midday.

Perimeter of Rectangles & Simple Shapes

Everyday application of Perimeter of Rectangles & Simple Shapes.
★Key Method & Rules
Perimeter is the total boundary distance around the outside of a 2D shape.

Rectangle perimeter = 2 x (length + width) or sum of all 4 sides.
✎Step-by-Step Example
A rectangular garden is 9 metres long and 6 metres wide. Find the total fencing needed to enclose it.

  3. Perimeter = 30 metres
✓Remember!
Don't forget to include all 4 sides of a rectangle, not just the two labelled sides.
!Watch Out!
Perimeter is measured in simple units (m, cm), NOT square units.

Recognising 2D & 3D Shapes & Angles

Everyday application of Recognising 2D & 3D Shapes & Angles.
★Key Method & Rules
2D shapes: square, rectangle, triangle, circle, pentagon, hexagon. 3D shapes: cube, cuboid, cylinder, sphere, cone, pyramid.

Right angle = 90° (square corner). Straight line = 180°. Full turn = 360°.
✎Step-by-Step Example
Identify the 3D shape that has 6 rectangular faces, 12 edges, and 8 vertices.
  1. 6 rectangular faces = Cuboid (rectangular prism)
  2. A cube has 6 square faces; a cuboid has rectangular faces
