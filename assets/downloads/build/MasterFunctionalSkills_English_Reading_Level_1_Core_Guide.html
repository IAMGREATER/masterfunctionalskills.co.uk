Pearson Edexcel Level 1 Reading • Specification Overview

▶Pearson Edexcel Level 1 Reading Assessment Format

Examination Structure: The Pearson Edexcel Functional Skills English Level 1 Reading paper is an externally assessed, 60-minute examination worth 30 marks.

Candidates must read and comprehend two to three non-fiction texts drawn from real-world contexts (such as workplace safety notices, community leaflets, operational emails, news articles, and web pages).

  • Question Types: Multiple-choice questions, straightforward retrieval, explaining vocabulary in context, identifying organizational features, and distinguishing fact from opinion.
  • Timing Strategy: Aim for approximately 2 minutes per mark. Spend 10–12 minutes reading the source texts carefully before tackling the questions.
  • No Dictionaries: Dictionaries and spellcheckers are not permitted during the examination.
✓Golden Reading Rule
Always re-read the specific paragraph referenced in the question before selecting your answer.
!Examiner Warning
Never rely on personal background knowledge — every answer must be evidenced directly from the text.

Reading Topic 1: Fact or Opinion

Editorial column and news articles balancing verified objective facts against editorial opinions.
★DfE Criteria & Core Concept

Curriculum Standard:
• L1.9 / L2.9: Identify and distinguish between fact and opinion in workplace and informative texts.
• L2.14: Recognise bias and determine whether statements represent verifiable truth or subjective interpretation.

Key Concept: A fact is verifiable using data, records, dates, measurements, or official statistics. If a statement can be checked against records and proven true or false, it is a fact — even if you personally disagree with it.

✓Golden Rule
Opinions reflect feelings, beliefs, interpretations, or recommendations. Look out for evaluative adjectives ('superb', 'appalling', 'vital') and modal verbs ('should', 'must', 'ought to').
!Examiner Trap
Just because a senior doctor or consultant says 'This is the best ward management app available', that does not make it a fact. It remains an informed professional opinion.

▶Source Text • NHS Healthcare Compliance
Extract • Hospital Ward Inspection Report Analysis:

Read the extract from the hospital audit report:
'Ward 4 admitted 142 patients in October. The nursing staff provided exceptional, heartwarming care throughout the winter surge. Patient discharge waiting times exceeded 3 hours.'
(a) Identify one fact.
(b) Identify one opinion.
(c) Explain why the opinion cannot be verified as an objective fact.
▶Tutor Step-by-Step Analysis Strategy
  1. Step 1 (Identify the verifiable factual statement): Option A: 'Ward 4 admitted 142 patients in October' OR Option B: 'Patient discharge waiting times exceeded 3 hours.' Both can be verified from hospital admission logs. [1 Mark (A1 Fact)]
  2. Step 2 (Identify the subjective opinion): 'The nursing staff provided exceptional, heartwarming care throughout the winter surge.' [1 Mark (A1 Opinion)]
  3. Step 3 (Explain why the opinion is unverifiable): 'Exceptional' and 'heartwarming' are evaluative value judgments based on subjective personal feelings rather than clinical numerical metrics. [1 Mark (A1 Justification)]

Reading Topic 2: Words in Context & Meaning

Examining detailed dictionary entries and contextual definitions using a magnifier.
★DfE Criteria & Core Concept

Curriculum Standard:
• L1.10 / L2.10: Identify meanings of words and phrases and determine how meaning is conveyed.
• L2.11: Understand and explain implicit and explicit meanings across extended texts.

Key Concept: Never define a word in isolation. Look at the sentences before and after the target word to see how it operates within the passage's vocational situation.

✓Golden Rule
Literal words express their exact dictionary definition. Figurative words create comparisons that must be translated into plain English.
!Examiner Trap
A word like 'critical' can mean 'finding fault' or 'of crucial clinical importance'. Always select the definition that fits the vocational scenario.

Reading Topic 4: Presentational Features

Magazine editorial layout showcasing headlines, callout boxes, subheadings, and formatting devices.
★DfE Criteria & Core Concept

Curriculum Standard:
• L1.12 / L2.12: Recognise and explain how presentational features aid understanding and convey information.
• L2.13: Analyse how formatting, organizational signposts, and visual features support the writer's purpose.

Key Concept: You never receive marks simply for naming a feature (e.g. 'bullet points'). You must explain WHAT that feature achieves for the reader in that specific text.

✓Golden Rule
A main heading states the overarching topic or hooks interest; subheadings break down complex multi-step processes into scannable chunks.
!Examiner Trap
Alliteration, rhetorical questions, and metaphors are language techniques. Bold fonts, tables, bullet points, and headers are presentational features.

Reading Topic 7: Language Devices & Rhetorical Techniques

Presentation speech delivery with rhetorical devices, stage directions, and audience engagement.
★DfE Criteria & Core Concept

Curriculum Standard:
• L1.14 / L2.14: Identify language techniques and explain how they influence the reader.
• L2.15: Analyse rhetorical devices used to persuade, inform, critique, or advocate in workplace communications.

Key Concept: To score full marks on language questions in Pearson Edexcel, follow the 3-step formula: 1. Technique Name -> 2. Direct Quote -> 3. Impact on reader thoughts/feelings.

✓Golden Rule

!Examiner Trap
Writing 'it catches the reader's eye and makes them read on' is the #1 reason candidates fail language questions.

★DfE Criteria & Core Concept

Curriculum Standard:
• L2.14: Recognise bias and determine whether arguments are balanced or one-sided.
• L2.16: Trace lines of argument and evaluate whether conclusions are supported by rigorous evidence.

Key Concept: A balanced, objective text presents both advantages and disadvantages, citing opposing perspectives before responding with evidence. A biased text omits counter-evidence entirely.

!Examiner Trap

Essential Glossary of Reading & Comprehension Terminology

Audience: The specific group of readers a text is written for (e.g. staff, patients, general public).

Bias: A one-sided viewpoint that favors one perspective over another, often lacking balance.

Caption: A brief text explanation placed beneath an image or diagram.

Context: The surrounding words, sentences, or background situation that gives a word or text its meaning.

Fact: A verifiable piece of information that can be proven true by records, measurements, or evidence.

Headline / Heading: The main title of an article or document designed to state the subject clearly.

Implicit Meaning: Information that is suggested or hinted at rather than directly stated.

Informative Text: A text whose main purpose is to give clear, factual information to the reader.

Layout Feature: A visual or structural element (columns, bullet points, boxes) that organizes content.

Opinion: A personal belief, attitude, or feeling that cannot be verified as an objective fact.

Persuasive Text: A text designed to convince the reader to take action or agree with a specific viewpoint.

Purpose: The main reason why the author wrote the text (to inform, instruct, explain, or persuade).

Retrieval: Finding and extracting specific facts, figures, or dates directly from a text.

Subheading: A smaller heading that divides an article or document into clear sections.

Tone: The mood, feeling, or attitude conveyed by the writer's choice of words.
