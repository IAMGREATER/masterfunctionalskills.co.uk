Golden Rules for Weight & Mass

Golden Rule 1: The 1,000 Factor for Mass

1 tonne = 1,000 kg • 1 kg = 1,000 g • 1 g = 1,000 mg. Always multiply by 1,000 to go down a unit and divide by 1,000 to go up.

Golden Rule / Method: Tonnes × 1000 → Kilograms × 1000 → Grams
Golden Rule 2: Gross Weight vs Net Weight vs Tare Weight

Gross Weight = Total weight (packaging + goods). Tare Weight = Weight of empty container/vehicle. Net Weight = Goods only (Gross - Tare).

Golden Rule / Method: Gross Weight = Net Goods + Tare Packaging

Core Skill 2: Skill 2: Vehicle Payload, Tare Weight & Safe Working Loads (SWL)

Checking lift and van weight limits including pallets, cages, and drivers.

  • Total Loaded Pallet Weight: (Number of Boxes × Weight per Box) + Empty Wooden Pallet Weight.
  • Maximum Number of Crates on a Lift: (Max Safe Load − Operator/Cage Weight) ÷ Weight of 1 Crate (always round DOWN to whole crates for safety!).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Skill 3: Weight-Based Dosage & Catering Portion Calculations

Applying mg/kg clinical rules and per-kilogram cooking times.

Two high-frequency exam applications of weight:

  1. Clinical Dosage by Body Mass: If a drug requires 15 mg per kg of body weight for a 64 kg patient: 64 × 15 = 960 mg.
  2. Roasting Time Rules: "Cook for 20 minutes per 500 g plus 25 minutes extra." For a 2.5 kg (2,500 g) joint: there are five 500 g blocks → (5 × 20) + 25 = 125 mins (2 hrs 5 mins).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1/2)

Vocational Context: Logistics & Freight Transport

A wooden freight pallet weighs 24 kg when empty. It is loaded with 45 boxes of clinical supplies. Each box has a mass of 800 grams.
(a) Calculate the total gross weight of the loaded pallet in kilograms.
(b) The tail-lift has a safe working load of 75 kg. Can this pallet be safely lifted?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate total weight of boxes in grams
45 boxes × 800 g = 36,000 grams.
Criteria: M1: Multi-item mass multiplication
Step 2 Convert box mass to kilograms
36,000 g ÷ 1,000 = 36 kg.
Criteria: M1: Grams to kilograms conversion
Step 3 Calculate gross pallet weight and evaluate limit
Gross weight = 36 kg (boxes) + 24 kg (pallet) = 60 kg.
60 kg < 75 kg. Yes, the tail-lift can safely lift the pallet.
Criteria: A1: Accurate gross weight and safety confirmation
Final Accredited Answer: (a) Gross Weight = 60 kg | (b) Yes, 60 kg is within the 75 kg safe working load.
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Notice how converting 36,000 g to 36 kg first makes adding the 24 kg pallet effortless.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2 Problem Solving)

Vocational Context: Hospital Portering Operations

A hospital porter needs to take 32 bags of soiled linen down in a service lift.
• Each linen bag weighs 12.5 kg.
• The porter uses a metal roll-cage that weighs 35 kg when empty.
• The porter himself weighs 82 kg and rides in the lift with the cage.
• The service lift has a maximum weight limit of 0.35 tonnes.

Can the porter transport all 32 bags in just 2 lift trips? Show full working.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Convert maximum lift limit from tonnes to kilograms
0.35 tonnes × 1,000 = 350 kg maximum per trip.
Criteria: M1: Tonnes to kg conversion (350 kg)
Step 2 Subtract porter and empty roll-cage weight to find max linen weight per trip
Porter + Cage = 82 kg + 35 kg = 117 kg.
Max linen weight per trip = 350 kg - 117 kg = 233 kg.
Criteria: M1: Subtracting tare/operator weight
Step 3 Calculate maximum bags per trip (or weight of 16 bags per trip)
16 bags per trip × 12.5 kg = 200 kg of linen per trip (or 233 ÷ 12.5 = 18.64 → max 18 bags per trip).
Criteria: M1: Bag weight comparison per trip
Step 4 Conclude whether 2 trips are sufficient
Since 200 kg ≤ 233 kg (or 2 × 18 = 36 bags ≥ 32 bags), yes, he can transport all 32 bags in 2 trips (e.g. 16 bags per trip = 317 kg total weight).
Criteria: A1: Correct conclusion supported by exact figures
Final Accredited Answer: Yes (16 bags per trip weighs 200 kg + 117 kg porter/cage = 317 kg per trip, which is under the 350 kg limit).
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Always remember to subtract the weight of the porter and the empty cage on EVERY trip!

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Adding Grams and Kilograms Directly
Adding 24 kg + 36,000 g = 36,024 is an error caused by failing to convert to common units.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always convert to the same unit before adding.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A parcel courier van can carry a maximum payload of 1.2 tonnes. The driver has loaded 950 kg of parcels. Can a further 320 kg parcel be loaded without exceeding the limit?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Max payload = 1.2 tonnes = 1,200 kg.
2. Current + new parcel = 950 + 320 = 1,270 kg.
3. 1,270 kg > 1,200 kg. No, it exceeds the limit by 70 kg.
Final Correct Answer: No, it exceeds the payload limit by 70 kg (1,270 kg > 1,200 kg).
Examiner Tip: Convert 1.2 tonnes to 1,200 kg before comparing.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
A catering pack contains 2.4 kg of grated cheese. A chef uses 65 grams of cheese on each pizza. How many pizzas can the chef top from one pack?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Convert 2.4 kg to grams: 2.4 × 1,000 = 2,400 g.
2. Divide by 65 g: 2,400 ÷ 65 = 36.92... → 36 full pizzas.
Final Correct Answer: 36 full pizzas
Examiner Tip: Round down to 36 because there is not enough cheese left for a 37th pizza.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A veterinary antibiotic is prescribed at a daily dose of 20 mg per kg of body weight, split into 2 equal doses (morning and evening). Calculate the mass of a single evening dose in grams for a dog weighing 35 kg.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Total daily dose = 35 kg × 20 mg = 700 mg.
2. Single evening dose = 700 mg ÷ 2 = 350 mg.
3. Convert mg to grams: 350 ÷ 1,000 = 0.35 grams.
Final Correct Answer: 0.35 g (350 mg)
Examiner Tip: Read carefully: the question asks for a SINGLE dose in GRAMS (divide mg by 1,000).
