Length & Dimensions Pearson Edexcel: L1.18 • L2.20
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (Warm-Up) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Level 1 Metric Length Subtraction) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
An electrician has a 15-metre drum of armoured cable.
She cuts two lengths measuring 4.65 metres and 680 centimetres.
How many metres of cable remain on the drum?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Convert 680 cm to metres: 680 ÷ 100 = 6.8 m; Total cut = 4.65 + 6.80 = 11.45 m [M1]
2. Remaining cable: 15.00 - 11.45 = 3.55 m [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 3.55 m (or 355 cm)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Subtracting 680 directly from 15 without converting cm to metres.
🔄 Quick Check: 3.55 m + 4.65 m + 6.80 m = 15.00 m.
Question 2 (Level 2 Precision Millimetre Stack Thickness) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A vertical storage rack holds 35 acrylic medical clipboards stacked flat on top of each other.
Each clipboard is 4.2 mm thick.
Calculate the total height of the stack in centimetres (cm).
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Total height in mm: 35 × 4.2 mm = 147 mm [M1]
2. Convert mm to cm (÷ 10): 147 ÷ 10 = 14.7 cm [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 14.7 cm
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Dividing by 100 (1.47 cm) instead of 10 when converting mm to cm.
🔄 Quick Check: 14.7 cm = 147 mm; 147 ÷ 35 = 4.2 mm.
Section B: Everyday & Workplace Maths Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Level 1 Ward Curtain Track Cut-List) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A hospital maintenance technician needs to cut 6 privacy curtain rails, each measuring 185 cm long.
Aluminium track is supplied in 4-metre lengths.
Offcuts cannot be joined together.

How many 4-metre lengths of aluminium track must be ordered?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Convert 4 m to 400 cm and find rails per length: 400 ÷ 185 = 2.16 → 2 full rails per 4 m length (30 cm offcut) [M1]
2. Divide 6 required rails by 2 rails per length: 6 ÷ 2 [P1]
3. State 3 lengths of 4 m track needed [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 3 lengths
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Adding 6 × 185 = 1,110 cm and dividing by 400 = 2.775 (which happens to round up to 3, but fails if 7 rails are needed).
🔄 Quick Check: 3 lengths × 2 rails per length = 6 full 185 cm rails.
Question 4 (Level 2 Corridor Equipment Bay Spacing with Gaps) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A ward manager wants to line up 5 mobile diagnostic trolleys along a wall measuring 4.5 metres long.
• Each trolley is 68 cm wide.
• Fire safety regulations require a minimum gap of 20 cm between adjacent trolleys, and at least 15 cm clearance at each end of the wall.

Calculate the total length required (in cm) and state how much spare wall length remains.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Width of 5 trolleys = 5 × 68 cm = 340 cm [M1]
2. 4 internal gaps between 5 trolleys = 4 × 20 cm = 80 cm; plus 2 end clearances = 2 × 15 cm = 30 cm → Total required = 340 + 80 + 30 = 450 cm [P1]
3. Compare with 4.5 m (450 cm) wall: 450 cm - 450 cm = 0 cm spare (fits with 0 cm remaining) [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 0 cm spare (Total required is exactly 450 cm / 4.5 m)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Counting 5 internal gaps between 5 trolleys instead of 4 internal gaps.
🔄 Quick Check: 15 + 68 + 20 + 68 + 20 + 68 + 20 + 68 + 20 + 68 + 15 = 450 cm.
Section C: Exam-Style Problem Solving Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Level 2 Timber Stud Partition Frame Costing) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A carpenter builds a rectangular stud partition frame measuring 4.2 m wide by 2.4 m high.
• The frame requires a top plate (4.2 m), a bottom plate (4.2 m), and 8 vertical studs (each 2.4 m high).
• Timber is sold in 2.4 m lengths at £4.80 each and 4.2 m lengths at £8.20 each.

Calculate the total length of timber used (in metres) and the total cost of the timber.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Top and bottom plates: 2 × 4.2 m = 8.4 m; 8 vertical studs: 8 × 2.4 m = 19.2 m [P1]
2. Total timber length: 8.4 + 19.2 = 27.6 metres [M1]
3. Cost of two 4.2 m lengths: 2 × £8.20 = £16.40; Cost of eight 2.4 m lengths: 8 × £4.80 = £38.40 [P1]
4. Total cost: £16.40 + £38.40 = £54.80 [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Length: 27.6 m | Total Cost: £54.80
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Counting only 2 vertical studs instead of all 8 vertical studs.
🔄 Quick Check: £16.40 + £38.40 = £54.80; 8.4 m + 19.2 m = 27.6 m.
Question 6 (Level 2 Wheelchair Ramp Gradient Compliance) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Building regulations state that a permanent wheelchair access ramp must have a maximum gradient of 1 : 12 (for every 1 cm of vertical rise, the horizontal run must be at least 12 cm).
• A clinic entrance step has a vertical rise of 45 cm.
• Furthermore, for any rise over 30 cm, a flat mid-ramp resting landing measuring 1.5 metres long must be added to the total horizontal length.
• The clinic forecourt has 6.8 metres of horizontal space available.

Is the 6.8-metre forecourt long enough to install a compliant ramp? Show full working in metres.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Calculate minimum horizontal ramp run: 45 cm × 12 = 540 cm [P1]
2. Convert 540 cm to metres: 540 ÷ 100 = 5.4 metres [M1]
3. Add mandatory 1.5 m resting landing (since 45 cm > 30 cm): 5.4 m + 1.5 m = 6.9 metres [P1]
4. Compare against 6.8 m forecourt: 6.9 m > 6.8 m (short by 0.1 m / 10 cm). No, the forecourt is not long enough. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: No (Requires 6.9 m including landing, which is 0.1 m / 10 cm longer than the 6.8 m forecourt)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Forgetting to include the mandatory 1.5 m resting landing required for rises over 30 cm.
🔄 Quick Check: 5.4 m + 1.5 m = 6.9 m > 6.8 m.
