Perimeter & Compound Shapes Pearson Edexcel: E3.8 • L1.22 • L2.24
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (Warm-Up) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Level 1 Rectangular Perimeter) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A rectangular hospital sensory garden measures 18.5 metres long by 11.2 metres wide.
Calculate the total perimeter of the garden.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Apply Perimeter = 2 × (Length + Width): 2 × (18.5 + 11.2) = 2 × 29.7 [M1]
2. Evaluate: 59.4 m [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 59.4 m
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Multiplying 18.5 × 11.2 (= 207.2 m² area) instead of adding all four sides.
🔄 Quick Check: 18.5 + 11.2 + 18.5 + 11.2 = 59.4 m.
Question 2 (Level 2 Finding Missing Width from Known Perimeter) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A rectangular physiotherapy exercise mat has a total perimeter of 16.4 metres.
The length of the mat is 5.4 metres.
Calculate the width of the mat.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Subtract both lengths from perimeter: 16.4 - (2 × 5.4) = 16.4 - 10.8 = 5.6 m (or halve perimeter: 8.2 - 5.4) [M1]
2. Divide remaining length by 2: 5.6 ÷ 2 = 2.8 m [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 2.8 m
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Subtracting only one length (16.4 - 5.4 = 11.0 m) instead of two lengths.
🔄 Quick Check: 2 × (5.4 + 2.8) = 2 × 8.2 = 16.4 m.
Section B: Everyday & Workplace Maths Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Level 1/2 L-Shaped Ward Floor Perimeter) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
The diagram below shows an L-shaped clinical reception floor.
Work out the missing side lengths a and b, and calculate the total perimeter of the floor.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Find missing vertical side a: 10.2 m - 4.8 m = 5.4 m [M1]
2. Find missing horizontal side b: 12.5 m - 5.5 m = 7.0 m [M1]
3. Sum all 6 sides: 12.5 + 4.8 + 5.5 + 5.4 + 7.0 + 10.2 = 45.4 m [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 45.4 m (a = 5.4 m, b = 7.0 m)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Adding only the 4 printed numbers (12.5 + 4.8 + 5.5 + 10.2 = 33.0 m) and omitting sides a and b.
🔄 Quick Check: Outer bounding box perimeter = 2 × (12.5 + 10.2) = 2 × 22.7 = 45.4 m.
Question 4 (Level 2 Skirting Board Packs with Doorway Deduction) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A joiner is fitting timber skirting board around a rectangular treatment room measuring 6.4 m long by 4.2 m wide.
• The room has two doorways measuring 0.9 m and 1.1 m wide (which do not require skirting board).
• Skirting board is sold in 2.4-metre lengths (offcuts can be used on straight walls).

How many 2.4-metre lengths of skirting board must the joiner purchase?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Full room perimeter: 2 × (6.4 + 4.2) = 21.2 m [M1]
2. Deduct both doorways: 21.2 - (0.9 + 1.1) = 19.2 m net skirting required [P1]
3. Divide by 2.4 m pack length: 19.2 ÷ 2.4 = 8 lengths [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 8 lengths
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Forgetting to subtract the two doorway widths (21.2 ÷ 2.4 = 8.83 → 9 lengths).
🔄 Quick Check: 8 × 2.4 m = 19.2 m + 2.0 m doorways = 21.2 m.
Section C: Exam-Style Problem Solving Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Level 2 Semi-Circular Courtyard Edging Cost) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A hospital wellbeing courtyard is shaped as a rectangle with a semi-circle joined to one end:
• The rectangular section has two long parallel sides of 15.0 metres each, and one straight end wall of 8.0 metres.
• The opposite end is a semi-circle of diameter 8.0 metres.
• Flexible steel lawn edging is installed around the entire outer boundary.
• Edging is sold in 5-metre rolls at £24.50 per roll. Use π = 3.14.

Calculate the total cost of the steel edging rolls required.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Curved semi-circular arc length (1/2 × π × d): 0.5 × 3.14 × 8.0 = 12.56 m [P1]
2. Add the 3 straight outer sides: 12.56 + 15.0 + 15.0 + 8.0 = 50.56 m total perimeter [M1]
3. Calculate 5 m rolls needed: 50.56 ÷ 5 = 10.112 → round UP to 11 whole rolls [P1]
4. Total cost: 11 × £24.50 = £269.50 [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: £269.50 (11 rolls required for 50.56 m perimeter)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Including the internal 8.0 m dashed join line inside the courtyard, or rounding 10.112 rolls down to 10 rolls.
🔄 Quick Check: 10 rolls = 50 m (short by 0.56 m). 11 rolls × £24.50 = £269.50.
Question 6 (Level 2 Security Fencing Posts & Panels Budget Verification) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A medical oxygen storage compound is a rectangle measuring 14 m long by 10 m wide.
• Security fencing is erected around all four sides, except for a 4-metre security gate opening on one wall.
• Fence panels are 2 metres wide and cost £42.00 each.
• A steel fence post (costing £18.00 each) is required at both ends of the fence and between every adjacent panel (so the number of posts is 1 more than the number of panels).
• The security gate itself costs £310.00.

The estates manager has a total materials budget of £1,700.00. Is the budget sufficient?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Total perimeter = 2 × (14 + 10) = 48 m; Net fencing length = 48 m - 4 m gate = 44 m [P1]
2. Number of 2 m panels = 44 ÷ 2 = 22 panels; Number of posts = 22 + 1 = 23 posts [M1]
3. Calculate total cost: (22 × £42.00) + (23 × £18.00) + £310.00 = £924.00 + £414.00 + £310.00 = £1,648.00 [P1]
4. Compare and conclude: £1,648.00 ≤ £1,700.00. Yes, the budget is sufficient with £52.00 remaining. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Yes, sufficient (Total cost is £1,648.00, leaving £52.00 under the £1,700 budget)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Using 22 posts instead of 23 posts, or forgetting to deduct the 4 m gate before dividing by 2 m.
🔄 Quick Check: £924 + £414 + £310 = £1,648; £1,700 - £1,648 = £52 surplus.
