Welcome to the Scatter Graphs, Correlation & Lines of Best Fit blueprint. In Pearson Edexcel Level 2, scatter graphs test your ability to distinguish mathematical correlation from real-world contextual relationships, identify anomalous outlier points, and construct accurate lines of best fit to interpolate values. Review our high-visibility calculation cards below to see how full marks are secured!

Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • L2.32 – Draw and interpret scatter diagrams, recognise positive, negative, and zero correlation, identify outliers, and draw/use a line of best fit.

Golden Rules for Scatter Graphs, Correlation & Lines of Best Fit

Golden Rule 1: Positive (Upward) • Negative (Downward) • Zero (Random)

Positive Correlation: As X increases, Y increases (points slope upwards left-to-right). Negative Correlation: As X increases, Y decreases (points slope downwards left-to-right). Zero Correlation: No relationship (random cloud).

Golden Rule / Method: Positive = Both Increase • Negative = One Up, One Down • Zero = No Pattern
Golden Rule 2: Line of Best Fit Must Be ONE Straight Ruled Line (Never Join Dots!)

Use a clear ruler to draw a single straight line through the center of the data trend, with roughly equal numbers of points above and below (ignoring any extreme outlier). Never force it through (0, 0) unless the points point there!

Golden Rule / Method: One Straight Ruled Line • Balance Points Above/Below • Ignore Outliers

Core Skill 1: Stating Correlation Type vs Contextual Relationship

How to answer "State the type of correlation" vs "Describe the relationship".

  • "State the type of correlation": Write Positive correlation, Negative correlation, or No correlation.
  • "Describe the relationship": Link both real-world variables in a sentence: "As outdoor temperature increases, daily heating gas consumption decreases (negative correlation)."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Drawing & Using the Line of Best Fit for Interpolation

Showing dashed reading lines on the grid to secure method marks.

  1. Draw your straight Line of Best Fit across the data range with a ruler.
  2. Locate the given X-value on the horizontal axis, rule a dashed line straight UP to your line of best fit, and straight ACROSS to the Y-axis.
  3. Ignore Outliers: If one point sits far away from the main trend cluster, circle it as an outlier and ignore it when positioning your line of best fit.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Why Extrapolation Beyond the Data Range Is Unreliable

Explaining the danger of predicting far outside the plotted points.

If a scatter graph plots car ages from 1 to 8 years against value, using the line of best fit to predict the value of a 20-year-old car is unreliable because:

  • 20 years lies outside the range of the collected data (extrapolation).
  • The linear trend may not continue (e.g. the line would eventually predict a negative car value!).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2)

Vocational Context: Fleet Fuel Efficiency Optimization

A fleet manager plots cargo payload (kg) on the X-axis against fuel consumption (litres per 100 km) on the Y-axis for 10 delivery vans.
(a) What type of correlation is shown if fuel consumption rises as payload increases?
(b) Using the line of best fit, a 500 kg payload corresponds to 8.2 litres per 100 km. Estimate the total fuel required for a 600 km round trip carrying a 500 kg load.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Identify & State Correlation Type
Trend Assessment
Positive Correlation
As payload increases, fuel consumption per 100 km increases proportionally.
Criteria: A1: Correct correlation (Positive)
Step 2 Interpolate Fuel Rate at 500 kg Payload
Line of Best Fit Reading
At 500 kg payload → 8.2 litres per 100 km
Criteria: M1: Rate extraction
Step 3 Scale Up to Total Trip Distance (600 km)
Multi-Unit Distance Scaling
600 km ÷ 100 km = 6 units
6 × 8.2 litres = 49.2 litres
Criteria: A1: Accurate total fuel (49.2 litres)
Final Accredited Answer: (a) Positive correlation | (b) 49.2 litres
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
600 km is 6 lots of 100 km, so multiply the 100 km rate (8.2 L) by 6.
Sonya's Examiner Tip: When examiners ask you to describe the relationship between two variables, do not just write the single word “positive”. State both: “There is a positive correlation: as vehicle payload increases, the fuel consumption increases.” This guarantees both the mathematical and contextual marks!
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2)

Vocational Context: Hospital Renewable Energy Auditing

An energy manager records daily sunshine hours and solar electricity generated (kWh) across 7 days:
• (2 hrs, 30 kWh), (3 hrs, 45 kWh), (4 hrs, 60 kWh), (5 hrs, 12 kWh), (6 hrs, 90 kWh), (7 hrs, 105 kWh), (8 hrs, 120 kWh).
(a) Identify the coordinates of the outlier day (when an inverter fault occurred).
(b) Ignoring the outlier, use the linear trend to estimate the electricity generated on a day with 5.5 hours of sunshine, and calculate the financial saving if electricity costs £0.24 per kWh.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Identify Anomaly / Outlier Point
Outlier Detection
Outlier Point = (5 hrs, 12 kWh)
At 5 hours, production collapsed to 12 kWh instead of the expected ~75 kWh due to equipment fault.
Criteria: A1: Correct outlier identification (5 hrs, 12 kWh)
Step 2 Interpolate Linear Output at 5.5 Hours Sunshine
Trend Rate Calculation
Consistent gradient rate: 30 kWh ÷ 2 hrs = 15 kWh per sunshine hour
5.5 hrs × 15 kWh/hr = 82.5 kWh
Criteria: M1 / A1: Accurate interpolation (82.5 kWh)
Step 3 Compute Financial Savings at £0.24 per kWh
Financial Electricity Valuation
82.5 kWh × £0.24 = £19.80
Criteria: A1: Accurate financial saving (£19.80)
Final Accredited Answer: (a) Outlier = (5 hrs, 12 kWh) | (b) 82.5 kWh generated, saving £19.80
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Always exclude the outlier before finding the line of best fit!
Subi's Assessor Pro-Tip: In exam questions featuring an outlier, candidates often mistakenly bend their line of best fit to pass near the anomalous point. Never do this! Mark schemes award marks for drawing a straight ruler line following the valid data cluster, ignoring anomalous faults entirely.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Joining the Plotted Points Dot-to-Dot
Connecting scatter points with a zigzag line instead of drawing ONE straight line with a ruler scores 0 marks for the line of best fit.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Use a ruler to draw a single straight line through the middle of the cluster.
Examiner Pitfall: Forcing the Line of Best Fit Through (0, 0)
A negative correlation line (or a positive line with a baseline offset) does NOT pass through the origin (0, 0).
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Only pass through (0, 0) if the plotted points clearly aim at the origin.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A scatter graph plots the age of second-hand delivery vans (years) against their resale value (£).
(a) State the type of correlation you would expect.
(b) Describe the relationship in words.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Type: Negative correlation.
2. Relationship: As the age of the van increases, its resale value decreases.
Final Correct Answer: (a) Negative correlation | (b) As van age increases, resale value decreases
Examiner Tip: State both the statistical name (Negative) and the real-world meaning.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
On a scatter graph of Patient Age (X-axis) against Blood Pressure (Y-axis), the line of best fit passes through (30 years, 118 mmHg) and (60 years, 136 mmHg). Use linear interpolation to estimate the blood pressure for a 45-year-old patient.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. 45 years is the exact midpoint between 30 and 60 years.
2. Midpoint Y = (118 + 136) ÷ 2 = 254 ÷ 2 = 127 mmHg.
Final Correct Answer: 127 mmHg
Examiner Tip: Since 45 is halfway between 30 and 60, the reading on the straight line of best fit is halfway between 118 and 136.
Exam Practice Question 3 2 Marks
A scatter diagram plots staff height (cm) against their monthly overtime hours for 25 nurses. The points are scattered randomly across the grid. State the type of correlation and whether height can be used to predict overtime.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Zero (or no) correlation.
2. No, height cannot be used to predict overtime hours because there is no relationship between the variables.
Final Correct Answer: No (zero) correlation; height cannot predict overtime
Examiner Tip: When points form a random cloud with no upward or downward slope, state "No correlation".
