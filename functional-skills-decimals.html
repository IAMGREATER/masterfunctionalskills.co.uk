Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • L1.10 / L2.10 – Read, write, order and compare decimals up to three decimal places.
  • L1.11 / L2.10 – Perform the four operations with decimals up to two decimal places in practical contexts.

Golden Rules for Decimals: Four Operations & Place Value

Golden Rule 1: Always Write Money to 2 Decimal Places

In Functional Skills, writing £14.5 or £14.500 loses the final accuracy mark. Currency in pounds must ALWAYS have exactly 2 decimal places (£14.50).

Golden Rule / Method: Mandatory: £14.50 (NOT £14.5)
Golden Rule 2: Dividing by Decimals: Shift the Decimal Point

Never divide by a decimal divisor. Multiply both numbers by 10, 100, or 1,000 to turn the divisor into a whole number before using the bus stop.

Golden Rule / Method: £18 ÷ 0.25 = £1,800 ÷ 25 = 72

Core Skill 1: Skill 1: Aligning Decimals for Column Addition & Subtraction

Preventing positional errors during decimal arithmetic.

To calculate £142.60 - £58.45:

142.60
- 58.45
£84.15

Always pad whole numbers or 1-decimal-place numbers with trailing zeros (e.g. write £19 as 19.00) before subtracting.

Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Skill 2: Multiplying Decimals & Restoring Decimal Places

How to multiply rates and weights with 1, 2, or 3 decimal places.

  1. Ignore the decimal points and multiply the digits as whole numbers (e.g. for 2.45 × 1.2, calculate 245 × 12 = 2,940).
  2. Count total digits after the decimal point in the original numbers (.45 has 2 digits + .2 has 1 digit = 3 digits total).
  3. Move the decimal point 3 places left from the end of 2940 → 2.940 = 2.94.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Skill 3: Dividing by a Decimal (Equivalent Fraction Scaling)

Turning any decimal division into a straightforward whole-number division.

Decimal Division Scale Both Numbers By Equivalent Whole Division
14.4 ÷ 0.6 × 10 (1 d.p. in divisor) 144 ÷ 6 = 24
4.5 ÷ 0.15 × 100 (2 d.p. in divisor) 450 ÷ 15 = 30
3.6 ÷ 0.009 × 1,000 (3 d.p. in divisor) 3,600 ÷ 9 = 400

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1/2 Non-Calculator)

Vocational Context: Clinical Pharmacy Fluid Dispensing

A clinical pharmacist has 4.5 litres of sterile saline solution. She dispenses the solution into dose ampoules containing 0.15 litres each.
How many complete dose ampoules can she fill?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Set up the decimal division
4.5 ÷ 0.15
Criteria: M1: Division setup
Step 2 Multiply dividend and divisor by 100 to remove decimal in divisor
4.5 × 100 = 450
0.15 × 100 = 15
Calculation becomes: 450 ÷ 15
Criteria: M1: Decimal shift method
Step 3 Perform integer division
450 ÷ 15 = 30 ampoules
Criteria: A1: Accurate whole number answer
Final Accredited Answer: 30 complete dose ampoules
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Notice how shifting the decimal point by two places turns an intimidating decimal problem into basic mental arithmetic (450 ÷ 15 = 30).
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2 Problem Solving)

Vocational Context: Healthcare Transport Expenses

A pathology courier drives 42.8 miles on Monday, 38.65 miles on Tuesday, and 53.55 miles on Wednesday.
• Her van uses 0.08 litres of diesel per mile.
• Diesel costs £1.45 per litre.
• The hospital reimburses her at a flat rate of £0.15 per mile.

Does the hospital mileage reimbursement cover her actual diesel cost for these three days? Show exact figures.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate total mileage across the 3 days
42.80 + 38.65 + 53.55 = 135.00 miles.
Criteria: M1: Accurate decimal addition of miles
Step 2 Calculate total diesel used and actual fuel cost
Diesel used = 135 × 0.08 = 10.8 litres.
Actual fuel cost = 10.8 × £1.45 = £15.66.
Criteria: M1: Fuel consumption and cost calculation
Step 3 Calculate hospital mileage reimbursement
Reimbursement = 135 miles × £0.15 = £20.25.
Criteria: M1: Reimbursement calculation
Step 4 Compare reimbursement against actual cost
£20.25 > £15.66 (surplus of £4.59). Yes, the reimbursement covers her diesel cost with £4.59 to spare.
Criteria: A1: Correct decision with both exact currency figures
Final Accredited Answer: Yes (Reimbursement is £20.25 vs actual diesel cost of £15.66, leaving a £4.59 surplus).
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Align the decimal points carefully when adding 42.80 + 38.65 + 53.55 so you spot that the hundredths (.80 + .65 + .55 = 2.00) sum to a clean whole number (135 miles).

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Dropping the Trailing Zero at the End of Currency
Writing £3.4 on an invoice instead of £3.40 will forfeit the accuracy mark on Pearson Edexcel marking schemes.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always review money answers and ensure exactly two digits follow the decimal point.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A parcel courier charges £6.50 for the first kilogram, plus £1.85 for each additional kilogram. Calculate the cost to deliver a 7 kg package.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Additional kilograms = 7 - 1 = 6 kg.
2. Additional cost = 6 × £1.85 = £11.10.
3. Total cost = £6.50 + £11.10 = £17.60.
Final Correct Answer: £17.60
Examiner Tip: Don't forget to subtract the first 1 kg before multiplying by £1.85.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
Without a calculator, work out 18.9 ÷ 0.07.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Multiply both numbers by 100 to clear the 2 decimal places in 0.07: 1,890 ÷ 7.
2. 1,890 ÷ 7 = 270.
Final Correct Answer: 270
Examiner Tip: Check using inverse multiplication: 270 × 0.07 = 18.9.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A laboratory technician has a 2.5 litre bottle of reagent. She uses 0.375 litres for Test A, 0.85 litres for Test B, and fills 4 sample tubes with 0.125 litres each. How much reagent remains in the bottle?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Sample tubes = 4 × 0.125 = 0.500 litres.
2. Total used = 0.375 + 0.850 + 0.500 = 1.725 litres.
3. Remaining = 2.500 - 1.725 = 0.775 litres.
Final Correct Answer: 0.775 litres
Examiner Tip: Pad 2.5 to 2.500 and 0.85 to 0.850 so all numbers have 3 decimal places.
