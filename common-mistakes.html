Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1 / L2 Reading Standards – Extract exact evidence, distinguish layout from language devices, and answer both halves of two-part questions.
  • L1 / L2 Writing SPaG & Format – Eliminate comma splices, apply full document formatting conventions, and maintain homophone and verb agreement accuracy.

Golden Rules for Top 10 High-Frequency English Exam Mistakes & Fixes

Golden Rule 1: Format Conventions Are Non-Negotiable in Writing

In Writing tasks, omitting standard format features (sender/recipient addresses and date in a formal letter; To/Subject header in an email; headline and subheadings in an article/report) immediately penalises your organisation mark.

Golden Rule / Technique: Letter: 2 Addresses + Date + Matched Sign-off | Report: Title + Subheadings
Golden Rule 2: Eliminate the Comma Splice Forever

Joining two complete standalone sentences with a comma alone is the #1 grammatical penalty in Pearson Edexcel Writing papers. Separate them with a full stop, link them with a semicolon, or add a coordinating conjunction ('and', 'but', 'so').

Golden Rule / Technique: Wrong: 'The shift ended, I went home.' -> Right: 'The shift ended, so I went home.'

Core Skill 1: The Top 5 Reading Paper Mistakes & How to Fix Them

Why candidates lose easy marks in Reading comprehension and analysis.

Common Reading Mistake Why It Loses Marks Examiner-Approved Fix
1. Confusing Language vs Layout Writing 'bullet points' or 'bold heading' when asked for a language feature scores 0. Language = words/phrases (metaphor, rule of three, rhetorical question). Layout/Presentation = visual formatting (bold, subheadings, boxes).
2. Vague Effect Statements Writing "It catches the reader's eye and makes them read on" is banned by mark schemes. State the specific emotion or thought triggered: "It provokes urgency about fire safety" or "It reassures nervous patients."
3. Using Personal Knowledge Giving real-world workplace advice that is not printed in the source text scores 0. Treat the source text as your only universe of facts. Every answer must be lifted or deduced directly from the text.
4. Ignoring the Second Half of a Prompt Naming a technique without giving the requested quotation or explanation drops 50% of the marks. Tick off each command verb in the question (e.g. Identify [✓] + Quote [✓] + Explain [✓]).
5. Isolated Text Summaries Describing Text A and Text B separately without linking them caps comparison marks. Use "Whereas Text A states..., Text B argues..." in every comparison sentence.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: The Top 5 Writing & SPaG Mistakes & How to Fix Them

Eliminate the grammatical and structural errors that cost candidates a pass in Writing.

  1. The Comma Splice: Joining two independent clauses with a comma ("The delivery arrived late, the stock was damaged."). Fix: Replace the comma with a full stop, semicolon, or ', and'.
  2. Mismatched Letter Salutations: Pairing "Dear Sir/Madam" with "Yours sincerely". Fix: Dear Sir/Madam → Yours faithfully; Dear Mr/Ms [Surname] → Yours sincerely.
  3. Homophone & Apostrophe Confusions: Mixing up their / there / they're, your / you're, or writing it's for possession ("the company changed it's policy" → should be its policy).
  4. Non-Standard Verb Forms: Writing "we could of gone" instead of "we could have gone", or "the staff was unhappy" / "all patients was seen" instead of "were".
  5. Ignoring Bullet Point Prompts: Every Writing task gives 3–4 bullet points telling you what to cover. Omitting even one bullet point caps your Content mark!
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: The 5-Minute Final Proofreading Protocol

A systematic four-pass check to rescue 3–5 SPaG marks at the end of every Writing exam.

Do not read your essay passively at the end of the exam. Scan through four targeted passes:

  • Pass 1 (Format Check): Have I included addresses/date (letter), To/Subject (email), or Headline/Subheadings (article/report)?
  • Pass 2 (Comma Splice Scan): Look at every comma you wrote. Is there a complete sentence on both sides of it? If yes, turn that comma into a full stop or semicolon!
  • Pass 3 (Capital Letters & Apostrophes): Check capital 'I', days of the week, months, street names, and check every it's / its.
  • Pass 4 (Double-Letter Spelling Traps): Verify high-frequency exam spellings: accommodation, necessary, recommend, opportunity, sincerely, disappointed, environment, immediately.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 2 Marks (Reading Technique Diagnostic)

Vocational Context: Reading Exam Answer Audit

An exam source text states: 'Over 92% of dental nurses reported back strain before ergonomic stools were introduced. Are you still risking your spine on outdated seating?'
The exam question asks: 'Give one language feature used in this extract and explain its effect. [2 Marks]'
A candidate writes: 'Bold font — it catches the reader's eye and makes them read on.'
Explain two reasons why this candidate scored 0 marks, and write a full-mark model answer.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Diagnose Mistake 1 (Presentational vs Language)
'Bold font' is a presentational/layout device, not a language feature. The candidate needed to choose the statistic ('92%') or the rhetorical question ('Are you still risking your spine...?').
Criteria: 1 Mark [Diagnostic 1]
Step 2 Diagnose Mistake 2 (Generic Effect Phrase) & Write Model Answer
'Catches the reader's eye and makes them read on' is a vague cliché that earns 0 marks. Model Answer: Rhetorical question ('Are you still risking your spine on outdated seating?') — directly challenges the reader to reflect on their own health and creates urgency to upgrade their seating.
Criteria: 1 Mark [Diagnostic 2 & Fix]
Final Accredited Response: Mistakes: 1. Named a layout feature (bold font) instead of a language feature. 2. Gave a vague cliché ('makes them read on'). | Full-Mark Answer: Rhetorical question ('Are you still risking your spine...?') — prompts dental staff to worry about their own posture and persuades them to switch to ergonomic stools.
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Whenever a question asks for a 'language feature', banned answers include bullet points, bold text, subheadings, pictures, and text boxes.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Writing SPaG Diagnostic)

Vocational Context: Workplace Health & Safety Letter Proofreading

Read this closing extract from a candidate's formal letter to an unnamed Council Environmental Health Officer (opened with 'Dear Sir/Madam'):
'The fire exits in warehouse B was blocked by wooden pallets, this is a serious hazard, the manager should of cleared there walkways immediately. Yours sincerely, J. Patel.'
Identify and correct four distinct errors in grammar, punctuation, spelling, or letter convention. [4 Marks]
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Fix Subject-Verb Agreement
'The fire exits... was blocked' -> plural subject ('exits') requires plural verb 'were blocked'.
Criteria: 1 Mark [Grammar Agreement]
Step 2 Eliminate Comma Splices
Replace the commas joining independent clauses with full stops or semicolons: '...wooden pallets. This is a serious hazard; therefore, the manager...'
Criteria: 1 Mark [Punctuation Control]
Step 3 Fix Non-Standard Modal & Homophone
'should of' -> 'should have'; 'there walkways' -> possessive 'their walkways'.
Criteria: 1 Mark [Spelling & Usage]
Step 4 Fix Formal Letter Sign-Off Convention
Because the letter opened with 'Dear Sir/Madam', the sign-off must be 'Yours faithfully', not 'Yours sincerely'.
Criteria: 1 Mark [Format Convention]
Final Accredited Response: 'The fire exits in Warehouse B were blocked by wooden pallets. This is a serious hazard; the manager should have cleared their walkways immediately. Yours faithfully, J. Patel.'
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
If a group of words can stand alone as a complete sentence, never join it to the next complete sentence with just a comma.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Using 'Yours Sincerely' with 'Dear Sir/Madam'
This classic error is penalised in every exam series. Remember the 'S' rule: never put two S's together (Sir + Sincerely = Wrong!).
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Named recipient (Dear Mr Smith) = Yours sincerely. Unnamed recipient (Dear Sir/Madam) = Yours faithfully.
Examiner Pitfall: Writing Under the Minimum Word Count in Writing Tasks
When a Level 2 task specifies 'Write 250–300 words', writing only 140 words severely restricts your ability to develop detailed points and caps your composition mark.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Aim squarely for the target word band by writing 4 well-developed paragraphs (Introduction, 2 Main Body Paragraphs, Conclusion).

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 3 Marks
Correct the three SPaG errors in this sentence:
'The doctors surgery was closed on wednesday, all emergency patients was directed to A&E.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Apostrophe of possession: 'doctors surgery' must be 'doctor's surgery' (or 'doctors' surgery') [1 Mark].
2. Capital letter for day of the week: 'wednesday' must be 'Wednesday' [1 Mark].
3. Comma splice and verb agreement: Replace the comma with a full stop (or '; therefore,') and change 'was directed' to 'were directed' [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: 'The doctor's surgery was closed on Wednesday. All emergency patients were directed to A&E.'
Examiner Tip: Always check plural subjects ('patients') to ensure they are paired with plural verbs ('were', not 'was').
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Identify and correct three errors in this workplace email sentence:
'Their is no excuse for arriving late, staff could of checked the train timetable on they're phones.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Homophone 1: 'Their is' should be 'There is' [1 Mark].
2. Comma splice & modal verb: Replace comma after 'late' with a full stop or 'because', and change 'could of' to 'could have' [1 Mark].
3. Homophone 2: 'they're phones' (they are) should be possessive 'their phones' [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: 'There is no excuse for arriving late; staff could have checked the train timetable on their phones.'
Examiner Tip: Test 'they're' by expanding it to 'they are' — 'on they are phones' makes no sense, proving you need 'their'.
Exam Practice Question 3 2 Marks
Read this short text:
'Warning: Forklift trucks operate in Aisle 6. Wear high-visibility jackets at all times.'
A student is asked to identify one language feature and writes: 'The word Warning is in bold.' Explain why this is wrong and name one correct language feature from the extract.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Why it is wrong: 'Bold' is a presentational/layout device, not a language feature [1 Mark].
2. Correct language feature: Imperative command verb ('Wear high-visibility jackets...') or direct, cautionary vocabulary ('Warning') [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Bold font is a presentational feature, not a language feature. A correct language feature is the imperative verb command ('Wear high-visibility jackets at all times').
Examiner Tip: Imperative verbs ('Wear', 'Keep', 'Do not enter') are the most common language feature in safety notices.
