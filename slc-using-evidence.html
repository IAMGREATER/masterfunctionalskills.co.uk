Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L2.4 – Express opinions and arguments and support them with relevant and persuasive evidence.
  • L1.1 / L2.1 – Identify and evaluate relevant supporting data and evidence in spoken discussions and presentations.

Golden Rules for Using Evidence & Persuasive Data in Speech

Golden Rule 1: Follow the Claim → Evidence → Impact Formula

Never state a naked opinion ('We need new hoists'). Pair every claim with concrete evidence ('Breakdowns rose 40% last quarter') and explain the human or financial impact ('Delaying patient transfers by 20 minutes').

Golden Rule / Technique: Claim (What we need) → Evidence (Statistic/Audit) → Impact (Why it matters)
Golden Rule 2: Cite Authoritative Bodies & Workplace Audits Verbally

Anchor your spoken points using authoritative sources: HSE safety guidelines, CQC regulations, NHS staff surveys, Citizens Advice reports, or internal company audit figures.

Golden Rule / Technique: Phrasing: 'According to our Q3 safety audit...', 'In line with HSE manual handling guidelines...'

Core Skill 1: The Three Pillars of Persuasive Spoken Evidence

Combine numbers, rules, and real-world examples to make any spoken argument bulletproof.

Evidence Pillar Why It Persuades Spoken Model Phrasing
1. Quantitative Data & Costs Proves financial scale and return on investment (ROI). "Replacing halogen floodlights across our three depots will cut annual electricity expenditure by £14,500."
2. Regulatory & Legal Standards Shows mandatory compliance (HSE, CQC, GDPR). "Under the Health and Safety at Work Act, we are legally required to provide adequate ventilation in the workshop."
3. Comparative Pilot / Case Study Proves the solution already works in practice. "When Ward 3 piloted staggered visiting hours in August, corridor congestion fell by 35% with zero complaints."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Before & After: Upgrading Vague Assertions into Evidenced Level 2 Arguments

See how adding one realistic statistic and source satisfies Criterion L2.4 immediately.

Unsubstantiated Opinion (Earns Zero Evidence Credit):

"Everyone knows that the staff canteen food is way too expensive and nobody eats there anymore."

Evidenced Level 2 Argument (Ticks Criterion L2.4):

"According to last month's staff wellbeing survey, 74% of employees stopped buying hot meals in the canteen after the price of a main meal rose to £6.80, leading to £400 of daily food waste."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: How to Prepare 3 'Pocket Statistics' During Your Pre-Exam Research

Never walk into an SLC assessment without 3 memorable figures on your notepad.

  • 1 Percentage Stat: e.g. "68% of respondents in the national survey..."
  • 1 Financial / Cost Figure: e.g. "An initial outlay of £1,200 that saves £3,500 per year..."
  • 1 Named Organisation / Report: e.g. "Research published by the Royal College of Nursing / HSE / Local Government Association..."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 Level 1 Supporting Opinions Benchmark (L1.3)

Vocational Context: Local Town Council Parking Discussion

In a group discussion about boosting Saturday trade for local High Street shops, support the idea of offering '2 Hours Free Parking' in council car parks using a clear reason and a real-world example.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 State the core opinion clearly
'I believe introducing two hours of free Saturday parking in the town centre car park is the best way to help independent shops.'
Criteria: Clear Opinion (L1.3)
Step 2 Give the financial reason why shoppers currently avoid the High Street
'Currently, families have to pay £4.50 just to park for ninety minutes, so many drive to the out-of-town retail park where parking is free.'
Criteria: Logical Reason & Price Detail
Step 3 Provide a concrete neighbouring town example
'When our neighbouring town, Market Drayton, introduced two hours of free Saturday parking last summer, High Street footfall increased by a quarter.'
Criteria: Concrete Supporting Example
Final Accredited Response: 'I believe introducing two hours of free Saturday parking in the town centre is the best way to boost local trade. Currently, drivers pay £4.50 for ninety minutes, which pushes families toward out-of-town retail parks with free parking. When our neighbouring town trialled two hours of free Saturday parking last summer, High Street visitor numbers rose by 25%.'
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Comparing current costs (£4.50) with a successful trial in a neighbouring town makes your point grounded and persuasive.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 Level 2 Persuasive Evidence Benchmark (L2.4)

Vocational Context: Warehouse Distribution Logistics

A finance director questions why the depot needs £18,000 for three electric pallet trucks when manual pump trucks cost only £300 each. Deliver an evidenced spoken business case.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 State the core claim focusing on long-term return on investment
'While manual pump trucks have a low upfront price, investing £18,000 in three powered pallet trucks delivers substantial health and productivity savings.'
Criteria: Strategic Business Claim (L2.3)
Step 2 Introduce internal HSE accident and agency cost data
'According to our HSE accident log, manual-handling back strains caused 64 lost working days last year, costing the depot £9,200 in temporary agency cover alone.'
Criteria: Quantitative Audit Evidence (L2.4)
Step 3 Project turnaround efficiency and payback period
'Furthermore, trials in Bay 1 showed powered trucks cut lorry unloading times from 45 minutes to 22 minutes. Consequently, the £18,000 investment pays for itself within fourteen months while protecting our operatives from injury.'
Criteria: Measurable ROI & Impact
Final Accredited Response: 'While manual trucks have a lower upfront cost, electric pallet trucks deliver substantial long-term savings. According to our HSE accident log, manual-handling strain injuries caused 64 lost working days last year, costing £9,200 in temporary agency cover. Furthermore, trials showed lorry turnaround times dropped from 45 minutes to 22 minutes. The investment pays for itself within 14 months while protecting staff wellbeing.'
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Comparing the hidden cost of staff injury leave (£9,200/year) against the equipment price creates an unarguable Level 2 business case.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Making Sweeping Generalisations ('Everyone Thinks...', 'It's Obvious...')
Saying 'Everyone hates the new uniform' or 'It is obvious this will save millions' does not count as persuasive evidence under Criterion L2.4.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Replace 'everyone' with a specific percentage or survey source: 'In last week's poll, 78% of ward staff reported...'
Examiner Pitfall: Dumping Raw Numbers Without Explaining Their Human Impact
Reciting a string of numbers without explaining what they mean for patients, staff, or customers leaves listeners cold.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always follow a statistic with 'which means that...' to translate data into real-world impact.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
You are presenting an argument for installing chilled water dispensers on hospital wards for nursing staff. Combine one physiological fact and one operational benefit into a 40-word spoken statement.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Cite an authoritative source and physiological fact (e.g. Royal College of Nursing research showing a 2% drop in hydration reduces concentration by 15%).
2. Connect hydration directly to reduced medication charting errors and lower mid-shift fatigue.
Final Model Answer: 'Clinical studies cited by the Royal College of Nursing show that even a 2% drop in hydration impairs concentration and cognitive focus by 15%. Installing chilled water dispensers on every ward will directly combat mid-shift fatigue and reduce prescription charting errors.'
Examiner Tip: Citing recognised bodies like the Royal College of Nursing (RCN), HSE, or NICE immediately elevates your spoken authority.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Upgrade this weak, unsubstantiated spoken opinion into a persuasive Level 2 evidenced contribution for a college meeting:
'We should make all textbooks available online as PDFs because heavy books are annoying to carry and cost too much money.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Replace 'annoying to carry and cost too much' with concrete financial data (e.g. £140 average textbook cost per student).
2. Add accessibility evidence (screen-reader compatibility and zoom functions for learners with dyslexia or visual impairments).
3. State the impact on course completion.
Final Model Answer: 'Providing free digital PDF textbooks on the college portal would remove a major financial barrier for adult learners, who currently spend an average of £140 per term on printed manuals. Furthermore, digital editions allow learners with dyslexia to adjust font sizes and use text-to-speech software on their phones, which increased assignment submission rates by 22% in the Business department last year.'
Examiner Tip: Notice how pairing a financial figure (£140 per term) with an inclusivity benefit (dyslexia software) makes the argument twice as strong.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
During a discussion on workplace security, a colleague says: 'Replacing our lost visitor paper book with an electronic tablet sign-in costs £600—that's a waste of money.'
Deliver a 3-sentence counter-argument using legal/regulatory evidence (GDPR & fire safety) to prove why the £600 tablet is necessary.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Acknowledge the £600 cost.
2. Cite GDPR data protection law (open paper visitor books let anyone read previous visitors' names, phone numbers, and car registrations, which breaches GDPR).
3. Cite fire safety compliance (instant cloud evacuation roll-call access on a warden's phone).
Final Model Answer: 'While £600 is an upfront outlay, our current open paper visitor book leaves every visitor's full name, mobile number, and vehicle registration visible to the public, which directly breaches GDPR privacy regulations. An electronic tablet encrypts visitor details immediately and prints a photo lanyard badge. Furthermore, during a fire alarm, wardens can access the live digital visitor list on their phones at the assembly point in seconds.'
Examiner Tip: Whenever a peer objects on cost, counter with GDPR data protection or Fire Safety compliance—legal requirements always trump minor costs!
