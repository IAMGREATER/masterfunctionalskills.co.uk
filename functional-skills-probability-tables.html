Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • L2.34 / L2.35 – Calculate probabilities of combined independent events using sample space tables, two-way grids, and tree diagrams.
  • L2.35 – Calculate expected frequencies from theoretical or experimental probabilities.

Golden Rules for Probability Outcomes, Sample Space & Combined Events

Golden Rule 1: The AND / OR Laws — 'AND = Multiply (×)' • 'OR = Add (+)'

For two independent events A and B: the probability that BOTH happen (A AND B) is P(A) × P(B). The probability that one OR the other mutually exclusive outcome happens is P(A) + P(B).

Golden Rule / Method: P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B) • P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)
Golden Rule 2: Sample Space Grids Show Every Equally Likely Combination

When combining two spinners, two dice, or two menu choices, draw a 2D sample space grid. Total possible outcomes = Rows × Columns. Count the matching cells and divide by total cells.

Golden Rule / Method: Total Grid Outcomes = Outcomes(Event 1) × Outcomes(Event 2)

Core Skill 1: Sample Space Grids for Two Combined Events

Systematically listing all combinations in a 2D table so none are missed.

When two 4-sided spinners (numbered 1 to 4) are spun and their scores are added together, there are 4 × 4 = 16 equally likely outcomes:

Outcomes scoring 7 or more: 3 cells out of 16 → P(Score ≥ 7) = 3/16.

Core Skill 2: Probability Tree Diagrams & Multiplying Along Branches

How to calculate "Both Pass", "Both Fail", and "Exactly One Passes".

  • Each Pair of Branches Sums to 1: If P(Pass) = 0.9, then the lower branch P(Fail) = 0.1.
  • Multiply Along Branches: P(Pass AND Pass) = 0.9 × 0.9 = 0.81.
  • Add Separate End Outcomes: P(Exactly One Fails) = P(Pass, Fail) + P(Fail, Pass) = (0.9 × 0.1) + (0.1 × 0.9) = 0.09 + 0.09 = 0.18.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: At Least One Event Shortcut: 1 − P(None)

The fastest way to find the probability that at least one alarm or backup triggers.

Instead of adding three separate branches for "at least one passes", simply calculate P(Both Fail) and subtract from 1:

  • P(At Least One Works) = 1 − P(Both Fail).

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2)

Vocational Context: Critical Care Power Redundancy

A hospital intensive care unit has two independent backup generators, Generator A and Generator B.
• The probability that Generator A fails during a storm is 0.05.
• The probability that Generator B fails during a storm is 0.04.
(a) Calculate the probability that both generators fail at the same time.
(b) Calculate the probability that neither generator fails (both work).
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Multiply failure probabilities for P(Both Fail)
P(A fails AND B fails) = 0.05 × 0.04 = 0.002.
Criteria: M1 / A1: Accurate combined failure probability (0.002)
Step 2 Find working probabilities for each generator
P(A works) = 1 − 0.05 = 0.95.
P(B works) = 1 − 0.04 = 0.96.
Criteria: M1: Complement probabilities (0.95 and 0.96)
Step 3 Multiply working probabilities for P(Both Work)
P(Both work) = 0.95 × 0.96 = 0.912 (or 91.2%).
Criteria: A1: Accurate combined working probability (0.912)
Final Accredited Answer: (a) P(Both fail) = 0.002 | (b) P(Both work) = 0.912
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Notice that 0.05 × 0.04 = 0.002 (three decimal places), NOT 0.02! Never add probabilities when asked for "BOTH" (AND).
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2)

Vocational Context: Charity Fundraising Event Planning

At a hospital charity fair, players pay £2.00 per game to spin two fair spinners:
• Spinner A has 3 equal sections numbered 1, 2, 3.
• Spinner B has 4 equal sections numbered 2, 4, 6, 8.
• The player wins a £5.00 cash prize if the sum of the two spinners is 9 or more.
If 180 people play the game, calculate the expected net profit raised for the hospital charity.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Determine total outcomes in the sample space and winning combinations (Sum ≥ 9)
Total outcomes = 3 × 4 = 12 equally likely combinations.
Sums ≥ 9: (1+8=9), (2+8=10), (3+6=9), (3+8=11) → 4 winning outcomes.
Criteria: M1: Sample space 4 out of 12 (1/3)
Step 2 Calculate expected number of winners out of 180 players
P(Win) = 4/12 = 1/3.
Expected winners = (1/3) × 180 = 60 winners.
Criteria: M1: Expected winners (60)
Step 3 Calculate total entry revenue minus total prize payouts
Total Revenue = 180 × £2.00 = £360.00.
Total Prize Payout = 60 × £5.00 = £300.00.
Expected Net Profit = £360.00 − £300.00 = £60.00.
Criteria: M1 / A1: Accurate net profit (£60.00)
Final Accredited Answer: £60.00 expected net profit (P(Win) = 4/12 = 1/3; 60 expected winners)
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
List all 12 pairs in a 3×4 grid so you do not miss any winning combination!

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Adding Probabilities Instead of Multiplying for "Both Happen" (AND)
Computing P(A and B) as 0.05 + 0.04 = 0.09 instead of 0.05 × 0.04 = 0.002.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Remember: AND = Multiply (×); OR = Add (+).
Examiner Pitfall: Decimal Place Error When Multiplying Small Decimals (0.2 × 0.3 = 0.06, NOT 0.6)
Writing 0.2 × 0.3 as 0.6 instead of 0.06.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Count total decimal digits: 1 dp + 1 dp = 2 decimal places in the product (0.06).

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 3 Marks
The probability that a delivery train arrives on time on Monday is 0.8, and the probability it arrives on time on Tuesday is 0.7 (independent events). Calculate:
(a) The probability that it arrives on time on both days.
(b) The probability that it is late on both days.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. P(Both on time) = 0.8 × 0.7 = 0.56.
2. P(Late Mon) = 1 − 0.8 = 0.2; P(Late Tue) = 1 − 0.7 = 0.3.
3. P(Both late) = 0.2 × 0.3 = 0.06.
Final Correct Answer: (a) 0.56 | (b) 0.06
Examiner Tip: Find the individual late probabilities first (0.2 and 0.3), then multiply them together (0.06).
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Using the train probabilities from Question 1 (On time Mon = 0.8, On time Tue = 0.7), calculate the probability that the train arrives on time on exactly one of the two days.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Way 1 (On time Mon AND Late Tue) = 0.8 × 0.3 = 0.24.
2. Way 2 (Late Mon AND On time Tue) = 0.2 × 0.7 = 0.14.
3. Total P(Exactly one day on time) = 0.24 + 0.14 = 0.38.
Final Correct Answer: 0.38
Examiner Tip: Check: 0.56 (both on time) + 0.06 (both late) + 0.38 (one of each) = 1.00!
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
Two fair 6-sided dice are rolled and their scores are added together. Out of the 36 possible outcomes, how many outcomes give a total score of 10 or more, and what is the probability as a simplified fraction?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Sums ≥ 10: (4,6), (5,5), (6,4) [sum 10]; (5,6), (6,5) [sum 11]; (6,6) [sum 12] → 6 outcomes.
2. Probability = 6 / 36 = 1 / 6.
Final Correct Answer: 6 outcomes; Probability = 1/6
Examiner Tip: List the pairs systematically: three ways to make 10, two ways to make 11, one way to make 12 = 6/36 = 1/6.
