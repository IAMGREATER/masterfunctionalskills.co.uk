Evaluating Online Services and Information

Assess the reliability, credibility, and security of online suppliers and information sources: evaluate customer reviews, check terms & conditions, verify return policies, and check consumer rights.

Evaluate website credibility using domain extensions, contact details, and independent reviews Identify customer rights: Consumer Rights Act, refund policies, and cancellation periods Spot deceptive online practices (hidden subscription fees, fake review patterns)
On This Page:
1. Key Concepts 2. Worked Exam Model 3. Interactive Quiz 4. Practice Exam Task

Step-by-Step Teaching & Key Concepts

Core Knowledge
1
Checking Website Authenticity & Credibility

Not every website that appears on Google is legitimate. Check these four credibility factors:

  1. Domain Extension: Trusted UK public bodies end in .gov.uk (government) or .ac.uk (universities). Be sceptical of obscure domains like .xyz, .top, or .biz.
  2. Physical Address & Landline Phone: Legitimate UK businesses provide a registered Companies House number, full postal address, and working contact details.
  3. SSL Certificate (Padlock & HTTPS): Encrypts communications.
  4. Professional Design & Spelling: Numerous typos, poor grammar, and blurry logos are warning signs of scam copycat sites.
2
Spotting Fake Reviews

Many fraudulent sellers post fake 5-star reviews to deceive shoppers. Look out for:

  • Dozens of 5-star reviews posted on the exact same date.
  • Vague, generic praise with no specific mention of the product features.
  • Check independent third-party review sites (like Trustpilot) rather than relying only on testimonials hosted on the seller's own homepage.
Website Credibility Checklist
Working UK Phone & Postal AddressVerified
Clear 14-day refund & returns policyCompliant
Independent Trustpilot rating below 2.0High Risk
3
Terms, Conditions, and Consumer Rights

Under the UK Consumer Contracts Regulations, online shoppers have a statutory 14-day cooling-off period to cancel most orders and receive a full refund. Always check who pays for return postage before completing a transaction.

Step-by-Step Exam Worked Model

Accredited Model

Here is how this skill is tested in the real Pearson Edexcel onscreen digital assessment, broken down with official examiner mark codes ([B1] knowledge, [M1] process, [A1] accuracy).

Exam Scenario Prompt: A clinic worker is tasked with ordering ergonomic desk chairs from an unfamiliar online office supplier offering 70% discounts. State two checks the worker must carry out before making payment to confirm that the supplier is reputable and legitimate.
Step 1: First verification check [B1]
Check independent customer reviews on third-party platforms (such as Trustpilot) to see other buyers' experiences.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Independent third-party validation reveals real service quality.
Step 2: Second verification check [B1]
Verify the website's physical contact details (postal address, registered company number, landline telephone) and clear returns policy.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Confirms physical corporate accountability and statutory consumer compliance.
Tutor Abdul's Exam Golden Rule: In Level 1 exams, whenever evaluating a new supplier, look for: 1. Independent reviews; 2. Physical contact details and returns policy.

Onscreen Assessment Task:
You are purchasing clinical training equipment online. You find a site offering prices half of retail value, but there is no telephone number, only an email address, and all customer reviews are 5-star reviews posted on the same day. Explain whether you should buy from this site and give reasons.

Accredited Exam Benchmark Solution

Decision: Do not purchase from this website; it is highly likely to be fraudulent. Reason 1: Lack of a physical address or telephone number means you cannot contact the business if items never arrive. Reason 2: Clustered 5-star reviews posted on the same day are a classic sign of fabricated/fake reviews.
Official Marking Criteria:
  • [1 Mark]: Clear decision not to buy / flagging fraud risk.
  • [1 Mark]: Citing missing contact details and suspicious fake review patterns.
