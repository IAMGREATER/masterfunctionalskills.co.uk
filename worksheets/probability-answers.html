Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (Warm-Up) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Mutually Exclusive Probabilities Summing to 1) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
When a patient calls an out-of-hours triage line, the probability of being connected immediately is 0.35, and the probability of waiting under 2 minutes is 0.48.
Calculate the probability that a caller waits 2 minutes or longer.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Sum the known mutually exclusive probabilities: 0.35 + 0.48 = 0.83 [M1]
2. Subtract from 1.00: 1.00 - 0.83 = 0.17 (or 17%) [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 0.17 (or 17/100 or 17%)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Multiplying 0.35 × 0.48 instead of subtracting their sum from 1.
🔄 Quick Check: 0.35 + 0.48 + 0.17 = 1.00.
Question 2 (Level 1 Foundation — 2 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A medical supply drawer contains 15 blue tourniquets, 10 green tourniquets, and 5 yellow tourniquets. One is selected at random. What is the probability that it is green? Give your answer as a simplified fraction.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Total tourniquets = 15 + 10 + 5 = 30. [M1]
2. P(Green) = 10 / 30 = 1 / 3. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 1/3
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Pearson Edexcel mark schemes explicitly reject ratios (1 : 4) and verbal phrases ("likely" or "1 out of 5") when a numerical probability is requested.
🔄 Quick Check: Add all categories first (15 + 10 + 5 = 30) to find the denominator.
Section B: Everyday & Workplace Maths Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Hospital Emergency Blood Bank Type Selection (Level 1/2 Foundation)) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A hospital blood bank refrigerator stores 50 units of blood:
• Group O: 22 units | Group A: 18 units | Group B: 7 units | Group AB: 3 units
One unit is selected at random.
(a) What is the probability that the unit is Group O? Give your answer as a simplified fraction.
(b) What is the probability that the unit is NOT Group AB? Give your answer as a decimal.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Calculate P(Group O) and simplify fraction: P(Group O) = 22 / 50 = 11 / 25. [M1 / A1]
2. Calculate P(Group AB) as a decimal: P(Group AB) = 3 ÷ 50 = 0.06. [M1]
3. Subtract from 1 for P(NOT Group AB): 1 − 0.06 = 0.94 (or 47 ÷ 50 = 0.94). [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) 11/25 | (b) 0.94
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Always write a fraction (1/5), a decimal (0.2), or a percentage (20%).
🔄 Quick Check: Never write probability as a ratio like "11 : 25"—always write 11/25, 0.44, or 44%.
Question 4 (Workplace Application — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A staff charity spinner has four sections: £5 Prize (P = 0.25), £10 Prize (P = 0.10), £20 Prize (P = 3x), and No Prize (P = 10x). Calculate the value of x and the probability of winning a £20 Prize.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Sum of known probabilities = 0.25 + 0.10 = 0.35. [M1]
2. Remainder for 3x + 10x (13x) = 1.00 − 0.35 = 0.65. [P1]
3. x = 0.65 ÷ 13 = 0.05. [P1]
4. P(£20 Prize) = 3 × 0.05 = 0.15. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: x = 0.05; P(£20 Prize) = 0.15
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Dividing Total by Favourable (e.g. 50 ÷ 22 = 2.27) produces a number greater than 1, which is impossible for any probability.
🔄 Quick Check: All mutually exclusive sectors on a spinner must sum to 1.00.
Section C: Exam-Style Problem Solving Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Quality Control Component Testing & Expected Batch Failures (Level 2 Multi-Step)) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A quality engineer tests a random sample of 250 digital thermometers:
• 235 Pass Grade A, 9 Pass Grade B, and the rest Fail.
(a) Calculate the experimental probability (relative frequency) that a thermometer Fails, as a decimal.
(b) The factory manufactures a batch of 4,000 thermometers. Each failed thermometer costs £8.50 to scrap and replace. Calculate the expected cost of failed thermometers in this batch.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Find number of failed thermometers in the 250 sample: Failed = 250 − (235 + 9) = 250 − 244 = 6 failed thermometers. [M1]
2. Calculate relative frequency of failure: P(Fail) = 6 ÷ 250 = 0.024 (or 2.4%). [A1]
3. Calculate expected failures in 4,000 batch and multiply by £8.50: Expected failures = 0.024 × 4,000 = 96 thermometers. | Expected scrap cost = 96 × £8.50 = £816.00. [M1 / A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) P(Fail) = 0.024 | (b) £816.00 (96 expected failures)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: The smaller number (part) always goes on top of the larger number (total).
🔄 Quick Check: Expected Frequency = Probability × Batch Size (0.024 × 4,000 = 96 units).
Question 6 (Level 2 Multi-Step Problem Solving — 2 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Two quality inspectors test batches from the same machine. Inspector A tests 20 items and finds 1 fault. Inspector B tests 500 items and finds 18 faults. Whose estimate of the fault probability is more reliable, and why?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Inspector B’s estimate (18/500 = 0.036) is more reliable. [M1]
2. Reason: Inspector B used a much larger sample size (500 trials vs 20 trials). [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Inspector B, because a larger sample size (500 vs 20) gives a more reliable estimate
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Stating a conclusion without showing the full numerical comparison values.
🔄 Quick Check: In statistics, a larger sample size always gives a more reliable relative frequency.
