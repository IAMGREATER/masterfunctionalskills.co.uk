Three Non-Negotiable Exam Golden Rules

Golden Rule 1: Always Resize Images From Corner Handles Only!

Dragging the top, bottom, or side middle handles squashes or stretches faces and logos out of proportion. Corner handles lock height-to-width ratio naturally.

✗ Dragging the middle-right resize dot inward and making people look thin
✓ Drag diagonally using any of the 4 Corner circular handles
Golden Rule 2: Switch Wrap Text From 'In Line With Text' to Square/Tight

By default Word locks pictures like one giant character inside a single sentence line. Switching Text Wrapping to Square or Tight lets text flow neatly beside images.

✗ Leaving image as 'In Line With Text' and hitting Spacebar to push it sideways
✓ Select picture > Picture Format > Wrap Text > Square (or Top and Bottom)
Golden Rule 3: Crop Distracting Background Before Enlarging

Use the Crop tool to trim messy edges or empty desk space around an object so your subject fills the frame cleanly without shrinking text space.

✗ Dropping a 4000px blurry wide photo across an entire A4 sheet
✓ Use Picture Format > Crop, position thoughtfully, and add Alt Text

Step-by-Step Teaching & Key Concepts

1
Inserting a Picture

Adding photos or company logos enhances visual interest and communication:

  1. Click on the page where you want the picture to appear.
  2. Go to the Insert tab on the top ribbon.
  3. Click Pictures > This Device.
  4. Browse to your folder (e.g. Pictures), click your file, and click Insert.
Copy Picture: Ctrl + C • Paste Picture: Ctrl + V
2
Resizing Images Proportionally (No Squashing!)

When you click on an inserted picture, a border with eight small circular dots (called sizing handles) appears around the edges.

  • CORRECT: Drag from a CORNER handle: Both the width and height change together equally. The image stays proportional and natural.
  • INCORRECT: Dragging from side or top handles: This squashes or stretches the photo, making people look abnormally wide or squashed.
Examiner Marking Trap: Examiners will deduct marks if an image appears visibly stretched or distorted. Always drag from the corner circles!
3
Cropping and Text Wrapping

To trim away unwanted background clutter:

  1. Click the picture to open the Picture Format tab.
  2. Click the Crop tool ().
  3. Drag the black crop bars inward to cut away unwanted parts, then press Enter.
  4. Click Wrap Text > Square so your text flows smoothly around the image rather than pushing it onto a new page.

Accredited Worked Exam Models (2 Scenarios)

Study both realistic assessment scenarios below alongside official examiner mark allocations ([B1] knowledge, [M1] process, [A1] accuracy).

Worked Model 1 • Core StandardStep-by-Step Accredited Guide
Exam Scenario Prompt: A health worker inserts a photo of an ambulance into a safety poster. The photo is too large and spills onto page 2. Explain how to reduce the size of the photo without distorting its shape.
Step 1: Select the picture [B1]
Click on the ambulance photo to display the sizing handles.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Sizing handles must be active to resize.
Step 2: Resize proportionally [M1]
Click and drag inward from one of the four CORNER handles until the photo fits neatly on page 1.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Corner handles scale width and height simultaneously to maintain aspect ratio.
Tutor Abdul's Exam Golden Rule: Always use the word 'corner' when answering image resizing questions in the exam.
Worked Model 2 • Applied Workplace Scenario Exam Scenario Model 2 • Distorted Clinic Logo Repair
Exam Scenario Prompt 2: A trainee dragged the bottom middle handle of a circular clinic logo upward so it now looks like a flat oval, and text refuses to sit cleanly beside it. State the exact two corrections required.
Step 1: Restore Aspect Ratio via Reset Picture / Corner Handle [B1][M1]
Click Picture Format > Reset Picture & Size (or tick 'Lock aspect ratio' in Size settings), then resize ONLY from a diagonal corner handle.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Recovers the true 1:1 circle shape immediately before scaling neatly.
Step 2: Apply Square Text Wrapping [B1]
Click Layout Options icon beside the logo > select 'Square' Text Wrapping and align cleanly next to the clinic heading.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Lets contact text sit gracefully alongside the logo without leaving awkward blank gaps.
Tutor Abdul's Distinction Tip: Pearson examiners award instant deductions for 'stretched/squashed graphics' on Part B leaflets — always inspect faces and logos for circular/natural proportions!

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Trap 1: Image Sitting Over Top of Headings ('In Front of Text')
Choosing 'In Front of Text' floats images over body words and hides crucial exam information.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Stick to **Square**, **Tight**, or **Top and Bottom** text wrapping.
Trap 2: Upscaling Tiny Low-Res Icons Into Huge Banners
Stretching a tiny 80×80 pixel thumbnail to half an A4 page creates blocky blurred pixelation.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Pick high-resolution photos matching intended display size.
Trap 3: Omitting Image Relevance to Text Audience
Pasting cartoon clip-art onto a formal medical appointment reminder.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Match image style/tone (clean photography vs icons) strictly to the stated target audience.
