Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • L1.23 / L2.28 – Calculate the mean, median, mode, and range for a discrete set of quantities.
  • L2.29 – Compare two data sets using an appropriate measure of average (mean or median) and the range.

Golden Rules for Averages (Mean, Median, Mode) & Range

Golden Rule 1: The Four Statistical Measures Defined

Mean = Total Sum ÷ Number of Values. Median = Exact middle value AFTER putting numbers in ascending order. Mode = Most frequent value. Range = Highest − Lowest (a measure of spread/consistency, NOT an average!).

Golden Rule / Method: Mean (Sum ÷ N) • Median (Ordered Middle) • Mode (Most) • Range (High − Low)
Golden Rule 2: Comparing Two Data Sets Requires TWO Statements

In Pearson Edexcel exams, a comparison question ALWAYS requires: 1. Compare an average in context (e.g. 'Ward A has a higher mean waiting time than Ward B'). 2. Compare consistency using the range (e.g. 'Ward B has a smaller range, meaning waiting times are more consistent').

Golden Rule / Method: Statement 1: Compare Average • Statement 2: Compare Range (Smaller = More Consistent)

Core Skill 1: Median with an Odd vs Even Number of Values

Ordering data first and finding the midpoint when two numbers share the middle.

  • Step 1 — Order the Numbers: Always sort from smallest to largest first!
  • Odd Count (e.g. 7 numbers): Cross off equally from both ends; the 4th number is the exact median.
  • Even Count (e.g. 6 numbers: 4, 7, 9, 12, 15, 18): Add the two middle numbers (9 and 12) and divide by 2: (9 + 12) ÷ 2 = 21 ÷ 2 = 10.5.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Working Backwards from a Given Mean (Reverse Mean)

Finding a missing score or target figure when the required mean is known.

Because Mean = Total ÷ Count, we can reverse the formula to Total = Mean × Count:

  1. Multiply the target mean by the total number of items: e.g. Mean of 5 days is 40 parcels → Total = 5 × 40 = 200 parcels.
  2. Add up the known 4 days: e.g. 38 + 42 + 35 + 41 = 156 parcels.
  3. Subtract to find the missing 5th day: 200 − 156 = 44 parcels.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Choosing Between Mean, Median, and Mode (Handling Outliers)

Knowing which average best represents skewed workplace data.

Measure Best Used When... Limitation
MeanData is evenly spread with no extreme outliers (uses every value)Distorted by one extreme high or low outlier
MedianSalaries or house prices where 1 extreme outlier would skew the meanIgnores the exact size of outer values
ModeShoe/uniform sizes or categorical stock orders (most popular item)May be no mode or more than one mode

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1)

Vocational Context: Ambulance Dispatch Performance

An ambulance station logs response times (in minutes) for 7 emergency calls:
8, 12, 7, 15, 8, 11, 9
(a) Find the mode.
(b) Find the median.
(c) Calculate the mean and the range.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Identify the mode and sort data in ascending order
Ordered list: 7, 8, 8, 9, 11, 12, 15.
Mode (most frequent) = 8 minutes.
Criteria: A1: Correct mode (8 mins)
Step 2 Extract the middle (4th) value for the median
The 4th value in the ordered list of 7 numbers is 9 minutes.
Criteria: A1: Correct median (9 mins)
Step 3 Calculate Mean (Sum ÷ 7) and Range (High − Low)
Sum = 7 + 8 + 8 + 9 + 11 + 12 + 15 = 70.
Mean = 70 ÷ 7 = 10 minutes.
Range = 15 − 7 = 8 minutes.
Criteria: M1 / A1: Accurate mean (10 mins) and range (8 mins)
Final Accredited Answer: (a) Mode = 8 mins | (b) Median = 9 mins | (c) Mean = 10 mins, Range = 8 mins
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Always write your ordered list down and count that you still have all 7 numbers before picking the middle value!
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2)

Vocational Context: Healthcare Clinical Performance Management

A hospital trust records the waiting times in minutes for 6 triage patients on Monday and compares them with Tuesday’s summary:
• Monday: 45, 62, 38, 55, 70, 42
• Tuesday Summary: Mean = 48 minutes, Range = 15 minutes.
Calculate the mean and range for Monday, and write two comparative statements comparing waiting times on Monday and Tuesday.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate Monday Mean (Sum ÷ 6)
Sum = 45 + 62 + 38 + 55 + 70 + 42 = 312 minutes.
Mean = 312 ÷ 6 = 52 minutes.
Criteria: M1: Accurate Monday mean (52 mins)
Step 2 Calculate Monday Range (Highest − Lowest)
Range = 70 − 38 = 32 minutes.
Criteria: A1: Accurate Monday range (32 mins)
Step 3 Statement 1 — Compare Averages in Context
Tuesday had a lower mean waiting time than Monday (48 mins vs 52 mins), so patients waited less time on average on Tuesday.
Criteria: A1: Valid contextual average comparison
Step 4 Statement 2 — Compare Consistency using Range
Tuesday had a smaller range than Monday (15 mins vs 32 mins), meaning waiting times on Tuesday were much more consistent.
Criteria: A1: Valid range consistency comparison
Final Accredited Answer: Monday Mean = 52 mins, Range = 32 mins. Tuesday had shorter average waits (48 vs 52 mins) and more consistent wait times (range 15 vs 32 mins).
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Smaller range ALWAYS means MORE CONSISTENT. Cite the actual numbers (52 vs 48 and 32 vs 15) in your sentences.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Picking the Middle Number Without Putting the List in Order First
Selecting 55 or 38 from the raw unsorted list instead of sorting from smallest to largest first forfeits all median marks.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always write "Ordered:" and tick off each number as you rewrite it.
Examiner Pitfall: Calling the Range an 'Average'
The range measures SPREAD (consistency), not average performance.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Never write "The average range was..."; write "The range was..., showing greater consistency."

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 3 Marks
Find the median, mode, and range for these 8 daily parcel deliveries: 14, 18, 12, 14, 22, 19, 14, 16.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Ordered: 12, 14, 14, 14, 16, 18, 19, 22.
2. Median (4th & 5th values: 14 and 16): (14 + 16) ÷ 2 = 15.
3. Mode (most frequent) = 14.
4. Range = 22 − 12 = 10.
Final Correct Answer: Median = 15, Mode = 14, Range = 10
Examiner Tip: For 8 numbers, average the 4th and 5th ordered values for the median.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
A student needs a mean score of 75 marks across 5 mock tests to qualify for an award. Their first 4 test scores are 72, 68, 81, and 74. Calculate the score they must achieve on the 5th test.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Required total across 5 tests = 5 × 75 = 375 marks.
2. Sum of first 4 tests = 72 + 68 + 81 + 74 = 295 marks.
3. Required 5th test score = 375 − 295 = 80 marks.
Final Correct Answer: 80 marks
Examiner Tip: Use Total = Mean × Count (75 × 5 = 375) and subtract the known 4 scores.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A clinic employs 4 receptionists earning £21,000, £22,000, £22,000, and £23,000, plus 1 consultant surgeon earning £112,000.
(a) Calculate the mean salary and the median salary of the 5 staff.
(b) Explain which average is fairer to represent a typical worker at the clinic.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Total salary = 21,000 + 22,000 + 22,000 + 23,000 + 112,000 = £200,000. Mean = £200,000 ÷ 5 = £40,000.
2. Median (3rd ordered value) = £22,000.
3. The median (£22,000) is fairer because the mean (£40,000) is heavily skewed upward by the consultant’s £112,000 outlier salary.
Final Correct Answer: (a) Mean = £40,000, Median = £22,000 | (b) Median, as it is not distorted by the £112,000 outlier
Examiner Tip: Whenever there is an extreme outlier, the median is the best average to choose.
