Point of View, Bias & Line of Argument Pearson Edexcel: L1.9 • L2.11 • L2.14
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (The Basics) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Level 1 Identifying the Writer's Stance & Supporting Reasons) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Read this letter to a local newspaper:
'Replacing our town's weekly market with a luxury flat development is a short-sighted mistake. The market provides affordable fresh fruit for low-income families and supports 45 independent local traders.'

State the writer's point of view on the flat development and give the two reasons they use to support their view.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. State writer's stance: Strongly against / opposed to replacing the weekly market with luxury flats [A1]
2. Extract both supporting reasons: (1) Provides affordable fresh fruit for low-income families, and (2) supports 45 independent local traders [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Stance: Opposes the flat development. Reasons: 1. Market provides affordable fruit for low-income families. 2. Supports 45 local traders.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Stating only 'the writer thinks it is a mistake' without extracting the two supporting reasons.
🔄 Quick Check: Stance (opposes) + 2 concrete reasons = 2 marks.
Question 2 (Level 1 Distinguishing the Writer's View from an Opposing View) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Read this extract from a school governor's blog:
'Some parents argue that banning smartphones during the school day prevents them from contacting their children in an emergency. However, classroom trials prove that phone bans eliminate cyberbullying at breaktimes and boost maths test scores by 14%.'

Does the writer support or oppose banning smartphones in schools? Explain how the word 'However' helps you know.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify writer's true stance: The writer SUPPORTS banning smartphones during the school day [A1]
2. Explain pivot word: The first sentence states the parents' opposing worry, and 'However' pivots to the writer's own evidence (stopping cyberbullying and boosting maths scores by 14%) [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Supports banning smartphones. The first sentence reports parents' concerns, while 'However' introduces the writer's own positive evidence (14% higher maths scores and no cyberbullying).
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Misreading the first sentence ('Some parents argue...') as the writer's own opinion.
🔄 Quick Check: Always look after pivot words ('However', 'In reality') to find the author's real stance.
Section B: Real-Life & Workplace Practice Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Level 2 Tracing a Three-Stage Line of Argument) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Read this extract from a logistics director's proposal:
'1. Diesel prices have risen by 22% over the past year, eroding our delivery profit margins. 2. Fitting aerodynamic roof fairings to our 30 HGV lorries costs just £400 per vehicle and cuts fuel drag by 11%. 3. Consequently, the board should approve £12,000 for fleet roof fairings this quarter.'

Trace how the director builds their line of argument across the three numbered sentences.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Stage 1 (Identifies the financial problem): Rising diesel prices (up 22%) are shrinking company profit margins [A1]
2. Stage 2 (Proposes a costed solution with evidence): Roof fairings cost £400 per lorry and cut fuel drag by 11% [A1]
3. Stage 3 (Reaches logical conclusion/call to action): Uses 'Consequently' to urge the board to approve the £12,000 investment [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Stage 1 establishes the problem (22% diesel rise hurting profits); Stage 2 presents a proven solution (£400 roof fairings cutting drag by 11%); Stage 3 concludes with a £12,000 funding request.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Just copying the sentences out instead of labelling how each stage builds the argument (Problem -> Evidence -> Conclusion).
🔄 Quick Check: Problem (22% fuel rise) -> Solution (11% drag cut) -> Conclusion (Approve £12,000).
Question 4 (Level 2 Detecting Loaded Language & One-Sided Bias) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Read this extract from a campaign flyer opposing a wind farm:
'Greedy energy corporations want to blight our breathtaking valley with monstrous 150-metre steel turbines that will wreck local house prices forever.'

Explain why this extract is biased, quoting two examples of loaded language and identifying what perspective has been left out.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Quote loaded pejorative language 1: 'Greedy energy corporations' or 'blight our breathtaking valley' [A1]
2. Quote loaded hyperbole 2: 'monstrous 150-metre steel turbines' or 'wreck local house prices forever' [A1]
3. Identify one-sided omission: Completely omits any environmental benefits of renewable clean wind energy or lower electricity bills [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Biased because it uses emotionally loaded words ('Greedy', 'blight', 'monstrous', 'wreck... forever') and presents a one-sided attack that omits the clean energy benefits of wind power.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Calling '150-metre' biased when that is a physical height measurement; focus on 'Greedy', 'blight', and 'monstrous'.
🔄 Quick Check: 2 loaded quotes + 1 explanation of omitted counter-arguments = 3 marks.
Section C: Exam-Style Tasks Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Level 2 Evaluating Vested Commercial Interest & Credibility) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Read this article extract written by Marcus Vance, Managing Director of 'PureSpring Bottled Water Ltd':
'Municipal tap water flows through century-old lead and iron pipes that taint its flavour. Drinking PureSpring mineral water is the only safe, refreshing choice for health-conscious families.'

Evaluate the credibility and objectivity of this argument. Refer to the author's identity, their use of language, and the evidence provided.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Vested commercial interest: Written by the Managing Director of PureSpring Bottled Water Ltd, who profits directly from scaring people off tap water [M1]
2. Scaremongering / loaded language: Uses alarmist words ('century-old lead... pipes', 'taint') to disgust readers [A1]
3. Exaggerated exclusive claim: Claims PureSpring is 'the only safe, refreshing choice' [A1]
4. Lack of scientific evidence: Provides zero independent water-quality test data or regulatory statistics to back up the safety claim [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Highly biased and low credibility: the author is the Managing Director of PureSpring Bottled Water with a financial motive to boost sales, using alarmist language ('lead... taint') and exaggerated claims ('the only safe... choice') without independent lab data.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Failing to mention Marcus Vance's job title as Managing Director of the bottled water company.
🔄 Quick Check: Author's commercial motive + loaded quotes + lack of independent data = 4 marks.
Question 6 (Level 2 Comparing a Balanced Argument vs a Biased Argument) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Text A (Consumer Finance Guide):
'Buy-Now-Pay-Later (BNPL) schemes allow shoppers to spread the cost of a new washing machine over three interest-free instalments. However, missed payments trigger £12 late fees and can damage your credit score.'

Text B (PayLater App Advert):
'Why wait for payday? Instant PayLater is the smartest, most stress-free way to upgrade your wardrobe today with zero catch!'

Analyse which text presents a balanced argument and which text is biased, using evidence from both texts.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Classify Text A as balanced: Weighs up both the benefit (spreading cost over 3 interest-free instalments) and the risk (£12 late fees and credit score damage) [M1]
2. Note Text A's measured tone: Uses the neutral connective 'However' and factual figures to let the reader decide [A1]
3. Classify Text B as biased: Written by a commercial BNPL provider ('PayLater App Advert') promoting its own app [M1]
4. Quote Text B's one-sided superlative claims: Uses persuasive superlatives ('smartest, most stress-free') and the false reassurance 'zero catch!' while hiding late fees [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Text A is balanced because it presents both pros (interest-free instalments) and cons (£12 late fees, credit score risk) in a neutral tone. Text B is biased because it is a commercial advert using superlatives ('smartest, most stress-free', 'zero catch!') while hiding debt risks.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Not quoting the exact pros and cons from Text A that prove it is balanced.
🔄 Quick Check: Text A = pros + cons (balanced); Text B = commercial advert + 'zero catch!' (biased).
