Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.19 / L2.19 – Plan and organise written work coherently, selecting appropriate formats and layouts for the target audience.
  • L1.22 / L2.20 – Use planning strategies to sequence arguments logically, integrate all prompt bullets, and meet target word counts.

Golden Rules for Planning Written Responses

Golden Rule 1: Spend 5 Minutes Planning Before You Write a Word

In Pearson Edexcel and City & Guilds writing exams, candidates who spend 5 minutes jotting down a bullet-pointed paragraph plan score up to 30% higher on organisation, coherence, and task completion.

Golden Rule / Technique: Plan: PAF Check → Paragraph 1 (Intro) → Paragraph 2 & 3 (Core Bullets) → Paragraph 4 (Action/Close)
Golden Rule 2: Tick Off Every Prompt Bullet Point in the Exam Question

Exam questions always provide 3 or 4 bullet points that you MUST address. Number them 1 to 4 on your question paper and cross each one off as you map it into a specific paragraph.

Golden Rule / Technique: Every omitted bullet point results in an automatic penalty to your Content & Organisation mark.

Core Skill 1: The Vocational 5-Minute PAF & Paragraph Blueprint

How to deconstruct the exam prompt and map your response under timed exam pressure.

  1. Minute 1 — Decode PAF (Purpose, Audience & Format): Highlight who you are writing to (e.g. Hospital Estates Director), why (e.g. persuade them to improve staff parking), and in what form (e.g. Formal Letter).
  2. Minute 2 — Map the Prompt Bullets: Assign each bullet point from the exam prompt to a dedicated body paragraph so nothing is left out.
  3. Minute 3 — Brainstorm Evidence & Details: Add 1 realistic statistic, example, or workplace consequence next to each paragraph note (e.g. "25-minute delay at shift handover").
  4. Minute 4 — Select Discourse Markers & Punctuation Targets: Jot down 3 linking words (Furthermore, Consequently, Nevertheless) and remind yourself to use a semicolon and colon.
  5. Minute 5 — Confirm Format Frame: Note the exact greeting and sign-off required (e.g. Dear Mr Evans → Yours sincerely).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Level 1 vs Level 2 Planning & Structural Expectations

Key differences in detail, length, and paragraph sophistication between Level 1 and Level 2 writing tasks.

Planning Dimension Level 1 Benchmark Level 2 Benchmark
Target Word Count Task 1: 150–200 words | Task 2: 100–150 words Task 1: 250–300 words | Task 2: 200–250 words
Paragraph Structure 3–4 clear paragraphs with logical sequence (Intro, Main Points, Conclusion). 4–5 cohesive paragraphs including counter-argument or evaluation and subheadings where appropriate.
Evidence & Elaboration Direct expansion of every bullet point in the prompt using clear examples. Sustained persuasive or analytical development backed by realistic operational data, costs, or regulatory standards.

Core Skill 3: Before & After: Unplanned Rambling vs Planned Paragraph Structure

See how a 4-minute plan transforms disorganised thoughts into a high-scoring Level 2 opening.

Unplanned Draft (Repetitive, Jumbled Ideas — Band 1):

"I am writing about the staff canteen because the food is too expensive and also the microwaves are broken so night shift staff can't heat up their meals and we need healthy options too because everyone is eating crisps from the vending machine which is bad for health."

Planned Draft (Clear Topic Sentence & Sequenced Focus — Band 3):

"I am writing on behalf of the nursing team to propose three urgent improvements to the hospital staff canteen. Currently, night-shift staff have no access to hot nutritious meals after 20:00, and two of the three breakroom microwaves have been out of order for a fortnight."

Benchmark Exam Model 1 Level 1 Planning & Execution Benchmark (15 Marks)

Vocational Context: Local Community Event Organisation

You are organising a charity first-aid workshop and want to hire a room at Oakwood Community Centre on Saturday 14 November.
Write an email to the manager, Mr Samuels, to:
• Explain what the event is and how many people will attend
• Ask about room availability and projector equipment
• Enquire about weekend hire costs and parking.

Subject: Room Hire Enquiry – Charity First-Aid Workshop (14 November)

Dear Mr Samuels,

I am writing to enquire about hiring a training room at Oakwood Community Centre on Saturday 14 November from 10:00 to 14:00. We are holding a free community first-aid workshop for approximately 25 local residents.

Could you please confirm whether the Main Ground-Floor Hall is available on this date? For our practical demonstrations, we require a room equipped with a digital projector, a display screen, and seating for 30 people.

Finally, please could you send me your weekend hire rates, including any discounts for registered charities, and confirm whether delegates can use the centre car park free of charge?

Yours sincerely,
Alex Taylor

Notice how each bullet point from the question becomes one clear, self-contained paragraph. The examiner can tick off all three content requirements in seconds.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 Level 2 Full Planning & Writing Benchmark (18 Marks)

Vocational Context: Clinical Ward Operations & Digital Transformation

You are asked to write a 250-word proposal to your Clinical Lead recommending an electronic handover app.
Create a structured paragraph plan and draft the executive summary and recommendations addressing:
• Flaws in current paper notes
• Benefits of digital handover
• Proposed staff training timeline.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Plan Heading and Strategic Objective
Title: Proposal: Implementation of Electronic Ward Handover System
PAF: Formal Internal Proposal to Clinical Lead — analytical, persuasive, evidence-led.
Criteria: PAF & Layout Conventions (L2.23)
Step 2 Map Body Paragraphs 1 & 2 (Problem & Digital Solution)
Section 1 (Paper Flaws): Illegible handwriting and misplaced paper sheets delay shift handovers by 25 minutes daily.
Section 2 (Digital Benefits): Real-time bedside tablet updates, automated vital-sign alerts, and full GDPR encryption.
Criteria: Logical Sequencing & Detail (L2.20)
Step 3 Map Body Paragraph 3 (Phased Training Rollout)
Section 3 (Timeline): Two-week pilot on Ward 4 starting next month; 30-minute lunchtime workshops led by two ward 'super-users'.
Criteria: Feasible Action Plan & SPaG (L2.24)
Final Accredited Response: PROPOSAL: IMPLEMENTATION OF ELECTRONIC SHIFT HANDOVER APP
To: Clinical Lead | From: Senior Ward Coordinator | Date: 14 October 2026

1. Current Paper Handover Deficiencies
Currently, ward handovers rely on handwritten paper sheets that are frequently illegible or incomplete; as a result, incoming nursing teams lose an average of 25 minutes per shift clarifying medication changes.

2. Operational & Safety Benefits of Digital Handover
Transitioning to a secure tablet-based handover app will synchronise patient observations in real time, flag overdue medications automatically, and ensure full GDPR compliance.

3. Proposed Rollout & Training Timeline
• Weeks 1–2: Train two senior nurses as digital super-users during 30-minute lunchtime modules.
• Weeks 3–4: Pilot the electronic app on Ward 4 alongside paper backups before trust-wide deployment.
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
A tight 4-minute plan prevents mid-essay rambling and guarantees you include high-scoring Level 2 features such as numbered subheadings, bulleted timelines, and a semicolon.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Starting to Write Immediately Without a Plan
Candidates who skip planning inevitably repeat themselves in Paragraphs 2 and 3, lose control of sentence structure, and run out of time before writing a proper conclusion.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always spend 4 to 5 minutes sketching a 4-box paragraph outline in your planning space before typing your response.
Examiner Pitfall: Writing Your Entire Essay Inside the Planning Box
Some candidates write full sentences in the planning box and then run out of time to copy them into the final answer box. Examiners primarily mark the final response area.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Keep your plan strictly to short 3-to-5 word bullet notes (e.g. 'Para 2: Cost savings — £400/month').

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 3 Marks
Read this exam prompt: 'Write a formal letter to your local council complaining about dangerous potholes on Highfield Road, explaining the risks to cyclists and suggesting repairs.'
Create a structured 4-paragraph plan showing the exact focus of each paragraph and the correct letter sign-off.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Identify PAF: Formal complaint letter to Local Council Highways Department (Dear Sir/Madam → Yours faithfully).
2. Map Paragraph 1 (Purpose & Location) and Paragraph 2 (Specific risks to cyclists/vehicles).
3. Map Paragraph 3 (Requested resurfacing action & timeline) and Paragraph 4 (Formal closing call to action).
Final Model Answer: Paragraph 1: Purpose of letter and exact location of potholes on Highfield Road. | Paragraph 2: Safety hazards to cyclists (swerving into traffic, punctured tyres) and recent near-misses. | Paragraph 3: Request urgent inspection and full resurfacing within 14 days. | Paragraph 4: Request written confirmation of repair schedule + 'Yours faithfully,' sign-off.
Examiner Tip: Keep each paragraph focused on a single distinct stage: Situation → Impact/Risk → Solution → Formal Close.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
A candidate wrote this disorganised draft paragraph containing three mixed-up topics:
'Our warehouse needs electric charging points in the car park. Also the canteen vending machine is broken and staff want more salad options. Seven delivery drivers have bought electric cars this year and have nowhere to charge them during shifts.'
Edit and restructure this draft so it maintains a single coherent paragraph topic suitable for an estates proposal.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Delete the irrelevant sentence about the canteen vending machine and salad options (off-topic drift).
2. Combine the remaining two points logically: state the proposal first, then follow with the supporting staff evidence.
3. Add a cohesive linking word ('Furthermore' or 'Consequently') and a concluding benefit.
Final Model Answer: 'Our warehouse urgently requires the installation of electric vehicle (EV) charging points in the main staff car park. Seven delivery drivers have purchased electric cars this year; however, they currently have nowhere to charge their vehicles during shift hours. Installing four fast-charging bays will support staff retention and reduce our depot's carbon footprint.'
Examiner Tip: One paragraph = one core topic. If a sentence belongs to a different topic (like the canteen), delete it or move it to its own paragraph.
Exam Practice Question 3 4 Marks
You have 30 minutes to complete a Level 2 report (250–300 words) for your Care Home Manager on improving visitor check-in security.
1. Break down how you should allocate your 30 minutes across planning, writing, and proofreading.
2. Write the exact Title and three numbered subheadings you would put in your plan.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Time allocation: 5 minutes planning, 20 minutes drafting the report, 5 minutes proofreading SPaG.
2. Title: Formal report heading stating the exact topic.
3. Subheadings: 1.0 Current Security Vulnerabilities, 2.0 Proposed Digital Sign-In System, 3.0 Cost & Implementation Recommendations.
Final Model Answer: 1. Time Split: 5 mins Planning | 20 mins Writing | 5 mins Proofreading.
2. Report Structure:
Title: Report on Upgrading Visitor Check-In Security at Elmwood Care Home
• 1.0 Introduction & Current Sign-In Risks
• 2.0 Benefits of Electronic Visitor Badges & Lanyards
• 3.0 Recommendations & Rollout Costs
Examiner Tip: Writing down your report subheadings during the 5-minute planning phase gives you an instant scaffold to fill in during your 20-minute writing phase.
