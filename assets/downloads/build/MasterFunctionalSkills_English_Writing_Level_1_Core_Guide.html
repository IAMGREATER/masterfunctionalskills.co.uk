Pearson Edexcel Level 1 Writing • Specification Overview

▶Pearson Edexcel Level 1 Assessment Structure

Examination Structure: The Pearson Edexcel Functional Skills English Level 1 Writing paper is an externally assessed, 60-minute examination consisting of two functional writing tasks:

  • Task 1 (Extended Writing • 21 Marks • 35 Minutes): A structured workplace or community scenario requiring 150–250 words. Assessed on Composition (12 marks) and SPaG (9 marks).
  • Task 2 (Shorter Writing • 15 Marks • 25 Minutes): A targeted functional response of 100–180 words. Assessed on Composition (9 marks) and SPaG (6 marks).

Total Marks: 36 Marks • SPaG Weighting: 41.7% (15 marks total). Candidates must write in complete sentences with capital letters, full stops, and accurate common spelling.

✓Core Level 1 Rule
Always cover every bullet point in the question. Write in clear paragraphs separated by a blank line.
!Punctuation Trap
Check every sentence starts with a capital letter and ends with a full stop.

Making Greenwood Park Better for Everyone By Sam Taylor Greenwood Park is a wonderful green space in our town. Every week, many families, dog walkers, and children visit the park to exercise and enjoy the fresh air. However, there are several things that need urgent improvement. Current Problems Firstly, there is too much litter on the grass because there are not enough rubbish bins. Dogs sometimes eat leftover food packaging, which is dangerous. Secondly, several wooden benches are broken and have sharp splinters, so elderly residents have nowhere comfortable to sit. Ideas for Improvement To solve these problems, the council should install more recycling bins near the main gates. In addition, adding an outdoor gym with simple fitness equipment would encourage adults and teenagers to stay active. It would also be great to organize weekend football games or sports days for children. In conclusion, Greenwood Park is important to our community . If we fix the damaged seats and add new facilities , our park will be cleaner, safer, and more welcoming for all residents.

    Level 1 Functional Writing Glossary

    Address
    The recipient and sender postal details placed at the top of a formal letter.
    Adjective
    A describing word that gives more detail about a noun (e.g. 'cold rain', 'heavy boxes').
    Adverb
    A word that describes a verb, indicating how, when, or where an action occurs (e.g. 'quickly', 'yesterday').
    Apostrophe
    A punctuation mark (') used to show omission (don't = do not) or possession (the manager's desk).
    Article
    A piece of writing for a newspaper, magazine, or community website with a headline, subheadings, and paragraphs.
    Bullet points
    Symbols used to list key facts, actions, or recommendations clearly in reports, leaflets, and emails.
    Capital letter
    An uppercase letter used for the first word in a sentence, proper nouns (names, days, places), and 'I'.
    Comma
    A punctuation mark (,) used to separate items in a list or mark a brief pause in a sentence.
    Conjunction
    A linking word used to join words, phrases, or clauses together (e.g. 'and', 'but', 'because', 'although').
    Drafting
    Writing out your initial response using your plan before proofreading and checking.
    Email
    An electronic correspondence containing recipient address, subject line, greeting, message body, and sign-off.
    Exclamation mark
    A punctuation mark (!) used to express strong emotion, urgency, or warning. Use sparingly.
    Formal letter
    A structured letter written to a business, landlord, or authority figure with addresses, date, and formal sign-offs.
    Full stop
    A punctuation mark (.) used to mark the end of a complete statement.
    Headline
    The catchy title of an article or newsletter designed to attract the reader's attention.
    Leaflet
    An informational sheet using subheadings, bullet points, and helpful tips to advise or guide the reader.
    Noun
    A word representing a person, place, thing, or concept (e.g. 'student', 'college', 'computer').
    Paragraph
    A distinct section of writing dealing with a single main idea, separated by a blank line.
    Proofreading
    Carefully checking written work to find and correct spelling, punctuation, and grammar mistakes.
    Question mark
    A punctuation mark (?) placed at the end of a sentence that asks for information.
    Review
    A balanced evaluation of a service, shop, book, or product giving honest opinions and a recommendation.
    Salutation
    The greeting at the start of a letter or email (e.g. 'Dear Mr Smith', 'Dear Customer Service').
    Sentence
    A group of words containing a subject and a verb that expresses a complete thought.
    Sign-off
    The polite closing words of a letter or email (e.g. 'Yours sincerely', 'Kind regards').
    Subject line
    A brief heading at the top of an email summarising its main purpose.
    Verb
    An action or state word (e.g. 'deliver', 'inspect', 'calculate', 'is').
