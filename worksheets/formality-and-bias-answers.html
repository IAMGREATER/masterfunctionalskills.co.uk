Formality, Register & Detecting Bias Pearson Edexcel: Pearson Edexcel Functional Skills English (READING)
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (The Basics) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Detecting One-Sided Bias & Loaded Language) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Read this extract from a local newsletter:
'Greedy council bosses are forcing through an outrageous £2.50 parking charge that will destroy our beloved park forever.'
Explain why this sentence is biased and quote two loaded words that reveal the writer's prejudice.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Explain bias: Presents a one-sided attack without giving the council's reason for the £2.50 charge [A1]
2. Quote two emotive/loaded words: 'Greedy', 'outrageous', or 'destroy' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: It presents a one-sided opinion with no counter-evidence, using loaded emotive words ('Greedy', 'outrageous', 'destroy').
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Quoting the factual figure '£2.50' instead of the biased adjectives.
🔄 Quick Check: Are the quoted words emotionally loaded rather than neutral facts?
Question 2 (Core Skill Application — 2 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A trade union newsletter states: 'Greedy depot bosses are deliberately slashing shift allowances to line their own pockets.'
Explain how the writer's language indicates bias in this sentence, quoting specific words.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Pejorative loaded language: The author uses emotive, insulting words ('Greedy', 'slashing') to attack management [1 Mark]. [M1]
2. Unproven accusation/one-sided view: Claims bosses act 'to line their own pockets' without presenting management's operational reasons or financial evidence [1 Mark]. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Uses loaded, insulting vocabulary ('Greedy', 'slashing') and makes an unproven personal accusation ('line their own pockets') without giving management's perspective.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: An article is NOT automatically biased just because the author reaches a clear conclusion, provided they weigh up evidence from both sides fairly first.
🔄 Quick Check: Always quote the exact loaded words inside quotation marks when answering bias questions.
Section B: Real-Life & Workplace Practice Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Identifying Register and Rewriting for Formal Compliance (Level 1/2)) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Read this draft sentence from a trainee manager's letter to a client:
'Sorry our driver messed up your Tuesday delivery — we'll sort out a replacement parcel ASAP.'
(a) Identify two features that make this sentence too informal for a client letter. [1 Mark]
(b) Rewrite the sentence in an appropriate formal register. [1 Mark]
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify informal colloquialisms and contractions: Uses colloquial phrasal verbs ('messed up', 'sort out'), a contraction ('we'll'), and an informal conversational abbreviation ('ASAP'). [A1]
2. Convert into formal professional Standard English: 'Please accept our sincere apologies for the error with your delivery on Tuesday; we will arrange a replacement consignment immediately.' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) Informal features: slang/phrasal verbs ('messed up', 'sort out') and contraction ('we'll'). | (b) Formal rewrite: 'We sincerely apologise for the error regarding your Tuesday delivery and will dispatch a replacement order immediately.'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Look for unfair suppression of counter-arguments, vested financial interest, or emotionally loaded insults before declaring a text biased.
🔄 Quick Check: Replacing phrasal verbs ('messed up' -> 'error', 'sort out' -> 'arrange') is the fastest way to elevate register in formal letters and reports.
Question 4 (Exam-Style Task — 2 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Read this extract from a staff health guide:
'Ditch the lift and take the stairs! You'll burn calories, boost your mood, and dodge that mid-afternoon slump.'
Describe the register and tone of this extract and give one language feature that creates it.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Register and tone: Informal, lively, friendly, and encouraging [1 Mark]. [M1]
2. Language feature: Colloquial imperatives ('Ditch the lift', 'dodge'), contraction ('You'll'), or direct address ('your mood') [1 Mark]. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Register/Tone: Informal, friendly, and upbeat. | Feature: Colloquial verbs ('Ditch', 'dodge') or contraction ('You'll').
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Writing 'don't', 'can't', or 'it's' in a formal letter to a council or employer signals inconsistent register control.
🔄 Quick Check: Pair a clear tone adjective (informal/upbeat) with a quoted word from the sentence.
Section C: Exam-Style Tasks Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Auditing a Commercial Software Pitch for Bias (Level 2)) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Read the promotional extract written by the Sales Director of MedTech Solutions:
'Our revolutionary CareFlow tablet system is unquestionably the most advanced clinical app ever built. Outdated paper charts are a catastrophic waste of taxpayer funds, and any care home that fails to install CareFlow immediately is putting residents in grave danger.'
Explain why this extract is biased, supporting your answer with two quotations and commenting on the writer's position. [3 Marks]
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Quote and explain hyperbolic superlative language: 'Unquestionably the most advanced clinical app ever built' — uses an unverified commercial superlative as if it were an indisputable fact. [A1]
2. Quote and explain loaded fear-mongering language: 'Catastrophic waste' and 'putting residents in grave danger' — uses emotionally manipulative language to guilt care home managers into buying the product. [A1]
3. Evaluate the author's vested commercial interest: The writer is the Sales Director of MedTech Solutions, meaning they have a direct financial motive to praise CareFlow and omit any setup costs or software limitations. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: The text is biased because it is written by the Sales Director of MedTech Solutions, who has a financial motive to sell the product. It uses exaggerated superlatives ('unquestionably the most advanced') and emotionally loaded fear tactics ('putting residents in grave danger') without providing independent clinical evidence.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Always expand contractions to 'do not', 'cannot', and 'it is' in formal tasks.
🔄 Quick Check: In Functional Skills Reading, linking the author's job title/vested motive to their choice of loaded words secures maximum marks.
Question 6 (Level 2 Mastery Challenge — 3 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Read this extract from a consumer technology review:
'While the Apex Laptop boasts a crisp 4K display and a 14-hour battery life, its £1,600 price tag and lack of USB-A ports make it difficult to recommend for budget-conscious students.'
Is this extract balanced or biased? Explain your answer with evidence from the text. [3 Marks]
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Judgment: The extract is balanced / objective [1 Mark]. [M1]
2. Evidence of positives: It acknowledges specific strengths ('crisp 4K display and a 14-hour battery life') [1 Mark]. [M1]
3. Evidence of negatives: It weighs those strengths fairly against drawbacks ('£1,600 price tag and lack of USB-A ports') to give a reasoned conclusion [1 Mark]. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Balanced: The reviewer weighs up positive features ('crisp 4K display', '14-hour battery life') alongside negative limitations ('£1,600 price tag', 'lack of USB-A ports') using measured, non-emotive language.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Failing to proofread spelling, punctuation, and grammar after completing the draft.
🔄 Quick Check: Look out for exam questions where the text is actually balanced! Mentioning both the pros and cons quoted in the text proves why it is unbiased.
