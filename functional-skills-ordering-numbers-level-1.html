Official Level 1 Specification & Scope (L1.N.2)

Regulated Ofqual Functional Skills Mathematics Level 1 learning outcomes (Open Awards, Pearson Edexcel, City & Guilds, NCFE & AQA):

  • Recognise and use both positive and negative numbers in practical contexts such as temperatures and bank balances.
  • Order positive and negative whole numbers from coldest/lowest to warmest/highest.
  • Find the difference across zero (e.g. from −6°C up to 11°C) using a number-line bridge through 0.

Core Level 1 Golden Rules: Ordering Positive and Negative Numbers (Level 1)

Golden Rule 1: Further Left Means Smaller (Colder)

With negative numbers, a larger-looking digit is actually smaller: −12°C is colder (lower) than −4°C.

Level 1 Key Rule: −12 < −7 < −2 < 0 < 5
Golden Rule 2: Bridge Through Zero for Temperature Gaps

To find the rise from a negative to a positive number, count up to 0 first, then from 0 up to the positive value.

Level 1 Key Rule: From −6°C to +9°C: 6 + 9 = 15°C rise
Golden Rule 3: Underline Ascending vs Descending

Ascending / Lowest first starts with the biggest negative; Descending / Highest first starts with the largest positive.

Level 1 Key Rule: Check order direction before writing final list!

Core Skill 1: Number Line Sequencing & Temperature Spans Across Zero

Navigating positive and negative numbers without falling into the magnitude illusion trap.

The Full-Width Continuum Number Line
Negative to Positive Direction
1. Magnitude Reversal in Negatives

The larger the number looks, the smaller it actually is when negative:
−14.5 < −3.2 (because −14.5 is further below zero).

2. Temperature Spans Across Zero

To find the temperature rise from −6°C to +15°C:
6 (up to zero) + 15 (above zero) = 21°C.

Core Skill 2: Padding Decimals with Trailing Zeros (Equalising Decimal Places)

Eliminating length bias when comparing thousandths, hundredths, and tenths.

The Zero-Padding Comparison System
No-Squint Decimal Alignment

Unpadded decimals create optical illusions (e.g. seeing 2.4 as smaller than 2.049 because 2.049 has more digits). Always pad every number to matching decimal places with zeros before ranking:

Original Measurement Padded to 3 Decimal Places Thousandths Reading Accredited Rank (Smallest → Largest)
2.049 mm 2.049 2,049 1st (Smallest)
2.08 mm 2.080 2,080 2nd
2.375 mm 2.375 2,375 3rd
2.4 mm 2.400 2,400 4th (Largest)
Final Ascending Order (Restored to Original Format)
2.049  <  2.08  <  2.375  <  2.4

Core Skill 3: Using Inequality Symbols (<, >) & Direction Terminology

Accredited notation for Pearson Edexcel Section A and Section B questions.

The Inequality Mouth & Sorting Direction System
Examiner Standard
Sorting Directions
Ascending Order: Smallest to Largest
Going UP like stairs: −8, −2, 0, 4, 11
Descending Order: Largest to Smallest
Going DOWN like a slide: 11, 4, 0, −2, −8
The Wide Mouth Rule

The open mouth ALWAYS points toward the GREATER value:

−3 > −9 (−3°C is warmer than −9°C)
0.08 < 0.4 (0.08 is smaller than 0.40)

Vocational Context: Commercial Catering Chiller & Freezer Log

Five storage units in a catering kitchen show temperatures of: 4°C, −18°C, −5°C, 0°C, and −12°C. Order these from coldest to warmest, and find the temperature difference between the coldest and warmest unit.
Step
Level 1 Method & Working
Marking Focus
Step 1 Sort negatives from furthest below zero
−18, then −12, then −5
→ −18°C, −12°C, −5°C
[M1] Negatives ordered accurately
Step 2 Append zero and positive temperatures
Add 0°C then 4°C
→ −18°C, −12°C, −5°C, 0°C, 4°C
[A1] Complete ascending list
Step 3 Bridge through 0 from −18°C to 4°C
18 steps to 0 + 4 steps above 0
→ 18 + 4 = 22°C
[A1] Correct temperature range 22°C
Final Exam Answer: −18°C, −12°C, −5°C, 0°C, 4°C; Difference = 22°C

Common Level 1 Examiner Traps to Avoid

Level 1 Examiner Trap: Ordering Negatives Backwards
Writing −3, −8, −15 as 'lowest to highest'. Remember −15 is furthest below zero so −15 comes first!
Level 1 Examiner Trap: Subtracting Distances Across Zero
Calculating 18 − 4 = 14°C instead of 18 + 4 = 22°C when bridging from −18°C to +4°C.

Interactive Level 1 Exam Practice Questions

Level 1 Practice 1: Overnight Weather Station 2 Marks
Overnight temperatures in four towns were −9°C, 3°C, −2°C, and −11°C. Write them from lowest to highest.
Level 1 Worked Solution & Mark Breakdown:
Compare negative values (M1): −11 is lowest, then −9, then −2
Full ordered sequence (A1): −11°C, −9°C, −2°C, 3°C
Final Answer: −11°C, −9°C, −2°C, 3°C
Level 1 Practice 2: Bank Account Overdraft Recovery 2 Marks
A current account balance starts at −£35. A wage payment of £120 is paid in. What is the new balance?
Level 1 Worked Solution & Mark Breakdown:
Clear the £35 overdraft to £0 (M1): 120 − 35
State positive balance (A1): £85
Final Answer: £85
