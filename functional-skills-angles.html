Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • L1.21 / L2.23 – Measure, classify, and calculate missing angles on straight lines (180°) and around a point (360°).
  • L2.23 – Understand and use 3-figure bearings measured clockwise from North, including reverse (back) bearings.

Golden Rules for Angles, Bearings & Turn Navigation

Golden Rule 1: The Three Iron Rules of Three-Figure Bearings

1. Always measure from NORTH (000°). 2. Always measure CLOCKWISE. 3. Always write as THREE DIGITS (e.g. 045°, 008°, 235°).

Golden Rule / Method: From North (000°) • Clockwise • 3 Digits (e.g. 065°)
Golden Rule 2: Straight Line = 180° | Full Point = 360° | Back Bearing = ±180°

Angles on a straight line add to 180°. Angles around a point add to 360°. To find a reverse (back) bearing: if the forward bearing is under 180°, ADD 180°; if over 180°, SUBTRACT 180°.

Golden Rule / Method: Straight Line = 180° • Point = 360° • Back Bearing = Forward ± 180°

Core Skill 1: Angle Classification & Core Point/Line Rules

Acute, right, obtuse, straight, and reflex angle definitions and missing angle rules.

  • Acute Angle: Less than 90° (e.g. 42°).
  • Right Angle: Exactly 90° (quarter turn, marked with a square corner).
  • Obtuse Angle: Between 90° and 180° (e.g. 135°).
  • Straight Line: Exactly 180° (half turn). Missing angle on a line = 180° − known angles.
  • Reflex Angle: Between 180° and 360° (e.g. 240°).
  • Vertically Opposite Angles: Where two straight lines cross (X-shape), opposite angles are equal.

Core Skill 3: Calculating Reverse (Back) Bearings

Finding the exact return bearing from destination B back to origin A.

  • Case 1 — Forward Bearing < 180°: ADD 180°.
    Example: Bearing of B from A is 068° → Bearing of A from B = 068° + 180° = 248°.
  • Case 2 — Forward Bearing > 180°: SUBTRACT 180° (and pad to 3 digits if under 100°).
    Example: Bearing of B from A is 252° → Bearing of A from B = 252° − 180° = 072°.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1)

Vocational Context: Hospital Perimeter Security Monitoring

A 360° panoramic security dome camera is divided into four monitoring zones around a central point. Zone A covers 95°, Zone B covers 110°, and Zone C covers 82°.
(a) Calculate the angle covered by Zone D.
(b) State whether Zone D is an acute, obtuse, or reflex angle.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Sum the three known zone angles
95° + 110° + 82° = 287°.
Criteria: M1: Summing known angles around a point
Step 2 Subtract from 360° (full turn around a point)
360° − 287° = 73°.
Criteria: A1: Accurate angle for Zone D (73°)
Step 3 Classify 73°
Since 73° is less than 90°, it is an ACUTE angle.
Criteria: A1: Correct classification (Acute)
Final Accredited Answer: (a) Zone D = 73° | (b) Acute angle
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Angles around a central point always add up to a full circle of 360°.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2)

Vocational Context: Emergency Medical Helicopter Dispatch

An emergency air ambulance takes off from Hospital Helipad H and flies 45 km on a bearing of 074° to an incident site A.
(a) Calculate the 3-figure back bearing required to fly directly from incident site A back to Hospital Helipad H.
(b) On a radar map with scale 1 cm : 6 km, what is the plotted distance between H and A in cm?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Apply the back-bearing rule for 074° (< 180°)
Since 074° is less than 180°, add 180°: 074° + 180° = 254°.
Criteria: M1: Adding 180° for reverse bearing
Step 2 State return bearing in 3-figure format
Return bearing from A to H = 254°.
Criteria: A1: Accurate 3-figure bearing (254°)
Step 3 Calculate radar map distance using scale 1 cm : 6 km
45 km ÷ 6 km = 7.5 cm.
Criteria: M1 / A1: Accurate scaled distance (7.5 cm)
Final Accredited Answer: (a) Return bearing = 254° | (b) Map distance = 7.5 cm
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Always check which location comes after the word "FROM": "bearing of H FROM A" means stand at A, face North, and turn clockwise.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Writing Bearings with Only Two Digits
Writing "74°" instead of "074°" forfeits the bearing accuracy mark.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always pad bearings under 100° with a leading zero (e.g. 009°, 045°, 074°).
Examiner Pitfall: Measuring Anti-Clockwise from North or Measuring from South/East
Measuring 60° West of North and writing 060° instead of 360° − 60° = 300°.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: If given an angle West of North (anti-clockwise), subtract it from 360° to get the clockwise bearing.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Two straight conveyor tracks cross each other. One of the four angles formed at the intersection is 64°. Calculate the size of the obtuse angle next to it on the straight line.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Angles on a straight line sum to 180°.
2. 180° − 64° = 116°.
Final Correct Answer: 116°
Examiner Tip: Check: 116° + 64° = 180°.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
A coastguard lifeboat sails from Harbour P to a fishing vessel Q on a bearing of 218°. Calculate the 3-figure bearing of Harbour P from vessel Q.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Since 218° > 180°, subtract 180°: 218° − 180° = 38°.
2. Write in mandatory 3-figure format: 038°.
Final Correct Answer: 038°
Examiner Tip: Do not forget the leading zero on 038°!
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A radar dish points due North (000°). It rotates 135° clockwise, then rotates 48° anti-clockwise. What is its final 3-figure bearing?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Clockwise turn from 000° = 135°.
2. Anti-clockwise turn subtracts angle: 135° − 48° = 87°.
3. Write as 3-figure bearing: 087°.
Final Correct Answer: 087°
Examiner Tip: Clockwise = Add (+); Anti-clockwise = Subtract (−).
