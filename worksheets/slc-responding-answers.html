Responding to Questions & Feedback Pearson Edexcel: Pearson Edexcel Functional Skills English (SLC)
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (The Basics) Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks Total
Question 1 (Answering a Challenging Question) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Write a respectful, evidence-backed spoken response to the question on older staff using a rota app.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Validate the concern respectfully: "That's a very fair point, and we certainly don't want anyone feeling left behind." [M1]
2. Practical solution provided: "We will offer 10-minute one-to-one training sessions and keep a printed paper copy of the rota on the staff noticeboard as backup." [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: "That's a very fair concern, and we want to make sure everyone feels supported.
To help staff adapt, team leaders will run brief one-to-one walkthroughs, and we will continue posting a printed rota on the staffroom board so nobody misses a shift."
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Never say "They'll just have to learn." In Functional Skills, you must show respectful empathy and practical solutions.
🔄 Quick Check: Empathy + Practical Action = 2 Marks.
Question 2 (Using PREP Method) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Write a 40–50 word spoken response using the PREP method countering the idea that staff won't recycle.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Point & Reason: State that staff will recycle if bins are clearly labelled and convenient. [M1]
2. Example & Point: Cite evidence from Depot B and restate the benefit. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: "I understand your doubt, but I believe staff will recycle if we make it effortless.
The reason people throw everything in one bin is unclear signage.
When our Leeds depot installed colour-coded bins with clear picture stickers, general waste dropped by 40%.
Clear bins make recycling second nature."
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Ensure all 4 steps of PREP are present: Point, Reason, Example, Point.
🔄 Quick Check: P + R + E + P = Full Marks.
Section B: Real-Life & Workplace Practice Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks Total
Question 3 (Defending Healthy Eating Proposal) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Deliver a clear 3-sentence response explaining how healthy canteen meals will stay affordable.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Acknowledge price worry: "I completely understand the concern about lunch prices." [M1]
2. Operational cost control: Explain that using seasonal ingredients and bulk grain/pasta keeps raw costs down. [M1]
3. Price cap promise: Confirm a fixed £3.50 meal deal price so staff know they won't pay £7 or £8. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: "I completely understand the worry about price, especially with the cost of living.
However, our catering team will buy seasonal vegetables and pulses in bulk, which actually cost less than processed meats.
We are introducing a fixed £3.50 hot meal deal so nutritious food remains affordable for every worker."
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Give a concrete figure or fact (£3.50 meal deal) rather than vague promises like "it won't be too expensive."
🔄 Quick Check: Acknowledge + Method + Concrete Price Cap = 3 Marks.
Question 4 (Handling Unknown Technical Details) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Write a professional 2-sentence response when asked for a specific technical figure you do not know.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Honest, professional acknowledgement: "I don't have the exact wattage rating with me right now, but I can check the manufacturer spec sheet and email that over today." [M1]
2. Pivot to known benefit: "What I can confirm is that these LED tubes cut overall electricity usage by 60% compared to our existing fluorescent tubes." [M2]
Award Final Answer Mark: "I don't have the exact kilowatt-hour rating in my notes, but I will check the manufacturer spec sheet and confirm that figure with you after this talk.
What I can confirm today is that these tubes reduce overall lighting electricity by 60% and last five times longer."
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Never guess or make up a technical number. Acknowledge, offer to check, and state the big-picture saving you do know.
🔄 Quick Check: Honest admission + Offer to follow up + Known fact = 3 Marks.
Section C: Exam-Style Tasks Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks Total
Question 5 (Defending 2-Hour Email Target — PREP) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Deliver a complete PREP response defending the 2-hour email response target against concerns about staff overload.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Point (P): State that fast email replies will actually reduce the overall phone call pressure. [M1]
2. Reason (R): Explain that customers only ring the phones because their emails go unanswered for days. [M1]
3. Example (E): Share practical tools: standard response templates for top 5 questions cut typing time to 30 seconds. [M1]
4. Point (P): Conclude that fast replies protect staff from angry chase-up calls. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (Point): "I appreciate your concern about workload, but answering emails within two hours will actually make our working day less stressful.

(Reason): Right now, over half of our incoming phone calls are from frustrated customers asking why nobody replied to their email from Tuesday.

(Example): By using pre-written response templates for our top five common queries—like returns and tracking—staff can reply in thirty seconds rather than typing from scratch.

(Point): Answering quickly stops double-contact and reduces the overall volume of angry phone calls, protecting staff from burnout."
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Address the core worry directly (staff stress) and show how your idea helps them rather than just repeating company policy.
🔄 Quick Check: P + R + E + P + Direct solution to problem = 4 Marks.
Question 6 (Adapting Plan for Community Lounge) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Write a 4-sentence spoken response validating the resident's worry and adapting the plan to protect social lounge seating.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Warm validation: "Thank you for raising that; our older visitors are the heart of this community centre." [M1]
2. Clear reassurance: "No one is being pushed out, and social connection is just as vital as digital support." [M1]
3. Compromise adjustment: "We will protect the entire sunny window area with comfortable armchairs, hot drinks, and daily newspapers." [M1]
4. Shared benefit: "The computer desks will only use the quiet back wall, meaning everyone has their own welcoming space." [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: "Thank you for raising that important point; our regular visitors are the heart of this centre, and nobody is being pushed out.
Social connection and having a warm place to chat are just as essential as digital access.
To protect our social space, we will keep the entire front lounge with comfortable armchairs, free hot tea, and daily newspapers completely untouched.
The computer desks will only occupy the back quiet room, so everyone in our community has a welcoming space."
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: A defensive reply ("The funding requires computers") fails L2.3 and L2.7. Show empathy and adapt the proposal flexibly.
🔄 Quick Check: Validate + Reassure + Concrete Space Allocation + Welcoming Tone = 4 Marks.
