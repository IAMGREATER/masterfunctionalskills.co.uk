Linear measurement problems in Pearson Edexcel tests test two vital abilities: converting between metres, centimetres, and millimetres before calculating, and solving physical cutting problems without falling into the 'offcut trap'. Golden Rules for Length & Dimensions

Golden Rule 1: Always Align with 0 on Measuring Tools

When reading a ruler or tape measure, check whether measurement starts at the 0 mark or at the physical end of the tool.

Golden Rule / Method: Start at 0, NOT the edge of the ruler
Golden Rule 2: Convert All Dimensions to the Same Unit Before Calculating

If room length is 4.2 m and skirting board is sold in 240 cm lengths, convert both to centimetres (420 cm and 240 cm) or both to metres (4.2 m and 2.4 m).

Golden Rule / Method: Never add or divide metres and centimetres directly

Core Skill 1: Length Measurement Benchmarks & Unit Selection

Real-world physical benchmarks for rapid estimation and unit checking.

  • Millimetres (mm): Precision engineering, timber thickness, tablet diameters (10 mm = 1 cm).
  • Centimetres (cm): Furniture dimensions, shelving, clothing sizes (100 cm = 1 m).
  • Metres (m): Room dimensions, garden fencing, corridor lengths (1,000 m = 1 km).
  • Kilometres (km): Road transport distances and courier routes.
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Cutting Lengths from Timber / Pipe Stock (No-Joining Rule)

Why you must divide each stock length individually when offcuts cannot be glued together.

Suppose you need 10 shelves each measuring 90 cm, and timber planks are sold in 240 cm lengths:

  1. Check how many full shelves fit into 1 plank: 240 cm ÷ 90 cm = 2.66... → 2 full shelves per plank (with a 60 cm offcut that is too short for a 3rd shelf).
  2. Divide required shelves by shelves per plank: 10 shelves ÷ 2 per plank = 5 planks needed.
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Spacing Items with Gaps Along a Wall or Fence

Calculating posts, shelving brackets, or hospital bed bays with mandatory clearance gaps.

When placing 4 lockers (each 60 cm wide) along a 3.5 m (350 cm) wall with a 15 cm gap between each locker:

  • 4 lockers have 3 internal gaps between them (Gaps = Items - 1).
  • Total width used: (4 × 60 cm) + (3 × 15 cm) = 240 cm + 45 cm = 285 cm.
  • Remaining wall space: 350 cm - 285 cm = 65 cm.
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1/2)

Vocational Context: Community Facilities Maintenance

A community clinic corridor handrail is 14.4 metres long. Timber rails are sold in sections of 240 cm each.
(a) How many sections are needed to cover the entire handrail length?
(b) If each section costs £18.50, calculate the total cost.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Convert dimensions to common units (metres to cm)
Unit Conversion (×100 cm per metre)
14.4 m × 100 = 1,440 cm
Always harmonize lengths into identical units before attempting division.
Criteria: M1: Unit conversion to centimetres
Step 2 Divide total length by section length
Total Length ÷ Section Length
1,440 cm ÷ 240 cm = 6 sections
Exactly 6 complete timber sections are required end-to-end.
Criteria: A1: Accurate section count
Step 3 Calculate total expenditure
Quantity × Unit Price
6 × £18.50 = £111.00
Remember to include trailing zero pence (.00) for all monetary values.
Criteria: A1: Accurate cost calculation
Final Accredited Answer: (a) 6 sections needed | (b) Total cost = £111.00
Sonya’s Examiner Tip Matching Units Rule
Dividing 14.4 by 240 without converting gives 0.06 sections — which is impossible in the real world! Always ensure both values share the same unit (cm or m) before dividing, and use common sense to verify your magnitude.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Mixing Units in Division or Joining Unjoinable Offcuts
Dividing metres by centimetres directly, or assuming small wood offcuts can be glued together to form a structural shelf.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Convert all lengths to cm first and check how many whole pieces fit into 1 stock length.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A roll of medical fabric tape is 5 metres long. A nurse cuts 8 strips measuring 35 cm each. How many centimetres of tape remain on the roll?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. 5 m = 500 cm.
2. 8 strips × 35 cm = 280 cm used.
3. Remaining = 500 - 280 = 220 cm (or 2.2 m).
Final Correct Answer: 220 cm (or 2.2 m)
Examiner Tip: Convert 5 metres to 500 cm first.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
A picture framer needs to make a rectangular frame measuring 65 cm long by 45 cm high. Allowing an extra 15 cm in total for corner mitre cuts, what total length of moulding is needed in metres?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Frame perimeter = 2 × (65 + 45) = 2 × 110 = 220 cm.
2. Add cutting allowance = 220 + 15 = 235 cm.
3. Convert to metres: 235 ÷ 100 = 2.35 metres.
Final Correct Answer: 2.35 m
Examiner Tip: Check the unit requested in the final sentence (metres).
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
An office wall is 4.6 metres long. The facilities manager places a filing cabinet (80 cm wide) in the corner, followed by 3 identical desks (each 115 cm wide). How much spare wall length remains in centimetres?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Wall length = 4.6 m = 460 cm.
2. 3 desks = 3 × 115 cm = 345 cm.
3. Total furniture width = 80 cm + 345 cm = 425 cm.
4. Remaining wall length = 460 - 425 = 35 cm.
Final Correct Answer: 35 cm
Examiner Tip: Convert 4.6 m to 460 cm right at the start.
