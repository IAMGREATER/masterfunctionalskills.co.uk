Specification & Learning Outcomes

Students must be able to demonstrate mastery of the following regulated outcomes:

  • L1.22 – Calculate the perimeter of simple shapes, including rectangles and combinations of rectangles.
  • Set out step-by-step mathematical working clearly, stating appropriate units of linear measurement (mm, cm, m).

Golden Rules for Perimeter

Golden Rule 1: Perimeter is the Outside Distance — Walk the Entire Boundary

Perimeter is strictly the total distance around the outside edge of a 2D shape. Imagine walking around the perimeter of the field or floor. Never add inside partition lines or dashed splitting lines used in working out compound shapes.

Examiner Tip: Mark each side with a small tick as you add it to your running total so you don't miss or double-count sides.
Golden Rule 2: Slant Heights Count for Perimeter; Perpendicular Heights Do Not

When finding the perimeter of parallelograms or trapeziums, you must use the outer slanted side lengths. The perpendicular height inside the shape is only used for area calculations, never for perimeter!

Units Check: Perimeter is always measured in linear units (mm, cm, m) — never square units (cm² or m²).
Example 1: Perimeter of a Rectangle (Garden Border)

A healthcare facility garden bed is in the shape of a rectangle with length 18 m and width 7.5 m. Calculate the total perimeter of the garden bed.

[2 Marks]
Perimeter = 2 × (Length + Width)  or  Side + Side + Side + Side
Step 1: Opposite sides of a rectangle are identical in length (18 m and 7.5 m).
Step 2: Add all four outer sides: 18 + 18 + 7.5 + 7.5 = 51 m.
Step 3: Alternative formula: 2 × (18 + 7.5) = 2 × 25.5 = 51 m.
Final Perimeter:
51 m

Example 2: Perimeter of a Parallelogram (Distractor Traps)

Below is a parallelogram flower border with a horizontal base of 14 m, a slanted side of 9 m, and an internal perpendicular height of 7 m. Calculate the perimeter.

[2 Marks]
Perimeter = 2 × (Base + Slanted Side)
Step 1: Identify the boundary edges: Base = 14 m, Slant Side = 9 m.
Step 2: Discard the perpendicular height (7 m) — it is a trap intended for area!
Step 3: Add the 4 outer sides: 14 + 14 + 9 + 9 = 46 m.
Final Perimeter:
46 m

Example 3: Perimeter of a Compound L-Shape (Finding Missing Sides)

A community healthcare clinic office suite is shaped like an L. The top edge is 16 m, the left outer edge is 12 m, the bottom-right step is 9 m wide, and the bottom-left edge is 7 m wide. Calculate the total perimeter.

[3 Marks]
Step 1: Find missing horizontal side: Top (16 m) − Bottom-left (7 m) = 9 m (matches right overhang).
Step 2: Find missing vertical side: Total Left Height (12 m) − Right Step Height (7 m) = 5 m.
Step 3: Add all 6 outer sides: 16 + 5 + 9 + 7 + 7 + 12 = 56 m.
Rule: In any rectangular L-shape, Perimeter = 2 × (Max Width + Max Height) = 2 × (16 + 12) = 56 m!
Final Perimeter:
56 m

Example 4: Compound Shape with Semicircular Arc (Level 1)

A hospital emergency entrance door consists of a rectangle measuring 4 m wide by 6 m high, topped by a semicircular arch with diameter 4 m. Calculate the perimeter of the doorway frame (to 2 decimal places).

[3 Marks]
Semicircular Arc = ½ × π × d  (π ≈ 3.142)
Step 1: Full circle circumference = π × d = 3.142 × 4 = 12.568 m.
Step 2: Half circle arc = 12.568 ÷ 2 = 6.284 m.
Step 3: Add outer straight sides: Left (6 m) + Right (6 m) + Bottom (4 m) = 16 m.
Step 4: Total perimeter = 16 + 6.284 = 22.28 m.
Total Boundary:
22.28 m

Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Trap 1: Adding Inside Boundary Lines in Compound Shapes

When you split a compound shape into rectangles or triangles, students often accidentally add the dotted partition line into their perimeter sum.

Solution: Highlight only the exterior edge of the shape with a colored pen before doing your calculations.
Trap 2: Forgetting to Halve the Circle Circumference

The formula π × d gives the circumference of a whole circle. For a semicircle, you must divide by 2. If the semicircle is standalone, remember to add the straight diameter across the bottom!

Semicircle Perimeter = (½ × π × d) + d (if enclosed).

Question 1: Warehouse Skirting Board
[2 Marks]

A storage warehouse room has a rectangular floor measuring 22 m in length and 8.5 m in width.

Skirting board is to be installed around the perimeter of the room. Calculate the total metres of skirting board needed.

Official Tutor Step-by-Step Mark Scheme
2 / 2 Marks Awarded
Step 1 (Formula & Method) M1 Method Mark
Perimeter = 2 × (Length + Width) = 2 × (22 + 8.5)
Add the two perpendicular dimensions together (30.5 m), then double for both opposite pairs. (Alternative valid method: 22 + 22 + 8.5 + 8.5).
Step 2 (Calculate Final Total) A1 Accuracy Mark
2 × 30.5 = 61 m
Compute final linear length. Ensure correct metric units (metres) are included.
Correct Final Answer:
61 metres
Examiner Marking Tip: Remember that perimeter is linear length (m), NOT square units (m²). Writing m² for perimeter will lose accuracy marks in Functional Skills exams!

Question 2: Compound L-Shaped Reception Area
[3 Marks]

An L-shaped patient reception area has outer measurements: Top = 14 m, Left = 10 m, Bottom-left width = 6 m, and Right-hand drop = 4 m.

Work out the perimeter of the room.

Official Tutor Step-by-Step Mark Scheme
3 / 3 Marks Awarded
Step 1 (Find Missing Horizontal Edge) M1 Method Mark
Missing horizontal = 14 − 6 = 8 m
Subtract the known bottom-left segment (6 m) from the total top width (14 m).
Step 2 (Find Missing Vertical Edge) M1 Method Mark
Missing vertical = 10 − 4 = 6 m
Subtract the known right-hand drop (4 m) from the total left height (10 m).
Step 3 (Sum All 6 Sides) A1 Accuracy Mark
14 + 4 + 8 + 6 + 6 + 10 = 48 m
Add all outer edges together. (Shortcut check for rectilinear L-shapes: 2 × (14 + 10) = 48 m).
Correct Final Answer:
48 metres
Examiner Marking Tip: When calculating compound perimeters, count tick marks around the perimeter to ensure all 6 sides are included. Never accidentally add internal boundary lines!
Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE Open Awards Highfield
Question 3: Circular Patio Lighting
[3 Marks]

A circular patio has a diameter of 7 m. A decorative rope light is to be placed all the way around the outside edge of the patio.

Calculate the length of rope light required to 2 decimal places. Use π = 3.142.

Official Tutor Step-by-Step Mark Scheme
3 / 3 Marks Awarded
Step 1 (Circumference Formula) M1 Method Mark
Circumference = π × d = 3.142 × 7
Use the circumference formula for circles with given diameter (d = 7 m).
Step 2 (Perform Multiplication) M1 Method Mark
3.142 × 7 = 21.994 m
Calculate the raw product before rounding.
Step 3 (Rounding to 2 Decimal Places) A1 Accuracy Mark
21.994 → 21.99 m
Round to 2 decimal places. The third decimal place is 4 (< 5), so round down to 21.99 m.
Correct Final Answer:
21.99 metres
Examiner Marking Tip: When the exam states "Use π = 3.142", always type 3.142 into your calculator instead of pressing the π button. Pearson Edexcel mark schemes check against 3.142 arithmetic.
