Section A: Core Skills (Warm-Up) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Level 1 Linear Formula Substitution) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A medical courier firm calculates delivery charges (£) using the formula:
C = 18.50 + 0.45m, where m is the distance in miles.
Calculate the delivery charge for a journey of 40 miles.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Substitute m = 40 and multiply first: 0.45 × 40 = £18.00 [M1]
2. Add fixed fee: £18.50 + £18.00 = £36.50 [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: £36.50
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Adding 18.50 + 0.45 first (18.95) and multiplying the sum by 40.
🔄 Quick Check: (£36.50 - £18.50) ÷ 0.45 = £18.00 ÷ 0.45 = 40 miles.
Question 2 (Level 2 Rearranging / Inverse Formula) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
An industrial oven heats up according to the formula T = 22 + 14m, where T is the temperature in °C and m is the time in minutes.
How many minutes does it take for the oven to reach 204°C?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Subtract starting temperature: 204 - 22 = 182 [M1]
2. Divide by heating rate: 182 ÷ 14 = 13 minutes [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 13 minutes
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Dividing 204 by 14 before subtracting 22.
🔄 Quick Check: 22 + (14 × 13) = 22 + 182 = 204°C.
Section B: Everyday & Workplace Maths Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Level 1 Paediatric Clinical Dose Rule) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A hospital pharmacist uses Clark's Rule to calculate a child's medication dose:
Child Dose (mg) = (Child Weight in lbs × Adult Dose in mg) ÷ 150

Calculate the child dose for a child weighing 45 lbs when the adult dose is 500 mg.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Substitute values into numerator: 45 × 500 = 22,500 [M1]
2. Divide by 150: 22,500 ÷ 150 [P1]
3. Evaluate: 150 mg [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 150 mg
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Dividing 150 by 45 instead of following numerator ÷ 150.
🔄 Quick Check: (150 × 150) ÷ 500 = 22,500 ÷ 500 = 45 lbs.
Question 4 (Level 2 Stopping Distance Formula with Squaring) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
The stopping distance d (in feet) of a vehicle travelling at speed v (in mph) is modelled by the formula:
d = v + (v² ÷ 20)

Calculate the stopping distance when a driver is travelling at 30 mph.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Calculate v²: 30² = 30 × 30 = 900 [M1]
2. Divide by 20: 900 ÷ 20 = 45 [P1]
3. Add v (30): 30 + 45 = 75 feet [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 75 feet
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Calculating 30² as 30 × 2 = 60 instead of 30 × 30 = 900.
🔄 Quick Check: 30 + (900 ÷ 20) = 30 + 45 = 75 feet.
Section C: Exam-Style Problem Solving Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Level 2 Wind Turbine Power Output Formula) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
An energy engineer calculates the electrical power P (in kilowatts, kW) generated by a wind turbine using the formula:
P = 0.0006 × A × v³
where A = 400 m² is the blade sweep area and v is the wind speed in m/s.

Calculate the power generated when the wind speed is 10 m/s, and find the value of electricity produced in 5 hours if electricity is worth £0.25 per kWh.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Evaluate v³: 10³ = 10 × 10 × 10 = 1,000 [P1]
2. Substitute into formula: P = 0.0006 × 400 × 1,000 = 240 kW [M1]
3. Energy in 5 hours: 240 kW × 5 hours = 1,200 kWh [P1]
4. Value at £0.25/kWh: 1,200 × £0.25 = £300.00 [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: £300.00 (Power = 240 kW; Energy = 1,200 kWh)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Evaluating 10³ as 30 instead of 1,000.
🔄 Quick Check: £300 ÷ £0.25 = 1,200 kWh; 1,200 ÷ 5 = 240 kW.
Question 6 (Level 2 Crane Safe Working Load Radius Evaluation) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
The maximum safe load L (in tonnes) for a mobile crane at a horizontal boom radius r (in metres) is given by:
L = (180 ÷ r) - 4.5
A site manager needs to lift a prefabricated modular clinic unit weighing 10.2 tonnes at a horizontal radius of 12 metres.

Is it safe for the crane to lift this modular unit at a 12-metre radius? Show your substitution and state a clear conclusion.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Substitute r = 12 into formula: L = (180 ÷ 12) - 4.5 [P1]
2. Perform division first: 180 ÷ 12 = 15 [M1]
3. Subtract 4.5: L = 15 - 4.5 = 10.5 tonnes maximum safe load [P1]
4. Compare and conclude: 10.2 tonnes ≤ 10.5 tonnes. Yes, it is safe to lift the unit (with 0.3 tonnes margin). [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Yes, safe (Max safe load is 10.5 tonnes, which exceeds the 10.2-tonne unit by 0.3 tonnes)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Subtracting 12 - 4.5 = 7.5 before dividing 180.
🔄 Quick Check: (10.5 + 4.5) × 12 = 15 × 12 = 180.
