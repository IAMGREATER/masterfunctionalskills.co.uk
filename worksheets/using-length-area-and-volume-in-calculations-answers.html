Section A: Core Skills (Warm-Up) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Converting Linear Measurements Before Area Calculation) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A rectangular clinic corridor measures 15 metres long by 180 centimetres wide.
Calculate the floor area of the corridor in square metres (m²).
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Convert width from centimetres to metres first: 180 cm ÷ 100 = 1.8 m [M1]
2. Multiply length by width: 15 m × 1.8 m = 27 m² [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 27 m²
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Multiplying 15 × 180 = 2,700 without converting 180 cm to 1.8 m first.
🔄 Quick Check: 27 m² ÷ 15 m = 1.8 m = 180 cm.
Question 2 (Level 1 Foundation — 3 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A rectangular concrete base measures 5.0 m long, 4.0 m wide, and 15 cm deep. Calculate the volume of concrete required in cubic metres (m³).
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Convert 15 cm to metres: 15 ÷ 100 = 0.15 m. [M1]
2. Volume = 5.0 × 4.0 × 0.15 = 3.0 m³. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 3.0 m³ (or 3 m³)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Computing 15 m × 8 m × 6 cm = 720 m³ is 100 times too big!
🔄 Quick Check: 15 cm = 0.15 m; 20 m² × 0.15 m = 3.0 m³.
Section B: Everyday & Workplace Maths Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Hospital Consultation Room Vinyl Flooring Installation (Level 2 Core)) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A clinical consultation room floor measures 6.0 m long by 4.0 m wide. Non-slip vinyl tiles are sold in packs:
• Each pack covers 2.5 m² and costs £28.50.
• The installer adds 10% extra area for cutting wastage.
Calculate the total cost of the packs required to tile the room.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Calculate floor area: 6.0 m × 4.0 m = 24.0 m². [M1]
2. Add 10% cutting wastage: 24.0 m² × 1.10 = 26.4 m². [M1]
3. Divide by 2.5 m² pack coverage and round UP: 26.4 ÷ 2.5 = 10.56 packs → Round UP to 11 whole packs. [A1]
4. Calculate total cost: 11 packs × £28.50 = £313.50. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: £313.50 (11 packs required)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Convert cm to m (÷ 100) before multiplying.
🔄 Quick Check: Always round packs UP (10.56 → 11 packs) so you do not leave 1.4 m² of floor bare!
Question 4 (Workplace Application — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A rectangular fish tank measures 80 cm long, 40 cm wide, and 50 cm high. It is filled with water to 90% of its height. How many litres of water are in the tank? (1,000 cm³ = 1 litre).
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Water height = 90% of 50 cm = 45 cm. [M1]
2. Water volume = 80 × 40 × 45 = 144,000 cm³. [P1]
3. Convert to litres: 144,000 ÷ 1,000 = 144 litres. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 144 litres
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Rounding 14.4 bags down to 14 bags leaves the job unfinished.
🔄 Quick Check: Divide cm³ by 1,000 to convert directly into litres.
Section C: Exam-Style Problem Solving Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Car Park Gravel Resurfacing Depth, Tonnage & Bulk Bag Costing (Level 2 Multi-Step)) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A clinic resurfaces a rectangular overflow car park measuring 15 m long by 8 m wide with gravel laid to an even depth of 6 cm.
• 1 m³ of gravel weighs 1.6 tonnes.
• Gravel is sold in 0.8-tonne bulk bags costing £64.00 per bag.
Calculate the total cost of the bulk bags required.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Convert 6 cm depth into metres (÷ 100): 6 cm = 0.06 m. [B1]
2. Calculate volume of gravel in m³: Volume = 15 m × 8 m × 0.06 m = 7.2 m³. [M1]
3. Multiply volume by 1.6 tonnes per m³: Total weight = 7.2 × 1.6 = 11.52 tonnes. [M1]
4. Divide by 0.8 tonnes per bag and round UP: 11.52 ÷ 0.8 = 14.4 bags → Round UP to 15 bags. [M1]
5. Multiply 15 bags by £64.00: 15 × £64.00 = £960.00. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: £960.00 (15 bulk bags required for 11.52 tonnes / 7.2 m³)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: In construction and decorating, even .01 of a pack requires buying the next full pack.
🔄 Quick Check: Converting 6 cm to 0.06 m at Step 1 prevents the fatal error of calculating 15 × 8 × 6 = 720 m³!
Question 6 (Level 2 Multi-Step Problem Solving — 4 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A painter needs to paint a wall measuring 8.5 m long by 2.8 m high with 2 coats of paint. One tin of paint covers 12 m² and costs £21.50. Calculate the total cost of the paint tins required.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Wall area = 8.5 × 2.8 = 23.8 m². [M1]
2. Area for 2 coats = 23.8 × 2 = 47.6 m². [P1]
3. Tins needed = 47.6 ÷ 12 = 3.966... → Round UP to 4 tins. [P1]
4. Total cost = 4 × £21.50 = £86.00. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: £86.00 (4 tins)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Stating a conclusion without showing the full numerical comparison values.
🔄 Quick Check: Remember to double the wall area for 2 coats before dividing by 12 m²!
