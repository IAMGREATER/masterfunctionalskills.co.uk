Angles in Triangles, Quadrilaterals & Polygons Pearson Edexcel: Pearson Edexcel L1 & L2 Mathematics
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (Warm-Up) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Interior Angles of a Quadrilateral) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A four-sided hospital reception desk (quadrilateral) has three known interior angles measuring 90°, 115°, and 82°.
Calculate the size of the fourth interior angle.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Sum the three known angles: 90° + 115° + 82° = 287° [M1]
2. Subtract from 360° (sum of angles in a quadrilateral): 360° - 287° = 73° [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 73°
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Using 180° (triangle total) instead of 360° for a 4-sided shape.
🔄 Quick Check: 90° + 115° + 82° + 73° = 360°.
Question 2 (Level 1 Foundation — 2 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
An irregular four-sided parking bay (quadrilateral) has three interior angles measuring 90°, 115°, and 78°. Calculate the size of the fourth interior angle.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Sum of known angles = 90° + 115° + 78° = 283°. [M1]
2. Fourth angle = 360° − 283° = 77°. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 77°
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Doing 6 × 180° = 1,080° for a hexagon instead of (6 − 2) × 180° = 720°.
🔄 Quick Check: Angles in any 4-sided quadrilateral always add up to 360°.
Section B: Everyday & Workplace Maths Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Architectural Roof Gable Truss Angle Calculation (Level 1 Foundation)) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A timber roof truss forms an isosceles triangle. The top ridge angle between the two equal rafters is 76°.
Calculate the size of each of the two identical base angles at the eaves.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Recall total interior angle sum of a triangle: Interior angles of a triangle sum to 180°. [B1]
2. Subtract known ridge angle from 180°: 180° − 76° = 104° remaining for both base angles. [M1]
3. Divide remainder by 2 for the identical base angles: 104° ÷ 2 = 52°. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Each base angle = 52°
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Always subtract 2 from the number of sides first, or use the exterior angle method (360° ÷ n).
🔄 Quick Check: Check: 52° + 52° + 76° = 180°. Both base angles are equal because it is an isosceles triangle.
Question 4 (Workplace Application — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Calculate the size of:
(a) One exterior angle of a regular pentagon (5 sides).
(b) One interior angle of a regular pentagon.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Exterior angle = 360° ÷ 5 = 72°. [M1]
2. Interior angle = 180° − 72° = 108°. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) 72° | (b) 108°
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Diagrams rarely write "90°" in digits; they show a small square box in the corner.
🔄 Quick Check: Interior + Exterior at any vertex always lies on a straight line (180°).
Section C: Exam-Style Problem Solving Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Hexagonal Gazebo Paving & Mitre Saw Cut Angle (Level 2 Multi-Step)) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A carpenter builds a timber gazebo deck in the shape of a regular hexagon (6 sides).
(a) Calculate the total sum of the interior angles of the hexagon.
(b) Calculate the size of one interior corner angle.
(c) Each corner joint is made by mitre-cutting two timber beams equally so they meet at the corner angle. At what angle must each beam be cut?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Calculate interior angle sum: (n − 2) × 180°: (6 − 2) × 180° = 4 × 180° = 720°. [M1]
2. Divide by 6 sides to find one interior angle: 720° ÷ 6 = 120° (or Exterior = 360° ÷ 6 = 60°; Interior = 180° − 60° = 120°). [M1]
3. Halve the corner angle for the two mitred beams: 120° ÷ 2 = 60° per timber beam. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) 720° | (b) 120° | (c) 60° mitre cut per beam
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: As soon as you see a square corner symbol, write "90°" next to it on the diagram.
🔄 Quick Check: Using Exterior = 360° ÷ 6 = 60° then Interior = 180° − 60° = 120° is a lightning-fast check!
Question 6 (Level 2 Multi-Step Problem Solving — 3 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A right-angled triangular ramp has a 90° corner and an elevation angle of 18° at the base. Calculate the size of the top acute angle.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Sum of known angles = 90° + 18° = 108°. [M1]
2. Top angle = 180° − 108° = 72°. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 72°
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Stating a conclusion without showing the full numerical comparison values.
🔄 Quick Check: In a right-angled triangle, the two acute angles always add up to 90° (90° − 18° = 72°).
