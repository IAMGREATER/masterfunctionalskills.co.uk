Capacity & Liquid Volume Units Pearson Edexcel: L1.18 • L2.20 • L2.24
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Procedural Fluency Check Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Level 1 Millilitre Dispensing Cups) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A hospital catering urn holds 4.5 litres of hot water.
How many full 180 ml cups of tea can be poured from the urn?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Convert 4.5 litres to millilitres: 4.5 × 1,000 = 4,500 ml [M1]
2. Divide by 180 ml: 4,500 ÷ 180 = 25 cups [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 25 cups
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Dividing 4.5 by 180 without converting litres into millilitres.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: 25 × 180 ml = 4,500 ml = 4.5 litres.
Question 2 (Level 2 IV Infusion Flow Rate) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A patient is prescribed 1.2 litres of saline via an intravenous drip over 5 hours.
Calculate the required infusion pump flow rate in millilitres per hour (ml/hr).
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Convert 1.2 litres to millilitres: 1.2 × 1,000 = 1,200 ml [M1]
2. Divide by 5 hours: 1,200 ÷ 5 = 240 ml/hr [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 240 ml/hr
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Dividing 1.2 by 5 = 0.24 without converting to millilitres.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: 240 ml/hr × 5 hours = 1,200 ml = 1.2 L.
Section B: Workplace & Functional Scenarios Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Level 1 Disinfectant Dilution Ratio & Capacity) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A domestic supervisor mixes surface sanitiser using concentrate and water in the ratio 1 : 9.
She needs to fill 8 spray bottles, each with a capacity of 750 ml.

How many millilitres (ml) of neat concentrate does she need in total?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Total mixed solution required: 8 × 750 ml = 6,000 ml [M1]
2. Total ratio parts: 1 + 9 = 10 parts [P1]
3. Concentrate (1 part): 6,000 ml ÷ 10 = 600 ml [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 600 ml
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Dividing 6,000 ml by 9 instead of 10 total parts.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: 600 ml concentrate + 5,400 ml water = 6,000 ml = 8 × 750 ml.
Question 4 (Level 2 3D Cuboid Tank Volume to Litres) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A stainless-steel ultrasonic cleaning bath is a cuboid measuring 50 cm long, 30 cm wide, and 24 cm deep.
For safe operation, the bath is filled to 75% of its total depth.
Use: 1,000 cm³ = 1 litre.

How many litres of cleaning fluid are needed?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Calculate full volume (or 75% depth = 18 cm): 50 × 30 × 24 = 36,000 cm³ [M1]
2. Calculate 75% volume: 36,000 × 0.75 = 27,000 cm³ [P1]
3. Convert cm³ to litres (÷ 1,000): 27,000 ÷ 1,000 = 27 litres [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 27 litres
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Forgetting to multiply by 75% (giving 36 litres).
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: 50 cm × 30 cm × 18 cm (75% of 24 cm) = 27,000 cm³ = 27 L.
Section C: Pearson Edexcel Problem Solving & Justification Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Level 2 Ward 24-Hour Patient Fluid Balance Audit) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A nurse completes a 24-hour fluid balance chart for a post-operative patient:
• Fluid Intake: Two 1.25-litre IV saline bags; 3 cups of water (220 ml each); 1 bowl of soup (300 ml).
• Fluid Output: Morning urine 850 ml; Afternoon urine 920 ml; Overnight catheter bag 1.15 litres; Surgical wound drain 180 ml.

Calculate the patient's net fluid balance in millilitres (ml) (Intake minus Output) and state whether it is positive or negative.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Convert and sum Intake in ml: (2 × 1,250) + (3 × 220) + 300 = 2,500 + 660 + 300 = 3,460 ml [P1]
2. Convert and sum Output in ml: 850 + 920 + 1,150 + 180 = 3,100 ml [P1]
3. Subtract Output from Intake: 3,460 - 3,100 = +360 ml [M1]
4. State final balance: +360 ml (Positive fluid balance of 360 ml) [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: +360 ml (Positive fluid balance: 3,460 ml intake vs 3,100 ml output)
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Counting only one 1.25 L saline bag or forgetting to convert 1.15 L to 1,150 ml.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: 3,100 ml + 360 ml = 3,460 ml.
Question 6 (Level 2 Cylindrical Heating Oil Tank Refill Budget) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A rural care home has a cylindrical heating oil tank with a diameter of 1.6 metres and a length of 2.5 metres.
• The tank is currently 1/4 full.
• The manager orders enough oil to fill the tank to 90% of its total capacity.
• Heating oil costs 68p per litre.
• Use π = 3.14 and 1 m³ = 1,000 litres.

Calculate the cost of the heating oil order. Give your answer to the nearest penny.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Radius = 1.6 ÷ 2 = 0.8 m. Full tank volume = 3.14 × 0.8² × 2.5 = 3.14 × 0.64 × 2.5 = 5.024 m³ = 5,024 litres [P1]
2. Percentage of capacity to add: 90% - 25% (1/4) = 65% of full capacity [M1]
3. Litres required: 5,024 × 0.65 = 3,265.6 litres [P1]
4. Total cost at £0.68/L: 3,265.6 × £0.68 = £2,220.608 → £2,220.61 [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: £2,220.61 (3,265.6 litres needed)
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Using diameter (1.6 m) instead of radius (0.8 m) in the cylinder volume formula.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: 90% of 5,024 L = 4,521.6 L; 25% = 1,256 L; 4,521.6 - 1,256 = 3,265.6 L × £0.68 = £2,220.61.
