Comparing Texts & Contrasting Perspectives Pearson Edexcel: L1.10 • L2.12 • L2.15
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (The Basics) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Level 1 Identifying a Shared Similarity) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Read the two extracts about a hospital car park:
Text A (Estates Notice): 'Due to severe morning congestion, the South Car Park will become staff-only from 1st November, with 80 new spaces added.'
Text B (Patient Forum): 'Everyone knows morning congestion at the hospital is awful, but banning patients from the South Car Park will make us late for appointments!'

Identify one point of agreement between Text A and Text B, and quote a phrase from each text to prove it.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify shared agreement: Both texts agree that morning traffic/parking congestion at the hospital is severe [A1]
2. Supporting quotes: Text A ('severe morning congestion') and Text B ('morning congestion at the hospital is awful') [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Both agree morning parking congestion is bad ('severe morning congestion' / 'morning congestion... is awful').
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Stating a difference ( banning patients) when the question specifically asks for a point of agreement.
🔄 Quick Check: Check both quotes refer to the exact same issue (morning congestion).
Question 2 (Level 1 Identifying Contrasting Viewpoints) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Read the two extracts on cashless supermarket checkouts:
Text A (Store Manager): 'Card-only self-checkouts halve queue waiting times and eliminate till cash-counting mistakes at closing time.'
Text B (Senior Citizens' Group): 'Card-only rules exclude pensioners who rely on physical cash notes to budget their weekly pension.'

Using a comparative connective (e.g. whereas), explain how Text A and Text B disagree about card-only checkouts.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. State Text A's positive operational view: Halves queue times and stops cash-counting errors [A1]
2. Link with 'whereas/in contrast' to Text B's negative social view: Excludes pensioners who budget using physical cash [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Text A praises card-only tills for halving queues and stopping cash errors, whereas Text B criticises them for excluding pensioners who budget with cash.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Writing two separate sentences without a comparative connective linking the viewpoints.
🔄 Quick Check: Does your sentence contain 'whereas', 'however', or 'in contrast'?
Section B: Real-Life & Workplace Practice Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Level 1/2 Comparing Factual Claims Across Workplace Texts) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Read the two extracts on open-plan offices:
Text A (Facilities Architect): 'Removing partition walls cuts office heating bills by 25% and encourages spontaneous teamwork between departments.'
Text B (Staff Survey Summary): 'While staff agree that cross-department teamwork has improved, 68% complain that constant phone noise ruins their concentration.'

Identify one similarity and two differences in the information given by Text A and Text B.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Similarity: Both texts agree that open-plan offices improve teamwork between departments [A1]
2. Difference 1 (Focus/Benefit): Text A highlights a 25% saving on heating bills, which Text B does not mention [A1]
3. Difference 2 (Drawback): Text B highlights that 68% of staff suffer ruined concentration from noise, whereas Text A only presents positives [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Similarity: Both agree teamwork improves. Diff 1: Text A cites a 25% heating saving. Diff 2: Text B reports 68% of staff lose concentration due to noise.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Giving only one difference when the 3-mark tariff requires one similarity and two differences.
🔄 Quick Check: 1 similarity + 2 differences = 3 marks.
Question 4 (Level 2 Comparing Writer Tone and Attitude) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Read the two extracts on a proposed four-day working week:
Text A (HR Pilot Report): 'The six-month trial demonstrated a 40% reduction in staff sick leave while maintaining 100% customer service output.'
Text B (Small Business Owner): 'This reckless fad will bankrupt high-street shops that cannot afford to pay full-time wages for part-time hours!'

Compare the tone of Text A and Text B, supporting your answer with one quotation from each text.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify Text A tone & quote: Objective, formal, and measured tone ('demonstrated a 40% reduction in staff sick leave') [A1]
2. Identify Text B tone & quote: Alarmist, angry, and emotive tone ('reckless fad will bankrupt high-street shops!') [A1]
3. Use explicit comparative phrasing contrasting data-led calmness with emotional outrage [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Text A uses a formal, objective tone backed by statistics ('40% reduction'), whereas Text B uses an angry, alarmist tone with emotive language ('reckless fad will bankrupt...').
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Describing what the texts are about without naming the tone (objective vs alarmist/angry).
🔄 Quick Check: Have you named the tone AND copied a short quote for both Text A and Text B?
Section C: Exam-Style Tasks Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Level 2 Comparing Language Techniques Across Two Texts) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Text A (Electric Fleet Press Release):
'Switching to zero-emission vans has cut our depot carbon footprint by 120 tonnes and lowered maintenance costs by 30%.'

Text B (Delivery Driver Blog):
'Being stranded on a freezing motorway with a dead battery is every driver's worst nightmare! How can we deliver on time when chargers are always broken?'

Compare how the writers of Text A and Text B use language techniques to convey their views on electric vans.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Text A View & Technique 1: Positive view supported by statistics/numerical facts ('120 tonnes', '30%') [A1]
2. Text A Technique 2: Professional, factual corporate diction ('zero-emission vans', 'lowered maintenance costs') to build logical credibility [A1]
3. Text B View & Technique 1: Negative/frustrated view using emotive language and hyperbole ('freezing motorway', 'worst nightmare!') [A1]
4. Text B Technique 2: Rhetorical question ('How can we deliver on time when chargers are always broken?') to challenge management [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Text A promotes electric vans using factual statistics ('120 tonnes', '30%') in a professional register, whereas Text B criticises their reliability using emotive hyperbole ('worst nightmare!') and a rhetorical question ('How can we deliver on time...?').
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Listing techniques from Text A only, or forgetting to explain how the techniques reflect contrasting views.
🔄 Quick Check: Verify at least two techniques/quotes from Text A are contrasted against two from Text B.
Question 6 (Level 2 Comparing Three Sources) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Read all three extracts on night-shift working:
Text A (Medical Journal): 'Rotating night shifts disrupt natural circadian rhythms and increase long-term cardiovascular risk by 19%.'
Text B (Warehouse Worker): 'The 25% night-shift pay premium adds £350 a month to my wages, and the roads are completely traffic-free at 5 a.m.'
Text C (Union Advisor): 'Night workers suffer higher fatigue rates, though free hot meals on night shifts significantly boost morale.'

Using evidence from all three texts, explain the advantages and disadvantages of night-shift work.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Disadvantage from Text A: Disrupts circadian rhythms and raises cardiovascular risk by 19% [A1]
2. Disadvantage from Text C: Higher fatigue rates among night workers [A1]
3. Advantage from Text B: 25% pay premium (£350 extra per month) and traffic-free commuting at 5 a.m. [A1]
4. Advantage from Text C: Free hot meals boost staff morale [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Disadvantages: Disrupts sleep/circadian rhythms and increases heart risk by 19% (Text A), plus higher fatigue (Text C). Advantages: £350/month (25%) extra pay and traffic-free travel (Text B), plus morale-boosting free hot meals (Text C).
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Omitting one of the three texts — you must draw evidence from Text A, Text B, and Text C to gain all 4 marks.
🔄 Quick Check: Did you cite Text A, Text B, and Text C explicitly?
