Question 1 Worked Solution NHS Hospital Ward Stock Tracking
[3 Marks Available]
A healthcare ward starts Monday with 450 boxes of nitrile gloves.
During the week, the deliveries and usage are:
• Tuesday: Used 165 boxes
• Wednesday: New delivery received of 240 boxes
• Thursday: Used 195 boxes
• Friday: Used 110 boxes
Calculate how many boxes of gloves remain on the ward at the end of Friday.
Calculate total stock added: 450 + 240 = 690 boxes
[M1]
Calculate total stock used: 165 + 195 + 110 = 470 boxes
[M1]
Calculate remaining stock: 690 - 470 = 220 boxes
[A1]
Correct Final Answer: 220 boxes
Pearson Reverse Check Method: 220 remaining + 470 used = 690 total.
Common Exam Trap / Pitfall: Adding used boxes or subtracting deliveries.
Question 2 Worked Solution Commercial Trade Cash Float
[3 Marks Available]
A retail pharmacy assistant counts the cash drawer float at the end of the day.
The opening float was £150.00.
Cash sales recorded through the till total £1,248.50.
Cash paid out for emergency delivery postage was £24.80.
When counting the cash drawer, the assistant finds £1,370.00.
Is the cash drawer balanced, over, or short? State the exact amount of difference.
Expected total = Opening float (£150.00) + Sales (£1,248.50) - Payout (£24.80)
[M1]
Expected cash = £1,398.50 - £24.80 = £1,373.70
[M1]
Compare actual (£1,370.00) with expected (£1,373.70): £1,370.00 - £1,373.70 = -£3.70 (Short by £3.70)
[A1]
Correct Final Answer: Short by £3.70 (Expected £1,373.70, actual £1,370.00)
Pearson Reverse Check Method: £1,370.00 + £3.70 = £1,373.70.
Common Exam Trap / Pitfall: Forgetting to add the opening float to the expected total.
