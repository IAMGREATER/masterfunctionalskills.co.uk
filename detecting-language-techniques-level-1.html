Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.12 – Identify and analyse how language features and rhetorical devices convey meaning and influence the reader.

Golden Rules for Detecting Language Techniques & Rhetorical Devices

Golden Rule 1: The 3-Part Formula (Name -> Quote -> Specific Impact)

Simply writing 'Rhetorical question' earns at most 1 mark. To secure full marks on 2-mark and 3-mark language questions, state the technique name, copy the exact short quotation, and explain how it makes the reader think or feel about that specific topic.

Golden Rule / Technique: Formula: Technique Name + Exact Quote + Specific Psychological Effect
Golden Rule 2: Memorize the PERSUADE Mnemonic

P: Personal Pronouns / Direct Address ('you', 'we') • E: Emotive Language • R: Rhetorical Questions • S: Statistics & Facts • U: Urgent Imperative Verbs • A: Alliteration • D: Metaphor / Simile (Descriptive Imagery) • E: Exaggeration (Hyperbole) & Rule of Three.

Golden Rule / Technique: PERSUADE covers 95% of all language features tested in Functional Skills.

Core Skill 1: Rhetorical Devices & Reader Impact Master Glossary

The essential language techniques assessed across Pearson Edexcel, City & Guilds, and NCFE papers.

Language Device Vocational Example Specific Effect on the Reader
Rule of Three (Triad) "Safe, reliable, and compassionate care." Creates a rhythmic, memorable summary that convinces the reader the service is thorough and complete.
Direct Address ('You') "Your vigilance can prevent warehouse accidents." Speaks directly to the individual reader, placing personal moral responsibility on them to act safely.
Rhetorical Question "Can our wards afford another winter of shortages?" Forces the reader to mentally supply the obvious answer ('No'), compelling agreement with the writer.
Hyperbole (Exaggeration) "Staff are drowning in mountains of pointless admin." Dramatises the scale of staff workload to provoke sympathy and urgent management reform.
Imperative Verbs "Isolate the circuit before removing the panel." Delivers an authoritative, unambiguous command that leaves no room for hesitation in hazardous tasks.
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Decoding Figurative Language: Metaphors, Similes & Idioms

How to explain non-literal imagery clearly in Level 1 Reading answers.

Level 1 texts frequently use figurative imagery to make abstract workplace issues vivid and memorable:

  • Metaphor (states one thing IS another): "Community pharmacies are the front line of winter healthcare." → Compares pharmacies to a military battlefront, emphasising how vital and pressured pharmacists are in protecting hospitals.
  • Simile (compares using 'like' or 'as'): "New agency staff are treated like disposable parts." → Highlights how dehumanised and easily replaceable temporary workers feel.
  • Idiom (established figurative phrase): "Cutting maintenance budgets is a ticking time bomb." → Conveys that ignoring repairs will inevitably result in a sudden, catastrophic breakdown.
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Upgrading Vague Effect Answers into Full-Mark Explanations

Side-by-side comparison of zero-mark clichés versus accredited Pearson Edexcel explanations.

0-Mark Vague Clichés (Avoid!)

  • "It makes the reader want to read on."
  • "It sticks in the reader's head."
  • "It makes the text more interesting and punchy."

Full-Mark Specific Effects (Use!)

  • "It provokes guilt about wasted food and urges households to compost."
  • "It builds institutional credibility by proving the training cut accidents by 40%."
  • "It creates a sense of shared collective duty ('we', 'our') among staff."
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 2 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Reading Level 1)

Vocational Context: Local Blood Donation Drive

Read the poster extract:
'Every blood donation can save up to three lives! Book your 45-minute appointment at the Town Hall this Friday and become a local lifesaver.'
Identify two language features used in this extract and give the matching quote for each. [2 Marks]
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Identify Feature 1 and quote
Numerical fact / statistic + exclamation: 'Every blood donation can save up to three lives!'
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Feature 1]
Step 2 Identify Feature 2 and quote
Imperative command / direct address / alliteration: 'Book your 45-minute appointment' or 'local lifesaver'.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Feature 2]
Final Accredited Response: 1. Statistic / Fact: 'save up to three lives'. | 2. Direct address / Imperative command: 'Book your 45-minute appointment' (or alliteration: 'local lifesaver').
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Always pair the name of the language feature with the exact words quoted from the extract.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Confusing Layout Devices with Language Features
Listing 'bullet points', 'subheadings', or 'bold text' when asked for a language technique scores zero. Those are presentational/layout devices.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Language techniques relate strictly to words, figures of speech, and sentence grammar (metaphors, alliteration, rhetorical questions, rule of three).
Examiner Pitfall: Quoting a Whole Paragraph Instead of the Exact Device
If you identify 'alliteration' and quote a 25-word sentence, the examiner cannot tell which two words you think are alliterative.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Quote only the crisp 2–6 words that contain the technique (e.g., 'caring colleagues collapse').

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A warehouse safety poster displays the slogan: 'Stop. Look. Listen. Protect your life around forklifts today.'
Name one language device used in the slogan and explain its persuasive effect on warehouse operators.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Device: Rule of three / short imperative verbs ('Stop. Look. Listen.') or direct address ('Protect your life') [1 Mark].
2. Effect: Delivers punchy, authoritative commands that cut through workplace distraction and force operators to check for moving forklifts immediately [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Device: Imperative verbs / Rule of three ('Stop. Look. Listen.'). | Effect: Delivers urgent, memorable commands that prompt workers to pause and check for hazards immediately.
Examiner Tip: Short one-word commands are called imperative verbs or minor sentences.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Read this extract from a coastal conservation leaflet:
'Every single minute, a rubbish truck of plastic is dumped into our suffocating oceans. Will you help us turn the tide on marine pollution before our beaches become toxic wastelands?'
Identify two language features with quotes [2 Marks] and explain how they persuade the reader [1 Mark].
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Feature 1 & Quote: Emotive personification/metaphor ('suffocating oceans' / 'toxic wastelands') or shocking statistic ('a rubbish truck of plastic every single minute') [1 Mark].
2. Feature 2 & Quote: Rhetorical question / direct address ('Will you help us turn the tide...?') [1 Mark].
3. Effect: Creates shock and alarm at the scale of ocean damage and directly challenges the individual reader to join the clean-up effort [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Features: 1. Emotive imagery ('suffocating oceans' / 'toxic wastelands'). 2. Rhetorical question with direct address ('Will you help us turn the tide...?'). Effect: Shocks the reader with vivid imagery of environmental harm and places personal responsibility on them to act.
Examiner Tip: Always connect the imagery ('suffocating oceans') to the emotional response it triggers (alarm/guilt).
Exam Practice Question 3 2 Marks
Read this sentence from an apprenticeship recruitment brochure:
'Earn while you learn: gain practical skills, industry qualifications, and debt-free confidence.'
Identify two language techniques used in this sentence.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Technique 1: Internal rhyme / imperative catchphrase ('Earn while you learn') or direct address ('you') [1 Mark].
2. Technique 2: Rule of three ('practical skills, industry qualifications, and debt-free confidence') [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: 1. Direct address / internal rhyme ('Earn while you learn'). | 2. Rule of three ('practical skills, industry qualifications, and debt-free confidence').
Examiner Tip: Look out for three noun phrases separated by commas — that is a classic Rule of Three!
