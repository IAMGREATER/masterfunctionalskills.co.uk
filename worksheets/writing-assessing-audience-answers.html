Audience, Tone & Register Pearson Edexcel: Pearson Edexcel Functional Skills English (WRITING)
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (The Basics) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Adapting Register & Salutation to Match the Target Audience) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
State the correct opening salutation and closing sign-off for:
(a) A formal complaint letter to an unnamed Chief Executive (`Dear Sir or Madam,`)
(b) A formal job application letter to a named recruiting manager (`Dear Ms Patel,`)
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. (a) Unnamed recipient ('Dear Sir or Madam,'): Close with 'Yours faithfully,' [A1]
2. (b) Named recipient ('Dear Ms Patel,'): Close with 'Yours sincerely,' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) Yours faithfully, | (b) Yours sincerely,
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Pairing 'Dear Ms Patel' with 'Yours faithfully' (Rule: Never put two S's together — Sir/Madam = Faithfully; Named = Sincerely).
🔄 Quick Check: Sir/Madam → Faithfully; Surname → Sincerely.
Question 2 (Core Skill Application — 2 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Explain why writing: 'Hey guys, grab your big coats cos the ward heating is busted today lol' is inappropriate for an official hospital staff email memo, and rewrite it in an appropriate professional register.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Critique: Identify the casual slang ('Hey guys', 'cos', 'busted', 'lol') which is unprofessional for clinical workplace records. [M1]
2. Rewrite: Provide a clear, polite semi-formal staff advisory. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Critique: Uses informal slang ('cos', 'busted', 'lol') and casual greeting ('Hey guys') which undermine professional communication.
Professional Rewrite: 'Please be advised that the Ward 4 heating system is currently undergoing emergency repair; staff are permitted to wear plain navy thermal fleeces during today's shift.'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Starting formally with 'Dear Sir or Madam, I am writing to complain...' and then slipping into 'Anyway, it's a total rip-off and you need to sort it ASAP' in Paragraph 3 ruins register consistency.
🔄 Quick Check: Always provide both the brief explanation of why the original fails and the complete rewritten sentence.
Section B: Real-Life & Workplace Practice Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Level 1 Model: Courteous Email to Colleagues About Shared Kitchen Hygiene) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Rewrite this rude note left on the staffroom fridge as a polite, semi-formal email to all office staff:
'Whoever keeps leaving dirty mugs in the sink and mouldy milk in the fridge needs to grow up! Clean up your own mess or the manager will ban us from using the kitchen!'
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Replace hostile accusations with a collaborative team greeting: Use a constructive subject line and address colleagues collectively without pointing fingers. [A1]
2. State the two hygiene rules clearly and politely: Ask staff to place used mugs directly into the dishwasher and dispose of expired milk every Friday. [A1]
3. Explain the shared benefit for the team: Keeping the breakroom clean ensures a pleasant environment for everyone during lunch breaks. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: To: All Office Staff
Subject: Keeping Our Shared Staff Kitchen Clean and Tidy

Dear Everyone,

As our staff kitchen is busy during lunch breaks, please could we all work together to keep the area clean and hygienic?

Please rinse your mugs and place them straight into the dishwasher after use rather than leaving them in the sink. Additionally, our cleaning team will empty the refrigerator every Friday at 16:30, so please label your food containers and remove any expired milk before the weekend.

Thank you for your cooperation in keeping our breakroom pleasant for the whole team.

Best regards,
Office Facilities Team
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: During your final 5-minute proofread, check that your last paragraph is just as formal and controlled as your first.
🔄 Quick Check: Notice how using inclusive pronouns ('our kitchen', 'work together') achieves full compliance without alienating colleagues.
Question 4 (Exam-Style Task — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Rewrite this overly aggressive complaint paragraph into an assertive, formal Level 2 register suitable for a letter to a Training Provider Director:
'Your tutor didn't bother turning up for our first-aid exam on Tuesday and nobody even phoned us! Seven of my staff wasted a whole morning sitting in an empty room! You better give us our £700 back today!'
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Replace emotional accusations ('didn't bother turning up', 'wasted a whole morning') with objective facts. [M1]
2. Eliminate exclamation marks and aggressive demands ('You better give us'). [M1]
3. State the financial impact and formal request for a full £700 refund or rescheduled sitting. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'Regrettably, your assigned assessor failed to attend our scheduled First-Aid at Work examination on Tuesday 10 October, and no prior cancellation notice was communicated to our office. Consequently, seven members of our care team were removed from clinical duties for an entire morning without achieving their certification. I therefore request an immediate full refund of the £700 booking fee or a priority rescheduled assessment at our premises within seven days.'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Writing 'u', 'asap', 'cos', or ending sentences with '!!' in a Functional Skills exam immediately caps your SPaG and register marks.
🔄 Quick Check: Words like 'Regrettably' and 'Consequently' allow you to express strong dissatisfaction while remaining 100% professional.
Section C: Exam-Style Tasks Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Level 2 Model: Council Environmental Health Formal Complaint) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Draft the opening two paragraphs of a formal complaint letter to Ms Henderson, Head of Environmental Services, regarding commercial fly-tipping behind a residential care centre.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Select formal salutation and state immediate grievance: Use formal greeting ('Dear Ms Henderson,') and authoritative opening sentence stating exact location. [A1]
2. Maintain dignified, objective complaint register: Describe the hazard factually using precise vocabulary ('obstructing emergency access', 'attracting vermin') without slang or exclamation marks. [A1]
3. Specify requested statutory action and timeframe: Request an Environmental Health enforcement officer visit and clearance within 48 hours. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Dear Ms Henderson,

I am writing in my capacity as Facilities Manager at St Mary’s Residential Care Centre to register an urgent formal complaint regarding persistent commercial fly-tipping in the service lane adjacent to our rear fire exits.

Over the past fortnight, unknown contractors have deposited broken plasterboard, rusted metal sheeting, and unsealed paint canisters across the access driveway. Not only does this hazardous waste attract vermin within metres of our kitchen store, but it also severely obstructs emergency ambulance access for our frail residents. I therefore urge your department to dispatch an enforcement crew to clear the lane within 48 hours and install CCTV signage to deter further offences.

Yours sincerely,
David Clarke, Facilities Manager
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Write every word out in full Standard English and use a single full stop at the end of declarative sentences.
🔄 Quick Check: Notice how the tone is firm, urgent, and authoritative without ever becoming rude. Examiners reward precise vocabulary ('in my capacity as', 'obstructs emergency ambulance access').
Question 6 (Level 2 Mastery Challenge — 3 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Adapt this technical clinical instruction so it is warm, accessible, and easy to understand for an anxious patient reading a hospital pre-admission leaflet:
'Patients scheduled for general anaesthesia must maintain nil-by-mouth status from 00:00 prior to admission; non-compliance will result in immediate cancellation of the surgical procedure.'
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Translate technical jargon ('general anaesthesia', 'nil-by-mouth status', 'non-compliance') into plain, reassuring English. [M1]
2. Explain clearly that patients must not eat food or drink liquids after midnight. [M1]
3. Explain gently why this rule keeps them safe during their operation. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'To keep you completely safe during your operation, it is essential that your stomach is empty before you are given your anaesthetic. Please do not eat any food or drink any liquids (including water, tea, or chewing gum) after midnight on the night before your surgery. If you accidentally eat or drink after midnight, we may need to rearrange your operation for another day for your own safety.'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Failing to proofread spelling, punctuation, and grammar after completing the draft.
🔄 Quick Check: Explaining the safety reason ('so your stomach is empty') makes public leaflets reassuring rather than intimidating.
