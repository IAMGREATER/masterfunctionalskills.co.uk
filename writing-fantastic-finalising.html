Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.30 / L2.30 – Proofread and revise written work systematically, identifying and correcting errors in spelling, punctuation, and grammar.
  • L2.31 – Critically edit extended responses to improve register consistency, syntactic variety, and adherence to exam criteria.

Golden Rules for Proofreading, Editing & Error Hunting

Golden Rule 1: Reserve 5 Minutes After Each Task Strictly for Proofreading

Never write right up to the final second. Candidates who stop writing 5 minutes before the end of each task and run a structured 4-pass audit catch an average of 5 to 7 SPaG errors—often the exact difference between a narrow Fail and a clear Pass.

Golden Rule / Technique: 25 mins Plan & Write + 5 mins Dedicated Error Hunt = Protected SPaG Marks
Golden Rule 2: Separate Your Content Read-Through from Your Spelling Scan

When you read normally, your brain auto-corrects typos because it focuses on meaning. Do one pass reading sentences aloud in your head for grammar/punctuation, and a second pass scanning individual long words and homophones visually.

Golden Rule / Technique: Pass 1: Format & Prompt Check | Pass 2: Sentence Boundaries | Pass 3: Homophones & Spelling

Core Skill 1: The 4-Pass 5-Minute Exam Proofreading Protocol

Follow this exact sequence in the final 5 minutes of Task 1 and Task 2.

  1. Pass 1 — Format & Bullet Audit (60 seconds): Check your top headers and sign-off (e.g. Dear Mr... → Yours sincerely; Report Title + Subheadings; Email To/Subject). Count that all 3 or 4 prompt bullets are covered.
  2. Pass 2 — Comma Splice & Sentence Boundary Check (90 seconds): Look at every comma in your draft. If there is a complete sentence before the comma AND a complete sentence after the comma without and/but/so, change that comma to a semicolon (;) or full stop!
  3. Pass 3 — Capital Letters & Apostrophes (90 seconds): Check that every sentence starts with a capital, every proper noun (days, months, names, street names, 'I') is capitalised, and every possessive noun has its apostrophe (while its has none).
  4. Pass 4 — High-Risk Spelling & Homophone Scan (60 seconds): Check every instance of their/there/they're, to/too, practice/practise, and check long words against the exam paper.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Before & After: Annotated Proofreading Pass on a Student Draft

Watch how catching 6 small slips upgrades a Band 1 SPaG draft to Band 3.

Unchecked Student Draft (6 Hidden SPaG Errors):

"On tuesday morning i visited oakwood clinic for my blood test, the waiting room was freezing cold. Neither of the receptionists were helpful when Staff asked for there coats."

Post-Proofread Final Version (All 6 Errors Fixed):

"On Tuesday morning, I visited Oakwood Clinic for my blood test; the waiting room was freezing cold. Neither of the receptionists was helpful when staff asked for their coats."

Core Skill 3: Editing for Conciseness & Word Count Control

How to trim a bloated draft or expand a thin paragraph during your review.

  • If Your Draft Is Too Short (<200 words at L2): Add a specific real-world example or statistic to Paragraph 2 or 3 (e.g. "For instance, a recent staff survey revealed that 72% of evening workers...").
  • If Your Draft Is Repetitive: Delete duplicate adjectives (e.g. replace "very unique and completely essential" with "essential") and cut repeated points.

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 Level 1 Proofreading Benchmark (3 Marks)

Vocational Context: Customer Service Email Audit

Proofread this short customer email and correct the three errors (1 capital letter error, 1 homophone error, and 1 missing question mark):
'Dear Mrs Carter, I am sorry that your order did not arrive on wednesday. Our courier left the parcel at there local depot by mistake. Would you prefer us to redeliver the item tomorrow morning.'
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Fix the missing proper noun capital letter
Days of the week are proper nouns: change 'wednesday' to 'Wednesday'.
Criteria: Capitalisation (L1.30)
Step 2 Fix the homophone error
'at there local depot' requires the possessive pronoun 'their'.
Criteria: Homophone Correction
Step 3 Fix the terminal punctuation on the closing question
'Would you prefer...' is a direct question requiring a question mark (?) instead of a full stop.
Criteria: Question Mark Accuracy
Final Accredited Response: 1. wednesday → Wednesday | 2. there → their | 3. tomorrow morning. → tomorrow morning?
Corrected email: 'Dear Mrs Carter, I am sorry that your order did not arrive on Wednesday. Our courier left the parcel at their local depot by mistake. Would you prefer us to redeliver the item tomorrow morning?'
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Direct questions starting with 'Would you', 'Could you', or 'How can we' frequently lose marks because candidates forget the question mark at the end!
Benchmark Exam Model 2 Level 2 Proofreading & Editing Benchmark (4 Marks)

Vocational Context: Human Resources & Safeguarding Compliance

Proofread this draft internal email and identify and fix four distinct errors (1 format error, 1 punctuation error, 1 spelling/homophone error, 1 grammar error):

From: hr@communityhealth.org
Date: 14 October 2026

Dear All Staff,
All clinical staff must complete there annual safeguarding refresher by Friday, failure to do so will result in temporary suspension from ward duties. Each of the ward supervisors are responsible for logging team certificates.
Yours faithfully,
Karen White, HR Director
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Format Error
The email is missing a mandatory To: and Subject: line, and uses 'Yours faithfully' instead of 'Kind regards' or 'Yours sincerely'.
Criteria: Format Audit (L2.31)
Step 2 Punctuation Error (Comma Splice)
Comma splice between 'by Friday,' and 'failure to do so'. Replace the comma with a semicolon (;) or full stop.
Criteria: Punctuation Audit (L2.30)
Step 3 Spelling / Homophone Error
'complete there annual safeguarding' uses location 'there' instead of possessive 'their'.
Criteria: Spelling Audit
Step 4 Grammar Error (Subject-Verb Agreement)
'Each of the ward supervisors are' must be 'Each of the ward supervisors is' because 'Each' is singular.
Criteria: Grammar Audit
Final Accredited Response: 1. Format: Add 'Subject: Mandatory Annual Safeguarding Refresher' and change sign-off to 'Kind regards,'.
2. Punctuation: Fix comma splice after 'Friday' with a semicolon ('...by Friday; failure to do so...').
3. Spelling: Change 'there' to 'their'.
4. Grammar: Change 'Each of the ward supervisors are' to 'Each of the ward supervisors is'.
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Practising this 4-category scan (Format → Punctuation → Spelling → Grammar) trains your eye to spot every single examiner deduction before you click Submit.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Relying on Onscreen Exam Software to Auto-Capitalise or Spellcheck
Unlike Microsoft Word or smartphone keyboards, Pearson Edexcel and City & Guilds onscreen exam portals do NOT auto-capitalise 'i' to 'I' or underline spelling errors in red!
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Manually press Shift for every capital 'I', every sentence start, and every proper noun.
Examiner Pitfall: Leaving Missing Words Out When Typing Fast
Under exam pressure, your thoughts move faster than your fingers, leading to skipped words like 'I am writing [to] complain about [the] service'.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Point at each word on the screen with your cursor during your final read-through so you don't skip missing prepositions or articles.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Proofread this sentence and correct both errors:
'The doctors surgery closed it's doors early yesterday because of severe flooding.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Error 1: 'doctors surgery' is missing the possessive apostrophe → doctor's surgery (or doctors' surgery).
2. Error 2: 'it's doors' uses the contraction apostrophe instead of possessive pronoun 'its'.
Final Model Answer: 'The doctor's surgery closed its doors early yesterday because of severe flooding.'
Examiner Tip: Whenever you see two apostrophe words in the same sentence, check each one carefully: nouns need 's for possession, whereas 'its' never takes an apostrophe for possession.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Find and correct the three errors in this sentence from a job application letter:
'Having worked in retail for three years i have developed strong customer service skills, in addition I am always punctual and realiable.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Missing introductory comma and lowercase pronoun 'i': change 'years i' to 'years, I'.
2. Comma splice before 'in addition': change ', in addition' to '; in addition,' (or '. In addition,').
3. Spelling error: change 'realiable' to 'reliable'.
Final Model Answer: 'Having worked in retail for three years, I have developed strong customer service skills; in addition, I am always punctual and reliable.'
Examiner Tip: Never leave a lowercase 'i' anywhere in your exam response—it is one of the first things examiners penalise.
Exam Practice Question 3 4 Marks
A candidate wrote this closing paragraph to a named recipient (Dear Mr Thornton) at the end of a formal complaint letter:
'I hope you can sort this mess out soon cos we are fed up of waiting, i look forward to hearing from you.
Yours faithfully,
Sam'

Rewrite this closing paragraph and sign-off so that it achieves full marks for formal register, punctuation, and letter conventions.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Replace informal slang ('sort this mess out', 'cos', 'fed up of waiting') with formal business language.
2. Fix the comma splice and lowercase 'i' ('...waiting, i look...').
3. Change 'Yours faithfully' to 'Yours sincerely' (to match named recipient Mr Thornton) and provide a full name.
Final Model Answer: 'I trust that you will investigate and resolve this matter promptly, as this ongoing delay is causing significant inconvenience. I look forward to receiving your written response within seven days.

Yours sincerely,
Samira Begum'
Examiner Tip: Always sign off formal letters with both your first name and surname, and ensure 'Dear Mr Thornton' pairs with 'Yours sincerely'.
