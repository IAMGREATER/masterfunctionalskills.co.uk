Writing Exam Strategy, Allocation & Word Counts Pearson Edexcel: Pearson Edexcel Functional Skills English (WRITING)
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (The Basics) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Exam Time Allocation & SPaG Mark Weighting) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
In the 60-minute Pearson Edexcel Writing exam (36 marks total), Task 1 is a shorter functional task (15 marks) and Task 2 is an extended task (21 marks).
State what percentage of total Writing marks is awarded solely for Spelling, Punctuation, and Grammar (SPaG), and how many minutes you should allocate to Task 1 vs Task 2.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. SPaG weighting: Approx. 42% of total marks (15 out of 36 marks across both tasks) [A1]
2. Optimal 60-minute split: 25 minutes on Task 1 (15 marks) and 35 minutes on Task 2 (21 marks), including 5 minutes proofreading each [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 42% (15/36 marks) for SPaG; 25 minutes for Task 1 and 35 minutes for Task 2.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Spending 45 minutes on Task 1 and running out of time on the higher-tariff 21-mark Task 2.
🔄 Quick Check: 25 mins + 35 mins = 60 mins total exam time.
Question 2 (Core Skill Application — 2 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
In a 60-minute Writing exam where Task 1 is worth 21 marks (250–300 words) and Task 2 is worth 15 marks (200–250 words), how should you divide your 60 minutes, and why?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Allocate approximately 33–35 minutes to Task 1 (more marks and higher word count) and 25–27 minutes to Task 2. [M1]
2. Ensure both tasks include 4–5 minutes of planning and 4–5 minutes of proofreading. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Allocate 35 minutes to Task 1 (5m plan, 25m write, 5m check) and 25 minutes to Task 2 (4m plan, 17m write, 4m check) to reflect the mark weighting while guaranteeing both tasks are completed in full.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Even if your vocabulary and spelling are good, forgetting to divide your response into paragraphs automatically caps your Organisation mark at Band 1.
🔄 Quick Check: Never give any task less than 25 minutes—both tasks carry heavy SPaG marks.
Section B: Real-Life & Workplace Practice Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Level 1 Model: Deconstructing an Exam Prompt into a 30-Minute Game Plan) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Read this Level 1 exam prompt:
'Your local park has become littered and the children's swings are damaged. Write an email (150–200 words) to Councillor Green explaining the problems, how they affect local families, and what the council should do.'
Show the exact 5-step exam execution plan.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Extract PAF & Headers (Minute 1): Format: Formal Email | To: c.green@council.gov.uk | Subject: Urgent Repairs & Litter Clearance Needed at Victoria Park | Salutation: Dear Councillor Green, [A1]
2. Map 3 Paragraphs to the 3 Prompt Bullets (Minutes 2–5): Para 1: Overflowing bins and rusted broken chains on toddler swings. | Para 2: Families avoiding the park; broken glass hazard for children and dogs. | Para 3: Repair swings, install two larger recycling bins, and add weekly ranger patrol. [A1]
3. Draft & Proofread (Minutes 6–30): Write 175 words across 3 clear paragraphs, close with 'Yours sincerely,' and spend the final 4 minutes checking capital letters and full stops. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Complete 30-minute strategy: 5m PAF & 3-paragraph bullet map → 20m drafting (~175 words with To/Subject headers and 'Yours sincerely') → 5m SPaG check.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: If you realise at the end of the exam that you forgot paragraph breaks, press Enter twice before each major topic shift to create clear paragraphs.
🔄 Quick Check: Notice how turning the three prompt requirements directly into Paragraphs 1, 2, and 3 makes running out of ideas impossible.
Question 4 (Exam-Style Task — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A student wrote a 260-word formal letter that covered all the prompt bullets well, but lost 6 out of 9 SPaG marks because they joined almost every pair of sentences with a comma (comma splices).
Repair the three comma splices in their paragraph below:
'The community library is a vital hub for elderly residents, it provides free internet access and warm reading rooms, however the council plans to cut its funding by half, this will force the library to close on Saturdays.'
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Fix splice 1 ('...elderly residents, it provides...') with a semicolon or 'as it provides'. [M1]
2. Fix splice 2 ('...reading rooms, however...') by starting a new sentence: '. However,'. [M1]
3. Fix splice 3 ('...by half, this will...') by using ', which will force...'. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'The community library is a vital hub for elderly residents, as it provides free internet access and warm reading rooms. However, the council plans to cut its funding by half, which will force the library to close on Saturdays.'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: An unfinished 60-word response on Task 2 loses up to 10 marks—far more than you could ever gain by polishing Task 1 for an extra 15 minutes.
🔄 Quick Check: Never put ', however,' between two complete sentences—always use '; however,' or '. However,'!
Section C: Exam-Style Tasks Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Level 2 Model: Upgrading a Borderline Fail Draft into a Band 3 Pass) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Compare why Draft A scores only 8/21 (Fail) while Draft B scores 19/21 (Distinction-level Pass) on the same Level 2 report task about staff car park congestion.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Analyse Draft A (8/21 — Borderline Fail): 'The car park is always full in the morning, people park on the grass and block ambulances, we should give out permits and let people cycle.' | Why it fails: No report title or subheadings, only 25 words, two comma splices, vague detail. [A1]
2. Analyse Draft B (19/21 — Top Band Pass): Includes bold Report Title, To/From/Date, numbered subheadings (1.0 Current Congestion Hazards, 2.0 Permit & Car-Share Proposals), complex sentences, semicolon, and realistic figures. [A1]
3. Key Examiner Takeaway: Adding structural headings, expanding each bullet to 70–80 words with concrete examples, and eliminating comma splices doubles the score. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Draft B achieves 19/21 by combining full report formatting (Title + numbered subheadings), sustained operational detail (260 words), and varied Level 2 punctuation (colon, semicolon, and zero comma splices).
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Watch the onscreen clock strictly: when 30 minutes have elapsed, move immediately to Task 2.
🔄 Quick Check: You don't need to be a novelist to pass Level 2 Writing—you just need clear formatting, 4 structured paragraphs, and clean sentence punctuation.
Question 6 (Level 2 Mastery Challenge — 4 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Read this exam prompt: 'Write a report (250–300 words) for your Retail Store Manager on ways to reduce single-use plastic packaging in the store.'
Write out the 5-item Examiner Tick-List showing the exact Format headers, Discourse markers, Complex sentence starter, Colon, and Semicolon you will include in this report.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Format headers: Title + To/From/Date + 3 numbered subheadings. [M1]
2. Three discourse markers: Furthermore, Consequently, However. [M1]
3. Fronted complex sentence: 'Whilst biodegradable bags cost 4p more per unit, customers strongly support...' [M1]
4. Colon and Semicolon examples tailored to plastic reduction. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 1. Format: Title ('Report on Reducing Single-Use Plastic Packaging'), To/From/Date header, and numbered subheadings (1.0 Introduction, 2.0 Packaging Findings, 3.0 Recommendations).
2. Discourse Markers: Furthermore, Consequently, Nevertheless.
3. Complex Sentence: 'Whilst loose produce paper bags cost slightly more to procure, they eliminate over 10,000 plastic wrappers monthly.'
4. Colon & Semicolon: 'We recommend three immediate changes: ...' (colon) and 'Plastic carrier bag sales have dropped by 40%; however, bakery film waste remains high.' (semicolon).
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Failing to proofread spelling, punctuation, and grammar after completing the draft.
🔄 Quick Check: Planning your exact colon and semicolon sentences before you start writing ensures you never forget them under exam stress.
