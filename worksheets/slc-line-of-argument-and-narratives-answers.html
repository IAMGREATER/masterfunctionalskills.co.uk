Structuring Arguments & Formal Presentations Pearson Edexcel: Pearson Edexcel Functional Skills English (SLC)
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (The Basics) Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks Total
Question 1 (Using Signpost Words) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Write two clear signpost sentences: one to introduce your first main point, and one to lead into your final conclusion.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Opening signpost introduces point 1 clearly: "To kick off my presentation, let's examine how flexible start times cut morning commute stress..." [M1]
2. Closing signpost transitions to conclusion: "In conclusion, allowing staff to arrive between 8am and 10am will boost punctuality and team morale." [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Opening Signpost: "To start with my first point, flexible morning start times will immediately cut rush-hour travel delays for our staff."
Closing Signpost: "In conclusion, introducing flexible hours improves work-life balance and keeps staff happy and punctual."
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Never jump straight into facts without warning the audience. Listeners need signpost phrases to follow along easily.
🔄 Quick Check: Clear start signpost + Clear wrap-up signpost = 2 Marks.
Question 2 (Attention-Grabbing Hook & Roadmap) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Write a short, engaging opening hook and roadmap (35–45 words) proposing electric delivery vans.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Engaging opening question/statistic: "Did you know our delivery vans spend over £2,500 every single month just on diesel fuel?" [M1]
2. Clear roadmap of presentation: "Today, I will show how switching to electric vans will cut running costs, lower emissions, and boost our green company image." [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: "Did you know our delivery fleet spends over £2,500 every month on diesel fuel?
Good morning everyone. Today, I'll explain how moving to electric vans will cut fuel bills, reduce carbon emissions, and protect our business from future clean-air charges."
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Keep the opening punchy. Don't spend 2 minutes introducing yourself; state the topic and give the roadmap immediately.
🔄 Quick Check: Hook + Roadmap + Under 45 words = Full Marks.
Section B: Real-Life & Workplace Practice Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks Total
Question 3 (3-Part Spoken Talk Structure) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Create a 3-part talk outline explaining why all staff should receive basic first-aid training.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Introduction with clear purpose: Welcome audience and explain that accidents can happen anywhere, anytime. [M1]
2. 3 logical, sequenced body points: (a) Fast response in emergencies before ambulances arrive, (b) Staff confidence and safety awareness, (c) Meets workplace legal requirements. [M1]
3. Clear conclusion and floor opening: Summarise the life-saving benefit and ask for audience questions. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Introduction: "Accidents can happen in seconds, whether it's a slip, a burn, or someone fainting.
Point 1: Rapid action: immediate CPR and first aid doubles survival chances before paramedics arrive.
Point 2: Confidence: trained staff stay calm and know exactly what to do instead of panicking.
Point 3: Legal duty: ensures our store complies with Health and Safety regulations.
Conclusion: Training everyone takes just one day and saves lives. Thank you for listening, any questions?"
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Ensure each of the 3 points covers a distinct idea rather than repeating the same thought three times.
🔄 Quick Check: Intro + 3 Distinct Points + Conclusion = 3 Marks.
Question 4 (Cue Card Bullets) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Convert the long paragraph into 4 concise Cue Card bullet points (maximum 6 words per bullet).
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Bullet 1 (Morale/Retention): "Boost staff morale & retention" (5 words). [M1]
2. Bullet 2 (Cost problem): "£15,000 spent on recruitment turnover" (5 words). [M1]
3. Bullets 3 & 4 (Solution & Schedule): "Rotate Friday and Monday days off" (6 words) and "Keeps lines open five days" (5 words). [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: • Boosts team morale and retention
• Cuts £15k annual replacement costs
• Rotate Friday and Monday off
• Full phone coverage maintained
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Writing full sentences on cue cards tempts you to read aloud. Keep it to short prompt phrases so you look up at your listeners.
🔄 Quick Check: 4 bullets + All under 6 words + Covers all facts = 3 Marks.
Section C: Exam-Style Tasks Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks Total
Question 5 (Full Presentation Plan: Mentoring Scheme) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Write a complete 4-minute presentation plan proposing a Staff Mentoring Scheme for new starters.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Hook & Purpose: Welcome listeners, state the topic clearly, and mention that 30% of new starters feel anxious in week one. [M1]
2. Point 1 (Buddy System): Pair every apprentice with an experienced colleague for friendly daily check-ins. [M1]
3. Point 2 & 3 (Confidence & Cost): Speeds up training time by 2 weeks and cuts costly early resignations. [M1]
4. Conclusion & Q&A: Wrap up the business benefit and invite questions from the panel. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Opening: "Good morning. Starting a new job is daunting—in fact, a recent survey found that nearly one in three new apprentices consider leaving in their first month due to feeling overwhelmed.
Today, I'm proposing a structured Staff Mentoring Scheme to make onboarding smooth and supportive.

Point 1: First, every new recruit is paired with an experienced buddy who provides guidance, answers daily questions, and introduces them to the team.

Point 2: Second, it speeds up learning. Instead of waiting for formal quarterly training, mentors give real-time feedback on daily tasks, cutting mistake rates by half.

Point 3: Third, it saves money. Retaining our apprentices saves thousands in recruitment costs and builds a loyal, confident team.

Conclusion: In conclusion, a buddy scheme costs nothing but time, yet transforms staff confidence. Thank you for listening, and I'd be happy to answer any questions."
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Always finish by inviting questions! In Task 1, floor questions are mandatory, so you must explicitly invite them.
🔄 Quick Check: Hook + 3 Structured Points + Conclusion + Q&A Invite = 4 Marks.
Question 6 (Concluding Summary & Q&A Invitation) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Write a 35-word concluding summary and Q&A invitation proposing free fruit and tea in the breakroom.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Clear wrap-up signpost: "To conclude my talk today..." [M1]
2. Restate two core benefits: healthy energy boost and team appreciation. [M2]
3. Polite question invitation: "Thank you for listening, and I'm ready to take your questions." [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: "To conclude, providing free fresh fruit and herbal teas in our breakroom is an affordable way to boost energy, improve health, and show staff they are valued.
Thank you for listening, and I welcome any questions."
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Keep the sign-off professional. Don't just stop talking abruptly or say "Yeah, that's it." Deliver a polite closing line.
🔄 Quick Check: Signpost + 2 Benefits + Q&A Invitation + Under 35 words = 4 Marks.
