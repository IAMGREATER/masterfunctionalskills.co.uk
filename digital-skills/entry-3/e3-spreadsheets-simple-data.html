Step-by-Step Teaching & Key Concepts

Core Knowledge
1
Understanding the Spreadsheet Grid

A spreadsheet is a powerful grid used for storing lists, managing budgets, and calculating totals:

  • Columns: Run vertically up and down, labelled with letters (A, B, C, D...).
  • Rows: Run horizontally left to right, labelled with numbers (1, 2, 3, 4...).
  • Cell Reference: The intersection of a column and a row. Column letter comes first, followed by the row number (e.g. cell C5 is column C, row 5).

2
Entering Labels and Numbers

Click into any cell to type:

  • Labels (Text): Word descriptions (e.g. 'Stationery', 'Total'). Automatically align to the left.
  • Numbers / Values: Digits used for maths. Automatically align to the right.
  • Currency Format: Highlight the numbers and click the £ (Currency) button on the ribbon so pennies display neatly with two decimal places (e.g. £15.00 instead of 15).
Workplace Supply Tracking: Clinics and dental practices track stock replenishment in spreadsheets so supervisors know exactly when medical gloves or sterile swabs need reordering.
3
Simple Formulas and AutoSum

Every spreadsheet formula MUST begin with an equals sign (=):

  • Simple Addition: Type =B2+B3 and press Enter.
  • AutoSum Function: Highlight your numbers and click the AutoSum (Σ) button on the ribbon. It automatically inserts =SUM(C2:C5) to add all numbers in that range.
AutoSum Shortcut: Alt + =

Authentic Pearson Edexcel Exam Model

Accredited Model

Here is how this skill is tested in the real Pearson Edexcel onscreen digital assessment, broken down with official examiner mark codes ([B1] knowledge, [M1] process, [A1] accuracy).

Exam Scenario Prompt: A community nursery tracks juice carton expenses in a spreadsheet. In cell C2 the cost is 12.5 and in C3 the cost is 8. Explain how to format the numbers as currency and write the formula needed in cell C4 to calculate the total cost.
Step 1: Format as Currency [B1]
Select cells C2 to C4 and click the Currency (£) button on the Home ribbon.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Formats numbers to display the pound symbol and two decimal places (£12.50 and £8.00).
Step 2: Write the calculation formula [B1]
=C2+C3 (or =SUM(C2:C3))
Tutor Marking Rationale: Formula correctly starts with '=' and adds the two cell references.
Tutor Abdul's Exam Golden Rule: Never type '=12.5+8' into cell C4 in an exam! You MUST use the cell references (=C2+C3) so that if prices change, the total updates automatically.
