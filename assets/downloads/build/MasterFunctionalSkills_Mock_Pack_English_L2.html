Level 2 Reading Strategies & Text Analysis

Critical reading unlocks full marks in both comparison and synthesis questions.
▶The 4 Core Reading Competencies (Pearson Criteria)
Fact vs Opinion: A fact can be proved with empirical data or dates. An opinion contains subjective value judgments ('superior', 'unfortunate', 'must').
Implicit Meaning (Subtext): Reading between the lines. Identify connotations, ironies, and subtle undertones in the author's diction.
Analyzing Writer's Tone: Identify whether the perspective is objective, cynical, impassioned, satirical, authoritative, or empathetic.
Comparative Synthesis: Directly connect Text A and Text B using comparative connectives ('Conversely', 'In parallel', 'Whereas').
✓Evidence Rule
Always quote short, exact phrases (2–5 words) rather than copying entire long sentences.
!Examiner Trap!
Do not just summarize what happened. Explain HOW the language choices affect the reader.

SPaG Marking Rubric & Band Descriptors

Accuracy in spelling, punctuation, and syntax secures up to 42% of total writing marks.
▶Official SPaG Mark Bands
Band 4 (High Pass / Distinction): Full range of punctuation (. , ; : — ? !) used with precision. Complex multi-clause sentences punctuated accurately. Rare spelling errors in ambitious vocabulary.
Band 3 (Clear Pass): Sentences consistently demarcated with full stops and capital letters. Commas used for clauses. Generally accurate spelling of common workplace terms.
Band 2 (Borderline): Occasional comma splices. Basic punctuation mostly correct. Some spelling errors in polysyllabic words.
Band 1 (Refer / Fail): Frequent sentence boundary errors (run-on sentences or fragments). Persistent spelling slips obstructing meaning.
★The Three Fatal Punctuation Errors to Eliminate

1. Comma Splices: Never join two complete sentences with just a comma. Use a full stop, semicolon, or coordinating conjunction (and, but, so).
2. Missing Question Marks: Ensure every rhetorical or direct question concludes with '?'.
3. Apostrophe of Omission vs Possession: 'It's' always means 'it is'. The possessive pronoun is 'its' (no apostrophe!).

Air pollution remains the largest environmental health risk in the United Kingdom today. Furthermore, air quality monitors confirm that local pedestrian footfall increased by 14%, as residents felt safer walking and cycling through revitalised high streets.

Critics argue that daily charging schemes penalise small enterprises and lower-income tradespeople who rely on older vans. However, municipal authorities have distributed over £45 million in targeted vehicle retrofit grants and electric vehicle purchase subsidies. As Mayor Richard Cole observed: 'Clean air cannot be treated as a luxury for the privileged; every child breathing city air deserves protection from irreversible lung damage.' We must accelerate the rollout of zero-emission zones nationwide before more lives are needlessly compromised.

Linguistic & Literary Glossary of Terms

Alliteration
Repetition of identical initial consonant sounds in consecutive words to create rhythm or emphasis.
Bias
A one-sided inclination or perspective that prevents impartial consideration of an issue.
Call to Action
A concluding directive urging the reader to take immediate, specific action.
Colloquial Language
Informal conversational expressions used to establish familiarity with the reader.
Emotive Language
Deliberate word choices designed to evoke strong feelings (sympathy, indignation, urgency) in the reader.
Hyperbole
Intentional exaggeration used for rhetorical effect, emphasis, or humour.
Imperative Verb
A command verb instructing the reader what to do (e.g. 'Register', 'Inspect', 'Submit').
Juxtaposition
Placing contrasting ideas, images, or arguments side by side to highlight differences.
Organizational Features
Visual and structural tools including subheadings, bullet points, callout boxes, and bold text.
Passive Voice
Grammatical construction where the object of an action becomes the subject, establishing an objective formal tone.
Rhetorical Question
A question posed for persuasive effect where no verbal response is expected from the audience.
Rule of Three (Tricolon)
Grouping three words, phrases, or arguments together to make them memorable and impactful.
Statistics & Evidence
Factual data, numerical percentages, and survey findings cited to bolster credibility.
Tone
The overall attitude or mood conveyed by the writer (e.g. authoritative, empathetic, critical, impassioned).
