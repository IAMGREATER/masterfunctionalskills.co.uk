1. Place Value up to 1,000,000
Number System
Millions, Thousands & Ones
Numbers are grouped in 3-digit periods separated by commas or spaces: Millions (M), Hundred Thousands (HTh), Ten Thousands (TTh), Thousands (Th), Hundreds (H), Tens (T), Units (U).
★ Golden Rule: Read large numbers in groups of three digits from left to right!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't confuse 'Eight Thousand' (8,000) with 'Eighty Thousand' (80,000).

2. Four Operations with Whole Numbers
Number System
Column Methods & Checking
Addition & Subtraction: Line up unit digits strictly on the right. Carry over when sum ≥ 10; borrow from next column to the left for subtraction. Multiplication: Long column. Division: Short 'bus stop'.
★ Golden Rule: Always check subtraction by adding your answer to the smaller number!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't subtract smaller from larger in the same column! If top digit is smaller, borrow 1 from left.

3. Negative Numbers (Temp & Debt)
Number System
Below Zero & Bank Overdrafts
Numbers below 0 have a minus sign (-). Moving RIGHT on the number line = getting warmer / paying in money (+). Moving LEFT = getting colder / spending money (-).
★ Golden Rule: -8°C is COLDER than -3°C!
⚠️ Watch Out: Overdraft balance: If balance is -£25 and you spend £10, new balance is -£35 (NOT -£15).

4. Rounding to 10, 100, 1000
Number System
Look at the Neighbour on the Right
Find the rounding place: Look at the next digit to the right: If 5 or more (5, 6, 7, 8, 9), ROUND UP. If 4 or less (0, 1, 2, 3, 4), ROUND DOWN (stay same). Fill trailing places with 0.
★ Golden Rule: 5 to 9 climbs the vine; 0 to 4 stays on the floor!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't delete digits! When rounding 3,428 to the nearest 10, it becomes 3,430, NOT 343.

5. Decimals: Place Value & Ordering
Number System
Tenths, Hundredths & Thousandths
To compare decimals: Line up decimal points vertically and pad with trailing zeros so all numbers have the same number of digits after the dot (e.g. 0.4 becomes 0.400).
★ Golden Rule: Add trailing zeroes to level the playing field before comparing decimals!
⚠️ Watch Out: 0.35 has more digits than 0.4, but 0.4 is LARGER! (0.40 > 0.35).

6. Operations with Decimals & Money
Number System
Line Up the Decimal Points
Always align the decimal points directly under each other when adding or subtracting money. Money must ALWAYS have TWO decimal places (e.g. £4.50, NEVER £4.5).
★ Golden Rule: Always write pounds with 2 decimal places: £6.20, NOT £6.2!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't mix pounds and pence without converting! £5 + 40p = £5.40, NOT £45.

7. FDP Common Benchmarks
Number System
Halves, Quarters & Tenths
1/2 = 0.5 = 50%. 1/4 = 0.25 = 25%. 3/4 = 0.75 = 75%. 1/10 = 0.1 = 10%. 1/5 = 0.2 = 20%. Memorising these visual equivalents saves time in exams.
★ Golden Rule: 1/5 is 20% (0.2), NOT 15%!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't confuse 0.1 (10%) with 0.01 (1%).

8. Simplifying Fractions
Number System
Divide Top and Bottom Equally
To simplify a fraction, divide both the numerator (top) and denominator (bottom) by their Highest Common Factor (HCF). Keep dividing until no common factors remain.
★ Golden Rule: Whatever you do to the top, you MUST do to the bottom!
⚠️ Watch Out: Never subtract numbers to simplify fractions — you can ONLY divide or multiply.

9. Fraction of an Amount (Basic)
Number System
Divide by Denominator, Times by Numerator
To find a unit fraction (1/n): Divide by n. To find a non-unit fraction (e.g. 3/4): 1. Divide by 4 to get 1 quarter. 2. Multiply result by 3 to get 3 quarters.
★ Golden Rule: Divide by bottom, multiply by top!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't stop after step 1! Dividing by 4 gives only ONE quarter (12 kg), you need THREE.

10. Percentages (Building Blocks)
Number System
10%, 5%, 1% Breakdown Method
Non-calculator percentage hacks: 10% = Divide by 10. 5% = Half of 10%. 1% = Divide by 100. 50% = Half (÷ 2). Combine blocks to find any percentage (e.g. 15% = 10% + 5%).
★ Golden Rule: Find 10% first — it unlocks 5%, 20%, 30%, and 15% instantly!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't divide by 15 to find 15%! You find 10% and add 5%.

11. Simple Ratios (2 Parts)
Number System
Add Parts, Divide Total, Multiply
To share £60 in ratio 1 : 3: 1. Add parts: 1 + 3 = 4 parts. 2. Divide total: £60 ÷ 4 = £15 (value of 1 part). 3. Multiply: Share 1 = £15, Share 2 = 3 × £15 = £45.
★ Golden Rule: Ratios must always be simplified to lowest whole numbers!
⚠️ Watch Out: Keep the order of the ratio correct: Person A : Person B must match the order in the text.

12. Direct Proportion (Recipes)
Number System
Unitary Method: Find for 1 First
To scale a recipe: 1. Divide ingredients by the original number of servings to find the amount for 1 person. 2. Multiply by the new number of people needed.
★ Golden Rule: Find the quantity for ONE person first, then multiply!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't just add 2 to the flour because 6 is 4 + 2 — you must scale proportionally!

13. Multiples, Factors & Primes
Number System
Definitions & Factor Pairs
Multiple = In the times table (Multiples of 5: 5, 10, 15...). Factor = Numbers that divide in exactly with no remainder. Prime = EXACTLY two factors: 1 and itself (2, 3, 5, 7, 11...).
★ Golden Rule: 1 is NOT a prime number because it only has 1 factor!
⚠️ Watch Out: Factors are smaller (or equal); multiples are larger (or equal).

14. Inverse Operations for Checking
Number System
Reverse Your Calculation for 1 Mark
Pearson exams award a dedicated mark for 'Show a check of your answer': Addition check with SUBTRACTION. Subtraction check with ADDITION. Multiplication check with DIVISION. Division check with MULTIPLICATION.
★ Golden Rule: Use reverse operations (add ↔ subtract, multiply ↔ divide) to check!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't repeat the same sum — examiners require the inverse operation!

15. Perimeter of Rectangle & Square
Measures & Space
Add All Four Outside Edges
Perimeter is the total boundary distance around a 2D shape. Rectangle: Perimeter = Length + Width + Length + Width = 2l + 2w. Units are linear (cm, m).
★ Golden Rule: Perimeter is the border fence around the shape!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't just add 8 + 5 = 13! A rectangle has FOUR sides, so you must add all 4 sides.

16. Perimeter of Regular Polygons
Measures & Space
Side Length × Number of Sides
A 'regular' polygon has sides that are ALL EQUAL in length: Equilateral Triangle = 3 × side. Pentagon = 5 × side. Hexagon = 6 × side. Octagon = 8 × side.
★ Golden Rule: Regular means all sides and angles are identical!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't count the sides incorrectly: Hexagon = 6, Octagon = 8, Pentagon = 5.

17. Area of a Rectangle
Measures & Space
Area = Length × Width
Area measures the flat 2D space inside a shape. Multiply length by width. Answers are ALWAYS written in square units (cm², m², mm²).
★ Golden Rule: Area is flat surface (carpet/grass), measured in square units (m²)!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't confuse Area (L × W) with Perimeter (L + W + L + W).

18. Area of a Triangle
Measures & Space
Area = (Base × Height) ÷ 2
1. Multiply base by perpendicular vertical height. 2. HALVE the answer (divide by 2) because a triangle is half of a rectangle.
★ Golden Rule: Always remember to divide by 2 for a triangle!
⚠️ Watch Out: Never use the slanted side length for area — only use vertical perpendicular height.

19. Volume of a Cuboid
Measures & Space
Volume = Length × Width × Height
Volume measures the 3D space inside a solid box. Multiply all three dimensions together: Length × Width × Height. Written in cubic units (cm³, m³).
★ Golden Rule: Volume is 3D capacity, measured in cubic units (cm³ or m³)!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't add the three dimensions — you must MULTIPLY them.

20. Metric Length Conversions
Measures & Space
mm • cm • m • km
1 cm = 10 mm. 1 m = 100 cm. 1 km = 1,000 m. Big to small unit = MULTIPLY (×). Small to big unit = DIVIDE (÷).
★ Golden Rule: Metres to cm: MULTIPLY by 100. cm to metres: DIVIDE by 100.
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't confuse mm and cm: 45 mm is 4.5 cm, NOT 450 cm.

21. Metric Weight Conversions
Measures & Space
1 kg = 1,000 g | 1 tonne = 1,000 kg
To convert kg to g: MULTIPLY by 1,000. To convert g to kg: DIVIDE by 1,000. For medicine: 1 g = 1,000 mg.
★ Golden Rule: 'Kilo' always means 1,000!
⚠️ Watch Out: Half a kilogram is 500 grams, NOT 50 grams.

22. Metric Capacity Conversions
Measures & Space
1 Litre = 1,000 ml
Litres to millilitres: MULTIPLY by 1,000. Millilitres to litres: DIVIDE by 1,000. Common amounts: 1/2 Litre = 500 ml. 1/4 Litre = 250 ml. 3/4 Litre = 750 ml.
★ Golden Rule: Milli means one-thousandth!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't divide by 100 — there are 1,000 millilitres in a litre.

23. Time: 12-Hour & 24-Hour Clock
Measures & Space
Converting & Timetables
To convert PM time to 24-hour: ADD 12 hours (e.g. 4:15 pm + 12 = 16:15). To convert 24-hour to PM: SUBTRACT 12 hours (e.g. 21:40 - 12 = 9:40 pm).
★ Golden Rule: Never write 'pm' after a 24-hour time (e.g. 20:30, NOT 20:30 pm)!
⚠️ Watch Out: 12:30 pm is afternoon (lunchtime) = 12:30. 12:30 am is midnight = 00:30.

25. Properties of 2D Shapes
Measures & Space
Sides, Angles & Symmetry
Right Angle = 90°. Triangle angles sum to 180°. Quadrilateral angles sum to 360°. Parallel lines never meet. Perpendicular lines meet at 90°.
★ Golden Rule: All angles in ANY triangle add up to 180°!
⚠️ Watch Out: A rhombus has 4 equal sides, but its angles are NOT 90° (a square has 90° angles).

26. Properties of 3D Solids
Measures & Space
Faces, Edges & Vertices
Face = Flat or curved surface. Edge = Line where two faces meet. Vertex (Vertices) = Corner point where edges meet. A cube/cuboid has 6 faces, 12 edges, 8 vertices.
★ Golden Rule: Vertices = Corners • Edges = Creases • Faces = Surfaces.
⚠️ Watch Out: A cylinder has 3 faces (2 flat circles, 1 curved), 2 edges, and 0 vertices!

27. Coordinates (x, y)
Measures & Space
Along the Corridor, Up the Stairs
Coordinates are written as (x, y): 1. First number (x) goes HORIZONTALLY along the bottom axis. 2. Second number (y) goes VERTICALLY up the side axis. Origin is (0, 0).
★ Golden Rule: 'x' comes before 'y' in the alphabet, and in coordinates (x, y)!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't mix up (2, 5) with (5, 2) — always read the bottom horizontal axis first.

Handling Data
Add All Up, Divide by Count
1. Add all numbers together to find total sum. 2. Count how many numbers there are. 3. Divide total sum by the count. Mean is the fair share.

30. Median (Middle Value)
Handling Data
Line Up Low to High, Pick Middle
1. Put numbers in numerical order from smallest to largest. 2. Cross off one from each end until you reach the middle number. If two remain in middle, find their midpoint.
★ Golden Rule: You MUST sort the numbers from smallest to largest first!
⚠️ Watch Out: Never pick the middle number of the unsorted list (in 9, 3, 7, 2, 8, '7' happens to be middle, but never rely on luck!).

Handling Data
The Most Common Item
Mode is the number or category that appears MOST OFTEN in the data. There can be one mode, more than one mode (bimodal), or no mode if all values appear once.

32. Range (Spread of Data)
Handling Data
Range = Highest - Lowest
Range is NOT an average — it measures how spread out the numbers are. Subtract the smallest number from the largest number. Smaller range = more consistent.
★ Golden Rule: Range = Highest minus Lowest!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't write '12 to 25' — the range is a SINGLE number obtained by subtracting (13).

Handling Data
Bundles of 5 (4 Vertical + 1 Diagonal Gate)
Tallies are grouped in bundles of five: four vertical marks (||||) with the fifth mark crossing diagonally as a gate across the bundle. Frequency is the total count written as a number.

Handling Data

37. Probability Words & Scale
Handling Data
Impossible to Certain
0 = Impossible (can never happen). 0.5 (1/2 or 50%) = Even Chance (e.g. flipping heads). 1 = Certain (guaranteed to happen). Unlikely is between 0 and 0.5; Likely is between 0.5 and 1.
★ Golden Rule: Probabilities on a number scale run strictly from 0 to 1!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't use words like 'Good chance' or 'Fifty-fifty' on an exam — use 'Likely' or 'Even chance'.
