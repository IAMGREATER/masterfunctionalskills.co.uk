Step-by-Step Teaching & Key Concepts

Core Knowledge
1
Choosing Slide Layouts

When adding a new slide (Ctrl + M), always select a layout suited to your content:

  • Title Slide: First slide of your deck. Contains presentation title, subtitle, and speaker name.
  • Title and Content: Standard slide layout. Has a title banner at the top and a large content box for bullet points, a table, or a chart.
  • Two Content / Comparison: Features two columns side by side — ideal for comparing pros and cons or placing a photo next to text.
New Slide: Ctrl + M • Start Slide Show: F5
2
Principles of Professional Slide Design

Avoid cluttered slides that overwhelm the audience:

  • The 6×6 Rule: Aim for no more than 6 bullet points per slide, with roughly 6 words per bullet.
  • High Contrast: Dark text on light backgrounds (or white text on deep navy) ensures clear legibility from the back of a meeting room.
  • Consistent Theme: Use the same fonts, brand colours, and layout styling across every slide in the deck.
Tutor Abdul's Golden Presentation Tip: Slides are visual prompts for the audience, not a teleprompter for you to read word-for-word! Use concise bullets on screen and write detailed explanations in your Speaker Notes.
3
Adding Speaker Notes & Slide Transitions

To present with confidence:

  • Speaker Notes: Click the Notes button at the bottom of PowerPoint. Type your private talking points here. When using Presenter View on a projector, only you can see the notes on your laptop screen!
  • Slide Transitions: The animation when moving from one slide to the next (e.g. Fade or Push). Keep transitions subtle and consistent across all slides; avoid spinning or noisy effects.

Authentic Pearson Edexcel Exam Model

Accredited Model

Here is how this skill is tested in the real Pearson Edexcel onscreen digital assessment, broken down with official examiner mark codes ([B1] knowledge, [M1] process, [A1] accuracy).

Exam Scenario Prompt: A trainer is designing a 5-slide presentation on 'Infection Control'. Explain two design rules they should follow to ensure the slides are accessible and professional, and state where the trainer should type their personal talking notes.
Step 1: First design rule [B1]
Use concise bullet points (short key phrases rather than huge paragraphs of text).
Tutor Marking Rationale: Prevents cognitive overload and maintains visual clarity.
Step 2: Second design rule [B1]
Ensure strong colour contrast between text and background (e.g. dark text on light cream/white background).
Tutor Marking Rationale: Ensures legibility and accessibility for audience members with visual impairments.
Step 3: Location for private notes [B1]
In the Speaker Notes pane at the bottom of the slide window.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Speaker notes are visible only to the presenter.
Tutor Abdul's Exam Golden Rule: In Level 1 exams, presentation questions test layout simplicity: 'Concise bullets', 'High contrast', and 'Speaker notes'.
