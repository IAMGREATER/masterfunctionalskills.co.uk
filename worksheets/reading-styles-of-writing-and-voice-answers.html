Tone, Style, Register & Writer's Voice Pearson Edexcel: L1.15 • L2.15 • L2.16
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (The Basics) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Level 1 Identifying Tone in Workplace Communications) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Match the two extracts to their exact tone:
Extract A (Maternity Ward Guide): 'Please do not worry if your newborn cries frequently during the first night; our experienced midwives are right next door at the touch of a button to help you settle your baby.'
Extract B (Site Manager Memo): 'Any operative caught tampering with scaffolding safety tags will face immediate disciplinary dismissal and permanent removal from site.'

Choose from: Humorous  |  Reassuring & Supportive  |  Stern & Authoritative  |  Apologetic, and quote two words from each extract that prove your choice.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Extract A: Reassuring & Supportive — proved by 'Please do not worry' / 'experienced midwives... to help you' [A1]
2. Extract B: Stern & Authoritative — proved by 'immediate disciplinary dismissal' / 'permanent removal' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Extract A: Reassuring & Supportive ('do not worry', 'help you'); Extract B: Stern & Authoritative ('immediate disciplinary dismissal').
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Selecting the right tone label without copying the supporting words from each extract.
🔄 Quick Check: Extract A calms anxious parents; Extract B enforces strict site discipline.
Question 2 (Level 1 Conversational vs Formal Style) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Read this extract from a staff health newsletter:
'Fed up with paying £5 for a soggy supermarket sandwich? Why not batch-cook a delicious pot of chilli on Sunday night — your tastebuds (and your bank balance!) will love you for it.'

Identify the style/register of this extract and give two features from the text that create this style.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify style: Informal, conversational, chatty, and friendly [A1]
2. Give two features: Colloquial opening question ('Fed up with... soggy supermarket sandwich?'), parenthetical aside with exclamation mark ('(and your bank balance!)'), or direct address ('your tastebuds') [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Style: Informal / conversational / friendly. Features: 1. Chatty questions ('Fed up with...?', 'Why not...?'). 2. Friendly aside and exclamation mark ('(and your bank balance!)').
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Saying the style is 'persuasive' (which is purpose) without stating that the register is 'informal/conversational'.
🔄 Quick Check: 'Fed up with' and 'soggy' are classic informal conversational choices.
Section B: Real-Life & Workplace Practice Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Level 1/2 Analysing an Apologetic & Conciliatory Tone) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Read this letter from a broadband director to customers:
'We sincerely apologise for the unacceptable four-hour network outage across North Oxfordshire yesterday afternoon. We fully understand how disruptive this was for homeworkers, and we have automatically credited £15 to your next bill as a gesture of goodwill.'

Describe the tone of this extract [1 Mark], quote two phrases that create it [1 Mark], and explain why the writer chose this tone [1 Mark].
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Describe tone: Apologetic, empathetic, polite, and professional [A1]
2. Quote two phrases: 'sincerely apologise' / 'unacceptable four-hour network outage' / 'gesture of goodwill' [A1]
3. Explain why chosen: Calms angry customers by taking full responsibility for the outage and offering automatic £15 compensation so they do not cancel their contract [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Tone: Apologetic, empathetic, and professional. Quotes: 'sincerely apologise' and 'gesture of goodwill'. Chosen to defuse customer anger and retain their loyalty after the outage.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Describing the tone as 'sad' instead of the professional term 'apologetic / conciliatory'.
🔄 Quick Check: Tone label + 2 quotes + customer retention explanation = 3 marks.
Question 4 (Level 2 Active vs Passive Voice & Sentence Pace) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Compare the style of these two workplace sentences:
Sentence 1 (Accident Investigation Report): 'The warehouse fire alarm was triggered at 14:22, and the building was evacuated within three minutes.'
Sentence 2 (Fire Warden Training Poster): 'Smash the glass! Sound the siren! Get everyone out now!'

Explain how the grammar and sentence length differ between Sentence 1 and Sentence 2, and how this affects the voice of each text.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Sentence 1 grammar & voice: Uses the passive voice ('was triggered', 'was evacuated') and a longer compound structure to create a calm, detached, objective clinical voice [A1]
2. Sentence 2 grammar & voice: Uses short, three-word imperative commands ('Smash', 'Sound', 'Get') and exclamation marks [A1]
3. Contrast effect on pace: Sentence 1 sounds like an official factual record after the event, whereas Sentence 2 creates a fast, urgent, commanding pace for an active emergency [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Sentence 1 uses passive verbs ('was triggered', 'was evacuated') for a calm, detached, objective audit style. Sentence 2 uses short three-word imperatives and exclamation marks ('Smash the glass!') to create an urgent, commanding pace.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Not naming 'passive voice' in Sentence 1 or 'short imperative sentences' in Sentence 2.
🔄 Quick Check: 'was triggered' = passive voice; 'Smash the glass!' = short active imperative.
Section C: Exam-Style Tasks Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Level 2 Detecting Sarcasm, Irony & Critical Voice) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Read this extract from a resident's column in a town newsletter:
'Congratulations to our brilliant council planners! Spending £800,000 on a "smart" traffic-light junction that has doubled morning tailbacks to three miles is truly a masterclass in modern engineering. Clearly, none of the committee ever drive to work at 8 a.m.'

Analyse the writer's tone and voice in this extract. Explain how the writer uses words and punctuation to convey their true attitude.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify true tone: Heavily sarcastic, ironic, bitter, and mocking (NOT genuinely praising the council) [M1]
2. Analyse verbal irony quotes: Uses mock praise ('Congratulations to our brilliant council planners!', 'truly a masterclass') to mean the exact opposite because the £800,000 lights doubled tailbacks [M1]
3. Analyse punctuation clues: Inverted commas (scare quotes) around '"smart"' mock the council's claim that the lights are intelligent [A1]
4. Analyse closing remark: 'Clearly, none of the committee ever drive to work at 8 a.m.' delivers a biting criticism that councillors are out of touch [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: The tone is sarcastic and mocking. The writer uses ironic praise ('brilliant council planners!', 'truly a masterclass') and scare quotes around '"smart"' to ridicule the £800,000 junction for doubling tailbacks to three miles.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Taking the opening sentence literally and claiming the writer is 'happy and praising the council'.
🔄 Quick Check: Doubling tailbacks to three miles proves 'brilliant' and 'masterclass' are sarcastic!
Question 6 (Level 2 Comparing Style, Voice & Register Across Two Texts) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Text A (Statutory Redundancy Notice):
'Pursuant to Section 188 of the Trade Union and Labour Relations Act, formal consultation regarding the proposed closure of the Swindon packaging plant will commence on 1st November.'

Text B (Plant Worker's Open Letter):
'After twenty years of loyal service, we found out our factory is shutting down from a cold, heartless piece of paper pinned to the canteen wall. What happens to our families now?'

Compare the style, tone, and voice of Text A and Text B, supporting your analysis with quotations.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Text A style & tone: Highly formal, legalistic, impersonal, and detached corporate tone [M1]
2. Text A evidence: Uses legal citations ('Pursuant to Section 188...') and formal Latinate verbs ('commence') with zero personal pronouns [A1]
3. Text B style & tone: Deeply personal, emotional, betrayed, and anguished first-person plural voice ('we', 'our families') [M1]
4. Text B evidence: Uses emotive adjectives ('loyal service', 'cold, heartless piece of paper') and a desperate rhetorical question ('What happens to our families now?') [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Text A adopts a cold, formal, legalistic voice ('Pursuant to Section 188', 'commence') with impersonal detachment. In contrast, Text B uses an emotional, betrayed first-person voice ('our families'), emotive adjectives ('cold, heartless'), and a rhetorical question to express workers' distress.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Comparing only the topic (plant closure) instead of contrasting the legalistic detachment of Text A with the emotional first-person voice of Text B.
🔄 Quick Check: Check you quoted from both texts and contrasted legal formality vs personal emotion.
