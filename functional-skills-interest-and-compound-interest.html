Golden Rules for Simple & Compound Interest

Golden Rule 1: Simple Interest Remains Constant Every Year

Simple interest is calculated only on the original principal amount. If £2,000 earns 5% simple interest, it earns £100 every single year.

Golden Rule / Method: Simple Interest = Principal × Rate × Time
Golden Rule 2: Compound Interest: Calculate Year by Year or Use a Multiplier

Compound interest adds each year's interest to the balance before calculating the next year's interest. Or multiply by the decimal multiplier for each year: Starting Balance × (1 + r)ⁿ.

Golden Rule / Method: Final Balance = Principal × (Multiplier)ⁿ

Core Skill 1: Skill 1: Year-by-Year vs Multiplier Method for Compound Interest

Two reliable methods for non-calculator and calculator papers.

To calculate £5,000 invested at 4% compound interest for 2 years:

  • Year-by-Year Method:
    Year 1: 4% of £5,000 = £200 → End of Year 1 Balance = £5,200.
    Year 2: 4% of £5,200 = £208 → End of Year 2 Balance = £5,408.00.
  • Multiplier Method (Calculator):
    Multiplier for +4% is 1 + 0.04 = 1.04.
    Final Balance = £5,000 × 1.04² = £5,000 × 1.0816 = £5,408.00.

Core Skill 3: Skill 3: Compound Depreciation (Decreasing Value Over Time)

Using a subtraction multiplier (1 - r)ⁿ for vehicle and equipment depreciation.

When an asset loses value each year (e.g. a medical van bought for £20,000 depreciates by 15% per year for 2 years):

  • Multiplier: 100% - 15% = 85% = 0.85.
  • Value after 2 years: £20,000 × 0.85² = £20,000 × 0.7225 = £14,450.00.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2)

Vocational Context: Healthcare Reserve Fund Management

A care home trustees board invests £12,000 in a fixed-rate savings bond paying 3% compound interest per annum for 3 years.
Calculate the total interest earned over the 3 years.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate Year 1 interest and balance
3% of £12,000 = 0.03 × 12,000 = £360.
Balance at end of Year 1 = £12,000 + £360 = £12,360.
Criteria: M1: Year 1 calculation
Step 2 Calculate Year 2 interest and balance
3% of £12,360 = 0.03 × 12,360 = £370.80.
Balance at end of Year 2 = £12,360 + £370.80 = £12,730.80.
Criteria: M1: Year 2 compound step
Step 3 Calculate Year 3 interest and total interest earned
3% of £12,730.80 = 0.03 × 12,730.80 = £381.92.
Final Balance = £13,112.72.
Total Interest Earned = £13,112.72 - £12,000 = £1,112.72.
Criteria: A1: Accurate total interest earned
Final Accredited Answer: Total Interest Earned = £1,112.72
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Notice the question asks for the TOTAL INTEREST EARNED, not the final balance! Always subtract the starting principal (£12,000).
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2 Problem Solving)

Vocational Context: Personal Savings Account Selection

A clinical nurse specialist wants to invest £8,000 for 2 years. She compares two accounts:
• Account A (Platinum Bond): Pays 4.5% compound interest per annum.
• Account B (Fixed Income Bond): Pays 4.6% simple interest per annum.

Which account will yield the higher balance at the end of 2 years, and by how much?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate final balance for Account A (4.5% compound for 2 years)
Multiplier = 1.045.
Balance A = £8,000 × 1.045 × 1.045 = £8,736.20.
Criteria: M1: Compound interest calculation for Account A
Step 2 Calculate final balance for Account B (4.6% simple for 2 years)
1 year simple interest = £8,000 × 0.046 = £368.00.
2 years interest = 2 × £368.00 = £736.00.
Balance B = £8,000 + £736.00 = £8,736.00.
Criteria: M1: Simple interest calculation for Account B
Step 3 Find the exact difference between the two balances
£8,736.20 - £8,736.00 = £0.20 (20p).
Criteria: M1: Difference calculation
Step 4 State final justified conclusion
Account A yields the higher balance by 20p (£8,736.20 vs £8,736.00).
Criteria: A1: Correct account (A) and exact difference (£0.20 / 20p)
Final Accredited Answer: Account A yields a higher balance by £0.20 / 20p (£8,736.20 vs £8,736.00).
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Do not round intermediate decimals when squaring 1.045 (1.045² = 1.092025) so you capture the exact 20p difference!

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Giving the Final Balance Instead of the Interest
If the question asks 'How much interest was earned?', stating £13,112.72 instead of £1,112.72 forfeits the final accuracy mark.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Highlight the word 'interest' or 'total balance' in the question stem.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A worker takes a loan of £3,500 at 6% simple interest per year. How much total interest will they pay over 4 years?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. 1 year interest = 6% of £3,500 = 0.06 × 3,500 = £210.
2. 4 years total interest = £210 × 4 = £840.00.
Final Correct Answer: £840.00
Examiner Tip: Simple interest is identical every year: £210 × 4.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
A saver deposits £6,500 into an ISA paying 4% compound interest per year. Calculate the total balance in the account after 2 years.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Year 1: £6,500 × 1.04 = £6,760.00.
2. Year 2: £6,760.00 × 1.04 = £7,030.40.
Final Correct Answer: £7,030.40
Examiner Tip: Remember to write the trailing zero (£7,030.40, not £7,030.4).
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A hospital buys an electric delivery van for £25,000. The van depreciates in value by 20% each year (compound depreciation). What is the value of the van after 3 years?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Multiplier for 20% decrease = 1 - 0.20 = 0.80.
2. After Year 1: £25,000 × 0.8 = £20,000.
3. After Year 2: £20,000 × 0.8 = £16,000.
4. After Year 3: £16,000 × 0.8 = £12,800.00.
Final Correct Answer: £12,800.00
Examiner Tip: Do NOT subtract 60% (3 × 20%)! Apply the 20% reduction year by year.
