Three Non-Negotiable Exam Golden Rules

Golden Rule 1: Bulleted Lists = Any Order; Numbered Lists = Strict Sequence

Use circular bullets for shopping lists, equipment checklists, or clinic benefits. Use 1, 2, 3 numbering ONLY for ordered instructions or rankings.

✗ Typing manual hyphens `- ` when an automatic numbered 1–4 step guide is asked
✓ Select lines > Home > Numbering (1, 2, 3) for fire drill evacuation order
Golden Rule 2: Match Alignment to Purpose (Centre Title, Left Body)

Left alignment creates clean straight vertical anchors that aid dyslexic readers. Use Centre align strictly for main titles or posters, and Right align for numbers/dates.

✗ Centre-aligning long 8-line body paragraphs (creates jagged edges)
✓ Title = Centre Align; Paragraphs & Bullet Lists = Left Align
Golden Rule 3: Press Enter Once Inside Lists — Twice to Exit Back to Normal

After your final bullet point, hitting Enter once creates an empty bullet; hitting Enter a second time (or clicking Bullets toggle off) exits the list cleanly.

✗ Leaving a stray lonely bullet dot sitting at the bottom of the page
✓ Press Enter twice on the empty bullet line to return to clean normal paragraph formatting

Step-by-Step Teaching & Key Concepts

1
Paragraph Alignment: Left, Centre, and Right

Alignment controls where your text sits across the width of the page:

  • Align Left (Ctrl + L): Standard for English text. Lines start neatly down the left margin with a jagged right margin. Easiest to read.
  • Centre (Ctrl + E): Positions text right in the middle. Perfect for titles, certificates, and poster headers.
  • Align Right (Ctrl + R): Aligns text against the right margin. Often used for dates and letter sender addresses.
Left Align: Ctrl + L • Centre Align: Ctrl + E • Right Align: Ctrl + R
2
Bulleted Lists vs Numbered Lists

Lists break up big blocks of text, making information quick to scan:

  • Bullet Points (•): Use when the order does not matter (e.g. equipment needed, ingredients, job perks).
  • Numbered Lists (1, 2, 3...): Use when the sequence is critical (e.g. recipes, evacuation procedures, login steps).
List Formatting Ribbon Tools
3
Removing and Adjusting Lists

To turn a list back into normal paragraphs, select the items and click the Bullets button again to toggle it off. To finish a list while typing, press Enter twice.

Tutor Abdul's Checklist Tip: In Entry 3 exams, you are frequently asked: 'Format the items into a bulleted list'. Simply select all the items and click the Bullets button once!

Step 1: Separate the points [B1]
Place each safety rule on its own separate line using Enter.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Each list entry requires its own line.
Step 2: Apply Bullet points [M1]
Select all five lines and click the Bullets button on the Home ribbon.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Converts plain lines into an aligned bulleted list with consistent left indentation.
Tutor Abdul's Exam Golden Rule: Numbered lists are for steps; bullet points are for items. Don't mix them up in the exam!
Worked Model 2 • Applied Workplace Scenario Exam Scenario Model 2 • Opening Times & Workshop Registration
Exam Scenario Prompt 2: A poster needs: (a) 'Workshop Registration Steps' written as a 3-step chronological order, (b) the main poster title centred at the top, and (c) contact email right-aligned in the top corner. Which three buttons accomplish this cleanly?
Step 1: Centred Title & Right-Aligned Header Detail [B1]
Highlight poster title > click Centre Align. Place cursor on top contact line > click Right Align.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Demonstrates exact matching of Left / Centre / Right align icons to brief layout rules.

Select the 3 registration steps > click the Numbering button (1, 2, 3) instead of Bullets because order matters (Register > Pay > Join).
Tutor Marking Rationale: Mark schemes specifically check whether learners choose Numbering over Bullets when sequence is essential.
Tutor Abdul's Distinction Tip: Avoid 'Justify' alignment inside narrow columns or short notices — it stretches giant white rivers between words that make text harder to read!

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Trap 1: Typing Fake '•' or '1.' Manually Instead of List Tool
Manual bullets don't wrap cleanly on second lines; wrapped words align underneath the bullet instead of hanging cleanly.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always select the items and apply Word's built-in **Bullets** or **Numbering** toolbar button.
Trap 2: Centre-Aligning Multi-Line Body Text
Centre-aligned long paragraphs force the eye to hunt for a different left margin on every line.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Keep all body text and bulleted lists **Left Aligned**.
Trap 3: Leaving Huge Blank Gaps Between Line Items
Hitting Enter 3 times between every bullet stretches a short checklist onto two pages.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Use Paragraph > Line Spacing (`1.15` or `1.5`) cleanly rather than stacking empty lines.
