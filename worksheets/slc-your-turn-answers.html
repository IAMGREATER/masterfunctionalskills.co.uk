Section A: Core Skills (The Basics) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Inviting a Quiet Group Member into the Discussion) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
During your assessed group discussion on workplace recycling, you notice that **Sam** has not spoken for four minutes while two other speakers dominate.
Write a polite, natural spoken intervention that **summarises the current point** and **hands the floor directly to Sam**.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Briefly summarise the current point ('We have heard strong points on bin placement in the canteen...') [A1]
2. Invite Sam by name with an open question ('...Sam, from your experience on the reception desk, what recycling challenges do you see in the main foyer?') [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'We have heard some great ideas about canteen bins so far—Sam, we haven't heard your perspective yet; what recycling challenges have you noticed at the main reception desk?'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Putting the quiet candidate on the spot aggressively ('Why haven't you said anything, Sam?').
🔄 Quick Check: Polite summary + warm, specific open invitation by name.
Question 2 (Core Skill Application — 3 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Look at Simulation Brief 2 (Logistics Depot Carbon & Waste Reduction with a £5,000 budget). Write a 40-word opening contribution to launch the group discussion, introduce a specific initiative with a figure, and hand over to a peer (Liam).
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Open the meeting and state the goal (20% waste/carbon reduction within our £5,000 budget). [M1]
2. Propose replacing single-use cardboard boxes with reusable plastic tote crates (eliminating 3,000 boxes a month for £2,200). [M1]
3. Ask Liam for his view. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'Good morning everyone. To hit our 20% waste reduction target within the £5,000 budget, I propose spending £2,200 on durable, stackable plastic tote boxes, which would eliminate 3,000 single-use cardboard cartons every month. Liam, how well would reusable crates fit onto our existing delivery vans?'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Delivering all three of your points in the first 90 seconds and then sitting back silently for the rest of the meeting fails interactive turn-taking criteria.
🔄 Quick Check: Starting the discussion first is a great way to conquer nerves and claim immediate leadership credit.
Section B: Real-Life & Workplace Practice Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Model 1 (Level 1): Pre-Discussion 5-Minute Preparation Card (Simulation Brief 3)) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
You are given 5 minutes to prepare for Simulation Brief 3 (Reducing Early Apprentice Drop-Out Rates). Create a completed '3-1-2' preparation card showing your 3 points, 1 statistic, and 2 questions.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. List 3 clear, practical contributions: 1. Pair every new apprentice with a second-year buddy. | 2. Protect 4 hours every Friday morning strictly for college assignments. | 3. Provide a £30 monthly bus pass subsidy for 16–19 apprentices. [A1]
2. Note 1 realistic supporting statistic: '25% of apprentices drop out in the first 3 months because they fall behind on coursework.' [A1]
3. Write 2 open questions to ask peers: Q1: 'How can we make sure supervisors don't pull apprentices out of their Friday study time?' | Q2: 'What qualities should we look for when choosing workshop mentors?' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 3 Points: (1) 1-to-1 Buddy Scheme, (2) Protected Friday morning study block, (3) £30/month travel pass.
1 Stat: 25% drop-out rate linked to coursework stress.
2 Questions: 'How do we protect Friday study hours during busy weeks?' and 'What training do buddies need?'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Pace your contributions: share Point 1 in the first third, Point 2 in the middle third, and summarise consensus at the end.
🔄 Quick Check: Walking into the discussion with this 6-line card in front of you guarantees you will never freeze or run out of things to say.
Question 4 (Exam-Style Task — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
During Simulation Brief 2, a peer proposes spending £45,000 on solar roof panels—even though the scenario brief states the total budget is only £5,000. Write a polite 3-sentence response that points out the budget constraint without embarrassing them and suggests a lower-cost lighting alternative.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Validate the long-term environmental benefit of solar panels. [M1]
2. Gently remind the group of the £5,000 maximum budget limit in the brief. [M1]
3. Suggest smart motion-sensor LED warehouse lighting for £1,800 instead. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'Solar roof panels would certainly deliver huge long-term carbon savings, Priya. However, because our total budget for this quarter is capped at £5,000, a £45,000 installation would need board approval next year. In the meantime, could we spend £1,800 installing motion-sensor LED switches in the warehouse aisles, which cuts lighting electricity by 35% immediately?'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: If a nervous candidate in your group hasn't spoken after 5 minutes, a good team player actively invites them in.
🔄 Quick Check: Checking proposals against the constraints in the brief (budget, deadline, headcount) proves sharp Level 2 analytical listening.
Section C: Exam-Style Tasks Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Model 2 (Level 2): Annotated Multi-Speaker Discussion Transcript (Simulation Brief 1)) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Analyse how Candidates A, B, and C achieve a unanimous Pass across all Level 2 criteria in this 3-turn exchange.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. 'To open our review of the proposed 12-hour shift rota, I looked at our recent staff survey where 62% of care assistants favoured working three 12-hour shifts per week to cut commuting costs. However, we need to evaluate how 12-hour shifts affect evening medication safety.': Introduces Quantitative Data & Agenda (L2.3, L2.4) [A1]
2. 'Building on Candidate A's point about medication safety, a report by the Royal College of Nursing found that clinical errors double during the eleventh and twelfth hours of a shift unless breaks are enforced. Could we mandate two protected 30-minute rest breaks per 12-hour shift?': Verbal Bridge + External Citation + Solution (L2.4, L2.7) [A1]
3. 'That directly solves the fatigue risk Candidate A raised. What if we pilot an optional hybrid rota on Elm Wing for eight weeks—allowing parents who need 8-hour school-run shifts to keep them, while trialling 12-hour shifts with guaranteed breaks? Amara, how would that work for the night team?': Synthesises Compromise + Invites Quiet Peer (L2.5, L2.7) [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: All three candidates demonstrate Level 2 mastery: citing survey/RCN data, building directly on the previous speaker's words, resolving conflict via a hybrid pilot, and inviting a quiet colleague by name.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Turn to the quiet member warmly and ask: 'David, we haven't heard your view yet—what do you think about option B?'
🔄 Quick Check: Notice how natural and collaborative the exchange is—nobody reads a speech; everyone reacts directly to what was just said.
Question 6 (Level 2 Mastery Challenge — 3 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Create a '3-1-2' Pre-Discussion Notepad Plan for a 12-minute discussion on 'How to Improve Healthy Eating & Wellbeing for Night-Shift Hospital Staff'.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Write 3 distinct practical proposals (24-hour healthy vending chiller, functioning breakroom microwaves, quiet dark rest pods). [M1]
2. Write 1 realistic statistic (e.g. 70% of night nurses rely on sugary snacks after 20:00). [M1]
3. Write 2 open questions to ask peers. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 3 Points: (1) Stock a subsidised 24-hour fresh soup/salad smart-fridge, (2) Install two commercial microwaves on every ward breakroom, (3) Create a quiet darkened 20-minute rest room.
1 Statistic: Trust survey showed 70% of night staff have no access to hot healthy food after 20:00.
2 Open Questions: 'How could catering restock the smart-fridge before the 19:30 shift starts?' and 'What impact does night-shift nutrition have on staff sickness rates?'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Failing to proofread spelling, punctuation, and grammar after completing the draft.
🔄 Quick Check: Practise writing a 3-1-2 plan in under 4 minutes for any workplace topic.
