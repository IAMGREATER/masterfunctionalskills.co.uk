Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.14 / L2.16 – Recognise and explain how presentational and organisational features aid understanding and convey information.
  • L2.17 – Analyse how formatting, typographic emphasis, and visual signposts support the writer's overall purpose.

Golden Rules for Presentational Features

Golden Rule 1: Link Every Feature to Specific Content & Reader Effect

You only get half the marks for naming a feature (e.g. 'bullet points' or 'bold heading'). To score full marks, state WHAT specific information the feature highlights in that text and HOW it helps the reader.

Golden Rule / Technique: Formula: [Presentational Feature] + [Specific Content Shown] + [How It Helps the Reader]
Golden Rule 2: Never Confuse Presentational Features with Language Features

Presentational features are visual formatting elements you can see without reading the words (bold, bullet points, subheadings, boxes, photos). Language features are word choices (metaphors, rhetorical questions, rule of three).

Golden Rule / Technique: Visual Formatting = Presentational | Word Choice & Figures of Speech = Language

Core Skill 1: Catalogue of Core Presentational Devices & Their Exact Effects

The standard typographical and visual features tested in Functional Skills Reading papers.

Presentational Feature How to Recognise It Accredited Exam Explanation (Effect)
Main Heading / Headline Largest bold title at the very top of the page. Immediately announces the core topic (e.g. 'New Fire Drill Rules') and grabs the target audience's attention.
Subheadings Smaller bold titles dividing sections of the body text. Breaks a long document into thematic sections so readers can scan straight to relevant information (e.g. 'Ticket Prices').
Bullet Points Indented list items marked with dots (•). Separates key facts or kit items into bite-sized points that are easy to digest at a glance without reading dense prose.
Numbered List Items marked 1, 2, 3 in order. Sets out a clear chronological sequence of steps that the reader must follow in exact order.
Bold / Capitalised Text Darker, thicker letters or ALL CAPS within a sentence. Creates visual contrast to emphasise vital warnings, deadlines, or key terms (e.g. 'DO NOT enter').
Shaded Box / Callout Panel Text enclosed in a coloured or bordered rectangle. Isolates high-priority details (such as emergency phone numbers or discount codes) from the surrounding paragraphs.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Digital & Webpage Presentational Features

Recognize hyperlinks, navigation bars, search boxes, and dropdown menus in online source texts.

Many Functional Skills source texts are mock webpages, staff intranets, or discussion forums. When asked for presentational/organisational features of an online text, look for:

  • Hyperlinks (Underlined / Blue Text): Allows the user to click directly to related pages, booking forms, or external policies for deeper detail.
  • Top Navigation Bar / Menu Tabs: Lets users switch rapidly between site categories (e.g. Home | Services | Book Appointment | Contact Us).
  • Search Bar & Filter Buttons: Enables users to type keywords and jump straight to specific articles without scrolling.
  • Star Ratings / Timestamped Avatars: In review or forum texts, visually shows user satisfaction scores at a glance and indicates when each comment was posted.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Level 1 Identification vs Level 2 Contextual Evaluation

How to tailor your answer to the mark tariff.

At Level 1 (e.g. "List two organisational features used in Text A"): Simply naming the features accurately ("1. Subheadings. 2. Bullet points.") secures full marks.
At Level 2 (e.g. "Explain how two presentational features help the writer achieve their purpose"): You MUST link each feature to the specific content of the text ("The bold red subheading 'URGENT PRODUCT RECALL' immediately alerts customers to the safety risk, while the bulleted list of serial numbers allows shoppers to check their kettle model quickly.").

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 2 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Reading Level 1)

Vocational Context: NHS Outpatient Appointment Letter

An outpatient letter includes the following formatted section:
WHAT TO BRING TO YOUR SCAN:
• Your appointment reference letter
• A list of your current prescribed tablets
IMPORTANT: Do not eat any solid food for 6 hours before your scan.
Identify two presentational features used in this extract and state what information each one highlights. [2 Marks]
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Identify Feature 1 and link to content
Bullet points — used to list the two items the patient needs to bring to the scan clearly.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Feature 1]
Step 2 Identify Feature 2 and link to content
Bold/capitalised text (and italics) — used on the heading 'WHAT TO BRING TO YOUR SCAN' and to emphasise the urgent fasting rule ('IMPORTANT: Do not eat...').
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Feature 2]
Final Accredited Response: 1. Bullet points: Clearly list the items the patient must bring. | 2. Bold and capital letters: Emphasise the section title and the crucial rule not to eat for 6 hours before the scan.
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Always mention what information the bullet points or bold text actually show!
Benchmark Exam Model 2 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Reading Level 2)

Vocational Context: Facility Management & Safety

A workplace emergency evacuation poster features:
• A large bold red banner heading: FIRE ACTION: IF YOU DISCOVER A FIRE
• A numbered list (1 to 4) setting out the alarm and exit steps
• A green shaded box at the bottom reading: 'Assembly Point: Car Park Zone C'.
(a) Identify two different presentational features used in the poster. [1 Mark]
(b) Explain how two of these features help staff during a real emergency. [2 Marks]
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Identify two distinct presentational features
Feature 1: Bold capitalised red banner heading. Feature 2: Numbered list (1 to 4) / Green shaded callout box.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Features]
Step 2 Explain the practical effect of the numbered list
The numbered list (1 to 4) presents the evacuation sequence in strict chronological order so panicked employees can follow the exact escape steps rapidly without reading dense paragraphs.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Effect 1]
Step 3 Explain the practical effect of the green shaded box
The green shaded box visually separates the final meeting location ('Assembly Point: Car Park Zone C') so fleeing staff can spot where to gather at a glance.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Effect 2]
Final Accredited Response: (a) Bold red banner heading, numbered list, and green shaded box. | (b) 1. The numbered list allows panicked staff to follow the escape steps in exact chronological order. 2. The green shaded box makes the Car Park Zone C assembly point stand out instantly.
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Never give vague answers like 'it makes it look pretty'. Always explain how the formatting helps a busy or panicked reader process the information faster.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Confusing Language Features with Presentational Features
Alliteration, rhetorical questions, and metaphors are language techniques. Bold fonts, tables, bullet points, subheadings, and shaded boxes are presentational features.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Ask: 'Is this a visual design/formatting choice on the page?' If yes, it is a presentational feature.
Examiner Pitfall: Saying 'It Makes the Page Look Less Boring'
Examiners award 0 marks for saying bullet points or subheadings 'make the text look nice/less boring'.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Use functional verbs: 'breaks complex information into scannable points', 'guides the reader's eye to urgent warnings', or 'enables rapid navigation'.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
An NHS appointment booking webpage features a prominent yellow shaded box at the top stating: 'URGENT: Please arrive 15 minutes before your scheduled MRI scan.'
Identify two presentational features used here and explain their purpose.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Features: Yellow shaded callout box AND bold capital letters on 'URGENT:' [1 Mark].
2. Purpose: Draws immediate visual attention to the time-sensitive arrival requirement (15 minutes early) so patients do not miss their MRI slot [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Features: Shaded yellow box and bold capital letters ('URGENT'). | Purpose: Immediately alerts patients to the requirement to arrive 15 minutes early before they read the rest of the page.
Examiner Tip: Identify both the container (shaded box) and the typography (bold capitals), then link them to the 15-minute arrival rule.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
A staff intranet guide on claiming travel expenses includes underlined blue text reading: 'Click here to download the Mileage Claim Form (PDF)'.
Name this presentational feature and explain how it helps employees.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Feature: Hyperlink (clickable link) [1 Mark].
2. Effect: Allows employees to open and download the required Mileage Claim Form directly with one click without having to search the intranet manually [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Feature: Hyperlink. | Effect: Gives staff instant one-click access to download the Mileage Claim Form without searching for it.
Examiner Tip: Whenever you see underlined blue clickable text in an online source, name it specifically as a 'hyperlink'.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A magazine article reviewing electric vans includes a large, bordered box in the middle of the page printing a quote in oversized italics: '"Switching our bakery vans to electric saved us £3,400 in diesel in just six months!" — Sarah Lin, Owner'.
Name this presentational feature [1 Mark] and give two reasons why the editor included it [2 Marks].
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Feature: Pull quote / callout quote box (with oversized italics) [1 Mark].
2. Reason 1: Breaks up dense columns of text and hooks skimming readers' attention [1 Mark].
3. Reason 2: Highlights the most persuasive real-life financial saving (£3,400 saved in six months) to convince other business owners [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Feature: Pull quote / boxed quotation in italics. | Reasons: 1. Breaks up the article layout to grab skimming readers' attention. 2. Emphasises the impressive £3,400 fuel saving to persuade business readers.
Examiner Tip: A quotation lifted from an article and printed in large font inside a box is called a 'pull quote' or 'callout quote'.
