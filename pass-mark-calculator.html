Understanding Functional Skills Pass Rules & Grade Boundaries

Official Ofqual regulatory guidance on how Functional Skills Maths and English exams are marked and awarded.

In Maths, do you have to pass Non-Calculator and Calculator separately?

No, you do not. Under official Ofqual regulations across all UK awarding bodies (Pearson Edexcel, City & Guilds, Open Awards, NCFE, and AQA), Functional Skills Mathematics is awarded strictly on your combined aggregate total mark.

For example, in Pearson Edexcel Maths Level 2, the exam consists of Section A (Non-Calculator, 16 marks) and Section B (Calculator, 48 marks) for a total of 64 marks. The pass cutoff is typically ~35–37 marks. If a student achieves 6/16 on Section A and 34/48 on Section B, their total is 40/64—which is an official PASS. There is no independent minimum hurdle for either section.

Why do many people think you must pass both sections independently?

This widespread misconception usually happens for two reasons:

  • Confusion with English: In Functional Skills English, Reading and Writing are separate standalone exams with independent pass thresholds. Candidates often assume Maths works the same way.
  • Mathematical pressure: Because Section A is 25% of the total marks in Maths, scoring 0 or very few marks puts immense mathematical pressure on the Calculator section (requiring 75%+ accuracy in Section B) to hit the aggregate pass mark. However, it is not an automatic regulatory fail.

Can a strong calculator section make up for non-calculator mistakes?

Yes, 100%. Because Section B (Calculator) represents approximately 75% of the total marks (e.g. 48 out of 64 marks in Edexcel, or 45 out of 60 in City & Guilds), confident calculator skills and thorough working out in Section B can easily lift your overall mark well above the pass threshold.

How does Functional Skills English marking differ from Maths?

In Functional Skills English (Level 1 & Level 2), Reading, Writing, and Speaking, Listening & Communicating (SLC) are distinct, independently accredited components. You must pass each component on its own merits (typically 55–60% on Reading and 55–60% on Writing). Any component you pass is permanently "banked"—if you pass Reading but fail Writing, you only need to resit the Writing paper.
