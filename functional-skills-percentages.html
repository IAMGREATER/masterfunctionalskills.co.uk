Specification & Learning Outcomes

Regulated Standards

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills criteria:

  • L1.13: Read, write, order and compare percentages in whole numbers.
  • L1.14: Calculate percentages of quantities, including simple percentage increases and decreases by 5% and multiples thereof with and without a calculator.
  • L2.5: Work out percentages of amounts and express one amount as a percentage of another.
  • L2.6: Calculate percentage change (any size increase and decrease) and original value after percentage change (Reverse Percentages).
  • Format all monetary outputs to two decimal places (e.g. £96.00) and verify answers against realistic workplace contexts.

Golden Rules for Percentage Mastery

Golden Rule 1: Master the Non-Calculator Building Blocks (10%, 1%, 5%, 50%)

For non-calculator exams, never multiply by large fractions manually. Build every percentage from basic building blocks:

10% Divide by 10
1% Divide by 100
5% Half of 10%
50% Divide by 2
25% Half of 50%
Example: 15% of £480 = 10% (£48) + 5% (£24) = £72.00
Golden Rule 2: Multiplier Mastery for Calculator Papers

On calculator papers, solve percentage increases and decreases in a single multiplication by converting the final percentage into a decimal multiplier:

  • Increase by 6%: 100% + 6% = 106% → Multiplier is 1.06
  • Increase by 15%: 100% + 15% = 115% → Multiplier is 1.15
  • Decrease by 20%: 100% − 20% = 80% → Multiplier is 0.80
  • Decrease by 7.5%: 100% − 7.5% = 92.5% → Multiplier is 0.925
Examiner Check: An increase always produces a multiplier > 1; a decrease always produces a multiplier < 1.
Golden Rule 3: The Golden Law of Reverse Percentages

When an exam question gives the final value after a percentage increase or discount and asks for the original starting price:

Original Amount = Final Amount ÷ Multiplier

Fatal Mistake: NEVER calculate the percentage of the final price and subtract or add it. The percentage was taken from the original unknown amount, not the final amount!

Skill 1: Calculating a Percentage of an Amount
Level 1 & Level 2 Core Skill
Core Formula
Amount = (Percentage ÷ 100) × Total

Workplace Scenario: An NHS outpatient clinic is allocated a monthly consumable budget of £640.00. Management specifies that exactly 15% of this budget must be reserved for surgical personal protective equipment (PPE).
How much money must be reserved for PPE?

Step 1
Find 10% (The primary building block):
Divide the total sum by 10:
£640.00 ÷ 10 = £64.00
Step 2
Find 5% (Half of 10%):
Halve the 10% figure calculated in Step 1:
£64.00 ÷ 2 = £32.00
Step 3
Combine blocks to achieve 15%:
Add the 10% and 5% values together:
£64.00 + £32.00 = £96.00
Check:
Calculator Method Verification:
Convert 15% to a decimal multiplier (15 ÷ 100 = 0.15):
0.15 × £640.00 = £96.00
Reserved PPE Allocation: £96.00
Mental Math Trick: To calculate 15% swiftly, always calculate 10% first, halve it for 5%, and add both figures together.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel • City & Guilds
Skill 2: Expressing One Amount as a Percentage of Another
Core Compliance & Audit Skill
Percentage of a Whole Formula
Percentage = (Part ÷ Whole) × 100%

Workplace Scenario: Meadow View Care Centre employs 80 healthcare support workers. An internal training audit reveals that 68 workers have completed mandatory Infection Prevention and Control certification.
What percentage of the workforce has completed the certification?

Step 1
Formulate the fraction:
Place the compliant part over the total whole workforce:
Fraction = 68 ÷ 80
Step 2
Convert the fraction to a decimal:
Divide the numerator by the denominator (use bus stop or calculator):
68 ÷ 80 = 0.85
Step 3
Multiply by 100 to convert to a percentage:
Shift the decimal point two places to the right:
0.85 × 100% = 85%
Workforce Certification Rate: 85% Completed
Rule to Remember: Divide the quantity you are interested in (68) by the total possible amount (80), then multiply by 100.

Skill 3: Percentage Increase (Multiplier Method)
High-Value Level 2 Exam Topic
The Increase Multiplier
Increased Value = Original Value × (1 + Percentage ÷ 100)

Workplace Scenario: An NHS senior support worker earns £22,400.00 annually. Following a national pay award, all staff receive a 4.5% salary increase.
Calculate their new annual salary.

Step 1
Determine the percentage multiplier:
Add the increase to the 100% original baseline and convert to a decimal:
100% + 4.5% = 104.5% → 104.5 ÷ 100 = 1.045
Step 2
Multiply original salary by multiplier:
Single-step calculation for full method marks:
£22,400.00 × 1.045 = £23,408.00
Non-Calc:
Non-Calculator Verification:
1% = £224.00 → 4% = £896.00 → 0.5% = £112.00
Total increase = £896.00 + £112.00 = £1,008.00
New salary = £22,400.00 + £1,008.00 = £23,408.00
New Increased Annual Salary: £23,408.00
Speed Rule: For an increase, always add to 100%. The decimal multiplier will always be greater than 1 (1.045).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel • City & Guilds
Skill 4: Percentage Decrease (Multiplier Method)
High-Value Level 2 Exam Topic
The Decrease Multiplier
Decreased Value = Original Value × (1 − Percentage ÷ 100)

Workplace Scenario: A patient transport service currently consumes 5,200 litres of fuel per quarter. Route optimization and hybrid engines decrease fuel consumption by 15%.
How many litres are used now?

Step 1
Determine the decrease multiplier:
Subtract the percentage decrease from 100%:
100% − 15% = 85% → Multiplier = 0.85
Step 2
Calculate new reduced fuel volume:
Multiply the original amount by 0.85:
5,200 × 0.85 = 4,420 litres
Non-Calc:
Non-Calculator Check:
10% of 5,200 = 520 L → 5% = 260 L
15% = 520 + 260 = 780 L reduction
5,200 − 780 = 4,420 litres
Reduced Fuel Consumption: 4,420 litres
Speed Rule: For a decrease, always subtract from 100%. The decimal multiplier will always be less than 1 (0.85).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel • City & Guilds
Skill 5: Reverse Percentages (Finding the Original Value)
Level 2 Distinction & Pass Criterion
The Reverse Percentage Formula
Original Amount = Final Amount ÷ Multiplier

Workplace Scenario: An NHS trust purchases an ultrasonic diagnostic scanner. The total supplier invoice including 20% VAT is £648.00.
Calculate the original pre-VAT price of the scanner before the tax was applied.

Step 1
Recognise the percentage representation:
The final invoice represents 100% of the equipment price plus 20% VAT:
100% + 20% = 120% = £648.00
Step 2
Find the decimal multiplier:
Convert 120% into a decimal:
120 ÷ 100 = 1.20
Step 3
Divide the invoice amount by the multiplier:
£648.00 ÷ 1.20 = £540.00
Verify:
Sanity Check (Add 20% back on):
10% of £540 = £54.00 → 20% = £108.00
£540.00 + £108.00 = £648.00 (Matches invoice exactly!)
Original Pre-VAT Price: £540.00
Key Exam Takeaway: The 20% tax was calculated from the £540 base, NOT from the £648 total. Always divide by the multiplier!
Level 2 Only Pearson Edexcel • City & Guilds

Worked Exam Model: Multi-Step Evaluation

5 Marks Pearson Edexcel Style
Real Exam Context: Commercial Procurement

A care facility needs to purchase 15 new specialist profiling beds. The standard catalog price is £420.00 per bed.
Two medical suppliers submit bids for the order:

Supplier Alpha:

Offers a straight 15% trade discount on the total list price of the entire order.

Supplier Beta:

Offers a flat £50 cash rebate for every whole £500 spent on the order.

Which supplier is cheaper, and by how much? Show complete step-by-step working to justify your answer.

Step 1
Calculate total undiscounted cost:
15 beds × £420.00 per bed:
15 × £420.00 = £6,300.00
Step 2
Calculate Supplier Alpha's discounted price:
Apply 15% discount using the multiplier 0.85 (100% − 15% = 85%):
£6,300.00 × 0.85 = £5,355.00
Step 3
Calculate Supplier Beta's rebate price:
Count how many whole £500 blocks exist in £6,300:
£6,300 ÷ 500 = 12.6 → exactly 12 whole £500 blocks
Total rebate = 12 × £50.00 = £600.00
Supplier Beta Final Cost = £6,300.00 − £600.00 = £5,700.00
Step 4
Compare both suppliers and find the difference:
£5,700.00 − £5,355.00 = £345.00
Final Conclusion & Justification: Supplier Alpha is cheaper by £345.00 (£5,355.00 vs £5,700.00).

Examiner Traps & Common Distractors

Trap 1: The "Reverse Percentage Fallacy"

If an item costs £120 including 20% VAT, students frequently calculate 20% of £120 (£24) and subtract it to get £96. This is fundamentally wrong!

Rule: £120 represents 120%. You must divide by 1.20 to find 100%: £120 ÷ 1.20 = £100.00.
Examiner Check: Always check your answer by adding 20% back onto your result. 20% of £100 is £20, which gives £120.
Trap 2: Decimal Multiplier Slip for Single-Digit Percentages

A very common slip under timed pressure is writing 1.4 for a 4% increase, or 0.3 for 3%.

  • 4% increase multiplier is 1.04 (1.4 represents a massive 40% increase!).
  • 3% discount multiplier is 0.97 (0.3 represents a 70% reduction!).
Rule: If the percentage is less than 10%, remember the zero after the decimal point (e.g. 5% = 0.05).
Trap 3: Stopping After Calculating the Change Value

When asked for the new price after a 12% discount on a £350 coat, students often calculate 12% (£42.00) and stop there, writing £42.00 in the answer box.

Prevention: Re-read the question stem. Did it ask for the amount of discount, or the final price paid? £350 − £42 = £308.00.

Practice Task 1: Access Ramp Maintenance Grant [3 Marks]

A community health center receives a discretionary maintenance grant of £560.00. The board votes to allocate exactly 35% of this grant towards improving wheelchair access ramps.

Calculate how much money is allocated to wheelchair ramp improvements.

Official Tutor Step-by-Step Mark Scheme
3 / 3 Marks Awarded
Step 1 (Find 10%) M1 Method Mark
£560.00 ÷ 10 = £56.00
Divide the total grant by 10 to establish the primary non-calculator building block.
Step 2 (Calculate 30% and 5%) M1 Method Mark
30% = 3 × £56.00 = £168.00  |  5% = £56.00 ÷ 2 = £28.00
Multiply the 10% value by 3 for 30%, and halve the 10% value for 5%.
Step 3 (Combine Components) A1 Accuracy Mark
£168.00 + £28.00 = £196.00
Sum both parts together to obtain the full 35% allocation.
Correct Final Answer:
£196.00
Examiner Marking Tip: Alternative valid method: 25% (half of half = £140.00) + 10% (£56.00) = £196.00. Both methods capture full method marks!

Practice Task 2: Ambulance Dispatch Satisfaction KPI [4 Marks]

A regional ambulance call centre conducted a quarterly survey. Out of 350 patient family responses received, exactly 287 rated the dispatch communication as "Good" or "Outstanding".

The regional NHS service target is for at least 80% of callers to rate dispatch communication as Good or Outstanding.

Has the call centre met the service target? You must show all your calculations to justify your answer.

Official Tutor Step-by-Step Mark Scheme
4 / 4 Marks Awarded
Step 1 (Formulate Fraction & Divide) M1 Method Mark
287 ÷ 350 = 0.82
Divide the number of positive ratings by the total responses received.
Step 2 (Convert to Percentage) M1 Method Mark
0.82 × 100 = 82%
Multiply the decimal by 100 to convert into a percentage value.
Step 3 (Compare with KPI Target) E1 Explanation Mark
82% > 80% Target Benchmark
State clearly that 82% is higher than the required 80% NHS service target.
Correct Final Answer:
Yes, target met (82% > 80%)
Examiner Marking Tip: On decision questions ("Has the centre met the target?"), stating just "Yes" scores 0 marks without the supporting 82% calculation and direct comparison.
FS Level 2 Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE Open Awards Highfield
Practice Task 3: Discounted Backup Generator [4 Marks]

A mobile health clinic purchases a backup diesel generator on a seasonal promotion. The promotional sale price is £408.00 after a 15% discount was taken off the original recommended retail price.

Calculate the original price of the generator before the discount was applied.

Official Tutor Step-by-Step Mark Scheme
4 / 4 Marks Awarded
Step 1 (Identify Baseline Percentage) P1 Process Mark
100% − 15% = 85% of Original Price
Recognize that £408.00 represents 85% of the pre-sale price.
Step 2 (Formulate Multiplier & Division) M1 Method Mark
Original Price = £408.00 ÷ 0.85
Divide the sale price by the decimal multiplier (0.85).
Step 3 (Calculate Final Value) A1 Accuracy Mark
£408.00 ÷ 0.85 = £480.00
Calculate the final quotient. State answer clearly formatted in pounds (£).
Correct Final Answer:
£480.00
Examiner Verification Tip: Always double check: 15% of £480.00 is £72.00. Then £480.00 − £72.00 = £408.00. This confirms your answer is 100% correct!
