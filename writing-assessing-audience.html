Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.22 / L2.22 – Write with a clear awareness of audience, adapting tone, register, and vocabulary appropriately.
  • L2.23 – Select sophisticated vocabulary and syntactic structures tailored to formal, semi-formal, and public audiences.

Golden Rules for Audience, Tone & Register

Golden Rule 1: Establish the Exact Register in Sentence One and Never Slip

Never mix registers midway through a task. A formal letter to a Council Director or Hospital CEO must maintain courteous, objective Standard English from the opening salutation to the final full stop.

Golden Rule / Technique: Formal = Objective, courteous, zero slang. Semi-formal = Collaborative, engaging, respectful.
Golden Rule 2: Eliminate Contractions in Formal Letters and Reports

In formal letters, job applications, and official reports, always write verbs in full: 'do not' instead of 'don't', 'cannot' instead of 'can't', 'it is' instead of 'it's', 'will not' instead of 'won't'.

Golden Rule / Technique: Zero contractions in formal documents = immediate boost to register & SPaG marks.

Core Skill 1: The 3-Tier Audience Register Calibration Matrix

How to adapt your greeting, vocabulary, and tone across the three main exam readerships.

Target Audience Required Register & Tone Model Phrasing Example
1. Senior Management / Council / Regulator Highly Formal: Courteous, objective, evidence-driven, zero contractions, no exclamation marks. "I am writing to formally request an urgent review of traffic safety measures outside St Luke's Primary School."
2. Workplace Colleagues / Team Members Semi-Formal Professional: Cooperative, clear, constructive, direct address (we, our team). "Please ensure all portable electrical equipment is unplugged and stored in Bay 2 before 17:30 on Friday."
3. General Public / Patients / Community Blog Accessible & Engaging: Warm, empathetic, jargon-free, persuasive, inclusive. "Whether you are a complete beginner or a seasoned runner, our Saturday Park Walk offers a welcoming morning for the whole family."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Assertive vs Aggressive Tone in Formal Complaints

How to complain firmly and persuasively in an exam task without sounding rude or abusive.

Aggressive / Emotional Rant (Penalised by Examiners):

"Your delivery driver was totally useless and rude!! He dumped my £400 washing machine in the rain and drove off! You lot are a disgrace and I want my money back right now!!"

Assertive, Dignified Formal Complaint (Full Marks):

"I am writing to express my extreme dissatisfaction regarding the delivery of my washing machine (Order Ref: WM-409) on 12 October. Despite paying £25 for indoor installation, the courier left the unpackaged appliance outside in heavy rain without ringing the doorbell. Consequently, I request a full refund of the £25 delivery fee and an inspection of the water-damaged packaging."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: High-Value Vocabulary Upgrades for Formal Tasks

Replace everyday conversational words with formal Standard English equivalents.

  • Instead of "ask for" → write "request" or "enquire about".
  • Instead of "tell you about" → write "inform you of" or "bring to your attention".
  • Instead of "fix the problem" → write "rectify the issue" or "resolve this matter".
  • Instead of "really bad" → write "unacceptable", "detrimental", or "substandard".
  • Instead of "help out" → write "assist", "facilitate", or "support".
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Benchmark Exam Model 1 Level 1 Audience & Tone Benchmark (3 Marks)

Vocational Context: Office Administration & Team Etiquette

Rewrite this rude note left on the staffroom fridge as a polite, semi-formal email to all office staff:
'Whoever keeps leaving dirty mugs in the sink and mouldy milk in the fridge needs to grow up! Clean up your own mess or the manager will ban us from using the kitchen!'
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Replace hostile accusations with a collaborative team greeting
Use a constructive subject line and address colleagues collectively without pointing fingers.
Criteria: Appropriate Semi-Formal Tone (L1.22)
Step 2 State the two hygiene rules clearly and politely
Ask staff to place used mugs directly into the dishwasher and dispose of expired milk every Friday.
Criteria: Clear Actionable Requests
Step 3 Explain the shared benefit for the team
Keeping the breakroom clean ensures a pleasant environment for everyone during lunch breaks.
Criteria: Positive Closing Register
Final Accredited Response: To: All Office Staff
Subject: Keeping Our Shared Staff Kitchen Clean and Tidy

Dear Everyone,

As our staff kitchen is busy during lunch breaks, please could we all work together to keep the area clean and hygienic?

Please rinse your mugs and place them straight into the dishwasher after use rather than leaving them in the sink. Additionally, our cleaning team will empty the refrigerator every Friday at 16:30, so please label your food containers and remove any expired milk before the weekend.

Thank you for your cooperation in keeping our breakroom pleasant for the whole team.

Best regards,
Office Facilities Team
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Notice how using inclusive pronouns ('our kitchen', 'work together') achieves full compliance without alienating colleagues.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 Level 2 Formal Register Benchmark (4 Marks)

Vocational Context: Public Environmental Health & Statutory Nuisance

Draft the opening two paragraphs of a formal complaint letter to Ms Henderson, Head of Environmental Services, regarding commercial fly-tipping behind a residential care centre.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Select formal salutation and state immediate grievance
Use formal greeting ('Dear Ms Henderson,') and authoritative opening sentence stating exact location.
Criteria: Formal Salutation & Purpose (L2.22)
Step 2 Maintain dignified, objective complaint register
Describe the hazard factually using precise vocabulary ('obstructing emergency access', 'attracting vermin') without slang or exclamation marks.
Criteria: Sustained Formal Register (L2.23)
Step 3 Specify requested statutory action and timeframe
Request an Environmental Health enforcement officer visit and clearance within 48 hours.
Criteria: Assertive Call to Action
Final Accredited Response: Dear Ms Henderson,

I am writing in my capacity as Facilities Manager at St Mary’s Residential Care Centre to register an urgent formal complaint regarding persistent commercial fly-tipping in the service lane adjacent to our rear fire exits.

Over the past fortnight, unknown contractors have deposited broken plasterboard, rusted metal sheeting, and unsealed paint canisters across the access driveway. Not only does this hazardous waste attract vermin within metres of our kitchen store, but it also severely obstructs emergency ambulance access for our frail residents. I therefore urge your department to dispatch an enforcement crew to clear the lane within 48 hours and install CCTV signage to deter further offences.

Yours sincerely,
David Clarke, Facilities Manager
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Notice how the tone is firm, urgent, and authoritative without ever becoming rude. Examiners reward precise vocabulary ('in my capacity as', 'obstructs emergency ambulance access').

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Inconsistent Tone Shifts Midway Through a Response
Starting formally with 'Dear Sir or Madam, I am writing to complain...' and then slipping into 'Anyway, it's a total rip-off and you need to sort it ASAP' in Paragraph 3 ruins register consistency.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: During your final 5-minute proofread, check that your last paragraph is just as formal and controlled as your first.
Examiner Pitfall: Using Text-Speak or Multiple Exclamation Marks (!!!)
Writing 'u', 'asap', 'cos', or ending sentences with '!!' in a Functional Skills exam immediately caps your SPaG and register marks.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Write every word out in full Standard English and use a single full stop at the end of declarative sentences.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Explain why writing: 'Hey guys, grab your big coats cos the ward heating is busted today lol' is inappropriate for an official hospital staff email memo, and rewrite it in an appropriate professional register.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Critique: Identify the casual slang ('Hey guys', 'cos', 'busted', 'lol') which is unprofessional for clinical workplace records.
2. Rewrite: Provide a clear, polite semi-formal staff advisory.
Final Model Answer: Critique: Uses informal slang ('cos', 'busted', 'lol') and casual greeting ('Hey guys') which undermine professional communication.
Professional Rewrite: 'Please be advised that the Ward 4 heating system is currently undergoing emergency repair; staff are permitted to wear plain navy thermal fleeces during today's shift.'
Examiner Tip: Always provide both the brief explanation of why the original fails and the complete rewritten sentence.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Rewrite this overly aggressive complaint paragraph into an assertive, formal Level 2 register suitable for a letter to a Training Provider Director:
'Your tutor didn't bother turning up for our first-aid exam on Tuesday and nobody even phoned us! Seven of my staff wasted a whole morning sitting in an empty room! You better give us our £700 back today!'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Replace emotional accusations ('didn't bother turning up', 'wasted a whole morning') with objective facts.
2. Eliminate exclamation marks and aggressive demands ('You better give us').
3. State the financial impact and formal request for a full £700 refund or rescheduled sitting.
Final Model Answer: 'Regrettably, your assigned assessor failed to attend our scheduled First-Aid at Work examination on Tuesday 10 October, and no prior cancellation notice was communicated to our office. Consequently, seven members of our care team were removed from clinical duties for an entire morning without achieving their certification. I therefore request an immediate full refund of the £700 booking fee or a priority rescheduled assessment at our premises within seven days.'
Examiner Tip: Words like 'Regrettably' and 'Consequently' allow you to express strong dissatisfaction while remaining 100% professional.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
Adapt this technical clinical instruction so it is warm, accessible, and easy to understand for an anxious patient reading a hospital pre-admission leaflet:
'Patients scheduled for general anaesthesia must maintain nil-by-mouth status from 00:00 prior to admission; non-compliance will result in immediate cancellation of the surgical procedure.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Translate technical jargon ('general anaesthesia', 'nil-by-mouth status', 'non-compliance') into plain, reassuring English.
2. Explain clearly that patients must not eat food or drink liquids after midnight.
3. Explain gently why this rule keeps them safe during their operation.
Final Model Answer: 'To keep you completely safe during your operation, it is essential that your stomach is empty before you are given your anaesthetic. Please do not eat any food or drink any liquids (including water, tea, or chewing gum) after midnight on the night before your surgery. If you accidentally eat or drink after midnight, we may need to rearrange your operation for another day for your own safety.'
Examiner Tip: Explaining the safety reason ('so your stomach is empty') makes public leaflets reassuring rather than intimidating.
