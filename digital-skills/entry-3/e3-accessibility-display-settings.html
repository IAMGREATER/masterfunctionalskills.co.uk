Three Non-Negotiable Exam Golden Rules

Golden Rule 1: Raise Display Scale (125%–150%) Without Shrinking Resolution

Windows Settings > System > Display Scale enlarges icons, menus, and text crisply while keeping your native sharp resolution intact.

✗ Dropping screen resolution down to blurry 800x600
✓ Increase Display Scale to 125% or 150% under Accessibility / Display Settings
Golden Rule 2: Match the Assistive Tool to the Impairment Type

Vision: High Contrast Themes, Magnifier (Win + Plus), Narrator/Screen Reader. Hearing: Live Captions / Visual Sound Alerts. Motor: Sticky Keys / Voice Typing.

✗ Turning on Captions to help someone with blurred vision read menus
✓ Turn on Magnifier or Screen Reader for vision; Captions for audio/video
Golden Rule 3: Enable Night Light / Warm Tint to Reduce Visual Fatigue

Night Light shifts harsh blue screen glare toward warmer cream/amber tones during extended reading or evening shifts.

✗ Leaving maximum blinding blue brightness on at night
✓ Adjust brightness slider comfortably + enable Night Light warm filter

Step-by-Step Teaching & Key Concepts

1
Adjusting Volume and Screen Brightness

Maintaining comfortable screen brightness and audio volume protects your eyes and ears from fatigue:

  • Audio Volume: Click the speaker icon in the bottom right corner of the taskbar. Drag the slider to the right to increase volume, or click the speaker symbol to mute.
  • Screen Brightness: Open Windows Settings (Win + I), click System > Display, and drag the brightness slider. Reducing brightness saves battery life and reduces glare in low light.

2
Text Size and High Contrast for Visual Clarity

For people with visual impairments or dyslexia, standard text can be difficult to read:

  • Make Text Bigger: In Settings, choose Accessibility > Text size. Drag the slider until sample text is comfortable to read without squinting, then click Apply.
  • High Contrast Themes: Replaces low-contrast grey backgrounds with distinct black and yellow/white high-visibility colour schemes, making letters clearly stand out.
Dyslexia Readability Tip: Pairing larger text sizes with a soft cream or peach background tint reduces visual stress and eliminates glare when reading long documents.
3
Magnifier and Screen Readers

Operating systems include built-in assistive tools:

  • Magnifier: Zooms in on any section of the screen (press Win + +).
  • Narrator / Screen Reader: Speaks the text on the screen aloud for users with visual disabilities.
Open Magnifier Zoom In: Win + Plus • Zoom Out Magnifier: Win + Minus • Exit Magnifier: Win + Esc

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Trap 1: Confusing Browser Zoom (Ctrl + +) With OS Scale
Ctrl + + inside Edge/Chrome only zooms the webpage, leaving tiny OS file dialog menus unchanged.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Use OS `Display > Scale` or `Accessibility > Text size` when all menus and system windows are too small.
Trap 2: Choosing 'Mute' Instead of Mono Audio
Muting cuts off all sound instead of merging Left+Right stereo channels into one clear ear.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Mention `Mono Audio` specifically whenever a user hears out of one ear better than the other.
Trap 3: Forgetting Sticky Keys for Single-Finger Shortcuts
Struggling with two-hand combos like Ctrl + Alt + Del.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Sticky Keys allows modifier keys (`Ctrl`, `Shift`, `Alt`) to be pressed sequentially one key at a time.
