Official Entry Level 3 Scope (E3.H.23)

What you need for Entry Level 3 Maths — and what is saved for Level 1 so you never waste time on harder topics:

Included at Entry Level 3
Reading and drawing single-series bar charts (scales in 1s, 2s, 5s, 10s, or 20s), comparing tallest/shortest bars, finding totals and differences, and checking titles/labels.
Saved for Level 1 (Not Tested Here)
Compound/clustered dual bar charts, pie chart angle calculations (360°), histograms, and scatter graphs. View Dual Bar Charts & Pie Charts (Level 1) →

3 Simple Entry 3 Golden Rules: Bar Charts

Golden Rule 1: Read the Number Axis Step Size Before Touching Any Bar!

Check whether the vertical axis goes up in 1s, 2s, 5s, or 10s. If a bar stops halfway between 10 and 20, its value is 15!

Entry 3 Example: Use a ruler horizontally from the flat top of the bar straight across to the number axis.
Golden Rule 2: Tallest Bar = Most • Shortest Bar = Least

For ‘How many MORE?’, read both bar heights and subtract! For ‘How many ALTOGETHER?’, read every bar height and add!

Entry 3 Example: Write each bar's number neatly on top of the bar first — it prevents reading slips!
Golden Rule 3: 4 Essentials When Drawing a Bar Chart

1) Even number scale starting at 0 • 2) Equal-width bars with equal gaps • 3) Both axes labelled • 4) Clear chart title.

Entry 3 Example: Always use a ruler and pencil!

Visual Bar Chart (Scale in 5s, Including Halfway Bar = 15)

Core Skill 1: Reading Bars That Stop Between Two Gridlines

Find the halfway value between the line below and the line above.

Gridlines go 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 → halfway between 6 and 8 is 7!
Gridlines go 0, 10, 20, 30 → halfway between 20 and 30 is 25!

Core Skill 2: Spotting What Is Missing on an Incomplete Bar Chart

Examiners often show a trainee's chart and ask 'Write down ONE thing missing' — check 0 at the bottom, axis labels, gaps between bars, and title!

Check uneven scales like 0, 5, 10, 15, 25
Every equal grid jump MUST add the exact same amount!
Sonya’s Entry 3 Exam Tip
Before answering any question on a bar chart, spend 10 seconds pencilling the exact value on top of EVERY bar. Once the numbers are written down, the whole question turns into simple Entry 3 addition and subtraction!

Entry Level 3 Step-by-Step Worked Exam Question

Entry Level 3 Exam Model 3 Marks (Non-Calculator)

Everyday Scenario: Community Café Muffin Sales Chart

A bar chart shows muffins sold on Monday:
• Blueberry: 25  • Chocolate: 35  • Lemon: 15  • Banana: 20
(a) How many more Chocolate muffins were sold than Lemon?
(b) Work out the total number of muffins sold on Monday.
Step
Clear Working Out
Mark Focus
Step 1 Subtract Lemon from Chocolate
35 − 15 = 20 more
[A1] 20 more
Step 2 Add all four bar heights
25 + 35 + 15 + 20
[M1] Add all 4 bars
Step 3 Calculate total cleanly
60 + 35 = 95 muffins
[A1] 95 muffins
Final Exam Answer: (a) 20 more | (b) 95 muffins altogether
Sonya’s Check Reminder: Always include units (£, p, cm, g, ml, or °C) and double-check you answered the exact question asked!

Common Entry 3 Mistakes to Avoid

Common Entry 3 Mistake: Reading Halfway Between 10 and 20 as 11
Halfway of a gap of 10 is +5 (so 15, not 11!).
Common Entry 3 Mistake: Drawing Bars Stuck Together With No Gaps
Standard bar charts for separate categories (Mon, Tue, Wed) should have neat, equal gaps between bars.

Interactive Entry Level 3 Practice Questions

Entry 3 Practice 1: Halfway Bar Reading 2 Marks
On a bar chart, the vertical scale goes up in 10s (`0, 10, 20, 30, 40`). The bar for 'Wednesday' stops exact halfway between 20 and 30, and 'Friday' stops right on 40. How many more is Friday than Wednesday?
Entry Level 3 Worked Solution:
Read Wednesday (1 Mark): 25
Subtract (1 Mark): 40 − 25 = 15
Final Answer: 15
Entry 3 Practice 2: Drawing the Final Bar 2 Marks
Total parcels delivered in 4 days = 80. Mon = 20, Tue = 15, Wed = 25. How tall should you draw the bar for Thursday?
Entry Level 3 Worked Solution:
Add Mon–Wed (1 Mark): 20 + 15 + 25 = 60
Subtract from 80 (1 Mark): 80 − 60 = 20
Final Answer: Height = 20
