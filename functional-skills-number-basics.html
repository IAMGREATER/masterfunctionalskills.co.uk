Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • E3.1 / L1.1 – Count, read, write, order, and compare whole numbers up to 1,000 and beyond.
  • E3.2 / L1.1 – Understand place value in 3-digit and 4-digit numbers and use zero as a positional placeholder.

Golden Rules for Number Basics: Place Value & Counting

Golden Rule 1: The Thousands, Hundreds, Tens & Units Model

In a 4-digit number like 3,482: 3 means 3 thousands (3,000), 4 means 4 hundreds (400), 8 means 8 tens (80), and 2 means 2 units (2). If a column is empty, write 0 to hold the place!

Golden Rule / Method: Thousands (1,000) • Hundreds (100) • Tens (10) • Units (1)
Golden Rule 2: Align Digits by the Right-Hand Units Column

When adding, subtracting, or comparing whole numbers of different lengths (e.g. 1,240 and 85), always line up the rightmost Units digits vertically.

Golden Rule / Method: Line up Units under Units, Tens under Tens, Hundreds under Hundreds

Core Skill 1: Partitioning & Place Value Breakdown up to 10,000

Splitting numbers into thousands, hundreds, tens, and units and using zero placeholders.

  • Expanded Partitioning: 4,658 = 4,000 + 600 + 50 + 8.
  • Zero Placeholders: "Two thousand and forty-seven" has no hundreds, so we write 2,047 (not 247!).
  • Comparing Size: Compare the leftmost column first: 842 > 799 because 8 hundreds is greater than 7 hundreds.

Core Skill 3: Column Addition, Subtraction & Reverse Checking

Setting out vertical calculations and checking by doing the opposite operation.

  • Column Addition: Add units first; if the sum is 10 or more, write the unit digit and carry the 1 ten to the next column.
  • Reverse Check for Subtraction: If 450 − 185 = 265, check by adding 265 + 185 = 450.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 2 Marks (Entry Level 3 / Level 1)

Vocational Context: Care Facility Domestic Services

A laundry assistant receives three deliveries of clean bath towels: 240 towels, 185 towels, and 75 towels.
Calculate the total number of towels received and show a reverse subtraction check.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Add the three delivery quantities in aligned columns
240 + 185 = 425 towels.
425 + 75 = 500 towels.
Criteria: M1 / A1: Accurate total (500 towels)
Step 2 Show a reverse operation check
500 − 75 = 425; 425 − 185 = 240.
Criteria: B1: Valid reverse check
Final Accredited Answer: 500 towels (Check: 500 − 75 − 185 = 240)
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Spot number bonds to 100: 185 + 75 = 260, and 240 + 260 = 500.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1/2)

Vocational Context: Healthcare Warehouse Stock Control

At the start of the month, a hospital store holds 3,050 boxes of disposable aprons.
• Week 1 issue: 685 boxes | Week 2 issue: 740 boxes | Week 3 issue: 825 boxes
• The procurement system automatically orders a restock of 1,500 boxes if remaining stock falls below 850 boxes.
Determine whether the automatic restock is triggered at the end of Week 3, and calculate the final stock after any restock.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Sum the boxes issued across Weeks 1, 2, and 3
685 + 740 + 825 = 2,250 boxes issued.
Criteria: M1: Total stock issued (2,250)
Step 2 Subtract issued stock from starting stock (3,050)
3,050 − 2,250 = 800 boxes remaining.
Criteria: M1: Remaining stock (800)
Step 3 Compare 800 with the 850 threshold and add 1,500 restock
Since 800 < 850, YES, the restock is triggered.
Final stock = 800 + 1,500 = 2,300 boxes.
Criteria: M1 / A1: Yes decision and 2,300 final stock
Final Accredited Answer: Yes, restock triggered (800 < 850) — Final stock = 2,300 boxes
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Notice how 685 + 825 = 1,510, plus 740 = 2,250.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Misaligning Digits in Column Addition
Writing 75 underneath the hundreds and tens columns of 240 instead of the tens and units columns.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always align the rightmost units digits vertically.
Examiner Pitfall: Omitting the Zero Placeholder in Words-to-Figures Questions
Writing "three thousand and fifty" as 350 instead of 3,050.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Check that a thousand number always has 4 digits (3,050).

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Write the number four thousand, and sixty-nine in figures, and state the value of the digit 6.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Thousands = 4, Hundreds = 0, Tens = 6, Units = 9 → 4,069.
2. Digit 6 is in the tens column → 60 (sixty).
Final Correct Answer: 4,069; value of 6 is 60
Examiner Tip: Include the 0 in the hundreds column so the 4 stays in the thousands place.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
A catering supplier packs soup tins in trays of 25 tins. A care home orders 14 full trays, plus 18 loose tins. How many soup tins are delivered in total?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Tins in trays = 14 × 25 = 350 tins.
2. Add loose tins = 350 + 18 = 368 tins.
Final Correct Answer: 368 tins
Examiner Tip: 4 trays of 25 = 100, so 12 trays = 300, and 14 trays = 350.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
Four regional clinics report their monthly patient registrations: Clinic A: 1,408, Clinic B: 1,084, Clinic C: 1,480, Clinic D: 1,048.
(a) Arrange the four numbers in ascending order (smallest to largest).
(b) Calculate the difference between the highest and lowest clinic registrations.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Ascending order: 1,048, 1,084, 1,408, 1,480.
2. Difference = 1,480 − 1,048 = 432 patients.
Final Correct Answer: (a) 1,048, 1,084, 1,408, 1,480 | (b) 432
Examiner Tip: Compare the hundreds column first (0 vs 4), then compare the tens column.
