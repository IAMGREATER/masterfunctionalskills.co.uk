Section A: Core Skills (The Basics) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Level 1 Compound Sentences with Conjunctions) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Join the two simple sentences below into a single compound sentence using a suitable coordinating conjunction (e.g. and, but, so, yet):
Sentence 1: The clinic refurbishment was delayed by six weeks.
Sentence 2: All scheduled patient consultations went ahead on time.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Select appropriate contrasting conjunction: 'but' OR 'yet' [M1]
2. Punctuate accurately with comma before conjunction: 'The clinic refurbishment was delayed by six weeks, but all scheduled patient consultations went ahead on time.' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: The clinic refurbishment was delayed by six weeks, but all scheduled patient consultations went ahead on time.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Using 'and' or 'so' which creates illogical meaning, or using a comma splice without a conjunction.
🔄 Quick Check: Both clauses are independent and express contrasting outcomes connected by 'but'.
Question 2 (Level 2 Adding Detail with 'Although') Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Join the two ideas below into one sentence starting with the word 'Although':
Idea 1: The ward team worked under intense winter pressures.
Idea 2: They maintained a 98% patient satisfaction rating throughout January.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Start with 'Although' and place a comma after the opening phrase [M1]
2. Accurate sentence: 'Although the ward team worked under intense winter pressures, they maintained a 98% patient satisfaction rating throughout January.' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Although the ward team worked under intense winter pressures, they maintained a 98% patient satisfaction rating throughout January.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Forgetting the comma after the opening phrase starting with 'Although'.
🔄 Quick Check: 'Although... pressures' is the opening phrase; 'they maintained... January' completes the sentence.
Section B: Real-Life & Workplace Practice Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Level 1 Subject-Verb Agreement Correction) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Identify and correct the three subject-verb agreement errors in the clinical handover note below:
'The team of nurses are meeting at 07:00. Either Sister Evans or Charge Nurse Davis were leading the handover. The supply of surgical gloves have been replenished.'
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. 'The team of nurses' (singular collective noun 'team') → change 'are' to 'is' [A1]
2. 'Either Sister Evans or Charge Nurse Davis' (singular subjects connected by 'or') → change 'were' to 'was' [A1]
3. 'The supply of surgical gloves' (singular subject 'supply') → change 'have' to 'has' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 1. are → is; 2. were → was; 3. have → has
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Matching the verb with the nearest plural noun ('nurses', 'gloves') instead of the true singular subject ('team', 'supply').
🔄 Quick Check: The team IS... Either one WAS... The supply HAS.
Question 4 (Level 2 Comma Splice Elimination) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
The sentence below contains a comma splice (two complete sentences incorrectly joined only with a comma):
'The patient showed signs of allergic reaction, the nurse administered antihistamine immediately.'

Show two different ways to correct this comma splice grammatically (e.g. using a semicolon, a conjunction, or two separate sentences).
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Method 1 (Conjunction): 'The patient showed signs of allergic reaction, so the nurse administered antihistamine immediately.' [A1]
2. Method 2 (Semicolon): 'The patient showed signs of allergic reaction; the nurse administered antihistamine immediately.' [A1]
3. Alternative Method (Full stop): 'The patient showed signs of allergic reaction. The nurse administered antihistamine immediately.' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Method 1: Add conjunction 'so'; Method 2: Use semicolon ';' or separate with a full stop.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Leaving the comma and just capitalizing the second clause.
🔄 Quick Check: Both clauses are independent clauses requiring either a coordinator, semicolon, or full stop.
Section C: Exam-Style Tasks Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Level 2 Workplace Incident Report Drafting) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A facilities apprentice reports a minor spill in a public hospital corridor.
Draft a concise, 4-sentence factual incident log for the health & safety register.
You must include:
• 1 simple sentence (stating exact time & location)
• 1 compound sentence (describing what spilled and who noticed it)
• 1 complex sentence starting with a subordinate conjunction (describing the hazard and immediate safety action taken)
• 1 sentence describing final resolution.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Simple sentence with clear time and location accurately punctuated [A1]
2. Compound sentence with coordinator ('and', 'but', 'so') joining two independent clauses [A1]
3. Complex sentence with dependent clause starting with subordinating conjunction ('Because', 'After', 'When') with comma [A1]
4. Concluding resolution sentence demonstrating accurate SPaG throughout [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Model: 'At 10:15 am, a liquid spillage occurred outside Outpatients Clinic 3. A visitor dropped a bottle of antiseptic solution, and the domestic assistant cordoned off the area immediately. Because wet linoleum presents a severe slip hazard, yellow warning cones were positioned around the perimeter. The area was cleaned, dried, and reopened safely by 10:35 am.'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Writing fragments or informal notes instead of grammatically complete sentences.
🔄 Quick Check: Sentence 1 is simple; Sentence 2 is compound; Sentence 3 is complex; Sentence 4 is complete.
Question 6 (Level 2 Formal Email Register Justification) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
An apprentice writes this email to the Clinical Director:
'Hey Dr Patel, just letting u know the printer in ward 2 is totally busted again lol. We need a new one asap coz it’s a proper joke. Cheers, Dave.'

Critique this email for a professional workplace environment.
Identify three specific register errors, explain why they are inappropriate, and rewrite the email in a polished, formal professional register.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify errors: Inappropriate informal greeting ('Hey Dr Patel'), text slang/abbreviations ('u', 'coz', 'asap', 'lol'), informal colloquialisms ('totally busted', 'proper joke', 'Cheers') [A1]
2. Explain: Lacks professional respect, undermines urgency, and fails NHS written communication standards [M1]
3. Provide polished model rewrite with formal salutation, concise explanation of the issue, business impact, and polite sign-off [A1]
4. Flawless SPaG throughout model response [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Critique: Unprofessional slang ('u', 'coz', 'lol'), casual greeting ('Hey'), colloquial tone ('proper joke'). Rewrite: 'Dear Dr Patel, I am writing to report that the printer in Ward 2 has malfunctioned again. This is causing significant administrative delays with patient handover sheets. Could you please authorize an urgent engineer callout or replacement? Kind regards, David Miller.'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Rewriting in semi-formal tone while still using contractions or vague requests.
🔄 Quick Check: Model uses formal salutation ('Dear Dr Patel'), full sentences, professional tone, and courteous sign-off.
