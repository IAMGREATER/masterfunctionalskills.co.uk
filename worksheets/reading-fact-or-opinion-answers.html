Fact or Opinion Pearson Edexcel: L1.13 • L2.11
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (The Basics) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Level 1 Fact Identification) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Read the following statements from a hospital newsletter:
1. 'The new outpatient wing opened on 14th September with 24 consultation rooms.'
2. 'The interior design of the waiting lounge is the most soothing in the entire county.'

Identify which statement is a fact and which is an opinion. Give one reason for each.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Statement 1 is a FACT: Contains verifiable date (14th Sept) and room count (24) [A1]
2. Statement 2 is an OPINION: 'Most soothing' is subjective value judgment that cannot be proven [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Statement 1 is a Fact; Statement 2 is an Opinion
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Confusing opinion with fact when an opinion sounds authoritative or confident.
🔄 Quick Check: Can Statement 1 be checked against official records? Yes. Can 'most soothing' be proven? No.
Question 2 (Level 2 Fact vs Opinion in a Workplace Email) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
An estates officer emails staff:
'Our energy consumption dropped by 18% following the installation of smart sensors. Everyone agrees that the new system is far superior to our previous heating setup.'

Extract one fact and one opinion from the email excerpt.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Fact: 'energy consumption dropped by 18%' (measurable statistic) [A1]
2. Opinion: 'Everyone agrees that the new system is far superior' (unsubstantiated generalization / subjective) [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Fact: 18% energy drop; Opinion: New system is far superior
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Quoting the whole sentence instead of identifying the exact factual vs opinion phrase.
🔄 Quick Check: 18% is a measurable data point (fact); 'far superior' is an evaluative judgment (opinion).
Section B: Real-Life & Workplace Practice Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Level 1 Healthcare Campaign Text Analysis) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Read this paragraph from a staff wellness campaign:
'Over 450 nurses attended our mindfulness workshop last month. Mental fatigue is an intolerable burden that destroys workplace productivity. The workshop ran for 45 minutes and offered practical breathing techniques.'

Identify two facts and one opinion from the text.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Fact 1: 'Over 450 nurses attended our mindfulness workshop last month' [A1]
2. Fact 2: 'The workshop ran for 45 minutes and offered practical breathing techniques' [A1]
3. Opinion: 'Mental fatigue is an intolerable burden that destroys workplace productivity' (emotive adjectives 'intolerable') [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Facts: 450 nurses attended; ran for 45 minutes. Opinion: Intolerable burden that destroys productivity.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Assuming 'practical techniques' makes the last sentence an opinion; the primary clause is factual.
🔄 Quick Check: 450 nurses and 45 minutes are verifiable records.
Question 4 (Level 2 Identifying Opinion Disguised as Fact) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A union newsletter writes:
'It is an undeniable truth that management's parking charge increase is an unfair penalty on dedicated staff who arrive at 6:30 am.'

Explain why the phrase 'It is an undeniable truth that...' does not make the statement a factual claim.
Identify the subjective language that confirms it is an opinion.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Explain: The writer uses rhetorical assertion ('undeniable truth') to present a subjective viewpoint as indisputable [P1]
2. Identify subjective word: 'unfair penalty' (evaluative opinion on fairness) [A1]
3. Identify subjective word: 'dedicated staff' (value judgment) [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Assertion does not equal evidence; 'unfair penalty' and 'dedicated' are subjective value judgments.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Believing that statements beginning with 'The fact is...' or 'It is true that...' are automatically factual.
🔄 Quick Check: 'Unfair' cannot be measured scientifically or proven in court without policy definition.
Section C: Exam-Style Tasks Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Level 2 Paired Text Analysis - Fact vs Opinion) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Text A (Trust Official Policy):
'The hospital shuttle bus operates every 15 minutes between 06:00 and 22:00, carrying an average of 1,200 passengers daily at a standard return fare of £1.50.'

Text B (User Feedback Forum):
'The shuttle service is utterly unreliable and always dangerously packed during morning handovers. The £1.50 ticket price is extortionate for junior apprentices.'

Compare Text A and Text B. Identify two facts in Text A and two opinions in Text B.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Text A Fact 1: 'operates every 15 minutes between 06:00 and 22:00' [A1]
2. Text A Fact 2: 'carrying average 1,200 passengers daily' OR 'fare of £1.50' [A1]
3. Text B Opinion 1: 'utterly unreliable and always dangerously packed' (exaggeration/hyperbole) [A1]
4. Text B Opinion 2: 'ticket price is extortionate' (subjective judgment) [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Text A Facts: Frequency (15 mins/06:00-22:00) & Passenger count (1,200). Text B Opinions: 'utterly unreliable' & 'extortionate'.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Mixing up the sources or classifying £1.50 in Text B as an opinion rather than 'extortionate'.
🔄 Quick Check: Text A provides verifiable operational parameters; Text B provides emotional user sentiment.
Question 6 (Level 2 Author Bias Evaluation & Justification) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A journalist writes: 'The council's disgraceful waste disposal policy has completely ruined local tourism, proving that councillors care nothing for residents.'

A reader complains: 'This article is biased and presents personal opinions as proven facts.'

Is the reader's complaint justified?
Analyse the language used by the journalist, cite at least two pieces of evidence from the text, and give a clear conclusion.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. State clear conclusion: Yes, the reader's complaint is fully justified [A1]
2. Evidence 1: Use of biased, emotive adjective 'disgraceful' to describe the policy without explaining its details [M1]
3. Evidence 2: Extreme hyperbole 'completely ruined local tourism' without citing any visitor statistics or revenue data [M1]
4. Evidence 3: Sweeping, unproven assumption about intentions ('councillors care nothing for residents') [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Justified (Uses emotive language 'disgraceful', unsubstantiated hyperbole 'completely ruined', and unsupported assumption of motives)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Writing 'Yes' without directly quoting and analysing the specific words used by the journalist.
🔄 Quick Check: None of the claims cite statistical evidence or balanced opposing council perspectives.
