Golden Rules for Multiplication & Scaling

Golden Rule 1: The Total Decimal Places Rule

When multiplying decimals, count the total number of decimal places across BOTH factors. Strip the decimal points, multiply as pure whole numbers, then count that exact total number of decimal places back in from the right of your final product.

Golden Rule / Method: 2.4 (1 d.p.) × 0.15 (2 d.p.) → 24 × 15 = 360 → Count 3 places back = 0.360 = 0.36 ✓
Golden Rule 2: Grid Method Eliminates Carrying Confusion

If long column multiplication confuses you with small carried digits, use the partition grid (box) method. Decomposing numbers into tens and units transforms multi-digit multiplication into 4 quick mental times tables plus clean column addition.

Golden Rule / Method: (30 + 4) × (20 + 7) → 600 + 210 + 80 + 28 = 918 ✓
Golden Rule 3: The Tens Row Placeholder Zero Rule

In two-line vertical multiplication, before you multiply by the tens digit, you MUST immediately write a 0 in the units column on Row 2. Forgetting this zero makes your second line 10 times too small and loses method and accuracy marks.

Golden Rule / Method: Row 2 of Column Multiplication MUST start with a green placeholder: [ 0 ]

Core Skill 3: Multiplying Decimals Without a Calculator

Strip the decimal points, multiply whole numbers, and count back total decimal places.

THE 3-STAGE DECIMAL MULTIPLICATION PROTOCOL
Worked Example: 3.6 × 0.25
STAGE 1: Count Total Decimal Digits
3.6 (1 d.p.) × 0.25 (2 d.p.)
1 + 2 = 3 Total Decimal Places

Ignore points temporarily and treat as whole integers: 36 × 25.

STAGE 2: Multiply Pure Integers
36 × 25
• 36 × 5 = 180
• 36 × 20 = 720
• 180 + 720 = 900

Whole number integer product is 900.

STAGE 3: Count 3 Places In From Right
9 0 0 → 0 . 9 0 0
= 0.9 ✓

Trailing zeros after a decimal can be dropped: 0.900 = 0.9.

Accredited Full-Mark Answers [A1]
  • 0.9 (Simplified standard format ✓)
  • 0.900 (Accepted by Pearson Edexcel mark scheme ✓)
  • 0.90 (Accepted in currency or measurement contexts ✓)
Common Zero-Mark Mistakes [0 / 1]
  • 9.0 × (Learner only counted 2 decimal places instead of 3)
  • 0.09 × (Learner counted 4 decimal places)
  • 900 × (Learner forgot to restore decimal points entirely)
Subi's Assessor Golden Rule: Never Multiply with Decimals in Place

"Never try to do column multiplication while keeping decimals in the rows! Trying to calculate with floating points on paper leads to confusion and misaligned digits. Strip them completely, calculate whole numbers, and hop the point back at the finish line."

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Trap 1: The Missing Tens Placeholder Zero
In two-line vertical multiplication, learners frequently forget that the second line multiplies by TENS, not units. Forgetting the placeholder zero makes the second line 10 times too small and costs 2 out of 3 marks!
WRONG METHOD (0 Marks):
Row 1: 138 × 4 = 552
Row 2: 138 × 2 = 276 (No zero!)
Total: 552 + 276 = 828 ×
CORRECT METHOD (Full Marks):
Row 1: 138 × 4 = 552
Row 2: 138 × 20 = 2,760
Total: 552 + 2,760 = 3,312 ✓
How to Avoid This Trap: The very moment your pencil moves to the second row, write 0 in the units column before computing anything else.
Trap 2: The Decimal Hours Trap (30 mins ≠ 0.3 hrs)
When calculating wage bills with hourly rates, learners often write 8 hours 30 minutes as 8.3 hours. Since there are 60 minutes in an hour, 30 minutes is 0.5 hours!
WRONG CONVERSION:
7 hours 15 mins = 7.15 hrs ×
8 hours 30 mins = 8.3 hrs ×
5 hours 45 mins = 5.45 hrs ×
CORRECT CONVERSION:
7 hours 15 mins = 7.25 hrs (15/60 = ¼) ✓
8 hours 30 mins = 8.5 hrs (30/60 = ½) ✓
5 hours 45 mins = 5.75 hrs (45/60 = ¾) ✓
How to Avoid This Trap: Divide minutes by 60 before multiplying by the hourly pay rate.
