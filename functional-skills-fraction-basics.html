Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • E3.3 / L1.8 – Read, write, and understand common unit and non-unit fractions (1/2, 1/4, 3/4, 1/3, 2/3, 1/5, 1/10) and equivalent fractions.
  • E3.4 / L1.9 – Find fractions of whole-number quantities and measurements in practical contexts.

Golden Rules for Fraction Basics: Halves, Quarters & Thirds

Golden Rule 1: Denominator (Bottom) = Total Equal Parts • Numerator (Top) = Selected Parts

The bottom number (denominator) tells you how many equal slices the whole is divided into. The top number (numerator) tells you how many of those slices you have.

Golden Rule / Method: Numerator (Top) ÷ Denominator (Bottom)
Golden Rule 2: Divide by the Bottom, Multiply by the Top

To find a fraction of an amount (e.g. 3/4 of £60): first divide £60 by the bottom number 4 (= £15 for 1/4), then multiply by the top number 3 (3 × £15 = £45).

Golden Rule / Method: Fraction of Amount = (Amount ÷ Bottom) × Top

Core Skill 1: Unit Fractions (1/n) vs Non-Unit Fractions (a/b)

Finding 1 part first and scaling up to multiple parts.

  • 1/2 of an amount: Divide by 2 (Halve it).
  • 1/4 of an amount: Divide by 4 (Halve it, then halve it again).
  • 3/4 of an amount: Divide by 4 to find 1/4, then multiply that answer by 3.
  • 2/5 of an amount: Divide by 5 to find 1/5, then multiply that answer by 2.

Core Skill 3: Finding the Remaining Fraction of a Whole (1 − Fraction)

Working out what fraction is left over after portions are used.

A whole unit is always equal to n/n (e.g. 4/4, 5/5, 8/8, 10/10):

  • If 3/8 of a ward budget is spent, the remaining fraction unspent is 8/8 − 3/8 = 5/8.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 2 Marks (Entry Level 3 / Level 1)

Vocational Context: Catering Food Distribution

A hospital kitchen receives a crate of 72 apples. The chef uses 3/4 of the apples for patient lunch trays.
How many apples does the chef use, and how many are left over?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Divide total apples (72) by denominator (4) to find 1/4
72 ÷ 4 = 18 apples.
Criteria: M1: Unit fraction division (18)
Step 2 Multiply by numerator (3) and find remainder
Used = 18 × 3 = 54 apples.
Left over = 72 − 54 = 18 apples.
Criteria: A1: Accurate used (54) and remaining (18)
Final Accredited Answer: 54 apples used, 18 apples left over
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Notice that if 3/4 are used, 1/4 (18 apples) is left over!
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1/2)

Vocational Context: Clinical Department Consumables Budget

A clinic manager has a monthly consumables budget of £960.
• She spends 1/3 of the budget on sterile dressings.
• She spends 3/8 of the original £960 budget on PPE gloves.
• The rest of the money is kept for diagnostic test strips.
Calculate how much money (£) is kept for diagnostic test strips.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate 1/3 of £960 for sterile dressings
£960 ÷ 3 = £320.
Criteria: M1: Dressings cost (£320)
Step 2 Calculate 3/8 of £960 for PPE gloves
(£960 ÷ 8) × 3 = £120 × 3 = £360.
Criteria: M1: Gloves cost (£360)
Step 3 Subtract both expenses from £960
£960 − (£320 + £360) = £960 − £680 = £280.
Criteria: M1 / A1: Accurate remainder (£280)
Final Accredited Answer: £280 kept for diagnostic test strips
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Check by adding all three parts: £320 + £360 + £280 = £960.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Dividing by the Top Number Instead of the Bottom Number
To find 3/4 of 60, dividing 60 by 3 (= 20) instead of dividing 60 by 4 (= 15).
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always memorise: "Divide by the BOTTOM, multiply by the TOP."
Examiner Pitfall: Assuming 1/3 is Larger than 1/2 Because 3 is Bigger than 2
When a whole is cut into more pieces, each piece is smaller! 1/2 (50%) is larger than 1/3 (33.3%), and 1/3 is larger than 1/4 (25%).
How to Avoid This Pitfall: For unit fractions (top number 1), the smaller the bottom number, the bigger the slice.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Calculate 2/5 of 85 metres.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Find 1/5: 85 ÷ 5 = 17 metres.
2. Find 2/5: 17 × 2 = 34 metres.
Final Correct Answer: 34 metres
Examiner Tip: 85 ÷ 5 = 17, then 17 × 2 = 34.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
Out of 40 staff members on a ward, 28 are female. Write the fraction of staff who are female in its simplest form.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Initial fraction = 28 / 40.
2. Divide top and bottom by 4: 28 ÷ 4 = 7; 40 ÷ 4 = 10 → 7/10.
Final Correct Answer: 7/10
Examiner Tip: Divide both numerator and denominator by their highest common factor (4).
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A charity fun run is 24 km long. A paramedic cyclist has completed 5/8 of the route. How many kilometres does she still have left to cycle?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Completed = (24 ÷ 8) × 5 = 3 × 5 = 15 km.
2. Remaining = 24 − 15 = 9 km (or 3/8 of 24 = 9 km).
Final Correct Answer: 9 km
Examiner Tip: Read carefully: the question asks for the distance LEFT to cycle (3/8 of 24 = 9 km).
