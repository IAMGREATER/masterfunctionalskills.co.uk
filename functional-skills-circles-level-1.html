Welcome to the Circles: Area, Circumference & Semi-Circles blueprint. Circle calculations are among the most frequently tested topics in Level 1 maths. Examiners always provide \pi = 3.14 on non-calculator or calculator papers. Remember the two golden rules: halve the diameter to get the radius before calculating area, and for semi-circle perimeters, never forget to add the straight diameter baseline!

Golden Rules for Circles: Radius, Diameter, Circumference & Area

Golden Rule 1: Circumference = π × d | Area = π × r² (Halve Diameter First!)

Circumference (distance around the outside rim) = 3.14 × Diameter. Area (flat space inside) = 3.14 × Radius × Radius. If the question gives you the Diameter, you MUST divide it by 2 to get the Radius before calculating Area!

Golden Rule / Method: Circumference = π × d • Area = π × r × r (where r = d ÷ 2)
Golden Rule 2: Semi-Circle Perimeter Includes the Straight Diameter Edge

The curved arc of a semi-circle is (π × d) ÷ 2. To enclose a semi-circular patio with edging, you MUST add the straight flat diameter across the bottom: Total Perimeter = ((π × d) ÷ 2) + d.

Golden Rule / Method: Semi-Circle Perimeter = (π × d ÷ 2) + Diameter

Core Skill 1: Radius vs Diameter & Full Circle Formulas

Distinguishing radius from diameter and avoiding the squaring trap.

  • Radius (r): Distance from the center point to the outer edge (e.g. 5 m).
  • Diameter (d): Full distance straight across through the center (e.g. 10 m). d = 2r and r = d ÷ 2.
  • Squaring Trap Warning: r² means r × r (e.g. 5 × 5 = 25), NEVER 5 × 2!

Calculating areas and full boundary perimeters of half and quarter circles.

Core Skill 3: Compound Stadium Shapes (Rectangle + Two Semi-Circular Ends)

Combining two semi-circles into one complete circle to save working time.

A running track, conference table, or pill-shaped flowerbed consists of a central rectangle with two semi-circular ends:

  • Two Semi-Circles = 1 Full Circle! Do not halve and then double—simply calculate 1 full circle of diameter equal to the rectangle's width.
  • Total Area: (Rectangle Length × Width) + (π × r²).
  • Total Outer Perimeter: (2 × Straight Length) + (π × d). (Do not include the internal dashed join lines!)
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1)

Vocational Context: Healthcare Facility Therapeutic Garden Landscaping

A hospital wellbeing garden features a circular lawn with a diameter of 12 metres. Use π = 3.14.
(a) Calculate the area of turf needed in m².
(b) Calculate the length of metal edging required to surround the circular perimeter.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Halve diameter to find the radius
Radius = 12 m ÷ 2 = 6 metres.
Criteria: B1: Radius identification (6 m)
Step 2 Calculate Area = 3.14 × r × r
Area = 3.14 × 6 × 6 = 3.14 × 36 = 113.04 m².
Criteria: M1: Accurate area calculation (113.04 m²)
Step 3 Calculate Circumference = 3.14 × Diameter
Circumference = 3.14 × 12 m = 37.68 metres.
Criteria: A1: Accurate circumference (37.68 m)
Final Accredited Answer: (a) Area = 113.04 m² | (b) Edging Length = 37.68 m
Sonya’s Examiner Tip Examiner Method
For Area, multiply radius by itself first (6 × 6 = 36), then by 3.14. For Circumference, multiply 3.14 by the full diameter (12).

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Using Diameter in the Area Formula Without Halving
Computing 3.14 × 12² = 452.16 m² instead of 3.14 × 6² = 113.04 m² makes your answer 4 times too large!
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always write "d = ... , r = ..." on your first line of working.
Examiner Pitfall: Multiplying Radius by 2 Instead of Squaring It
Calculating r² for r = 6 as 6 × 2 = 12 instead of 6 × 6 = 36.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Write the formula out as "Area = 3.14 × r × r" so you physically type the radius twice.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A circular rehabilitation hydrotherapy pool has a radius of 3.5 metres. Using π = 3.14, calculate:
(a) The circumference of the pool.
(b) The surface area of the pool (to 2 decimal places).
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Diameter = 3.5 × 2 = 7.0 m. Circumference = 3.14 × 7.0 = 21.98 m.
2. Area = 3.14 × 3.5 × 3.5 = 3.14 × 12.25 = 38.465 m² → 38.47 m².
Final Correct Answer: (a) 21.98 m | (b) 38.47 m² (or 38.465 m²)
Examiner Tip: Double the radius (3.5 × 2 = 7.0 m) before finding circumference.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
A semi-circular reception desk top has a straight back edge (diameter) of 2.4 metres. Using π = 3.14, calculate the top surface area of the desk in m² (to 2 decimal places).
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Radius = 2.4 m ÷ 2 = 1.2 m.
2. Full circle area = 3.14 × 1.2 × 1.2 = 4.5216 m².
3. Semi-circle area = 4.5216 ÷ 2 = 2.2608 m² → 2.26 m².
Final Correct Answer: 2.26 m²
Examiner Tip: Remember to divide the full circle area by 2 for a semi-circle.
Exam Practice Question 3 4 Marks
An athletics track consists of a central rectangle measuring 80 m long by 40 m wide, with two semi-circular ends of diameter 40 m. Using π = 3.14, calculate:
(a) The total outer perimeter of the track.
(b) The total area enclosed inside the track.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Two semi-circular arcs = 1 full circle circumference: 3.14 × 40 m = 125.6 m.
2. Add the two straight 80 m sides: 125.6 + 80 + 80 = 285.6 m.
3. Rectangle area = 80 × 40 = 3,200 m².
4. Two semi-circles area = 1 full circle (r = 20 m): 3.14 × 20 × 20 = 1,256 m².
5. Total Area = 3,200 + 1,256 = 4,456 m².
Final Correct Answer: (a) Perimeter = 285.6 m | (b) Total Area = 4,456 m²
Examiner Tip: Do NOT include the two 40 m internal join lines in the outer perimeter!
