Part 1: Quick-Check Answer Key
Quick answers for practice questions
Q1 Question 1: Facilitation Tool-Kit Phrases
(a) Breaking an awkward silence in the group → 'We've heard some great points on cost; what do people feel about the impact on staff time?'
(b) Bringing a runaway debate to a close → 'We've had a vigorous debate on both sides; let's take a quick vote so we can agree our recommendation.'
(c) Handling two speakers talking simultaneously → 'Hold on a second, Marcus, let's let Priya finish her thought and then we'll come right back to you.'
Q2 Question 2: Reaching Consensus
(a) All members agree on a compromise combining Option A's schedule with Option B's budget → Consensus (collaborative agreement)
(b) Two members refuse to budge and the meeting ends with no agreed action → Deadlock
(c) The manager says 'I don't care what you think, we are doing Option C' → Imposed Decision
Q3 Question 3: Overcoming Public Speaking Anxiety & Physical Preparation
(a) Feeling your heart pounding and breath shortening two minutes before speaking → Box breathing: inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 4, exhale for 4, hold for 4 to activate parasympathetic system
(b) Experiencing dry mouth and throat constriction right as your name is called → Take a slow sip of room-temperature water; avoid ice-cold or sugary drinks
(c) Hands shaking visibly when holding presentation cue cards → Rest hands lightly on the lectern or table, or hold sturdy thick cardstock rather than floppy paper
(d) Mind going completely blank in the middle of slide two → Pause, take a slow breath, look down at your numbered cue card, and read the next bullet point calmly; audiences rarely notice brief silences
(e) Tendency to speak at 200 words per minute due to adrenaline → Consciously pause at the end of every sentence and take a physical breath before speaking the next
(f) Voice sounding faint or trembling in a large room → Plant feet shoulder-width apart, roll shoulders back, and project voice from the belly rather than the throat
(g) Fixating on one unsmiling frowning face in the front row → Shift eye contact to friendly, nodding faces in the audience or look between foreheads across the room
(h) Fear of making a minor slip of the tongue → Simply smile, say 'Excuse me', restate the word correctly, and keep going; perfection is not required
Q4 Question 4: Self-Evaluation & Post-Performance Reflection
(a) 'My slides and statistics were very persuasive, but I spoke too quickly during the conclusion.' → Strength: Visuals and statistical evidence | Target: Vocal pacing and slowing down at the conclusion
(b) 'I answered all audience questions with confidence, but I kept my arms crossed during the opening.' → Strength: Knowledgeable Q&A delivery | Target: Open, welcoming non-verbal body language
(c) 'I maintained excellent eye contact across the whole room, but I forgot to mention the £15,000 budget figure.' → Strength: Audience engagement and gaze | Target: Content completeness and key data recall
(d) 'I explained the clinical procedure clearly without jargon, but my cue cards were out of order.' → Strength: Plain English communication | Target: Organization and pre-speech physical card numbering
(e) 'I managed my time perfectly within 3.5 minutes, but I didn't invite questions at the end.' → Strength: Strict timekeeping | Target: Audience participation and formal Q&A facilitation
(f) 'I stayed completely calm when the projector flickered, but my voice was a bit quiet at the back.' → Strength: Composure under technical failure | Target: Vocal projection and volume modulation
(g) 'I engaged the audience with a powerful opening story, but I read slide bullets verbatim.' → Strength: Compelling narrative hook | Target: Speaking naturally rather than reading slide text
(h) 'I facilitated the group debate by bringing in quiet members, but I didn't take notes of the decisions.' → Strength: Inclusive leadership | Target: Capturing written actions and summarizing agreements
Q5 Question 5: Exam Day Success Checklist for Functional Skills SLC
(a) Arriving 15 minutes early with photo ID, numbered cue cards, and water bottle → Essential (E)
(b) Memorizing a 500-word essay word-for-word the night before and trying to recite it → Harmful (H - causes severe panic if one word is forgotten; sounds robotic)
(c) Bringing a clean, professional visual aid (handout or digital slides) to support Level 1 points → Essential (E)
(d) Remaining completely silent during the group discussion so you don't make any grammar mistakes → Harmful (H - guarantees an automatic fail; must participate actively to be assessed)
(e) Listening attentively to peers and jotting down two questions to ask during their Q&A → Essential (E)
(f) Arguing aggressively with another candidate until they admit defeat → Harmful (H - violates professional collaborative discussion criteria)
(g) Wearing comfortable, smart-casual professional workplace attire → Essential (E)
(h) Using phone texting slang and street vernacular during an assessed formal presentation → Harmful (H - fails register, vocabulary, and formality assessment criteria)
Part 2: Real-Life Problem Solutions
Step-by-step methods and mark breakdown
Question 1 Worked Solution Multidisciplinary Consensus Simulation
[3 Marks Available]
Your group has 2 minutes remaining in an SLC formal discussion on: 'Should clinical staff rotas move to a flexible 4-day compressed week?'
Two members strongly favor the change, while one member has raised serious concerns about evening child-care arrangements.

As facilitator, write a 3-sentence spoken contribution that acknowledges both sides, proposes a practical trial compromise, and concludes the discussion on time.
Sentence 1: Summarize the two perspectives fairly (productivity/rest benefits vs childcare concerns)
[S1]
Sentence 2: Propose a constructive compromise (e.g. a voluntary 3-month pilot with flexible opting-in)
[S1]
Sentence 3: Secure group consensus and formally conclude the discussion
[A1]
Correct Final Answer: 'We have heard compelling arguments today regarding the rest benefits of a 4-day week, as well as crucial practical concerns regarding evening childcare commitments. As a workable compromise, I propose we recommend a voluntary 3-month pilot where interested staff can trial the compressed rota while others remain on standard shifts. Does everyone agree that this gives us the evidence we need without disadvantaging anyone?'
Quick Check Method: Acknowledges both sides, offers compromise, seeks agreement, closes on time.
Common Mistake to Avoid: Ignoring the childcare concern and forcing a one-sided vote.
