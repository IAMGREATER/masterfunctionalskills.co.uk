1. Main Points and Ideas
Reading Skills
What is the Big Picture?
The main point is the most important message the author wants you to understand. Read the title, opening sentence, and closing sentence to identify the central message.
★ Golden Rule: The main point is the overarching message, NOT a small illustrative detail!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't confuse an example with the main point.

2. Text Types: Inform, Persuade, Instruct
Reading Skills
Identify the Goal
Inform: Gives facts and data (news, timetables). Persuade: Wants you to buy or believe something (adverts, flyers). Instruct: Tells you how to do something step-by-step (recipes, user guides).
★ Golden Rule: Look for imperative verbs (Chop, Turn, Press) = INSTRUCT!
⚠️ Watch Out: Adverts inform you about prices, but their PRIMARY goal is to PERSUADE you to buy.

3. Headings & Subheadings
Reading Skills
Signposts for Quick Navigation
Main Heading: Tells you what the whole document is about. Subheadings: Break the text into topics so you can quickly scan and find specific details without reading every single word.
★ Golden Rule: Read the subheadings first to find where your answer is hiding!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't read the whole article line-by-line if a subheading tells you exactly where the topic is.

4. Bullet Points & Lists
Reading Skills
Clear, Distinct Items
Bullet points: Separate key items in a list so they stand out clearly. Numbered lists: Indicate a strict order of steps that must be done in sequence (1st, 2nd, 3rd).
★ Golden Rule: Use numbered lists when steps MUST be followed in exact sequence!
⚠️ Watch Out: Keep each bullet point short and grammatically consistent.

5. Images, Diagrams & Captions
Reading Skills
Visual Evidence & Explanations
Images and diagrams reinforce the written text. Captions are the short descriptive sentences directly below an image explaining what it shows.
★ Golden Rule: Always read the small print in the caption — it often contains exam answers!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't ignore the pictures — exam questions often ask how the graphic supports the article.

6. Fact vs Opinion (Core Test)
Reading Skills
True / False vs Personal Belief
Fact: Can be proven true with dates, measurements, or official records ('The shop opens at 8 am'). Opinion: What someone thinks or feels ('The shop is wonderful').
★ Golden Rule: If it uses words like 'best', 'delicious', or 'terrible', it is an OPINION!
⚠️ Watch Out: Numbers and dates are almost always facts.
DfE L1.R6 • Level 1 Turn over for Practice Challenge ➔ A6 Pocket Card
7. Skimming for Overall Gist
Reading Skills
Rapid 30-Second Glance
Skimming is fast reading to get the general idea: 1. Read the title and subheadings. 2. Glance at the first and last sentence of each paragraph. 3. Look at any pictures. Do NOT read every word.
★ Golden Rule: Skimming gives you the big picture without getting bogged down!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't read word-by-word when skimming — keep your eyes moving quickly down the page.

8. Scanning for Facts & Numbers
Reading Skills
Search like a Metal Detector
Scanning is searching for specific targets: Look for capital letters (names, places), numbers (dates, times, prices), or symbols (£, %, @). Ignore everything else until you spot your target.
★ Golden Rule: Lock your target in your mind before your eyes touch the page!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't start reading the whole text from the beginning when answering 'What time...' questions.

9. Simple Inference (Reading Clues)
Reading Skills
Detective Reading
Inference means working out meaning from hints in the text: If someone is wearing a heavy winter coat, gloves, and shivering, you infer that the weather is freezing.
★ Golden Rule: Inference is finding the hidden clue in the writer's description!
⚠️ Watch Out: Make sure your answer is directly supported by words in the text.

10. Vocabulary: Using Context Clues
Reading Skills
Guess the Word from the Sentence
If you don't know a word: 1. Read the words before and after it. 2. Look for examples or explanations in the sentence. 3. Try replacing it with a word you do know that makes sense.
★ Golden Rule: Read the whole sentence before deciding an unknown word is impossible!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't panic when you see long words — look for root words you already recognise.

11. Alphabetical Order & Directories
Reading Skills
Dictionaries & Index Pages
A to Z alphabetical order: Look at the 1st letter. If 1st letters are the same, look at the 2nd letter (Apple, Apricot). If 2nd letters match, look at the 3rd letter.
★ Golden Rule: Look at the 3rd and 4th letter if the first two letters are identical!
⚠️ Watch Out: Surnames in directories are listed Last Name first: 'Smith, John'.

12. Instructions & Step-by-Step
Reading Skills
Chronological Sequence & Imperatives
Instructions must be followed in order. Look for sequence words: First, Next, Then, After that, Finally. Action verbs (bossy verbs) start each instruction ('Turn off power').
★ Golden Rule: Instructions always use imperative 'bossy' verbs at the start of sentences!
⚠️ Watch Out: Never skip a step when answering questions about instructional texts.

13. Formal vs Informal Language
Reading Skills
Professional vs Casual Speech
Formal: Used with managers, council, doctors ('I am writing to enquire', no slang, full words). Informal: Used with friends, family ('Hi mate', contractions, emojis, casual phrases).
★ Golden Rule: Avoid contractions (don't, can't) when writing formal letters or reports!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't use informal slang like 'gonna' or 'cool' in workplace emails.

14. Comparing Two Simple Texts
Reading Skills
What is the Same? What is Different?
To compare two texts at Level 1: 1. State one piece of information that BOTH texts agree on. 2. State one difference between the two texts (e.g. price, opening time, or opinion).
★ Golden Rule: Always answer both parts: one similarity AND one difference!
⚠️ Watch Out: Make sure you name both Text A and Text B in your answer.

15. Target Audience (Who is it for?)
Reading Skills
Spotting Who the Text is Written For
To find the audience, look at who benefits from the information: Parents (school notices), Motorists (parking leaflet), Dog owners, Job seekers, Patients.
★ Golden Rule: Be specific when naming the audience (e.g. 'Gym members', NOT just 'adults')!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't just say 'everyone' — almost every text has a specific intended reader.

16. Informal Email to a Friend
Writing Skills
Friendly Greeting & Clear Points
Format: 1. Subject line. 2. Friendly greeting (Hi Sam / Dear Chloe). 3. Polite friendly opener ('Hope you are well'). 4. Clear details in 2 paragraphs. 5. Warm sign-off (Best wishes / See you soon).
★ Golden Rule: Always include a relevant Subject Line, even for friendly emails!
⚠️ Watch Out: Even informal emails must have capital letters and full stops in exams.

17. Formal Email (Enquiry/Absence)
Writing Skills
Polite Workplace Message
Format: 1. Clear subject line ('Absence Notification - 12 Oct'). 2. Polite greeting (Dear Mr Davies). 3. Factual reason for email. 4. Request for confirmation or action. 5. Formal sign-off (Kind regards).
★ Golden Rule: Keep workplace emails concise, polite, and professional!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't write text-speak (e.g. 'u', 'thx', 'c u') in any exam email.

18. Letter of Complaint (L1)
Writing Skills
Addresses • Clear Issue • Expected Remedy
Layout: Addresses and date at top. Opening: 'I am writing to complain about...'. Body: Factual details (date, receipt number, defect). Closing: What you want done ('I expect a full refund or replacement').
★ Golden Rule: Be polite and factual — never use rude or abusive language when complaining!
⚠️ Watch Out: Always state what resolution you expect (refund, apology, repair).

20. Writing an Event Flyer / Leaflet
Writing Skills
Who • What • Where • When • Cost
A flyer must include the 5 W's: What the event is. When (date & time). Where (venue). Cost (ticket price / free). Who to contact (email / telephone / website).
★ Golden Rule: Make dates, times, and venue stand out with bullet points!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't forget contact details — visitors need to know how to book or ask questions.

21. Writing a Review (L1)
Writing Skills
What You Liked & What Could Improve
Review structure: 1. Star rating (e.g. 4/5). 2. What you bought or experienced. 3. Positive features. 4. One thing that could be improved. 5. Overall recommendation.
★ Golden Rule: Give specific reasons for your opinion rather than just saying 'it was good'!
⚠️ Watch Out: Mention both pros and at least one small con to show balanced judgment.

22. Incident & Accident Report
Writing Skills
Strictly Factual & Clear
Workplace report format: 1. Date, time, and location of incident. 2. Persons involved and witnesses. 3. Factual description of what occurred (no guessing). 4. First aid given or action taken.
★ Golden Rule: Incident reports must be 100% factual — state only what happened!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't assign blame or write emotional rants in an incident report.

23. Basic Paragraphing (TiP ToP)
Writing Skills
New Idea = New Paragraph
Start a new paragraph whenever there is a change in: Time (later that day), Place (back at the office), Topic (turning to the cost), or Person (a new speaker). Leave a blank line between paragraphs.
★ Golden Rule: Leave a clear blank line between paragraphs in your exam!
⚠️ Watch Out: One giant unspaced block of writing loses marks for organisation.

24. Using Linking Connectives
Writing Skills
Joining Sentences Smoothly
Use connectives to connect ideas: Adding (and, also, as well as). Contrasting (but, however, although). Explaining cause (because, so, therefore). Sequencing (then, after that, finally).
★ Golden Rule: Linking words turn short choppy sentences into flowing prose!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't start every sentence with 'And' or 'Because'.

25. Writing Instructions (Bossy Verbs)
Writing Skills
Start with Imperative Verbs
When writing instructions, start each point with a clear command verb (imperative): 'Chop', 'Wash', 'Turn', 'Press', 'Check'. Keep sentences direct, short, and numbered.
★ Golden Rule: Put the action verb first: 'Press button', not 'You should press button'!
⚠️ Watch Out: Avoid polite padding like 'If you wouldn't mind pressing...' in user instructions.

26. Varying Sentence Lengths

33. Apostrophes for Contractions
SPaG Essentials
Replacing Missing Letters
An apostrophe replaces missing letters when two words are joined into one: do not → don't (o is missing). cannot → can't (no is missing). it is → it's (i is missing).
★ Golden Rule: The apostrophe sits exactly where the missing letter used to be!

34. Apostrophes for Possession
SPaG Essentials
Showing Who Owns What
Singular owner: Add 's after the owner's name (the boy's bike = bike belongs to one boy). Plural owners: If word already ends in s, just add apostrophe after the s (the boys' bikes = bikes belong to many boys).
★ Golden Rule: Ask: 'Who does it belong to?' Then put the apostrophe after the owner!
⚠️ Watch Out: 'CDs' and '1990s' do NOT have apostrophes (they are simple plurals).

35. Plural Spelling Rules
SPaG Essentials
-s, -es, -ies, -ves

38. Past Tense (-ed Rules)
SPaG Essentials
Regular Past Tense Suffixes
Most regular verbs add -ed (walk → walked). If verb ends in 'e', just add 'd' (like → liked). If short vowel + consonant, double consonant (stop → stopped, plan → planned).
★ Golden Rule: Double the consonant if the vowel before it is short (hop → hopped)!
⚠️ Watch Out: 'Hoped' (from hope) has one 'p'; 'hopped' (from hop) has two 'p's.

SPaG Essentials
Saw • Went • Bought • Came

44. Asking Open & Follow-Up Questions
Speaking & Listening
Open Questions (How/Why) vs Closed (Yes/No)
Closed questions (Do you...? Is it...?) get one-word Yes/No answers that stall discussions. Open questions (How would...? Why do you think...?) invite detailed opinions and examples.
★ Golden Rule: Use HOW, WHY, or WHAT to ask open questions and invite full answers!
⚠️ Watch Out: Ask at least two relevant questions to meet the passing criteria.

45. Turn-Taking & Collaborative Talk
Speaking & Listening
Acknowledge • Build • Invite (A-B-I)
Assessors grade how well you take turns: (1) Acknowledge ("Building on Ana's point..."), (2) Add your evidence, and (3) Invite quieter members ("What is your view on this, David?").
★ Golden Rule: Acknowledge the last speaker before introducing your own point!
⚠️ Watch Out: Never interrupt or dominate — invite quieter group members to speak.

46. Active Listening & Body Language
Speaking & Listening
Eye Contact • Posture • Measured Pace
Non-verbal communication carries half your impact: maintain steady eye contact across the room, nod to show active listening, speak at a measured pace, and avoid slang in formal talks.
★ Golden Rule: Match your tone and body language to the formality of the setting!
