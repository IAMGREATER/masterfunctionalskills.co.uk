Welcome to the Capacity, Liquid Volume & Tank Calculations blueprint. Liquid capacity questions appear in every Functional Skills paper — from measuring medicines and drink portions in millilitres (ml) and litres (L), to cubic meter (m³) water storage tanks. Master the golden metric conversions: 1,000 ml = 1 L, and 1 m³ = 1,000 L!

Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • L1.18 / L2.20 – Calculate and convert with metric units of capacity and volume.

Golden Rules for Capacity & Liquid Volume Units

Golden Rule 1: 1 Litre = 1,000 Millilitres = 1,000 cm³

To convert litres to millilitres, multiply by 1,000. To convert millilitres to litres, divide by 1,000. Remember the 3D volume bridge: 1 cm³ holds 1 ml, and 1 m³ holds 1,000 litres.

Golden Rule / Method: 1 Litre = 1,000 ml = 1,000 cm³ | 1 m³ = 1,000 Litres
Golden Rule 2: 1 Centilitre (cl) = 10 Millilitres

Catering and beverage bottles often use centilitres: 75 cl = 750 ml = 0.75 litres.

Golden Rule / Method: 1 cl = 10 ml | 100 cl = 1 Litre

Core Skill 2: Skill 2: Bridging 3D Geometric Volume (cm³ and m³) to Liquid Litres

How to convert tank dimensions in cm or m into litres of water or fuel.

  • From cm³ to Litres (÷ 1,000): A fish tank measuring 60 cm × 30 cm × 40 cm = 72,000 cm³ holds 72,000 ÷ 1,000 = 72 litres.
  • From m³ to Litres (× 1,000): A rainwater harvesting tank with volume 2.4 m³ holds 2.4 × 1,000 = 2,400 litres.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Skill 3: Clinical Fluid Balance Charts & IV Drip Flow Rates

Tracking fluid intake vs output and calculating ml/hour infusion durations.

  1. IV Infusion Rate: Rate (ml/hr) = Total Volume (ml) ÷ Time (hours). For example, 1.5 litres (1,500 ml) over 6 hours = 1,500 ÷ 6 = 250 ml/hr.
  2. Fluid Balance: Net Balance = Total Fluid Intake (ml) − Total Fluid Output (ml).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1/2)

Vocational Context: Clinical Ward Fluid Administration

A care home nurse prepares a 2-litre jug of oral rehydration fluid. She pours 150 ml into each resident's cup.
(a) How many full cups can she pour from the jug?
(b) How many millilitres of fluid remain in the jug?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Convert jug capacity from litres to millilitres
2 litres × 1,000 = 2,000 ml.
Criteria: M1: Litres to ml conversion
Step 2 Divide total volume by single cup volume
Total Volume ÷ Single Cup Capacity
2,000 ml ÷ 150 ml = 13.333... (13 r 50)
13 complete 150 ml cups can be filled, with liquid left over.
Criteria: M1: Division setup and execution
Step 3 State whole cups and remaining volume
Accurate Whole Cups & Exact Remainder
Used: 13 × 150 = 1,950 ml  |  Left: 2,000 − 1,950 = 50 ml
State both values explicitly: 13 full cups and 50 ml remainder.
Criteria: A1: Accurate cups and remainder in ml
Final Accredited Answer: (a) 13 full cups | (b) 50 ml remains in the jug.
Sonya’s Examiner Tip Truncation in Capacity
In physical capacity tasks, you cannot serve a fraction of a cup! You must round down to 13 full cups, and calculate the exact millilitres remaining (50 ml). Never round up to 14 cups because there is not enough liquid!
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2 Problem Solving)

Vocational Context: Horticultural Irrigation Engineering

A commercial nursery has a rectangular water storage tank measuring 2.5 m long, 1.2 m wide, and 0.8 m deep.
• The tank is currently completely empty.
• The manager needs to fill the tank to 80% of its maximum capacity.
• A water hose fills the tank at a rate of 32 litres per minute.
• Use: 1 m³ = 1,000 litres.

Will the tank reach 80% capacity within 1 hour?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate full tank volume in m³ and convert to litres
Cubic Metres to Litres (1 m³ = 1,000 L)
2.5 × 1.2 × 0.8 = 2.4 m³  →  2.4 × 1,000 = 2,400 L
The maximum capacity of the storage tank is 2,400 litres.
Criteria: M1: Tank volume in litres (2,400 L)
Step 2 Calculate 80% target volume
Target Volume (80% Capacity)
0.80 × 2,400 L = 1,920 litres
The nursery needs to pump in 1,920 litres of water.
Criteria: M1: 80% capacity calculation
Step 3 Divide required litres by flow rate (32 L/min)
Filling Time = Target Litres ÷ Flow Rate
1,920 litres ÷ 32 L/min = 60 minutes
Pumping at 32 litres every minute takes exactly 60 minutes.
Criteria: M1: Division by flow rate
Step 4 Compare against 1-hour (60-minute) limit
Accredited Decision & Comparison
60 minutes = exactly 1 hour → YES, it will reach 80%
State the direct 'Yes' conclusion and quote 60 minutes = 1 hour.
Criteria: A1: Accurate time (60 mins) and justified conclusion
Final Accredited Answer: Yes (80% capacity is 1,920 litres, which takes 1,920 ÷ 32 = 60 minutes = exactly 1 hour).
Subi’s Assessor Pro-Tip Metric Capacity Bridges
Memorise the two vital bridges: 1 m³ = 1,000 litres (for large water tanks), and 1,000 cm³ = 1 litre (for cartons and small containers). Never mix them up!

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Dividing 2 by 150 Without Converting to Millilitres
Doing 2 ÷ 150 gives 0.0133 — always convert litres to millilitres first!
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Both numbers must be in millilitres (or both in litres) before dividing.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A bottle of cough linctus contains 300 ml. The recommended adult dose is 15 ml three times daily. How many full days will this bottle last?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Daily dose = 15 ml × 3 = 45 ml per day.
2. Total days = 300 ÷ 45 = 6.666... days.
3. It will last for 6 full days (with 30 ml left over for day 7).
Final Correct Answer: 6 full days
Examiner Tip: Calculate the total daily dose (45 ml) first before dividing the bottle volume.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
A catering team needs 18 litres of orange juice for a conference. Orange juice is sold in cartons of 75 cl. How many cartons must they buy?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Convert both to millilitres (or litres): 18 L = 18,000 ml; 75 cl = 750 ml (0.75 L).
2. Divide: 18,000 ÷ 750 (or 18 ÷ 0.75) = 24 cartons.
Final Correct Answer: 24 cartons
Examiner Tip: 75 cl = 750 ml = 0.75 L.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A hospital patient is prescribed 1.8 litres of intravenous saline to be infused at a constant rate of 240 ml per hour. If the infusion starts at 08:15, at what time will it finish?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Convert 1.8 L to ml: 1.8 × 1,000 = 1,800 ml.
2. Duration = 1,800 ÷ 240 = 7.5 hours = 7 hours 30 minutes.
3. Finish time = 08:15 + 7 hours 30 minutes = 15:45 (3:45 pm).
Final Correct Answer: 15:45 (or 3:45 pm)
Examiner Tip: 0.5 hours = 30 minutes; 08:15 + 7h 30m = 15:45.
