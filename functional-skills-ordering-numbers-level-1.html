Welcome to the Ordering Numbers, Negative Scales & Decimal Padding blueprint. Arranging numbers in ascending (smallest to largest) or descending (largest to smallest) order tests two crucial techniques: plotting negative values on a vertical temperature scale, and padding decimals with trailing zeros to compare equal decimal lengths.

Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • L1.2 – Order and compare integers, decimals, and negative numbers.
  • L1.4 – Use inequality symbols (<, >, =) to express relationships between values.

Golden Rules for Ordering Numbers: Whole, Negative & Decimals

Golden Rule 1: Negative Numbers Work in Reverse

The further to the left on a number line, the smaller the number. Therefore, -18°C is smaller (colder) than -4°C.

Golden Rule / Method: -20 < -15 < -5 < 0 < 5
Golden Rule 2: Line Up Decimal Points and Pad with Trailing Zeros

To compare decimals such as 0.4, 0.375, and 0.09, pad them to equal lengths: 0.400, 0.375, 0.090 before ordering.

Golden Rule / Method: 0.090 < 0.375 < 0.400

Core Skill 1: Number Line Sequencing & Temperature Spans Across Zero

Navigating positive and negative numbers without falling into the magnitude illusion trap.

The Full-Width Continuum Number Line
Negative to Positive Direction
1. Magnitude Reversal in Negatives

The larger the number looks, the smaller it actually is when negative:
−14.5 < −3.2 (because −14.5 is further below zero).

2. Temperature Spans Across Zero

To find the temperature rise from −6°C to +15°C:
6 (up to zero) + 15 (above zero) = 21°C.

Core Skill 2: Padding Decimals with Trailing Zeros (Equalising Decimal Places)

Eliminating length bias when comparing thousandths, hundredths, and tenths.

The Zero-Padding Comparison System
No-Squint Decimal Alignment

Unpadded decimals create optical illusions (e.g. seeing 2.4 as smaller than 2.049 because 2.049 has more digits). Always pad every number to matching decimal places with zeros before ranking:

Original Measurement Padded to 3 Decimal Places Thousandths Reading Accredited Rank (Smallest → Largest)
2.049 mm 2.049 2,049 1st (Smallest)
2.08 mm 2.080 2,080 2nd
2.375 mm 2.375 2,375 3rd
2.4 mm 2.400 2,400 4th (Largest)
Final Ascending Order (Restored to Original Format)
2.049  <  2.08  <  2.375  <  2.4

Core Skill 3: Using Inequality Symbols (<, >) & Direction Terminology

Accredited notation for Pearson Edexcel Section A and Section B questions.

The Inequality Mouth & Sorting Direction System
Examiner Standard
Sorting Directions
Ascending Order: Smallest to Largest
Going UP like stairs: −8, −2, 0, 4, 11
Descending Order: Largest to Smallest
Going DOWN like a slide: 11, 4, 0, −2, −8
The Wide Mouth Rule

The open mouth ALWAYS points toward the GREATER value:

−3 > −9 (−3°C is warmer than −9°C)
0.08 < 0.4 (0.08 is smaller than 0.40)

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1 Non-Calculator)

Vocational Context: Vaccine Storage Compliance

A pharmacy technician records five temperature readings in vaccine freezer units: -4.2°C, 3.1°C, -6.8°C, -1.5°C, 0.8°C.
(a) Arrange these temperatures in ascending order (coldest to warmest).
(b) Calculate the temperature difference between the coldest and warmest unit.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Identify and order negative temperatures from coldest
Negative order (furthest below zero first):
−6.8°C < −4.2°C < −1.5°C
Criteria: M1: Accurate negative ordering
Step 2 Order remaining positive values and write complete ascending list
Positive order: 0.8°C then 3.1°C
−6.8°C, −4.2°C, −1.5°C, 0.8°C, 3.1°C
Criteria: A1: Correct ascending sequence
Step 3 Calculate range from -6.8°C to +3.1°C across zero
Distance from −6.8 to 0 is 6.8°C | Distance from 0 to 3.1 is 3.1°C
Total difference = 6.8 + 3.1 = 9.9°C
Criteria: A1: Correct temperature difference
Final Accredited Answer: (a) -6.8°C, -4.2°C, -1.5°C, 0.8°C, 3.1°C | (b) 9.9°C
Sonya’s Examiner Tip Examiner Method
'Ascending' always means smallest to largest. Always bridge through zero when finding the difference between a negative and a positive temperature.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Assuming Longer Decimals Are Bigger
Learners often think 0.125 is bigger than 0.3 because 125 is bigger than 3. In reality, 0.3 = 0.300, which is larger than 0.125.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always pad with zeros: compare 0.300 with 0.125.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Arrange these warehouse crate weights in descending order: 4.05 kg, 4.5 kg, 4.125 kg, 4.55 kg, 4.009 kg.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Pad to 3 decimal places: 4.050, 4.500, 4.125, 4.550, 4.009.
2. Arrange largest to smallest: 4.55 kg, 4.5 kg, 4.125 kg, 4.05 kg, 4.009 kg.
Final Correct Answer: 4.55 kg, 4.5 kg, 4.125 kg, 4.05 kg, 4.009 kg
Examiner Tip: 'Descending' means largest to smallest. Check the question wording carefully.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
Five business bank accounts show the following end-of-month balances: -£420, £150, -£85, -£1,020, £45.
List the balances in ascending order (lowest to highest).
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Isolate negative balances: -£420, -£85, -£1,020. Largest negative is smallest: -£1,020, -£420, -£85.
2. Add positive balances in order: £45, £150.
Final Correct Answer: -£1,020, -£420, -£85, £45, £150
Examiner Tip: An overdraft of -£1,020 is much lower (more in debt) than -£85.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
Overnight, the temperature in a walk-in blast chiller drops from 4.5°C by 22.8°C. What is the new temperature inside the blast chiller, and is it colder than the target requirement of -18.0°C?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. New temperature = 4.5 - 22.8 = -18.3°C.
2. Compare -18.3°C with -18.0°C: -18.3 < -18.0.
3. Conclusion: Yes, -18.3°C is 0.3°C colder than -18.0°C.
Final Correct Answer: -18.3°C (Yes, it is 0.3°C colder than the -18.0°C target)
Examiner Tip: Remember that -18.3°C is further below zero than -18.0°C, so it is colder.
