Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • E3.14 / L1.2 – Use, order, and compare positive and negative temperatures in practical contexts.
  • L1.2 / L2.1 – Calculate temperature changes and differences across 0°C and use Celsius/Fahrenheit conversion formulas.

Golden Rules for Temperature, Negative Numbers & Thermometer Scales

Golden Rule 1: Use Zero (0°C) as a Stepping Stone for Differences Across Zero

To find the difference between a negative temperature (e.g. −6°C) and a positive temperature (e.g. +15°C), find the distance from −6°C up to 0°C (6 degrees), then from 0°C up to +15°C (15 degrees), and ADD them together: 6 + 15 = 21°C.

Golden Rule / Method: Difference Across 0°C = Distance Below 0°C + Distance Above 0°C
Golden Rule 2: Larger Negative Digits Mean COLDER (Lower) Temperatures

Below zero, the scale counts downwards: −20°C is much colder (lower) than −4°C because it is 20 degrees below freezing!

Golden Rule / Method: −20°C is COLDER than −4°C • −4°C is WARMER than −20°C

Core Skill 1: Bridging Across 0°C for Temperature Rises, Drops & Differences

Visualising a vertical thermometer with 0°C in the middle.

  • Difference Between −8°C and +14°C: 8 degrees to reach 0°C + 14 degrees above 0°C = 22°C difference.
  • Difference Between Two Negatives (−18°C and −5°C): Both are below zero, so find the gap between 18 and 5: 18 − 5 = 13°C difference.
  • Rising from a Negative (−12°C rises by 7°C): Moves closer to zero: −12 + 7 = −5°C.

Core Skill 3: Celsius (°C) ↔ Fahrenheit (°F) Formula Conversions

Substituting positive and negative temperatures into F = 1.8C + 32.

  • Celsius to Fahrenheit: F = (1.8 × C) + 32 (or C × 9 ÷ 5 + 32).
    Example for 25°C: (1.8 × 25) + 32 = 45 + 32 = 77°F.
  • Fahrenheit to Celsius: C = (F − 32) ÷ 1.8.
    Example for 68°F: (68 − 32) ÷ 1.8 = 36 ÷ 1.8 = 20°C.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1)

Vocational Context: Hospital Food Safety Compliance

A catering supervisor checks the digital thermometer in the walk-in freezer, which reads −19°C. The main kitchen preparation room is at 22°C.
(a) Work out the difference in temperature between the walk-in freezer and the kitchen.
(b) A defrost cycle raises the freezer temperature by 5°C. What is the new freezer temperature, and is it still at or below the mandatory −18°C safety limit?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Bridge across 0°C for total temperature difference
From −19°C to 0°C = 19°C; from 0°C to 22°C = 22°C.
Total difference = 19 + 22 = 41°C.
Criteria: M1 / A1: 41°C difference
Step 2 Add 5°C rise to −19°C and compare with −18°C
−19°C + 5°C = −14°C.
No, −14°C is warmer than −18°C, so it breaches the safety limit.
Criteria: A1: −14°C and No conclusion
Final Accredited Answer: (a) 41°C difference | (b) −14°C — No, warmer than the −18°C limit
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
When temperature rises from −19°C by 5°C, it moves UP towards zero to −14°C (not −24°C!).
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2)

Vocational Context: Surgical Instrument Sterilisation

An American surgical autoclave manual states that instruments must be sterilised at a minimum temperature of 250°F.
• The UK hospital autoclave displays temperatures in Celsius (°C) and is currently set to 122°C.
• Use the formula F = 1.8C + 32.
Determine whether the UK setting of 122°C meets the minimum 250°F requirement.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Substitute C = 122 into 1.8 × C
1.8 × 122 = 219.6.
Criteria: M1: Multiplication step (219.6)
Step 2 Add 32 to find temperature in °F
219.6 + 32 = 251.6°F.
Criteria: M1 / A1: Accurate Fahrenheit value (251.6°F)
Step 3 Compare 251.6°F with 250°F minimum
Yes, 251.6°F is above the 250°F minimum requirement (by 1.6°F).
Criteria: A1: Valid Yes conclusion with figures
Final Accredited Answer: Yes — 122°C equals 251.6°F, which exceeds the 250°F minimum
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Follow BIDMAS in F = 1.8C + 32: always multiply 1.8 by C FIRST, then add 32!

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Subtracting 19 from 22 Instead of Bridging Across Zero (22 − 19 = 3°C)
Ignoring the minus sign on −19°C and doing 22 − 19 = 3°C instead of 22 + 19 = 41°C.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: If one temperature is negative and one is positive, ADD their distances from 0°C!
Examiner Pitfall: Thinking −14°C is Colder Than −18°C
In freezer safety questions, −14°C is WARMER than −18°C (closer to 0°C), which means the freezer is too warm!
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Draw a vertical thermometer: 0°C at the top, then −14°C, then −18°C below it.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Overnight, the outside temperature at an ambulance station drops from 4.5°C by 11.2°C. What is the new temperature?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Drop 4.5°C to reach 0°C.
2. Remaining drop below zero = 11.2 − 4.5 = 6.7°C.
3. New temperature = −6.7°C.
Final Correct Answer: −6.7°C
Examiner Tip: 4.5 − 11.2 = −6.7°C.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Five Cryo-Freezer compartments record temperatures of: −16.4°C, −21.0°C, −18.5°C, −15.9°C, −20.2°C.
(a) Arrange these temperatures from coldest to warmest.
(b) Find the range of these temperatures.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Coldest (most negative) to warmest: −21.0°C, −20.2°C, −18.5°C, −16.4°C, −15.9°C.
2. Range = difference between −21.0°C and −15.9°C = 21.0 − 15.9 = 5.1°C.
Final Correct Answer: (a) −21.0°C, −20.2°C, −18.5°C, −16.4°C, −15.9°C | (b) Range = 5.1°C
Examiner Tip: Both extremes are below zero, so the range is 21.0 − 15.9 = 5.1°C.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
Use the formula C = (F − 32) ÷ 1.8 to convert a patient fever reading of 102.2°F into degrees Celsius (°C).
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Brackets first: 102.2 − 32 = 70.2.
2. Divide by 1.8: 70.2 ÷ 1.8 = 39.0°C.
Final Correct Answer: 39°C (or 39.0°C)
Examiner Tip: Subtract 32 FIRST before dividing by 1.8!
