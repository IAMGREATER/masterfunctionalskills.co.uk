Surface Area of Prisms & Cylinders Pearson Edexcel: L2.24 • L2.25
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (Warm-Up) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Level 2 Cube Surface Area) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A sterile instrument storage box is a closed cube with side length 30 cm.
Calculate the total external surface area of the cube in cm².
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Area of 1 square face: 30 cm × 30 cm = 900 cm² [M1]
2. Multiply by 6 identical faces: 6 × 900 cm² = 5,400 cm² [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 5,400 cm²
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Calculating volume (30 × 30 × 30 = 27,000 cm³) instead of surface area.
🔄 Quick Check: 5,400 ÷ 6 = 900 cm²; √900 = 30 cm.
Question 2 (Level 2 Closed Cuboid Packaging Carton) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A pharmaceutical shipping carton is a closed cuboid measuring 50 cm long, 40 cm wide, and 20 cm high.
Calculate the total external surface area of the carton in cm².
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Pairs of faces: 2×(50×40) + 2×(50×20) + 2×(40×20) = 4,000 + 2,000 + 1,600 [M1]
2. Sum all 6 faces: 7,600 cm² [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 7,600 cm²
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Adding only 3 faces (2,000 + 1,000 + 800 = 3,800 cm²) and forgetting to double them for all 6 faces.
🔄 Quick Check: (2,000 + 1,000 + 800) × 2 = 3,800 × 2 = 7,600 cm².
Section B: Everyday & Workplace Maths Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Level 2 Open-Top Steel Tank Coating) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
An industrial water trough is an open-top cuboid (4 vertical sides and 1 flat base; no lid) measuring 2.5 m long, 1.2 m wide, and 0.8 m high.

Calculate the total external surface area of the 5 faces in m².
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Base area (1 face only): 2.5 m × 1.2 m = 3.0 m² [M1]
2. Front & Back walls: 2 × (2.5 × 0.8) = 4.0 m²; Left & Right ends: 2 × (1.2 × 0.8) = 1.92 m² [P1]
3. Sum all 5 faces: 3.0 + 4.0 + 1.92 = 8.92 m² [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 8.92 m²
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Including a top lid (2 × 3.0 = 6.0 m²) to get 11.92 m² when the trough is open-topped.
🔄 Quick Check: Full 6-face cuboid = 11.92 m² minus 3.0 m² open top = 8.92 m².
Question 4 (Level 2 Cylindrical Pillar Painting) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A hospital atrium has 4 concrete support pillars. Each pillar is a cylinder with a diameter of 0.6 metres and a height of 3.5 metres.
Only the curved vertical surface of each pillar is painted (not the top or bottom ends).
Use π = 3.14.

Calculate the total curved surface area to be painted across all 4 pillars in m².
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Curved surface area of 1 cylinder (π × d × h): 3.14 × 0.6 × 3.5 [M1]
2. Evaluate 1 pillar: 6.594 m² [P1]
3. Multiply by 4 pillars: 4 × 6.594 = 26.376 m² (accept 26.38 m²) [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 26.376 m² (or 26.38 m²)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Adding circular top/bottom areas (2πr²) when the pillars touch the floor and ceiling.
🔄 Quick Check: 26.376 ÷ 4 = 6.594; 6.594 ÷ 3.5 ÷ 0.6 = 3.14.
Section C: Exam-Style Problem Solving Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Level 2 Room Walls & Ceiling Plasterboard & Paint Cost) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A decorator is painting the 4 walls and the ceiling (not the floor) of a rectangular clinic room measuring 6.0 m long, 4.5 m wide, and 2.8 m high.
• The room has a door and window with a combined area of 5.8 m² (which are not painted).
• The walls and ceiling require 2 coats of hygienic scrubbable paint.
• Each 5-litre tin of paint covers 40 m² and costs £34.50.

Calculate the total cost of the paint tins required.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Ceiling area = 6.0 × 4.5 = 27.0 m²; 4 walls = 2×(6.0×2.8) + 2×(4.5×2.8) = 33.6 + 25.2 = 58.8 m² [P1]
2. Net paintable area (1 coat): 27.0 + 58.8 - 5.8 (door/window) = 80.0 m² [M1]
3. Area for 2 coats: 80.0 × 2 = 160.0 m² → Tins needed: 160 ÷ 40 = 4 tins [P1]
4. Total cost: 4 × £34.50 = £138.00 [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: £138.00 (4 tins required for 160 m²)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Forgetting to double the net area for 2 coats of paint.
🔄 Quick Check: 4 tins × 40 m² = 160 m² ÷ 2 coats = 80 m² net area.
Question 6 (Level 2 Triangular Prism Roof Tent Canvas Verification) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
An emergency medical triage tent is shaped as a triangular prism (including a sewn-in groundsheet base):
• Length of tent: 8.0 m
• Width of flat groundsheet base: 6.0 m
• Vertical height from ground to ridge: 4.0 m
• Sloping roof sides: two rectangles each measuring 8.0 m long by 5.0 m wide.

The manufacturer has 180 m² of waterproof canvas in stock. If 10% extra canvas must be added to the total surface area for seams and wastage, is 180 m² of canvas enough to make the tent?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Two triangular end faces (2 × 1/2 × base × height): 2 × (0.5 × 6.0 × 4.0) = 24.0 m² [P1]
2. Three rectangular faces (2 sloping roof sides + 1 base): 2×(8.0×5.0) + (8.0×6.0) = 80.0 + 48.0 = 128.0 m² [P1]
3. Total surface area = 24.0 + 128.0 = 152.0 m²; plus 10% wastage: 152.0 × 1.10 = 167.2 m² [M1]
4. Compare and conclude: 167.2 m² ≤ 180 m². Yes, 180 m² of canvas is enough (with 12.8 m² to spare). [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Yes (Requires 167.2 m² including 10% wastage, which is under the 180 m² available)
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Using the 5.0 m sloping edge as the vertical height of the triangle instead of 4.0 m.
🔄 Quick Check: 24 + 80 + 48 = 152 m²; 152 + 15.2 = 167.2 m² < 180 m².
