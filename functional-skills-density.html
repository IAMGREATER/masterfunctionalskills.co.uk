Golden Rules for Density & Compound Units

Golden Rule 1: Density, Mass & Volume Formula Triangle

Mass is at the top of the triangle:
• Mass = Density × Volume
• Density = Mass ÷ Volume
• Volume = Mass ÷ Density

Golden Rule / Method: Mass = Density × Volume | Density = Mass ÷ Volume | Volume = Mass ÷ Density
Golden Rule 2: Ensure Mass and Volume Units Match Density

If density is given in g/cm³, mass MUST be in grams and volume in cm³. If density is kg/m³, convert mass to kg and volume to m³.

Golden Rule / Method: g/cm³ → g and cm³ | kg/m³ → kg and m³

Core Skill 2: Skill 2: Combining 3D Volume Formulas with Density to Find Mass

How to solve two-stage geometry + density questions in Section B.

  1. Stage 1 (Find 3D Volume): Calculate the volume of the cuboid (L × W × H) or cylinder (3.14 × r² × h) making sure all dimensions match the density unit (all in cm or all in m).
  2. Stage 2 (Multiply by Density): Multiply Volume by Density (M = D × V).
  3. Stage 3 (Convert Mass if Required): Divide grams by 1,000 to get kilograms, or divide kilograms by 1,000 to get tonnes.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Skill 3: Pressure, Force & Area Compound Measures (P = F ÷ A)

Applying compound unit reasoning to floor loading and clinical pressure.

  • Pressure Formula: Pressure (N/m² or kg/m²) = Force / Weight ÷ Contact Area.
  • Finding Maximum Force: Force = Pressure × Area.
  • Rates of Flow / Population Density: Follow the exact unit label! E.g. patients per km² = Patients ÷ Area in km²; litres per minute = Litres ÷ Minutes.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2)

Vocational Context: Healthcare Construction Engineering

A concrete footing for an MRI machine foundation is a cuboid measuring 1.5 m long, 0.8 m wide, and 0.5 m deep. The reinforced concrete has a density of 2,400 kg/m³.
Calculate the total mass of the footing in kilograms.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate the volume of the cuboid footing
Volume = length × width × depth = 1.5 × 0.8 × 0.5 = 0.6 m³.
Criteria: M1: Cuboid volume calculation
Step 2 Apply Mass = Density × Volume formula
Mass = 2,400 kg/m³ × 0.6 m³ = 1,440 kg.
Criteria: M1: Density triangle multiplication
Step 3 State final mass with correct units
Total Mass = 1,440 kg (or 1.44 tonnes).
Criteria: A1: Accurate mass with unit
Final Accredited Answer: Total Mass = 1,440 kg
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Notice the units matched: volume was in m³ and density was in kg/m³, so the answer is directly in kg.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2 Problem Solving)

Vocational Context: Industrial Engineering & Lifting Compliance

A hospital engineering team needs to lift a solid steel counterweight block onto a roof plant room.
• The block is a cuboid measuring 50 cm long, 40 cm wide, and 25 cm high.
• Steel has a density of 7.8 g/cm³.
• The portable hoist has a maximum Safe Working Load (SWL) of 400 kg.

Can the portable hoist safely lift the steel block? Show full working.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate the volume of the cuboid in cm³
Volume = 50 cm × 40 cm × 25 cm = 50,000 cm³.
Criteria: M1: Cuboid volume in cm³
Step 2 Calculate mass in grams using Mass = Density × Volume
Mass = 7.8 g/cm³ × 50,000 cm³ = 390,000 grams.
Criteria: M1: Mass calculation in grams
Step 3 Convert mass from grams to kilograms
390,000 g ÷ 1,000 = 390 kg.
Criteria: M1: Conversion to kg
Step 4 Compare against 400 kg Safe Working Load
390 kg < 400 kg (10 kg below maximum limit). Yes, the hoist can safely lift the block.
Criteria: A1: Accurate mass (390 kg) and justified safety confirmation
Final Accredited Answer: Yes, safe to lift (Block mass is 390 kg, which is 10 kg under the 400 kg hoist limit).
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Because density is in g/cm³, keep dimensions in cm first to get grams (390,000 g), then divide by 1,000 at the end to get 390 kg!

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Multiplying Instead of Dividing for Density
Doing Mass × Volume to find density is a common error. Remember that Mass is at the top of the triangle (D = M ÷ V).
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Sketch the triangle: M at the top, D and V at the bottom.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A metal component has a volume of 80 cm³ and a mass of 632 grams. Calculate the density of the metal in g/cm³.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Density = Mass ÷ Volume = 632 ÷ 80.
2. 632 ÷ 80 = 7.9 g/cm³.
Final Correct Answer: 7.9 g/cm³
Examiner Tip: Divide 632 by 80 to obtain 7.9 g/cm³.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
A bottle of surgical spirit has a mass of 640 grams. Surgical spirit has a density of 0.8 g/cm³. Calculate the volume of the surgical spirit in cm³.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Volume = Mass ÷ Density = 640 ÷ 0.8.
2. 640 ÷ 0.8 = 6,400 ÷ 8 = 800 cm³.
Final Correct Answer: 800 cm³
Examiner Tip: Cover V in the M-D-V triangle: Volume = Mass ÷ Density.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
An oak timber beam is a cuboid measuring 2 m long, 0.2 m wide, and 0.15 m thick. Oak has a density of 750 kg/m³. Calculate the mass of 4 identical oak beams in kilograms.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Volume of 1 beam = 2 × 0.2 × 0.15 = 0.06 m³.
2. Mass of 1 beam = 0.06 × 750 = 45 kg.
3. Mass of 4 beams = 4 × 45 kg = 180 kg.
Final Correct Answer: 180 kg
Examiner Tip: Check units: all lengths are in metres and density is in kg/m³.
