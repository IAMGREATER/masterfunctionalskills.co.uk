Adapting Talk to Audience and Situation Pearson Edexcel: Pearson Edexcel Functional Skills English (SLC)
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Foundational Concept Check Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Adapting Spoken Register from Casual Peer Chat to Formal Board Briefing) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Adapt this casual spoken remark to a colleague so that it is appropriate for a formal presentation to the NHS Trust Finance Director:
'The old printers on Ward 4 are proper rubbish and keep jamming up, so we're wasting loads of cash fixing them.'
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Replace slang ('proper rubbish', 'loads of cash') with formal workplace diction ('unreliable', 'significant maintenance expenditure') [A1]
2. Frame as a clear, professional operational recommendation [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'The legacy printers on Ward 4 are experiencing frequent mechanical jams, which is generating significant maintenance expenditure and delaying clinical admin.'
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Keeping colloquialisms like 'loads of' or 'rubbish' when addressing senior management.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Professional vocabulary, measured tone, zero slang.
Question 2 (Core Skill Application — 2 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Rewrite this casual spoken remark so it is appropriate for a formal presentation to your College Board of Governors:
'Loads of students are quitting the evening maths class cos the buses stop running at 8pm and they can't get home.'
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Replace informal slang ('Loads of', 'quitting', 'cos') with formal educational register. [M1]
2. State the transport issue clearly and professionally. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'A significant number of adult learners have withdrawn from the evening Mathematics programme because local public bus services cease operating at 20:00, leaving students without reliable transport home.'
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Throwing around terms like 'SOP', 'KPI', 'DSE', or 'dyspnoea' when speaking to customers or patients creates confusion and fails audience adaptation criteria.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Replacing 'Loads of students are quitting cos...' with 'A significant number of adult learners have withdrawn because...' instantly elevates your spoken register.
Section B: Functional Workplace Application Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Model 1 (Level 1): Explaining a Vehicle Repair Delay to a Customer) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A mechanic tells you: 'Tell Mr Robinson his van won't be ready today because the supplier sent the wrong alternator bracket.'
Deliver a polite, 3-sentence spoken message to Mr Robinson that apologises, explains the delay clearly without blaming people, and confirms tomorrow's collection time.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Courteous greeting & sincere apology: 'Good afternoon Mr Robinson, I am calling from Metro Garage with an update on your transit van, and I want to apologise for a slight delay.' [A1]
2. Clear explanation & new completion time: 'Unfortunately, the replacement alternator bracket delivered this morning did not match your engine model, so we have ordered an express replacement arriving at 08:30 tomorrow.' [A1]
3. Reassurance & customer check: 'Our senior technician will fit and road-test it first thing, meaning your van will be ready for collection by 11:00 tomorrow morning—does that arrangement work for you?' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'Good afternoon Mr Robinson, I am calling from Metro Garage with an update on your van, and I sincerely apologise for a slight delay. Unfortunately, the alternator bracket delivered this morning did not match your engine model, so we have arranged an express replacement arriving at 08:30 tomorrow. Our technician will fit and test it immediately, so your van will be ready for collection by 11:00 tomorrow morning.'
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Either use plain English from the start or immediately define the term.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Always pair bad news (a delay) with a definite solution and an exact updated time ('ready by 11:00 tomorrow').
Question 4 (Exam-Style Task — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
An IT technician needs to tell a non-technical receptionist: 'Don't click links in phishing emails because malware will encrypt the local LAN server.'
Rewrite this spoken advice in 2–3 clear, jargon-free sentences that explain what to look out for and why it matters.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Explain 'phishing emails' in plain terms (suspicious emails pretending to be from banks or couriers). [M1]
2. Explain 'malware encrypting the LAN server' in plain terms (harmful software locking all our patient appointment files). [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'Please be extra cautious if you receive unexpected emails asking you to click a link or enter your password, even if they look official. Clicking a fake link can install harmful software that locks everyone out of our shared patient booking computer system. If an email ever looks unusual, please forward it straight to the IT desk before clicking anything.'
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Reciting memorised dictionary words that you wouldn't naturally use makes your delivery sound stiff and insincere.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Explaining the practical consequence ('locks everyone out of our booking system') helps non-technical colleagues understand why the rule matters.
Section C: Pearson Edexcel Exam-Style Analysis & Evaluation Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Model 2 (Level 2): Communicating a Discharge Plan to an Elderly Patient and Family) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A clinical handover note reads: 'Patient ambulates with Zimmer frame. Requires BID domiciliary care package post-discharge and PRN analgesia.'
How should the care coordinator communicate this verbally to the elderly patient (Mrs Davies) and her daughter (Claire)?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Establish warm, respectful rapport with both listeners: 'Good afternoon Mrs Davies, hello Claire. I would like to talk through the support package we have arranged so you feel safe and comfortable returning home tomorrow.' [A1]
2. Translate clinical terminology into accessible plain English: 'To help you walk steadily around your bungalow, our physiotherapist has provided this lightweight walking frame. We have also arranged for friendly home-care assistants to visit twice a day—once at 08:30 to help you get washed and dressed, and once at 19:00.' [A1]
3. Explain medication clearly and invite questions: 'Finally, the pharmacy has packed your pain-relief tablets, which you can take whenever your knee feels sore, up to four times a day. What questions do you both have about the visiting times?' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'Good afternoon Mrs Davies, hello Claire. I would like to run through the support we have arranged for your return home tomorrow. To help you walk steadily indoors, you will have this lightweight walking frame. We have also arranged for home carers to visit twice a day—at 08:30 and 19:00—and packed pain-relief tablets that you can take whenever your knee aches. What questions do you both have about the routine?'
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Aim for natural, composed professional Standard English—clear, courteous, and direct.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Functional Skills examiners award high marks when candidates replace clinical acronyms ('BID domiciliary', 'PRN analgesia') with warm, transparent explanations.
Question 6 (Level 2 Mastery Challenge — 4 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A parent arrives at a nursery reception desk upset because their child's favourite jacket has gone missing from the cloakroom. Using the LAST method (Listen/Acknowledge, Apologise, Solve, Thank/Check), write a calm, empathetic 4-sentence spoken response.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Acknowledge & Apologise: Validate the parent's frustration warmly. [M1]
2. Solve (Immediate Action): Offer to check the classroom lost-property box and garden area immediately. [M1]
3. Solve (Follow-up): Promise to put a notice on the parents' app if not found within 10 minutes. [M1]
4. Check satisfaction. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'I completely understand how upsetting it is to find Leo's jacket missing at pickup time, Mrs Green, and I am really sorry for the worry this has caused. I am going to check the Blue Room lost-property basket and the outdoor play area with his key worker right now. If we do not locate it within the next five minutes, I will send an urgent photo alert to all parents on the nursery app so anyone who picked it up by mistake can return it tomorrow morning. Does that sound helpful while you wait in reception?'
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Failing to proofread spelling, punctuation, and grammar after completing the draft.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Staying calm, using the child's name, and taking immediate practical action de-escalates upset parents or customers every time.
