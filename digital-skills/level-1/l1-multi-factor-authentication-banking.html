Step-by-Step Teaching & Key Concepts

Core Knowledge
1
Why Passwords Alone Are No Longer Enough

Billions of usernames and passwords are leaked on the dark web every year. If you use the same password on multiple websites, a data breach at one company puts all your accounts at risk.

Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA / 2FA) adds an extra layer of security. Even if a criminal steals your password, they CANNOT log in without your second authentication factor!

2
The Three Factors of Authentication

MFA combines at least two different categories:

  1. Something you KNOW: Password, PIN, or memorable security question.
  2. Something you HAVE: Physical smartphone receiving an SMS code, authenticator app (e.g. Microsoft Authenticator), or a physical hardware security key (YubiKey).
  3. Something you ARE (Biometrics): Fingerprint scan, facial recognition (Face ID), or retina scan.
Two-Factor Verification Prompt

3
Online Banking Security Rules

When managing business or personal finances online:

  • Always log out completely when finished.
  • Check statements weekly for unauthorised micro-charges.
  • Never approve an MFA prompt on your phone unless you are actively logging in yourself! Criminals who steal passwords trigger MFA prompts hoping you will click 'Approve' without thinking (MFA fatigue attack).

Authentic Pearson Edexcel Exam Model

Accredited Model

Here is how this skill is tested in the real Pearson Edexcel onscreen digital assessment, broken down with official examiner mark codes ([B1] knowledge, [M1] process, [A1] accuracy).

Exam Scenario Prompt: A dental practice introduces Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) for its staff patient database. Staff must now type their password AND enter a 6-digit code sent to their mobile phone. Explain how this prevents unauthorised access if a worker's password is stolen in a phishing attack.
Step 1: Explain the protection mechanism [B1]
The criminal only has the password (something you know), but they do not possess the worker's physical mobile phone (something you have) to receive the 6-digit code.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Identifies the requirement for two independent factors.
Step 2: State outcome [B1]
Without the second factor, the login attempt is blocked and patient records remain protected.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Confirms system denies unauthorized access.
Tutor Abdul's Exam Golden Rule: In Level 1 exams, questions on MFA test whether you know the three factors: something you know (password), something you have (phone/token), something you are (fingerprint).

Pearson Edexcel Benchmark Solution

Definition: Multi-Factor Authentication is a security process requiring users to provide two or more different forms of identification before gaining access. Factor 1 (Something you know): A strong private password or secret PIN. Factor 2 (Something you have/are): A one-time passcode generated on a registered authenticator app / phone, or a biometric fingerprint scan.
Official Marking Criteria:
  • [1 Mark]: Accurate definition of MFA requiring 2+ distinct verification steps.
  • [1 Mark]: Providing valid examples of two different authentication categories.
