Entry Level 3 Specification & Learning Outcomes (E3.W.13 / E3.W.14 / E3.W.15)

Regulated Ofqual Entry Level 3 English outcomes (Open Awards, Pearson Edexcel, City & Guilds & NCFE):

  • E3.W.13 – Use a range of punctuation correctly (e.g. full stops, capital letters, question marks, exclamation marks, commas).
  • E3.W.14 – Use mostly correct grammar (e.g. subject-verb agreement, consistent use of tense, definite and indefinite articles).
  • E3.W.15 – Use the first and second letters to sequence words in alphabetical order.
Entry Level 3 Focus: Using the 5 Entry Level 3 punctuation marks (capitals, full stops, question marks, exclamation marks, commas in lists) and accurate everyday grammar (was/were, is/are, past/present tense, a/an/the).

Core Entry Level 3 Golden Rules: Entry Level 3 SPaG: Punctuation, Capitals, Verbs & Tenses

Golden Rule 1: Master the 5 Core Entry 3 Punctuation Marks

1. Capital letters (starts, names, places, days, months, `I`) | 2. Full stops (.) | 3. Question marks (?) | 4. Exclamation marks (!) | 5. Commas (,) in lists.

Entry Level 3 Key Rule: Include at least one question (`?`) and one list with commas to show range!
Golden Rule 2: Match Singular vs Plural Verbs ('Was' vs 'Were', 'Is' vs 'Are')

One person/thing = is / was (The bus was late). More than one (or 'you / we / they') = are / were (We were early; The shops are open).

Entry Level 3 Key Rule: Never write 'we was' or 'they is'!
Golden Rule 3: Choose 'A' vs 'An' Using Vowel Sounds

Use an before vowel sounds (`a, e, i, o, u` and silent `h` like an hour): an apple, an email, an umbrella. Use a before consonant sounds: a ticket, a laptop.

Entry Level 3 Key Rule: 'Send an email' (never 'a email')!

Core Skill 1: Punctuating Sentences & Lists Cleanly (E3.W.13)

Check every capital letter, end mark, and list comma.

Capitals That Learners Forget
Always capitalise standalone I, days (Tuesday), months (November), towns (Manchester), and people's names.
Commas in a List
Separate items with commas and use 'and' before the last item: 'Bring a pen, notepad, and ruler.'

Vocational Context: Fixing Punctuation & Grammar in an Entry 3 Note

Correct all errors in these two sentences:
'on saturday i went to the market and buyed apples bananas and oranges. can you come with me next week'
Step
Entry Level 3 Method & Working
Marking Focus
Step 1 Sentence 1: Capitals, irregular past verb & list commas
Capital 'On', 'Saturday', 'I' + 'bought' + commas
→ 'On Saturday I went to the market and bought apples, bananas, and oranges.'
2 Marks — Capitals, past verb & list commas
Step 2 Sentence 2: Capital start & question mark
Direct question needs '?'
→ 'Can you come with me next week?'
2 Marks — Capital start + question mark
Final Exam Answer: 'On Saturday I went to the market and bought apples, bananas, and oranges. Can you come with me next week?'
Subi’s Entry Level 3 Exam Tip: Watch irregular past verbs: write went (not 'goed'), bought (not 'buyed'), and saw (not 'seed' or 'seen' alone: 'I saw', never 'I seen')!

Common Entry Level 3 Examiner Traps to Avoid

Entry Level 3 Examiner Trap: Writing 'I Seen' or 'I Done'
In standard written English, write I saw (or I have seen) and I did (or I have done)!
Entry Level 3 Examiner Trap: Forgetting '?' at the End of Questions
Any sentence starting 'Can you...', 'When does...', or 'Where is...' needs a question mark `?`.

Interactive Entry Level 3 Exam Practice Questions

Entry Level 3 Practice 1: Choose the Correct Verb or Article 3 Marks
Pick the right word in brackets:
1. We (was / were) waiting at the bus stop.
2. Please send me (a / an) email before lunch.
3. Yesterday Sam (see / saw / seen) the new timetable.
Entry Level 3 Worked Solution & Mark Breakdown:
Item 1 (1 Mark): were ('We were')
Item 2 (1 Mark): an ('an email')
Item 3 (1 Mark): saw ('Yesterday Sam saw')
Final Answer: 1. were | 2. an | 3. saw
Entry Level 3 Practice 2: Punctuate a List & Question 2 Marks
Add capitals, commas, and end punctuation:
'do we need milk bread tea and sugar from the shop'
Entry Level 3 Worked Solution & Mark Breakdown:
Capital & question mark (1 Mark): 'Do we...' + '...shop?'
List commas (1 Mark): 'milk, bread, tea, and sugar'
Final Answer: 'Do we need milk, bread, tea, and sugar from the shop?'
