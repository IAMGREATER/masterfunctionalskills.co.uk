Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.2 / L2.2 – Make requests and ask detailed questions using appropriate language in a wide range of contexts.
  • L2.1 / L2.7 – Use strategic questioning to clarify lines of argument, probe evidence, and draw peers into group discussions.

Golden Rules for Asking Questions Effectively

Golden Rule 1: Ask Open Questions ('How', 'Why', 'What Impact') to Unlock Detail

Closed questions ('Do you agree?', 'Is it expensive?') produce one-word 'Yes/No' answers that kill discussion momentum. Start your questions with 'How will...', 'What evidence...', or 'In what ways could...' to prompt rich answers.

Golden Rule / Technique: Closed: 'Will this save money?' (1 word) → Open: 'How much will this save over 12 months?' (Detailed)
Golden Rule 2: Ask At Least Two Probing Questions During Peer Presentations

In Task 1 (Presentations), you are assessed not only on your own talk, but also as an audience member asking questions after your peers' talks! Prepare two open probing questions for every speaker.

Golden Rule / Technique: Audience Rule: Listen actively → Note 2 gaps or impacts → Ask 1 Clarifying + 1 Probing Question

Core Skill 1: The 4-Type Questioning Taxonomy for Functional Skills Exams

Deploy all four question types across your group discussion and presentation Q&A.

Question Type Functional Purpose High-Scoring Exam Example
1. Open Exploratory Opens up a new topic or invites broad views. "What are the main challenges our team faces during the Friday evening rush?"
2. Clarifying Fact-Check Pins down exact numbers, dates, or definitions. "Could you clarify whether that £1,500 quote includes annual maintenance?"
3. Probing / Analytical Tests feasibility, risks, or deeper evidence (Level 2). "How will we maintain customer service standards if we reduce reception staffing by 20%?"
4. Collaborative / Inviting Brings a quiet peer into the conversation by name. "Priya, having worked on the night shift, how realistic does this timetable seem to you?"
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Converting Weak Closed Questions into High-Scoring Open Probes

Practise upgrading single-word Yes/No questions into analytical Level 2 questions.

  • Closed: "Is the new software hard to learn?"
    Open Upgrade: "What training support will staff need to master the new software confidently?"
  • Closed: "Did the trial work well?"
    Open Upgrade: "What measurable improvements did you see during the four-week pilot scheme?"
  • Closed: "Do you think we should ban cars from the town centre?"
    Open Upgrade: "How might pedestrianising the High Street affect elderly residents and disabled badge holders?"
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Polite Indirect Requests & Diplomatic Question Framing

How to ask challenging questions without sounding hostile or interrogative.

Instead of firing blunt questions ("Why didn't you check the cost?"), soften and elevate your question using a polite lead-in:

• "Thank you for a very clear presentation, Alex. Could you expand a little on how the scheme will be funded in Year 2?"
• "That is an interesting proposal. Have we considered what safeguards would be needed if a supplier delivery is delayed?"
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 Level 1 Questioning Benchmark (L1.2)

Vocational Context: Post-Presentation Q&A Session

A fellow candidate has just finished a 3-minute talk encouraging college students to cycle to campus instead of taking the bus. Formulate one clarifying question and one open question to ask them during the Q&A.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Acknowledge the speaker's talk politely
'Thank you for that interesting talk, Sam.'
Criteria: Courteous Framing (L1.2)
Step 2 Ask a specific Clarifying Question about campus facilities
'Could you clarify where the locked bike sheds are located on the main campus and whether students need to pay for a key fob?'
Criteria: Extracts Practical Detail
Step 3 Ask an Open Question about wet weather / winter safety
'What advice would you give to beginners who feel nervous about cycling in heavy rain or dark winter evenings?'
Criteria: Stimulates Detailed Response
Final Accredited Response: Question 1 (Clarifying): 'Thank you for your talk, Sam. Could you clarify where the secure cycle shelters are located on campus and how students apply for a key fob?'
Question 2 (Open): 'What practical advice would you give to students who feel nervous about cycling on busy roads during dark winter afternoons?'
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Asking a question that starts with 'What practical advice would you give...' gives the presenter a great chance to elaborate, earning marks for both of you!
Benchmark Exam Model 2 Level 2 Strategic Questioning Benchmark (L2.1, L2.2)

Vocational Context: Pharmaceutical Logistics Quality Assurance Meeting

During a stock integrity meeting, the depot supervisor announces: 'Cold-chain temperature deviations occurred four times in our refrigerated vans last month.'
Formulate three progressive questions (Clarifying → Probing Root Cause → Collaborative Action) to interrogate this issue thoroughly.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Clarifying Question (Pinpointing Scope & Data)
'Could you specify which delivery routes and which refrigerated vehicles accounted for those four temperature breaches?'
Criteria: Extracts Exact Facts (L2.1)
Step 2 Probing Question (Root-Cause Analysis)
'What did the vehicle data-loggers reveal about whether the breaches were caused by faulty chiller compressors or doors being left open during multi-drop unloading?'
Criteria: Probes Underlying Cause (L2.2)
Step 3 Action-Oriented Collaborative Question (Prevention)
'While engineering inspects those vans, how can we adjust our loading procedure tomorrow morning to guarantee zero stock spoilage?'
Criteria: Drives Collaborative Solution
Final Accredited Response: 1. Clarifying: 'Could you specify which vehicles and routes experienced those four temperature deviations?'
2. Probing: 'What did the telemetry logs show regarding whether the cause was compressor failure or prolonged door openings during deliveries?'
3. Action: 'How can we modify our loading protocol tomorrow morning to protect temperature-sensitive stock while those units are inspected?'
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Moving systematically from What happened → Why did it happen → How do we fix it tomorrow is the hallmark of Level 2 questioning.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Asking Hostile or Loaded Questions ('Don't You Think That's Stupid?')
Questions framed as attacks ('Why would anyone waste money on that?') make speakers defensive and fail criteria for collaborative professional register.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Frame challenging questions neutrally around risk and feasibility: 'How might we overcome the upfront cost of that option?'
Examiner Pitfall: Sitting Silently During Other Candidates' Q&A Sessions
To pass Criterion L1.2 / L2.2, the assessor must record evidence of you asking detailed questions. If you never raise your hand in Q&A, you risk failing that criterion.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Make it a rule to ask at least one well-formed question after EVERY peer presentation in your group.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Convert both of these closed Yes/No questions into detailed open probing questions:
1. 'Do you think moving the staffroom upstairs is a good idea?'
2. 'Will the new computer system save time?'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Rewrite Question 1 starting with 'How might...' or 'What impact...' to explore accessibility and break time.
2. Rewrite Question 2 starting with 'In what ways will...' or 'How much time...'.
Final Model Answer: 1. 'How might relocating the staffroom to the second floor affect staff who only have a fifteen-minute break or colleagues with mobility needs?'
2. 'In what specific ways will the upgraded booking software reduce administrative processing times each day?'
Examiner Tip: Starting questions with 'How might...' or 'In what specific ways...' makes a one-word answer impossible.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
A colleague proposes: 'We should replace all annual staff appraisals with monthly 15-minute informal check-ins.'
Write two open probing questions to test the practical feasibility of this proposal in a busy workplace.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Probe 1 (Time/Workload): Ask how line managers will schedule 12 meetings per employee alongside daily operational duties.
2. Probe 2 (Record-Keeping): Ask how long-term training and promotion targets will be documented consistently.
Final Model Answer: 1. 'With line managers supervising up to fifteen staff each, how will they find capacity to conduct fifteen separate check-ins every month during busy clinical shifts?'
2. 'What standardised form will we use during these informal chats to ensure staff training requests and pay-progression targets are still formally documented for HR?'
Examiner Tip: Good probing questions always test the two main workplace pressures: Time capacity and Documentation/Cost.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
You have just listened to a peer's presentation proposing that your local council should dim all residential streetlights by 50% after midnight to save electricity. Write a polite 2-sentence question that acknowledges their environmental point and probes the impact on shift workers and public safety.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Sentence 1: Acknowledge the energy-saving and carbon-reduction benefits outlined in their talk.
2. Sentence 2: Ask an open probing question about pedestrian safety for night-shift workers and elderly residents.
Final Model Answer: 'Thank you for a very well-researched presentation—the projected £40,000 saving on council electricity bills is certainly impressive. However, how would the council protect the personal safety of night-shift nurses and factory workers walking home after midnight on dimly lit residential streets?'
Examiner Tip: Complimenting a specific statistic from the speaker's talk before asking your question proves to the assessor that you were listening closely.
