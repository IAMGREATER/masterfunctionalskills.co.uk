Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.15 / L2.15 – Identify the tone and style of texts and explain how register, vocabulary, and grammar communicate attitude.
  • L2.16 – Evaluate how the writer's voice establishes credibility, empathy, urgency, or professional detachment.

Golden Rules for Tone, Style & Writer's Voice

Golden Rule 1: Tone is the Writer's Attitude & Emotion

Tone is not WHAT is said, but HOW it sounds if spoken aloud: reassuring, urgent, apologetic, sarcastic, enthusiastic, cautionary, or matter-of-fact. Always use a precise adjective to name the tone.

Golden Rule / Technique: Ban vague words like 'good/bad tone'. Use: Urgent, Reassuring, Apologetic, Critical, Formal.
Golden Rule 2: Style & Register Reflect Relationship with the Reader

Formal style uses passive voice, complex sentences, and specialist vocabulary to project authority. Conversational/informal style uses contractions, questions, and friendly idioms to build warmth and rapport.

Golden Rule / Technique: Formal Voice = Authoritative & Detached | Conversational Voice = Friendly & Approachable

Core Skill 1: The Tone Vocabulary Matrix for Exam Candidates

Precise adjectives to describe tone accurately in Level 1 & Level 2 Reading answers.

Precise Tone Label Linguistic Clues in the Text Typical Workplace Context
Authoritative / Direct Imperative verbs ("Ensure", "Do not"), modal verbs ("must"), statutory citations. Health & safety notices, standard operating procedures, legal memos.
Reassuring / Empathetic Supportive adjectives ("gentle", "confidential"), calming assurances ("don't worry, our team is here"). Patient information leaflets, mental health helplines, induction guides.
Apologetic / Conciliatory Expressions of regret ("sincerely apologise", "deeply regret"), offers of compensation. Customer complaint responses, service disruption updates.
Outraged / Critical Negative emotive words ("shambolic", "unacceptable"), exclamation marks, rhetorical questions. Letters of complaint, critical newspaper editorials, union petitions.
Enthusiastic / Promotional Positive superlatives ("thrilled", "unbeatable", "fantastic"), inviting commands. Tourism brochures, job recruitment adverts, product launches.

Core Skill 2: Active Voice vs Passive Voice (How Grammar Shapes Style)

Understand why formal reports use the passive voice while campaigns use the active voice.

Sentence grammar directly controls how personal or detached a writer's voice sounds:

Active Voice (Direct & Dynamic)

The subject performs the action:
"Our maintenance team repaired the boiler in two hours."
Effect: Sounds energetic, accountable, and direct.

Passive Voice (Formal & Detached)

The action is done to the subject:
"The boiler was repaired within two hours."
Effect: Sounds objective, clinical, and impersonal (common in scientific and audit reports).

Core Skill 3: How Sentence Length Controls Pace and Urgency

Why writers switch between short punchy sentences and complex multi-clause sentences.

  • Short Simple / Minor Sentences: (e.g., "Act now. Every minute matters. Check your smoke alarm tonight.") → Creates a rapid, urgent, punchy pace that demands immediate reader attention.
  • Long Complex Sentences: (e.g., "Following a comprehensive six-month consultation with ward matrons, estates engineers, and patient representatives, the Trust has approved a phased refurbishment.") → Creates a measured, formal, deliberate pace that conveys thoroughness and authority.

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 2 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Reading Level 1)

Vocational Context: NHS First Blood Donation Guide

Read this extract from a leaflet for first-time blood donors:
'Feeling a little nervous about your first donation? That is completely normal! Our warm, friendly nurses will chat with you every step of the way, and the needle pinch lasts less than a second.'
(a) Describe the tone of this extract. [1 Mark]
(b) Quote one phrase that helps create this tone. [1 Mark]
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Identify the writer's attitude toward nervous donors
The tone is reassuring, comforting, friendly, and supportive.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Tone Label]
Step 2 Quote a specific reassuring phrase
'That is completely normal!' or 'Our warm, friendly nurses will chat with you every step of the way'.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Supporting Quote]
Final Accredited Response: (a) Tone: Reassuring, friendly, and comforting. | (b) Quote: 'That is completely normal!' (or 'Our warm, friendly nurses will chat with you every step of the way').
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
'Reassuring' is one of the highest-scoring tone words whenever a healthcare or customer leaflet calms a reader's worries.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Reading Level 2)

Vocational Context: Healthcare Patient Relations

Read the opening paragraph of a letter from the Hospital Director:
'We have thoroughly investigated your grievance regarding your mother's care on Ward 2. We sincerely regret the unacceptable distress caused by the communication delay, and we have instituted immediate corrective safeguards.'
(a) Describe the tone and style of this extract. [1 Mark]
(b) Quote two words or phrases that establish this tone. [1 Mark]
(c) Explain why this tone and style are appropriate for the audience and situation. [1 Mark]
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Describe the tone and style accurately
The tone is formal, apologetic, empathetic, and accountable.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Tone]
Step 2 Quote two supporting words/phrases
Quote 1: 'sincerely regret' | Quote 2: 'unacceptable distress' (or 'immediate corrective safeguards').
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Quotes]
Step 3 Explain contextual appropriateness
It validates the family's upset with genuine empathy while using formal, professional language to reassure them that the hospital takes the error seriously and has fixed the system.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Appropriateness]
Final Accredited Response: (a) Formal, apologetic, and empathetic. | (b) 'sincerely regret' and 'unacceptable distress'. | (c) Acknowledges the family's upset respectfully while reassuring them that professional corrective action has been taken.
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Avoid generic words like 'good' or 'nice'. Pair precise tone descriptors like 'apologetic' and 'formal' with exact quotations.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Confusing Tone with the Overall Subject Matter
A text about a serious road accident might be written in a detached, factual, clinical tone (e.g. a police log) rather than an emotional one.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Focus on HOW the writer expresses the information (their word choice and attitude), not the raw topic itself.
Examiner Pitfall: Using Vague Words Like 'Positive Tone' or 'Negative Tone'
At Level 2, writing 'the tone is negative' is too broad to secure full marks.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Specify what KIND of positive or negative tone it is: 'enthusiastic and welcoming' vs 'critical, frustrated, and urgent'.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A dental hygiene flyer asks: 'Hate flossing? We get it — it feels like a total chore at bedtime! But just two minutes a night will keep your gums healthy and save you painful bills.'
Describe the style/tone of this extract and quote one colloquial (informal) phrase.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Style/Tone: Informal, conversational, friendly, and relatable [1 Mark].
2. Colloquial Quote: 'We get it' or 'feels like a total chore' [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Tone: Conversational, friendly, and informal. | Quote: 'We get it' (or 'feels like a total chore').
Examiner Tip: Both parts (an accurate tone adjective and an exact quoted informal phrase) are required for 2 marks.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
Read this extract from a laboratory safety protocol:
'Protective eye shields must be worn at all times. Unauthorised personnel are strictly prohibited from entering the centrifuge room.'
Describe the tone and style of this extract and identify one grammatical feature that creates it.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Tone/Style: Formal, authoritative, strict, and impersonal [1 Mark].
2. Grammatical feature: Passive voice ('must be worn', 'are strictly prohibited') or strong modal/adverbial emphasis ('must', 'strictly prohibited') [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Tone/Style: Formal, strict, and authoritative. | Feature: Passive voice ('must be worn') and strong modal/adverbial phrasing ('strictly prohibited').
Examiner Tip: Notice how passive voice ('must be worn') makes the rule sound like an objective, unbreakable law.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
Read this extract from a commuter's letter to a train company:
'Paying £4,200 a year to stand wedged like sardines in an unheated carriage while your company boasts about "record punctuality" is an absolute insult to hardworking passengers!'
Identify the writer's tone [1 Mark] and explain how two language or punctuation choices convey this tone [2 Marks].
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Tone: Outraged, angry, bitter, or sarcastic [1 Mark].
2. Choice 1: The simile 'wedged like sardines' and emotive noun 'absolute insult' highlight physical discomfort and disrespect [1 Mark].
3. Choice 2: Scare quotes around '"record punctuality"' and the final exclamation mark (!) convey bitter sarcasm and anger [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Tone: Outraged, bitter, and sarcastic. | Choices: 1. Simile ('wedged like sardines') and emotive phrase ('absolute insult') emphasise mistreatment. 2. Inverted commas around '"record punctuality"' and the exclamation mark show sarcasm and anger.
Examiner Tip: Quotation marks used around a phrase like '"record punctuality"' to mock a claim are a classic sign of a sarcastic/ironic tone!
