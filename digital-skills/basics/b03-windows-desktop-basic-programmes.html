Step-by-Step Practical Guide

1
1. Reading Your Screen Layout (Desktop, Taskbar & Start)

Once signed in, your main screen is the Desktop:

  • Start Button (Bottom-Left): Opens your full programme list (A–Z) and the Power button (Sleep / Restart / Shut down).
  • Search Box beside Start: Fast way to open anything — just click Search and type Word, Calc, Notepad, Calculator, or a file name.
  • Taskbar (Bottom Strip): Shows icons of programmes you pinned AND programmes currently open (with a small underline under active ones).
  • System Tray (Bottom-Right): Shows Wi-Fi bars, speaker volume, battery charge, clock, and date.
Tutor Tip: If a programme disappears, check the bottom Taskbar before opening it a second time — it is usually just minimised!
2
2. Window Controls Top-Right: Minimise, Maximise & Close

Every programme window has the exact same three buttons in the top-right corner:

  • Minimise ( _ ): Drops the window neatly down onto the Taskbar without closing anything. Click its Taskbar icon later to bring it right back.
  • Maximise / Restore ( □ ): Expands the window to fill the whole screen (click again to shrink it back into a smaller movable box).
  • Close ( × ): Exits the programme completely. Always save (Ctrl + S) before clicking ×!

Handy Pro Trick: Hold the Windows key + Left Arrow (and then click your second app) to sit an exam brief or browser on the left half and Word/Excel on the right half.

Tutor Tip: Use Alt + Tab on your keyboard anytime to flip smoothly between two open windows.
3
3. Everyday Programmes Tour (Microsoft vs Free Built-In / LibreOffice)

You do not need expensive paid software at home to learn or pass Functional Skills. Every Windows laptop includes free essentials, and free LibreOffice runs all Word/Excel/PowerPoint files:

  • File Explorer (Yellow Folder): Opens Documents, Downloads, Pictures, USB sticks, and creates folders.
  • Web Browser (Edge / Chrome / Firefox): Opens websites, webmail, search engines, and online forms.
  • Word / LibreOffice Writer: Types CVs, formal letters, posters, and formatted reports (.docx).
  • Excel / LibreOffice Calc: Builds tables, budgets, formulas, totals, sorting, and charts (.xlsx).
  • PowerPoint / LibreOffice Impress: Creates slide presentations for meetings and talks (.pptx).
  • Notepad / Snipping Tool / Calculator / Settings: Quick plain notes, capturing screenshots, sums, and Wi-Fi/Display/Sound adjustments.
Tutor Tip: Remember: .docx opens in both Word and LibreOffice Writer; .xlsx opens in both Excel and Calc.

Three Common Beginner Traps

Avoid Trap: Clicking the red X close button just to see another app

That shuts your programme completely. Use Minimise (_) or click directly on the other app icon along the bottom taskbar.

Avoid Trap: Opening 6 copies of the same document

Check the Taskbar first — clicking the existing icon brings back your open document without creating duplicate conflict files.

Avoid Trap: Trying to write a formatted CV inside Notepad

Notepad is plain unformatted text only (no bold headings, images, or tables). Open Word or LibreOffice Writer instead.
