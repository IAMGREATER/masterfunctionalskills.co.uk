Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • L1.6 / L2.6 – Round numbers to a given power of 10, specified decimal places, or significant figures.
  • L1.7 / L2.7 – Estimate answers to multi-step calculations by rounding each value to 1 significant figure.

Golden Rules for Rounding & Estimating

Golden Rule 1: The '5 or More Round Up' Rule

Look at the decider digit immediately to the right of your target column. If it is 5, 6, 7, 8, or 9, round UP. If 0, 1, 2, 3, or 4, keep the target digit the same.

Golden Rule / Method: 0–4: Stay the Same | 5–9: Round UP by 1
Golden Rule 2: 1 Significant Figure for Exam Estimations

Whenever an exam question begins with 'Use estimation to check...' or 'Estimate the value of...', round EVERY number to 1 significant figure BEFORE performing any calculation.

Golden Rule / Method: Estimate: 489 × 3.12 ≈ 500 × 3 = 1,500

Core Skill 2: Skill 2: Estimating Complex Calculations by Rounding to 1 s.f.

How to score full marks on estimation checks without doing long multiplication.

To estimate (58.7 × 19.4) ÷ 0.48:

  1. Round each number to 1 significant figure: 58.7 → 60; 19.4 → 20; 0.48 → 0.5.
  2. Write the rounded calculation clearly to secure the [M1] mark: (60 × 20) ÷ 0.5.
  3. Compute numerator: 60 × 20 = 1,200.
  4. Divide by 0.5 (remember dividing by 0.5 doubles the number): 1,200 ÷ 0.5 = 2,400.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Skill 3: Identifying Overestimates vs Underestimates

Explaining how rounding direction affects your estimated answer.

  • Overestimate: Occurs when you round both multiplied numbers UP (e.g. 194 × £4.85 → 200 × £5 = £1,000; actual is £940.90).
  • Underestimate: Occurs when you round both multiplied numbers DOWN (e.g. 312 × £2.15 → 300 × £2 = £600; actual is £670.80).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1/2 Non-Calculator)

Vocational Context: Healthcare Food Services Procurement

The catering manager orders 194 cartons of meal trays at £4.85 each.
(a) Show an estimate of the total expenditure by rounding each value to 1 significant figure.
(b) State whether your estimate is an underestimate or overestimate, giving a reason.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Round each value to 1 significant figure
194 rounds to 200 (1 sf)
£4.85 rounds to £5.00 (1 sf)
Criteria: M1: Rounding both values to 1 sf
Step 2 Compute estimated total
200 × £5 = £1,000
Criteria: A1: Correct estimated product (£1,000)
Step 3 Analyze whether overestimate or underestimate
Both numbers were rounded UP (194 → 200 and 4.85 → 5.00); therefore, £1,000 is an OVERESTIMATE.
Criteria: B1: Valid comparative justification
Final Accredited Answer: (a) Estimate = £1,000 | (b) Overestimate, because both 194 and £4.85 were rounded up.
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
If you calculate the exact answer (194 × 4.85 = £940.90) and then round that to £1,000, you score ZERO for step 1. You must round BEFORE multiplying.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2 Problem Solving)

Vocational Context: Estates & Facilities Cost Verification

A clinic room measures 7.85 m long by 4.12 m wide. Non-slip clinical vinyl flooring costs £29.50 per m², plus a fixed fitting fee of £185.00.
The contractor quotes £1,148.20. Use estimation (rounding each figure to 1 significant figure) to check if the contractor's quote is sensible.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Round room dimensions and unit cost to 1 significant figure
Length: 7.85 m ≈ 8 m | Width: 4.12 m ≈ 4 m | Cost/m²: £29.50 ≈ £30 | Fitting: £185 ≈ £200.
Criteria: M1: Rounding values to 1 s.f.
Step 2 Calculate estimated floor area and material cost
Estimated Area = 8 m × 4 m = 32 m².
Estimated Vinyl Cost = 32 × £30 = £960.
Criteria: M1: Area and material estimation
Step 3 Add estimated fitting fee
Total Estimated Cost = £960 + £200 = £1,160.
Criteria: A1: Correct estimated total (£1,160)
Step 4 Compare estimate with contractor quote
£1,160 is very close to the £1,148.20 quote. Yes, the contractor's quote is sensible.
Criteria: B1: Valid comparison and confirmation
Final Accredited Answer: Estimated total = £1,160 (Yes, the £1,148.20 quote is sensible as it is very close to £1,160).
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
In Pearson Edexcel "Use estimation to check" questions, writing down the rounded numbers (8 × 4 × 30 + 200) is mandatory for full marks.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Calculating Exact Value Before Rounding in Estimation Questions
Exam boards require you to demonstrate that you can estimate WITHOUT doing complex multi-digit arithmetic.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always write down the rounded numbers first (e.g. 200 × 5) before calculating anything.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Use 1 significant figure approximation to estimate the value of: (38.4 × 9.75) ÷ 0.48.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Round each to 1 sf: 38.4 ≈ 40; 9.75 ≈ 10; 0.48 ≈ 0.5.
2. Compute: (40 × 10) ÷ 0.5 = 400 ÷ 0.5 = 800.
Final Correct Answer: 800
Examiner Tip: Dividing by 0.5 is equivalent to multiplying by 2 (400 ÷ 0.5 = 800).
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
Round 14,682.739 to:
(a) 2 decimal places
(b) 2 significant figures.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. (a) Look at 3rd decimal digit (9 ≥ 5): rounds .739 up to .74 → 14,682.74.
2. (b) Look at 3rd significant digit (6 ≥ 5): rounds 14,000 up to 15,000.
Final Correct Answer: (a) 14,682.74 | (b) 15,000
Examiner Tip: When rounding whole numbers to 2 s.f., remember to keep placeholder zeros so 14,682 becomes 15,000 (NOT 15)!
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A charity event sells 392 tickets at £19.80 each. Expenses are £1,895. Estimate the net profit by rounding each number to 1 significant figure.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Round to 1 sf: 392 ≈ 400 tickets; £19.80 ≈ £20; £1,895 ≈ £2,000.
2. Estimated revenue = 400 × £20 = £8,000.
3. Estimated net profit = £8,000 - £2,000 = £6,000.
Final Correct Answer: £6,000
Examiner Tip: Show the rounded figures (400, £20, £2,000) clearly on your first line.
