Level 2 Essential Formulae: Geometry & Shapes

Geometry calculations underpin construction and engineering exams.
▶Geometric Formulae (Memorise for Section A & B)
Circumference of Circle:C = πd = 2πr (use π = 3.14 or 3.142)
Area of Circle:A = πr² (always square radius, NEVER multiply by 2!)
Area of Cylinder (Curved):Curved Surface Area = πdh = 2πrh
Volume of Cylinder:V = πr²h (Area of cross-section × height)
Volume of Triangular Prism:V = (½ × base × height) × length
Pythagoras Theorem:a² + b² = c² (where c is the hypotenuse)
✓Golden Geometry Rule
For semi-circles, divide circumference or area by 2. Add diameter if calculating perimeter.
!Examiner Trap!
Remember: πr² is NOT (πr)². Square radius first, then multiply by π.

Level 2 Essential Formulae: Measures & Statistics

▶Measures, Rates & Statistics Matrix
Speed, Distance & Time:Speed = Distance ÷ Time | Time = Dist ÷ Speed
Density, Mass & Volume:Density = Mass ÷ Volume | Mass = Density × Volume
Percentage Change:% Change = (Actual Change ÷ Original Value) × 100
Reverse Percentage:Original Value = New Value ÷ Decimal Multiplier
Estimated Mean (Grouped):Estimated Mean = Σ(midpoint × frequency) ÷ Σ(frequency)
Combined Probability:P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B) (for independent events)
Abdul
Time Trap Alert! 2 hours 15 minutes is 2.25 hours, NOT 2.15 hours! Always convert minutes to decimal hours by dividing by 60 before using speed formulas.

Exam Technique & Securing Process Marks

Every intermediate step secures process marks even if your final answer is wrong.
▶Pearson Mark Classification System
[P] Process Marks: Awarded for choosing the correct strategy (e.g. recognizing a 20% discount means multiplying by 0.80).
[M] Method Marks: Awarded for writing out standard formulas with given values substituted correctly.
[A] Accuracy Marks: Awarded strictly for the correct numerical calculation.
[B] Independent Marks: Awarded for correct units (£, m², kg), rounding, or valid written checks.
★How to Write a Guaranteed 1-Mark 'Check'

When asked to 'Show a check of your answer', use one of two methods:
1. Reverse Operation (Inverse): If 45 × 12 = 540, write Check: 540 ÷ 12 = 45.
2. Estimation Check: Round inputs to 1 significant figure: Check: 48.2 × 9.8 ≈ 50 × 10 = 500.
Trap: Simply rewriting 12 × 45 = 540 scores 0 marks!

Mathematical Glossary & Examination Vocabulary

Angle of Elevation
The angle between the horizontal line of sight and the line of sight up to an object.
Compound Interest
Interest computed on the initial principal and on all previous accumulated interest.
Critical Path
The sequence of project stages which determine the minimum time needed for an operation.
Decimal Multiplier
A single factor used to calculate percentage increase (e.g. 1.15 for +15%) or decrease (0.85 for -15%).
Density
Mass per unit volume (e.g. grams per cubic centimetre or kilograms per cubic metre).
Estimated Mean
An estimate of the arithmetic average for grouped continuous data using class interval midpoints.
Hypotenuse
The side opposite the right angle in any right-angled triangle, governed by Pythagoras' Theorem (a² + b² = c²).
Line of Best Fit
A straight trend line drawn on a scatter diagram representing the balance of points.
Margin of Error
The allowable deviation or tolerance in practical measurements or financial forecasts.
Process Mark [P]
A mark awarded by Pearson examiners for adopting an appropriate mathematical problem-solving method.
Range
The difference between the highest and lowest values in a numerical data set, representing spread.
Reverse Percentage
The mathematical technique to recover an original quantity before a percentage tax, markup, or discount was applied.
Scale Factor
The ratio of linear dimensions on a technical plan or map to the true real-world dimensions (e.g. 1:50).
Tolerance
The permissible limit of variation in a physical dimension or specification (e.g. 250 mm ± 2 mm).
