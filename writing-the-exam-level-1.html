Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • Level 1 Writing Exam Protocol – Complete two distinct extended writing tasks within 60 minutes under regulated exam conditions.
  • Content vs SPaG Mark Split – Maximise marks across both assessment grids: Content & Organisation (~58%) and SPaG (~42%).

Golden Rules for Writing Exam Strategy, Allocation & Word Counts

Golden Rule 1: Follow the Strict 30/30 Minute Split Across Both Tasks

The 60-minute exam contains two tasks. Never spend 45 minutes perfecting Task 1 and leave only 15 minutes for Task 2! Allocate 30 minutes per task (or 35 minutes on the longer task and 25 minutes on the shorter task).

Golden Rule / Technique: Task 1 (30 mins): 5m Plan + 20m Write + 5m Check | Task 2 (30 mins): 5m Plan + 20m Write + 5m Check
Golden Rule 2: Hit the 220–280 Word Sweet Spot (Quality Beats Quantity)

At Level 1, writing only 90 words caps your Content & Grammar marks at Band 1. Conversely, writing 550 words wastes time and multiplies your risk of spelling and comma-splice errors. Aim for 250–300 polished words on the main task and 200–250 words on the second task.

Golden Rule / Technique: Level 1 Sweet Spot: 150–200 words | Level 1 Focus: 150–200 words

Core Skill 1: Inside the Pearson Edexcel Level 1 Writing Mark Scheme (36 Total Marks)

Understand exactly where every mark comes from so you can target all examiner tick-boxes.

Assessment Category Mark Weighting What You Must Do to Score Top Band
1. Content, Audience & Format ~35% of total marks Address 100% of prompt bullets in convincing detail; use exact format layout (addresses/headings); maintain consistent tone.
2. Organisation & Cohesion ~23% of total marks Sequence into 4–5 clear paragraphs with blank lines between them; link ideas smoothly using discourse markers (Furthermore, Consequently).
3. Spelling, Punctuation & Grammar (SPaG) ~42% of total marks (9 marks per task!) Mix simple, compound, and complex sentences; use apostrophes, colons, and semicolons accurately; zero comma splices; accurate spelling of specialist vocabulary.
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: The 5-Point 'Examiner Tick-List' to Include in Every Response

Write this 5-item checklist at the top of your scratch paper in every exam.

  • [ ] 1. Format Header & Sign-Off: Addresses + Date + Salutation (Letter) OR Title + Subheadings (Report/Article) OR To + Subject (Email).
  • [ ] 2. Four Clear Paragraphs: Separated by a full blank line so paragraphing is visually unmistakable.
  • [ ] 3. Three Discourse Markers: e.g. Furthermore, However, Consequently (each followed by a comma).
  • [ ] 4. One Fronted Complex Sentence: Starting with Although..., Whilst..., or Because... with a comma after the first clause.
  • [ ] 5. One Colon (:) and One Semicolon (;): Used accurately to introduce a list/explanation and link two related clauses.
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Onscreen Exam Typing & Formatting Tips

How to make your response look clean inside the basic Pearson / City & Guilds onscreen text box.

  • Always Press Enter Twice Between Paragraphs: In plain text boxes, single line breaks look squashed. Leave a clear blank line between every paragraph, address block, and subheading.
  • Use Asterisks or Hyphens for Bullet Points: If the onscreen toolbar lacks a bullet icon, simply type a hyphen (- ) on a new line for each bullet point.
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Submitting a Single Unbroken 250-Word Block of Text
Even if your vocabulary and spelling are good, forgetting to divide your response into paragraphs automatically caps your Organisation mark at Band 1.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: If you realise at the end of the exam that you forgot paragraph breaks, press Enter twice before each major topic shift to create clear paragraphs.
Examiner Pitfall: Spending 45 Minutes on Task 1 and Leaving Task 2 Half-Finished
An unfinished 60-word response on Task 2 loses up to 10 marks—far more than you could ever gain by polishing Task 1 for an extra 15 minutes.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Watch the onscreen clock strictly: when 30 minutes have elapsed, move immediately to Task 2.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
In a 60-minute Writing exam where Task 1 is worth 21 marks (250–300 words) and Task 2 is worth 15 marks (200–250 words), how should you divide your 60 minutes, and why?
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Allocate approximately 33–35 minutes to Task 1 (more marks and higher word count) and 25–27 minutes to Task 2.
2. Ensure both tasks include 4–5 minutes of planning and 4–5 minutes of proofreading.
Final Model Answer: Allocate 35 minutes to Task 1 (5m plan, 25m write, 5m check) and 25 minutes to Task 2 (4m plan, 17m write, 4m check) to reflect the mark weighting while guaranteeing both tasks are completed in full.
Examiner Tip: Never give any task less than 25 minutes—both tasks carry heavy SPaG marks.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
A student wrote a 260-word formal letter that covered all the prompt bullets well, but lost 6 out of 9 SPaG marks because they joined almost every pair of sentences with a comma (comma splices).
Repair the three comma splices in their paragraph below:
'The community library is a vital hub for elderly residents, it provides free internet access and warm reading rooms, however the council plans to cut its funding by half, this will force the library to close on Saturdays.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Fix splice 1 ('...elderly residents, it provides...') with a semicolon or 'as it provides'.
2. Fix splice 2 ('...reading rooms, however...') by starting a new sentence: '. However,'.
3. Fix splice 3 ('...by half, this will...') by using ', which will force...'.
Final Model Answer: 'The community library is a vital hub for elderly residents, as it provides free internet access and warm reading rooms. However, the council plans to cut its funding by half, which will force the library to close on Saturdays.'
Examiner Tip: Never put ', however,' between two complete sentences—always use '; however,' or '. However,'!
Exam Practice Question 3 4 Marks
Read this exam prompt: 'Write a report (250–300 words) for your Retail Store Manager on ways to reduce single-use plastic packaging in the store.'
Write out the 5-item Examiner Tick-List showing the exact Format headers, Discourse markers, Complex sentence starter, Colon, and Semicolon you will include in this report.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Format headers: Title + To/From/Date + 3 numbered subheadings.
2. Three discourse markers: Furthermore, Consequently, However.
3. Fronted complex sentence: 'Whilst biodegradable bags cost 4p more per unit, customers strongly support...'
4. Colon and Semicolon examples tailored to plastic reduction.
Final Model Answer: 1. Format: Title ('Report on Reducing Single-Use Plastic Packaging'), To/From/Date header, and numbered subheadings (1.0 Introduction, 2.0 Packaging Findings, 3.0 Recommendations).
2. Discourse Markers: Furthermore, Consequently, Nevertheless.
3. Complex Sentence: 'Whilst loose produce paper bags cost slightly more to procure, they eliminate over 10,000 plastic wrappers monthly.'
4. Colon & Semicolon: 'We recommend three immediate changes: ...' (colon) and 'Plastic carrier bag sales have dropped by 40%; however, bakery film waste remains high.' (semicolon).
Examiner Tip: Planning your exact colon and semicolon sentences before you start writing ensures you never forget them under exam stress.
