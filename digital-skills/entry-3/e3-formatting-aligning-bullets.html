Step-by-Step Teaching & Key Concepts

Core Knowledge
1
Paragraph Alignment: Left, Centre, and Right

Alignment controls where your text sits across the width of the page:

  • Align Left (Ctrl + L): Standard for English text. Lines start neatly down the left margin with a jagged right margin. Easiest to read.
  • Centre (Ctrl + E): Positions text right in the middle. Perfect for titles, certificates, and poster headers.
  • Align Right (Ctrl + R): Aligns text against the right margin. Often used for dates and letter sender addresses.
Left Align: Ctrl + L • Centre Align: Ctrl + E • Right Align: Ctrl + R
2
Bulleted Lists vs Numbered Lists

Lists break up big blocks of text, making information quick to scan:

  • Bullet Points (•): Use when the order does not matter (e.g. equipment needed, ingredients, job perks).
  • Numbered Lists (1, 2, 3...): Use when the sequence is critical (e.g. recipes, evacuation procedures, login steps).
List Formatting Ribbon Tools
3
Removing and Adjusting Lists

To turn a list back into normal paragraphs, select the items and click the Bullets button again to toggle it off. To finish a list while typing, press Enter twice.

Tutor Abdul's Checklist Tip: In Entry 3 exams, you are frequently asked: 'Format the items into a bulleted list'. Simply select all the items and click the Bullets button once!

Accredited Model

Exam Scenario Prompt: A medical training guide contains five safety rules printed as a single cramped paragraph. Explain how the guide creator can format this information into a bulleted list to make it easier for trainees to read.
Step 1: Separate the points [B1]
Place each safety rule on its own separate line using Enter.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Each list entry requires its own line.
Step 2: Apply Bullet points [M1]
Select all five lines and click the Bullets button on the Home ribbon.
Tutor Marking Rationale: Converts plain lines into an aligned bulleted list with consistent left indentation.
Tutor Abdul's Exam Golden Rule: Numbered lists are for steps; bullet points are for items. Don't mix them up in the exam!
