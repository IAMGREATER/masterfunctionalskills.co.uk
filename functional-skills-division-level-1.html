Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • L1.3 – Divide whole numbers and decimals by whole numbers up to 2-digit divisors under non-calculator conditions.
  • L1.8 – Interpret remainders appropriately depending on context (e.g. transport minibuses, tile packs, manufacturing batches).
Subi's Assessor Blueprint: Master Division & Remainders!

"Division questions test two distinct skills in Pearson Edexcel exams: accurate arithmetic under the bus stop (handling carried remainders into decimals), and practical common-sense rounding. If you calculate 8.2 minibuses, you can't leave passengers behind, so you must round UP to 9! Let's explore each method with our high-visibility boards and step-by-step breakdown cards."

Golden Rules for Division & Remainders

Golden Rule 1: Practical Context Dictates How You Round Remainders

If booking passenger transport (taxis, coaches), purchasing material packs (tiles, laminate flooring), or buying paint tins, you MUST round UP to avoid leaving people stranded or jobs unfinished. If calculating full boxes packed, complete garments produced, or items you can afford on a budget, you MUST round DOWN.

Golden Rule / Method: People / Tins / Tile Packs → Round UP | Complete Units / Affordability → Round DOWN
Golden Rule 2: Convert Currency Remainders into Decimals with .00

Never write a remainder 'r 2' on a monetary problem! When dividing cash, append a decimal point and two trailing zeros (.00) to the dividend under the bus stop, then continue dividing to find exact pounds and pence.

Golden Rule / Method: £518 ÷ 4 → 518.00 ÷ 4 = £129.50 (Never write £129 r 2 ×)
Golden Rule 3: The Multiples Ladder for 2-Digit Divisors

When dividing by a 2-digit number (e.g. 16, 18, 24) without a calculator, always sketch a quick 5-step multiples ladder in your margin (×1, ×2, ×4, ×10, ×20) before starting. It prevents mental arithmetic freeze.

Golden Rule / Method: Multiples of 18: 18, 36, 72, 180, 360 → Instant chunking anchors

Core Skill 2: Dividing by 2-Digit Numbers Using Chunking & Multiples Ladders

Failsafe method for non-calculator division by numbers like 14, 16, 18, or 24.

WORKED METHOD: CHUNKING WITH MULTIPLES LADDER
Worked Calculation: 432 ÷ 18 (2-Digit Divisor)
Step A: Multiples Ladder in the Margin
1 × 18 = 18
2 × 18 = 36
4 × 18 = 72
10 × 18 = 180
20 × 18 = 360 (Double 10 × 18)
Step B: Subtract Big Known Chunks
Total: 432
− 360 (Chunk of 20 × 18)
Remaining: 72
− 72 (Chunk of 4 × 18)
Remaining: 0 ✓
STEP 1 Build Quick Margin Multiples
1×, 2×, 4×, 10×, 20×
Always double (18 → 36 → 72) and multiply by 10 (180 → 360). This takes under 15 seconds.
STEP 2 Subtract Chunk of 20
432 − 360 = 72 remaining
20 × 18 = 360. Subtracting 360 leaves only 72 left to divide.
STEP 3 Spot Final Chunk of 4
72 = 4 × 18
Our multiples ladder directly shows that 72 is exactly 4 × 18. Remainder is 0.
STEP 4 Combine the Two Chunks
20 + 4 = 24
Add the chunk counts together. Final quotient: 24 ✓
Subi's Assessor Golden Rule: Chunking Outsmarts Long Division

"Don't waste time trying to guess 18-times tables on the fly! The doubling multiples ladder (×1, ×2, ×4, ×10, ×20) lets you subtract huge friendly chunks (like 360) in seconds. It guarantees full method marks [M1]!"

Core Skill 3: Contextual Remainder Decision Matrix (Round Up vs Round Down)

Why Pearson Edexcel Tests Contextual Rounding

The mark scheme explicitly separates the [M1] Method Mark (for performing raw division, e.g. getting 8.875) from the [A1] Accuracy Mark (for applying human vocational reasoning to state 9 buses).

The Mathematical Rounding Trap (5/4 Rule)

Do NOT use pure math rounding rules! For example, 12.25 tins would round down to 12 in pure math, but in the real world 12 tins leaves 5 m² unpainted. Always ask: "Does this leave anything unfinished or anyone stranded?"

Sonya's Practical Exam Rule: Always State Both Raw Value and Rounded Decision

"Always write down the unrounded division result first (e.g. 142 ÷ 16 = 8.875 or 8 r 14) to bank your method mark [M1], followed by your rounded contextual sentence ('Therefore, 9 minibuses are required') to secure your accuracy mark [A1]!"

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1)

Vocational Context: Community Healthcare Shuttle Transport

A regional healthcare outpatient clinic needs to transport 142 patients to an off-site rehabilitation unit. Each patient shuttle minibus carries a maximum of 16 passengers.
(a) How many minibuses must be booked?
(b) How many empty seats will be on the final minibus?
Step & Focus
Visual Calculation Board & Method
Mark Criteria
Step 1 Divide total patients by minibus capacity Raw arithmetic division
Margin Multiples of 16
10 × 16 = 160 (Too high) 8 × 16 = 128 9 × 16 = 144
142 − 128 = 14 remainder
142 ÷ 16 = 8 remainder 14 (or 8.875)
Dividing 142 by 16 gives 8 full minibus loads with 14 patients still waiting.
[M1] Division process setup (142 ÷ 16)
Step 2 Apply contextual rounding rule Round UP decision
Booking 8 Minibuses:
8 × 16 = 128 seats → 14 patients stranded!
Booking 9 Minibuses:
9 × 16 = 144 seats → All 142 transported safely!
∴ Must round UP to book 9 minibuses.
[A1] Contextual rounding UP to 9 vehicles
Step 3 Calculate empty seats on the final vehicle Capacity vs patients
Total seating capacity: 9 × 16 = 144 seats
Empty seats: 144 − 142 = 2 empty seats
(Alternative check: 16 capacity − 14 passengers on bus 9 = 2 seats)
[B1] Correct empty seat count (2)
Final Accredited Answer: (a) 9 minibuses must be booked  |  (b) 2 empty seats on the final minibus
Assessor Method Checklist & Marking Guidance
In transport questions, examiners deduct marks if you write "8.875 buses". You cannot hire a fraction of a bus, and leaving passengers stranded scores zero marks. Always state the division calculation clearly to secure [M1] before writing the final whole integer [A1].

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Trap 1: Writing Decimal Remainders for Indivisible Items
Writing "8.875 minibuses" or "40.83 boxes" loses the final accuracy mark [A1] immediately. You cannot book 0.875 of a vehicle or ship 0.83 of a sealed box!
How to Avoid: Look at the unit! People, vehicles, and containers are indivisible — always round contextually to a whole number.
Trap 2: Writing Remainders Instead of Decimals for Money
Writing "£135 remainder 3" or "£135.5" forfeits marks. UK currency must always be calculated to 2 decimal places (£135.50).
How to Avoid: Place a decimal point and two zeros .00 under the bus stop as soon as you see a pound sign!
Trap 3: Blindly Using Mathematical Rounding Rules
When calculating paint or materials (e.g. 12.25 tins needed), mathematical rules round down to 12. But 12 tins leaves 5 m² of wall unpainted!
How to Avoid: Always ask: "Does this leave a job incomplete or someone stranded?" If yes, always round UP.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A medical supply depot produces 490 bottles of antiseptic spray. The bottles are packed into shipping cartons of 12 bottles each.
How many complete cartons can be shipped, and how many bottles are left over?
Marking Scheme Steps:
40 × 12 = 480 bottles
490 − 480 = 10 leftover bottles
490 ÷ 12 = 40 remainder 10
Final Correct Answer: 40 complete cartons (10 bottles left over)
Examiner Tip: The question specifically asks for complete cartons, so you must round DOWN to 40.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
Six colleagues win a syndicate lottery prize of £813 and split it equally. How much does each person receive? Give your answer in pounds and pence.
Marking Scheme Steps:
Short Division Setup: £813.00 ÷ 6
8 ÷ 6 = 1 r 2  →  21 ÷ 6 = 3 r 3  →  33 ÷ 6 = 5 r 3
3.0 ÷ 6 = .5  →  0 ÷ 6 = 0
Result = £135.50 ✓
Final Correct Answer: £135.50
Examiner Tip: Writing '£135.5' loses 1 accuracy mark. Standard UK currency format requires 2 decimal places with the trailing zero.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A decorator needs to tile a clinic treatment room wall with an area of 26 m². Tiles are sold in boxes that cover 1.5 m² each and cost £24.00 per box. Calculate the total cost of the boxes required.
Marking Scheme Steps:
Step 1 (Boxes needed): 26 ÷ 1.5 = 260 ÷ 15 = 17.333... boxes [M1]
Step 2 (Round UP): Must buy 18 full boxes (17 boxes only covers 25.5 m²) [M1]
Step 3 (Total Cost): 18 × £24.00 = £432.00 [A1]
Final Correct Answer: £432.00 (18 boxes)
Examiner Tip: Rounding down to 17 boxes leaves 0.5 m² of bare wall and loses 2 marks!
