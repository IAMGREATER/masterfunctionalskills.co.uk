Section A: Core Skills (Warm-Up) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Identifying Faces on a 2D Net) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A flat cardboard net consists of 1 square central base surrounded by 4 identical isosceles triangles attached to its four sides.
Name the 3D solid formed when this net is folded, and write down the number of vertices on the finished 3D solid.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify 3D solid from 1 square + 4 triangular faces: Square-based pyramid [M1]
2. Count vertices on folded solid (4 base corners + 1 apex): 5 vertices [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Square-based pyramid; 5 vertices
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Counting the 4 outer tips of the flat net as 4 separate apexes (they meet at 1 single apex when folded!).
🔄 Quick Check: 4 base corners + 1 top point = 5 vertices.
Question 2 (Calculating Overall Dimensions of a Unfolded Cuboid Net) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A closed medical vial box is a cuboid measuring length 12 cm, width 8 cm, and height 5 cm.
When unfolded into a standard cross-shaped net, the central strip consists of: Height (5 cm) + Length (12 cm) + Height (5 cm) + Length (12 cm).
Calculate the total length and total width of the flat cross-shaped net.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Total net length (4 panels in row): 5 + 12 + 5 + 12 = 34 cm [M1]
2. Total net width (central width 8 cm + two side flaps of 5 cm each): 5 + 8 + 5 = 18 cm [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Total length = 34 cm, Total width = 18 cm
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Forgetting that both the left and right side flaps add 5 cm each to the 8 cm central width.
🔄 Quick Check: 34 cm = 2×(12+5); 18 cm = 8 + 2×5.
Section B: Everyday & Workplace Maths Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Cardboard Area Required for a Closed Cuboid Net) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A sterile dressings box measures 20 cm long, 15 cm wide, and 10 cm high.
Calculate the total surface area of the 6-face net in cm².
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Top & Bottom faces: 2 × (20 × 15) = 2 × 300 = 600 cm² [M1]
2. Front/Back: 2 × (20 × 10) = 400 cm²; Left/Right: 2 × (15 × 10) = 300 cm² [M1]
3. Sum all 6 faces of the net: 600 + 400 + 300 = 1,300 cm² [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 1,300 cm²
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Calculating volume (20 × 15 × 10 = 3,000) instead of summing the 6 rectangular faces of the net.
🔄 Quick Check: 2 × (300 + 200 + 150) = 2 × 650 = 1,300 cm².
Question 4 (Open-Top Storage Tray Net Calculation) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A pharmacy shelf tray has no lid (open top) and measures 30 cm long, 20 cm wide, and 8 cm high.
The tray is stamped from a single rectangular sheet of plastic measuring 50 cm by 40 cm.
Calculate the area of plastic wasted after stamping out the 5-face net.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Area of 5-face open tray net: 1 Base (30×20 = 600) + 2 Long sides (2×30×8 = 480) + 2 Short sides (2×20×8 = 320) = 1,400 cm² [M1]
2. Area of rectangular plastic sheet: 50 cm × 40 cm = 2,000 cm² [P1]
3. Waste area: 2,000 - 1,400 = 600 cm² [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 600 cm²
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Including a second 30×20 top face when the question states the tray has an open top (no lid).
🔄 Quick Check: 1,400 cm² tray + 600 cm² waste = 2,000 cm² sheet.
Section C: Exam-Style Problem Solving Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Cylinder Net Dimensions & Surface Area) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A cylindrical sharps bin has a circular base of radius 10 cm and a vertical height of 25 cm.
Its net consists of two circles (top and bottom) and one curved rectangular wrap whose width equals the circumference of the circle (2 × π × r).
Using π = 3.14, calculate the total surface area of the cylinder net.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Area of two circular ends: 2 × (3.14 × 10²) = 2 × 314 = 628 cm² [P1]
2. Width of rectangular wrap (circumference): 2 × 3.14 × 10 = 62.8 cm [M1]
3. Area of rectangular wrap: 62.8 cm × 25 cm = 1,570 cm² [P1]
4. Total net surface area: 628 + 1,570 = 2,198 cm² [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 2,198 cm²
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Using diameter (20) instead of radius (10) in πr², or forgetting to double the circular area for both top and bottom.
🔄 Quick Check: 2πr(r + h) = 62.8 × (10 + 25) = 62.8 × 35 = 2,198 cm².
Question 6 (Cardboard Sheet Nesting & Cost Justification) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A medical packaging designer needs to stamp out 60 closed cube nets. Each cube has side length 10 cm.
Each unfolded cross-shaped net requires a bounding rectangle measuring 40 cm long by 30 cm wide on the stamping press.
Master cardboard sheets measure 120 cm by 90 cm and cost £3.50 per sheet.
The designer claims: 'We can stamp all 60 nets for no more than £25.00.'

Is the designer correct? Show full calculations to justify your answer.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Nets per master sheet: Along 120 cm = 120 ÷ 40 = 3; Along 90 cm = 90 ÷ 30 = 3 → 3 × 3 = 9 nets per sheet [P1]
2. Number of master sheets required for 60 nets: 60 ÷ 9 = 6.67 → Round UP to 7 whole sheets [M1]
3. Total cost of 7 sheets: 7 × £3.50 = £24.50 [P1]
4. Conclusion: Yes, the designer is correct because £24.50 is £0.50 below the £25.00 limit [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Yes — 9 nets fit per sheet, requiring 7 sheets at £3.50 = £24.50 (which is under £25.00).
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Multiplying 6.67 × £3.50 = £23.33 without rounding up to 7 whole master sheets first.
🔄 Quick Check: 7 sheets × 9 nets = 63 nets (covers 60 required); 7 × £3.50 = £24.50.
