Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.13 – Identify and distinguish between fact and opinion in workplace, instructional, and informative texts.
  • L2.14 – Recognise bias, detect opinions disguised as facts, and determine whether statements represent verifiable truth or subjective evaluation.

Golden Rules for Fact or Opinion

Golden Rule 1: Facts Can Be Objectively Verified by Evidence

A fact is verifiable using data, records, dates, measurements, or official statistics. If a statement can be checked against independent records and proven true or false, it is a fact — even if it sounds surprising.

Golden Rule / Technique: Test: Can an independent auditor, database, or measurement prove this true or false?
Golden Rule 2: Opinions Contain Subjective Value Judgments

Opinions reflect personal feelings, beliefs, predictions, or recommendations. Look out for evaluative adjectives ('superb', 'appalling', 'vital', 'unfair') and modal verbs ('should', 'ought to').

Golden Rule / Technique: Test: Could two reasonable people disagree on this word (e.g. 'reasonable', 'delicious')?

Core Skill 1: Spotting the Linguistic Clues for Facts vs Opinions

Key linguistic signals and verification tests used in vocational reading.

Feature Objective Fact Subjective Opinion
Proof Basis Dates, statistics, medical readings, test scores, legal statutes, timestamps. Personal perspectives, aesthetic impressions, moral judgments, unverified predictions.
Typical Vocabulary "recorded at 37.8°C", "exceeded 500 units", "founded in 1948", "contains 12g protein" "invaluable", "disappointing", "undoubtedly the finest", "unacceptable", "should be banned"
Agreement Test Universal once checked against official records. Varies from person to person depending on personal taste or priorities.

Core Skill 2: Separating Mixed Sentences (Fact + Opinion in One Sentence)

How Level 2 examiners hide a factual clause and an opinion clause inside the same sentence.

In Level 2 exams, writers rarely put facts and opinions in neat, separate sentences. Instead, they blend a verifiable statistic with an evaluative adjective in the same sentence. You must slice the sentence in half and quote only the relevant clause:

Mixed Sentence Example:

[FACT CLAUSE] "The council spent £1.4 million resurfacing High Street in March, [OPINION CLAUSE] which was a reckless waste of local taxpayers' money."

Exam Rule: If asked for a fact, quote "The council spent £1.4 million resurfacing High Street in March." Do NOT copy the second half of the sentence, or you will lose the mark for including an opinion!

Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Unmasking Opinions Disguised as Facts (Level 2 Critical Skill)

Recognize assertive introductory phrases and future predictions that masquerade as facts.

Persuasive writers often disguise their personal opinions using confident opener phrases or future claims:

  • False Fact Openers: Phrases like "It is an undeniable fact that...", "Everyone knows that...", or "Clearly, nobody can deny..." followed by a value judgment (e.g. "...remote working ruins team spirit") are still 100% opinions.
  • Unverified Future Predictions: A statement about the future (e.g., "Opening the new bypass will completely solve town centre traffic by next summer") cannot be proven true right now, making it an opinion/claim rather than an established fact.
  • Reported Survey Facts vs Direct Opinions: Note that "72% of surveyed patients rated the food as excellent" is a verifiable fact about what the survey recorded, whereas "The hospital food is excellent" is an opinion.
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 2 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Reading Level 1)

Vocational Context: Community Leisure Centre Promotion

Read the extract from the leisure centre leaflet:
'Riverside Gym opens at 6:00 a.m. seven days a week and features 40 cardiovascular machines. Our friendly personal trainers deliver the most inspiring fitness classes in the region.'
(a) Extract one fact from the leaflet. [1 Mark]
(b) Extract one opinion from the leaflet. [1 Mark]
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Extract a verifiable factual statement
'Riverside Gym opens at 6:00 a.m. seven days a week' (or 'features 40 cardiovascular machines'). Both can be checked and proven.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Fact]
Step 2 Extract the subjective opinion statement
'Our friendly personal trainers deliver the most inspiring fitness classes in the region.' ('friendly' and 'most inspiring' are personal judgments).
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Opinion]
Final Accredited Response: (a) Fact: 'Riverside Gym opens at 6:00 a.m. seven days a week' (or has 40 machines). | (b) Opinion: 'Our friendly personal trainers deliver the most inspiring fitness classes in the region.'
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
At Level 1, copy the exact sentence or clause cleanly without changing the words.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Treating Expert Quotes as Automatic Facts
Just because a senior doctor or chief executive says 'This is the best ward management app available', that does not make the claim itself a fact. It remains an informed professional opinion.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Ask: Is the statement an empirical measurement, or is it an expert's qualitative value judgment?
Examiner Pitfall: Copying a Whole Mixed Sentence for a Fact Question
If a sentence contains both a statistic and an emotive adjective ('The £50 ticket is a total rip-off') and you copy the entire sentence as a fact, you score 0 because 'total rip-off' is an opinion.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Trim your quotation so it includes only the factual clause.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A logistics fleet report states: 'The electric delivery vans cut depot fuel expenditure by £14,500 between January and June. They are undeniably the most stylish commercial vehicles on British roads today.'
Extract one fact and one opinion from this statement.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Fact: 'The electric delivery vans cut depot fuel expenditure by £14,500 between January and June.' (verifiable in accounting records) [1 Mark].
2. Opinion: 'They are undeniably the most stylish commercial vehicles on British roads today.' (subjective aesthetic judgment) [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Fact: Reduced fuel expenditure by £14,500 between January and June. | Opinion: Most stylish commercial vehicles on British roads today.
Examiner Tip: Do not be fooled by the word 'undeniably' — 'most stylish' is still a subjective opinion about appearance.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
Read this mixed sentence from a restaurant review:
'The bistro charges £18.50 for its two-course lunch menu, which is an outrageous price for such tiny portions.'
Separate this single sentence into one fact and one opinion.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Fact clause: 'The bistro charges £18.50 for its two-course lunch menu' (can be verified on the printed menu) [1 Mark].
2. Opinion clause: 'which is an outrageous price for such tiny portions' ('outrageous' and 'tiny' are subjective value judgments) [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Fact: The bistro charges £18.50 for its two-course lunch menu. | Opinion: It is an outrageous price for tiny portions.
Examiner Tip: Slice the mixed sentence cleanly at the comma so your fact contains zero evaluative adjectives.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
Read this extract from a campaign leaflet:
'It is a proven fact that the council's new 20 mph speed limit is a ridiculous inconvenience that will ruin every delivery driver's schedule.'
Explain why this statement is actually an opinion despite starting with 'It is a proven fact that...', and quote two subjective words or phrases as evidence. [3 Marks]
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Explanation: Starting a sentence with 'It is a proven fact' is a rhetorical assertion that does not make the claim verifiable; it also makes an unproven prediction about the future [1 Mark].
2. Subjective Quote 1: 'ridiculous inconvenience' (evaluative judgment) [1 Mark].
3. Subjective Quote 2: 'will ruin every delivery driver's schedule' (exaggerated future prediction) [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: The phrase 'It is a proven fact' is used persuasively to disguise a personal viewpoint. The quotes 'ridiculous inconvenience' and 'will ruin every delivery driver's schedule' are subjective judgments and unproven predictions.
Examiner Tip: Always ignore assertive openers like 'It is a fact that...' and test the actual words that follow!
