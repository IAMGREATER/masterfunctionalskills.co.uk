Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) and Online Banking — Answer Key

4. Transacting • Standard Code: L1.4.2
Style 1: Solutions (6 Drills)
Task 1 — Model Solution [2 Marks]

Which of the following is an example of 'Something you ARE' in biometric authentication?

A. Fingerprint scan or facial recognition (CORRECT)
B. A 4-digit card PIN
C. Your mother's maiden name
D. A USB memory stick
Tutor Diagnostic Explanation:

Biometrics evaluate unique physical human characteristics like fingerprints, retina, or facial geometry.

Task 2 — Model Solution [2 Marks]

What should you do if an authenticator app notification pops up on your phone asking you to 'Approve Login' when you are not currently logging in?

A. Click 'Deny' immediately and change your account password (CORRECT)
B. Click 'Approve' just in case
C. Turn off your phone battery
D. Ignore it and tell no one
Tutor Diagnostic Explanation:

An unexpected MFA prompt means someone has entered your password and is trying to access your account.

Task 3 — Model Solution [2 Marks]

Why is an SMS or Authenticator App code considered 'Something you HAVE'?

A. Because it requires physical possession of your registered mobile device (CORRECT)
B. Because you have to memorise it forever
C. Because you own the internet
D. Because it is stored in your computer RAM
Tutor Diagnostic Explanation:

Possession factors require holding the physical hardware device that generates or receives the dynamic code.

Task 4 — Model Solution [2 Marks]

When setting up Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA), why is a dedicated Authenticator App (such as Microsoft Authenticator) considered more secure than SMS text verification?

A. Authenticator apps are immune to SIM-swapping attacks and mobile network interception (CORRECT)
B. SMS text messages only work on desktop computers
C. Authenticator apps delete your password after every login
D. Mobile network operators charge £5 for every SMS code received
Tutor Diagnostic Explanation:

Criminals can execute SIM-swap fraud to divert SMS texts to a rogue SIM card. Authenticator apps generate time-based one-time passcodes (TOTP) locally on the device securely.

Task 5 — Model Solution [2 Marks]

What are the three common authentication factors used in digital identity verification systems?

A. Something you know (password), something you have (phone/token), and something you are (biometrics) (CORRECT)
B. Your name, your age, and your car registration
C. Your job title, your desk number, and your lunch hour
D. Your browser brand, your Wi-Fi name, and your screen size
Tutor Diagnostic Explanation:

True MFA requires combining at least two distinct categories: Knowledge (password/PIN), Possession (smartcard/authenticator token), and Inherence (fingerprint/facial scan).

Task 6 — Model Solution [2 Marks]

After completing online business banking transactions on a shared workplace computer, what two actions must you perform to secure the session?

A. Click 'Log Out' explicitly and close all browser windows (CORRECT)
B. Just close the browser tab without logging out
C. Leave the browser open on the screen so colleagues can check your work
D. Turn off the mouse only
Tutor Diagnostic Explanation:

Explicitly clicking 'Log Out' invalidates the session token on the bank's server. Closing the browser ensures temporary cache tokens cannot be hijacked by the next user.
