Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.1 / L2.3 – Communicate information, ideas, and opinions clearly and coherently in workplace contexts.
  • L2.5 – Use spoken techniques such as pitch, pace, tone, and pause to clarify and develop points effectively.

Golden Rules for Communicating Clearly, Coherently & Purposefully

Golden Rule 1: Master the Power of the Controlled 2-Second Pause

Rushing speech indicates nervousness and obscures technical instructions. Pause for 2 full seconds before stating major statistics or transitions; this commands authority and lets listeners process your point.

Golden Rule / Technique: Rule: 130–150 words per minute is the optimal professional speaking cadence.
Golden Rule 2: Apply the 4 Cs of Professional Spoken Delivery

Every spoken briefing must be Clear (unambiguous words), Concise (free from rambling tangents), Coherent (logical chronological order), and Courteous (respectful, composed tone).

Golden Rule / Technique: Clear • Concise • Coherent • Courteous

Core Skill 1: Four Physical Dimensions of Vocal & Non-Verbal Delivery

How assessors evaluate your physical delivery during formal presentations and briefings.

Projection & Clarity

Project from the diaphragm and enunciate word endings clearly so listeners at the back hear effortlessly without you shouting.

Pacing & Tempo

Maintain a steady 140 wpm. Slow down deliberately when delivering numbers, medication dosages, or safety deadlines.

Pitch & Emphasis

Avoid a flat monotone. Use downward vocal inflection at the end of statements to project confidence and certainty.

Non-Verbal Poise

Keep an open chest posture, uncrossed arms, purposeful hand gestures, and shared eye contact across all group members.

Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: The SBAR Framework for High-Stakes Spoken Handovers

Use Situation → Background → Assessment → Recommendation to structure any workplace update in 45 seconds.

SBAR Stage Purpose Spoken Example
S — Situation State immediately what the issue is and where. "I am calling regarding a walk-in chiller failure in the main kitchen."
B — Background Provide brief context (when it started, what was checked). "The temperature alarm sounded at 07:30, and the digital gauge currently reads 9°C instead of 4°C."
A — Assessment State the risk or operational impact. "We have £600 of fresh dairy and meat stock that will spoil if left above 8°C for two hours."
R — Recommendation State the exact action taken and what you need now. "We have moved all high-risk stock to Chiller 2; please dispatch an emergency refrigeration engineer before noon."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Eliminating Verbal Fillers & Crutch Words

How to replace 'um', 'like', 'basically', and 'you know' with composed silence.

Filler-Heavy Delivery (Sounds Unprepared & Hesitant):

"Um, basically what I wanted to say is, like, the car park is sort of dangerous at night, you know, because the lights don't really work properly."

Composed, Purposeful Delivery (Clear & Authoritative):

"Our primary safety concern is the staff car park after 17:00. [2-second pause] Currently, three of the four overhead floodlights are out of order, leaving evening-shift staff walking in complete darkness."
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 Level 1 Clarity & Coherence Benchmark (L1.1, L1.3)

Vocational Context: Residential Care Home Shift Changeover

Deliver a clear 45-second spoken handover informing the incoming afternoon care assistant that Resident Mrs Patel (Room 14) had a minor uninjured fall yesterday, requires a two-person walking transfer until 15:00, and has an updated chart on the tablet.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Identify the resident, room number, and background clearly
'Before we start the afternoon round, please note an important update for Mrs Patel in Room 14, who had a minor uninjured slip yesterday evening.'
Criteria: Clear Context (L1.1)
Step 2 State the exact mandatory safety instruction
'Until the physiotherapist reviews her at 15:00 today, Mrs Patel requires two care assistants to support her whenever she transfers from her chair to her walking frame.'
Criteria: Unambiguous Instruction (L1.3)
Step 3 Confirm documentation location
'I have logged her morning observations on the ward tablet under Room 14. Do you have any questions before we begin?'
Criteria: Coherent Close
Final Accredited Response: 'Before we start the afternoon round, please note an important update for Mrs Patel in Room 14, who had a minor uninjured slip yesterday evening. Until the physiotherapist reviews her at 15:00 today, she requires two care assistants to support her whenever she transfers to her walking frame. I have updated her mobility chart on the ward tablet.'
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
In healthcare handovers, stating Who (Mrs Patel, Room 14), Why (minor fall yesterday), and What action (2-person assist until 15:00) in chronological order prevents errors.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 Level 2 Delivery & Vocal Technique Benchmark (L2.3, L2.5)

Vocational Context: Logistics & Warehousing Operations

Deliver a 60-second verbal briefing to forklift operators regarding newly designated pedestrian safety walkways in Bay 3, indicating where vocal emphasis and pauses should be used.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Opening Hook & Clear Purpose
'Good morning team. Please give me your full attention for sixty seconds on a critical safety update regarding Loading Bay 3.'
Criteria: Establishes Immediate Authority (L2.3)
Step 2 Key Factual Directive with Vocal Stress & Pause
'As of 06:00 today, the yellow cross-hatched lanes are strictly pedestrian-only. [Pause for 2 seconds] Forklift trucks must not cross or park on these lines under any circumstances.'
Criteria: Pace, Stress & Pause Control (L2.5)
Step 3 Actionable Consequence & Sign-Off
'Breaching this walkway zone triggers an automatic HSE incident review. Please sign the briefing register on the clipboard before collecting your vehicle keys. Thank you.'
Criteria: Concise & Purposeful Close
Final Accredited Response: 'Good morning team. Please give me your full attention for sixty seconds on a critical safety update regarding Loading Bay 3. As of 06:00 today, the yellow cross-hatched lanes are strictly pedestrian-only. [2-second pause] Forklift trucks must not cross or park on these lines under any circumstances. Breaching this policy triggers an automatic HSE incident review. Please sign the briefing register before collecting your keys.'
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Notice how pausing for 2 seconds right after 'strictly pedestrian-only' makes the safety rule sink in immediately.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Verbal Tics and Crutch Words ('Um', 'Like', 'Basically', 'Sort Of')
Repeating 'like' or 'basically' every five words weakens your authority and distracts the assessor from the substance of your argument.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Slow your speaking pace by 15%. Whenever you need to think of your next word, close your mouth and take a calm breath instead of saying 'um'.
Examiner Pitfall: Trailing Off at the End of Sentences
Ending statements with '...so yeah, that's basically it' or letting your voice drop to a mumble makes your conclusion sound weak.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Finish every speaking turn with a complete, crisp sentence or a direct handover question.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Rewrite this rambling, filler-heavy spoken announcement so it is clear, concise, and professional for a GP surgery waiting room:
'Um, excuse me everyone, basically Dr Evans is running like half an hour late this morning because there was an emergency callout, so yeah, sorry about that, if you can't wait just tell the desk.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Remove all filler words ('Um', 'basically', 'like', 'so yeah').
2. Structure clearly: Polite greeting → Factual reason & 30-minute delay → Actionable option at reception.
Final Model Answer: 'Good morning everyone, may I have your attention please. Due to an urgent medical callout, Dr Evans's morning clinic is currently running approximately thirty minutes behind schedule. We sincerely apologise for the delay; if you are unable to wait, please speak to our reception team to rebook your appointment for a convenient time.'
Examiner Tip: Always state the exact delay time ('thirty minutes') and give listeners a clear next step.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Use the 4-step SBAR framework (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation) to write a 50-word spoken telephone report to your IT Helpdesk explaining that the main reception card-payment terminal has frozen.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Situation: Identify yourself and state that the main reception PDQ card machine has frozen.
2. Background: Explain that it displayed 'Error 404' at 09:15 and restarting the router did not fix it.
3. Assessment: Customers cannot pay by card and queues are building.
4. Recommendation: Request an urgent replacement handset from IT stores.
Final Model Answer: 'Hello IT Support, this is Sam at Main Reception. Our primary card-payment terminal has frozen completely. It displayed Error 404 at 09:15 this morning, and rebooting the terminal has not resolved the fault. Consequently, we cannot process customer card payments and a queue is building. Could a technician bring out the spare replacement terminal within the next fifteen minutes please?'
Examiner Tip: SBAR works in every industry—it gives your listener all the facts they need in four logical sentences.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
Look at this sentence from a formal presentation:
'Last year, avoidable packaging waste cost our distribution centre forty-two thousand pounds—enough to fund two full-time apprenticeships.'
Explain two specific vocal techniques (pace, pause, volume, or emphasis) you would use when speaking this sentence aloud to maximise its impact on the audience.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Technique 1 (Vocal Emphasis & Slowed Pace): Slow down and stress the words 'forty-two thousand pounds' to highlight the scale of the financial loss.
2. Technique 2 (Deliberate Pause): Pause for two full seconds at the dash before delivering 'enough to fund two full-time apprenticeships'.
Final Model Answer: 1. Slow the speaking pace and place strong vocal stress on each word of 'forty-two thousand pounds' so the financial figure stands out clearly.
2. Insert a deliberate two-second pause at the dash before delivering the comparison ('enough to fund two full-time apprenticeships') to let the audience absorb the human impact.
Examiner Tip: Numbers and statistics always land harder when you slow down and pause right after stating them.
