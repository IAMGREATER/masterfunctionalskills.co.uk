Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • L1.11 / L2.11 – Calculate direct proportion and scaling in vocational situations.
  • L2.11 – Understand and solve problems involving inverse proportion (e.g. workers and time).

Golden Rules for Direct & Inverse Proportion

Golden Rule 1: The Unitary Method for Direct Proportion

Find the value of ONE unit first by dividing, then multiply by the required quantity. If 6 uniforms cost £150, 1 uniform costs £25; 10 uniforms cost £250.

Golden Rule / Method: Total ÷ Known Quantity = 1 Unit → 1 Unit × Desired Quantity
Golden Rule 2: Inverse Proportion: More Workers = Less Time

When workers increase, the time taken DECREASES proportionally. Multiply workers by hours to get total 'person-hours', then divide by the new workforce.

Golden Rule / Method: Total Person-Hours = Workers × Time → New Time = Total ÷ New Workers

Core Skill 1: Skill 1: Direct vs Inverse Proportion Comparison Matrix

Recognising whether values increase together or move in opposite directions.

Feature Direct Proportion Inverse Proportion
Relationship As one quantity increases, the other increases at the same rate. As one quantity increases, the other decreases at the same rate.
Classic Scenarios Catering recipes, fuel consumption, currency exchange, hourly wages. Staffing a ward clean, building a wall, pumps emptying a tank.
Core Formula Divide to find 1 unit, then Multiply up. Multiply to find total work, then Divide by new team size.

Core Skill 2: Skill 2: Recipe & Ingredient Limiting-Factor Problems

Checking whether available store cupboard stock is enough for a scaled recipe.

To find the maximum number of portions you can make from fixed stock (e.g. recipe for 12 scones needs 300 g flour; you have 1,250 g flour):

  1. Find flour per 1 scone: 300 g ÷ 12 = 25 g per scone.
  2. Divide available flour by 1-scone weight: 1,250 g ÷ 25 g = 50 scones maximum.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Skill 3: Multi-Variable Workforce & Machine Rate Scaling

Calculating output when both machine count and operating hours change.

If 3 printers produce 1,800 leaflets in 2 hours, how many leaflets will 5 printers produce in 4 hours?

  • 1 printer in 2 hours: 1,800 ÷ 3 = 600 leaflets.
  • 1 printer in 1 hour: 600 ÷ 2 = 300 leaflets per printer-hour.
  • 5 printers in 4 hours: 300 × 5 × 4 = 6,000 leaflets.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1)

Vocational Context: Healthcare Catering Production

A hospital canteen recipe for 15 portions of vegetable soup requires 450 grams of carrots and 1.2 litres of stock.
Calculate the weight of carrots (in grams) and volume of stock (in litres) needed to make 40 portions.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Find the multiplier or unitary amount for 1 portion
Carrots for 1 portion = 450 g ÷ 15 = 30 g.
Stock for 1 portion = 1.2 L ÷ 15 = 0.08 L.
Criteria: M1: Unitary division by 15
Step 2 Multiply carrots by 40 portions
30 g × 40 = 1,200 grams of carrots.
Criteria: A1: Accurate carrot mass (1,200 g)
Step 3 Multiply stock by 40 portions
0.08 L × 40 = 3.2 litres of stock.
Criteria: A1: Accurate stock volume (3.2 L)
Final Accredited Answer: Carrots = 1,200 g | Stock = 3.2 litres
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Always find 1 portion first (450 ÷ 15 = 30 g) so you avoid awkward fractions.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2 Problem Solving)

Vocational Context: Logistics Hub Operations

It takes 6 warehouse operatives working at the same pace 3 hours and 20 minutes to unload and scan a freight container.
On Friday, the container arrives at 13:30 and must be completely unloaded by 16:00.
What is the minimum number of warehouse operatives needed to finish on time?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Convert original duration to minutes and find total person-minutes
3 hours 20 mins = (3 × 60) + 20 = 200 minutes.
Total workload = 6 operatives × 200 mins = 1,200 person-minutes.
Criteria: M1: Inverse proportion total effort in person-minutes
Step 2 Calculate available time window on Friday
From 13:30 to 16:00 = 2 hours 30 minutes = 150 minutes.
Criteria: M1: Available time calculation
Step 3 Divide total person-minutes by available minutes
1,200 person-minutes ÷ 150 minutes = 8 operatives.
Criteria: M1: Inverse proportion division
Step 4 State final staffing conclusion
8 warehouse operatives are required (2 additional operatives above the standard team of 6).
Criteria: A1: Accurate workforce requirement (8 operatives)
Final Accredited Answer: 8 warehouse operatives required (total workload is 1,200 person-minutes ÷ 150 available minutes).
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Converting hours and minutes into total minutes (200 mins and 150 mins) avoids recurring decimals like 3.333 hours!

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Applying Direct Proportion to Staffing Problems
Calculating 6 ÷ 4 × 6 = 9 hours when increasing from 4 to 6 workers is a catastrophic error because adding workers should speed up the work, not make it take longer!
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always ask: 'Should more workers take more time or less time?'

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A hospital kitchen recipe for 8 resident portions requires 600 grams of minced beef. How many grams of beef are required to prepare 20 resident portions?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. 1 portion = 600 ÷ 8 = 75 grams.
2. 20 portions = 75 × 20 = 1,500 grams (or 1.5 kg).
Final Correct Answer: 1,500 g (or 1.5 kg)
Examiner Tip: Find the amount for 1 portion first using the unitary method.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
It takes 5 industrial pumps 12 hours to drain a flooded basement. How long would it take 8 identical pumps to drain the same basement? Give your answer in hours and minutes.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Total pump-hours = 5 × 12 = 60 pump-hours.
2. Time for 8 pumps = 60 ÷ 8 = 7.5 hours.
3. 0.5 hours = 30 minutes → 7 hours 30 minutes.
Final Correct Answer: 7 hours 30 minutes (7.5 hours)
Examiner Tip: Remember that 0.5 hours is 30 minutes, NOT 50 minutes.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A delivery van travels 210 miles on 17.5 litres of diesel. How many litres of diesel will the van need for a journey of 336 miles at the same consumption rate?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Miles per litre = 210 ÷ 17.5 = 12 miles per litre.
2. Litres needed for 336 miles = 336 ÷ 12 = 28 litres.
Final Correct Answer: 28 litres
Examiner Tip: Dividing miles by litres (210 ÷ 17.5 = 12 miles/L) gives a clean whole number!
