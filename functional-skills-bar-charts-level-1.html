Welcome to the Bar Charts: Dual, Paired & Stacked (Composite) blueprint. In functional exams, bar charts test three skills: reading single and paired category bars accurately from calibrated scales, subtracting segment boundaries on stacked charts, and calculating percentage change between periods. Follow our clean step-down calculation cards below!

Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • L1.25 – Draw and interpret single, dual (grouped), and composite (stacked) bar charts with appropriate titles, axis labels, keys, and linear scales.

Golden Rules for Bar Charts: Dual, Component & Frequency

Golden Rule 1: Follow S-L-A-P-T Every Time You Draw a Bar Chart

Scale (even linear steps starting at 0) • Labels (both X and Y axes labelled with units) • Axes (ruled straight) • Points/Bars (accurate heights, equal widths, equal gaps) • Title & Key (clear chart title plus a legend/key for dual or stacked bars).

Golden Rule / Method: Scale • Labels • Axes • Bars (Equal Gaps) • Title & Key (SLAPT)
Golden Rule 2: Stacked (Component) Bars Require Subtraction for Top Segments

In a stacked bar chart, the bottom section is read directly from 0, but upper sections sit on top of lower sections. To find an upper section’s value: Top of Segment − Bottom of Segment.

Golden Rule / Method: Stacked Segment Value = Top Reading − Bottom Reading

Core Skill 1: Single vs Dual (Grouped) vs Component (Stacked) Bar Charts

How to read and draw the three bar chart formats tested in exams.

Single Bar Chart LEVEL 1

One isolated bar per category with equal widths and equal gaps between every bar.

Read bar height straight across to vertical axis
Dual (Grouped) Bar Chart LEVEL 1 & 2

Bars touch side-by-side in pairs. Requires a shaded Key (Legend) and clear gaps between groups.

Compare paired bars side-by-side; check key carefully
Component (Stacked) Bar LEVEL 1 FOCUS

Segments stack to form a total column. Read the top of segment − bottom of segment.

NEVER read mid-segment from 0! Subtract bottom line.
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Choosing a Sensible Linear Scale for the Vertical Axis

Avoiding uneven jumps and awkward grid subdivisions.

Always start the vertical frequency axis at 0 and go up in clean multiples of 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, or 100:

  • Never just copy the table numbers (e.g. 14, 29, 38, 65) unevenly up the axis—that loses the scale mark immediately!
  • If 1 major grid square (5 small squares) represents 10 units, then each small square = 10 ÷ 5 = 2 units.
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Calculating Percentage Changes & Ratios from Bar Heights

Combining bar chart extraction with percentage calculations.

After reading two bar heights (e.g. Q1 = 80 units, Q2 = 100 units):

  • Percentage Increase: ((New − Original) ÷ Original) × 100 = ((100 − 80) ÷ 80) × 100 = 25%.
  • Ratio in Simplest Form: 80 : 100 → divide both by 20 = 4 : 5.
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1/2)

Vocational Context: Surgical Theatre Performance Reporting

A dual bar chart compares Scheduled vs Emergency operations. In October, Scheduled is 85 and Emergency is 40. In November, Scheduled is 70 and Emergency is 55.
(a) Compare the total number of operations in October and November.
(b) Calculate the percentage increase in Emergency operations from October to November.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Sum Scheduled + Emergency for each month
October Total: 85 + 40 = 125 operations
November Total: 70 + 55 = 125 operations
Both months had identical totals (125 operations).
Criteria: M1: Summing paired bars (125 each)
Step 2 Find the increase in Emergency operations
Increase = 55 − 40 = 15 operations
Criteria: M1: Difference calculation (15)
Step 3 Divide by October original (40) and multiply by 100
(15 ÷ 40) × 100 = 37.5%
Criteria: A1: Accurate percentage increase (37.5%)
Final Accredited Answer: (a) Equal total of 125 operations in both months | (b) 37.5% increase
Sonya’s Examiner Tip Examiner Method
Always divide the change (15) by the ORIGINAL starting month (40), never the new month (55).

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Forgetting to Subtract the Lower Segment in a Stacked Bar Chart
Reading 27 tonnes instead of 27 − 12 = 15 tonnes for the middle segment.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always do: Top of Coloured Block − Bottom of Coloured Block.
Examiner Pitfall: Omitting the Key/Legend on a Dual or Stacked Bar Chart
Drawing two sets of bars without a key showing which shading represents which group loses 1 mark automatically.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always draw a small box labelled "Key" in the corner of your chart.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
On a bar chart vertical axis, major grid lines are labelled 0, 20, 40, 60, 80, with 4 equal subdivisions between each major label. A bar reaches 3 subdivisions above 40. What value does this bar represent?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Value of 1 subdivision = (20 − 0) ÷ 4 = 5 units.
2. Bar height = 40 + (3 × 5) = 40 + 15 = 55.
Final Correct Answer: 55
Examiner Tip: Divide the jump (20) by the number of spaces (4) to get 5 per line.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
A bar chart shows clinic attendances over 4 days: Monday: 42, Tuesday: 56, Wednesday: 38, Thursday: 60. Calculate the mean daily attendance across the 4 days.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Total = 42 + 56 + 38 + 60 = 196 patients.
2. Mean = 196 ÷ 4 = 49 patients per day.
Final Correct Answer: 49 patients per day
Examiner Tip: Extract all four bar heights first, sum them, and divide by 4.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
In a component (stacked) bar chart for staff transport, a bar reaches a total height of 80 staff. Car drivers occupy 0 to 44, Bus users occupy 44 to 68, and Cyclists occupy 68 to 80. How many more staff travel by Bus than by Bicycle?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Bus users = 68 − 44 = 24 staff.
2. Cyclists = 80 − 68 = 12 staff.
3. Difference = 24 − 12 = 12 more staff.
Final Correct Answer: 12 more staff
Examiner Tip: Find the size of each stacked segment by subtracting its bottom reading from its top reading.
