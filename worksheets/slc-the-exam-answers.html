Speaking, Listening & Communicating Exam Strategy & Grading Criteria Pearson Edexcel: Pearson Edexcel Functional Skills English (SLC)
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Foundational Concept Check Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Pearson Edexcel SLC Assessment Components & Level 1 vs Level 2 Tasks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
State the two mandatory assessed activities you must complete to pass Pearson Edexcel Functional Skills Speaking, Listening & Communicating at Level 2, and explain why reading a speech word-for-word from a full script is penalised.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Two L2 tasks: Task 1 = Formal Presentation + Q&A (approx. 4–5 mins + Q&A); Task 2 = Formal Group Discussion (approx. 10–15 mins with 3+ participants) [A1]
2. Script penalty: Reading word-for-word destroys eye contact and natural spoken intonation (use bulleted cue cards instead) [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Tasks: 1. Formal Presentation with Q&A; 2. Formal Group Discussion. Reading a full script blocks eye contact and natural spoken engagement—use bullet-point cue cards instead.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Turning up to the Presentation without preparing to answer audience questions afterwards.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Presentation + Q&A + Group Discussion = complete SLC assessment.
Question 2 (Core Skill Application — 3 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
State the three observable actions you must complete during the Task 2 Group Discussion to ensure Criterion L2.7 (respecting turn-taking rights and moving discussion forward) is ticked off by your assessor.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Action 1: Never interrupt or talk over a peer while they are speaking. [M1]
2. Action 2: Use a verbal bridge referencing the previous speaker's name and point. [M1]
3. Action 3: Actively invite a quieter participant into the conversation with an open question (and help summarise agreement). [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 1. Allow peers to finish their sentences without interrupting.
2. Use a verbal bridge that references a peer's point by name ('Building on Marcus's point...').
3. Actively invite a quieter group member to contribute with an open question and help summarise consensus.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Even if you score 90% in your Reading and Writing exams, Ofqual regulations state that your Functional Skills English certificate cannot be issued until your SLC Pass is logged.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Memorise these three actions as your non-negotiable discussion checklist.
Section B: Functional Workplace Application Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Model 1 (Level 1): Pre-Assessment 10-Minute Readiness Routine) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
How should a candidate use the final 10 minutes before entering the SLC assessment room (or joining the video call) to guarantee peak performance?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Audit Cue Cards & Notepad (Minutes 1–4): Check that your 3 or 4 presentation cue cards are numbered in order, with key statistics highlighted in yellow, and draw your 2-column listening grid on a blank notepad page. [A1]
2. Vocal & Breathing Warm-Up (Minutes 5–7): Take three slow diaphragmatic breaths, sip room-temperature water, and quietly rehearse your opening hook sentence. [A1]
3. Learn Your Group Members' First Names (Minutes 8–10): Introduce yourself warmly to your 3 or 4 fellow candidates and write their first names in seating order at the top of your notepad. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 1. Number cue cards & draw 2-column listening grid | 2. Slow breathing + rehearse opening sentence | 3. Note down fellow candidates' names in seating order so you can address them by name during the discussion.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Prepare your 4-minute presentation cue cards and discussion phrases with the same care as a job interview.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Writing down your group members' names in seating order at the top of your notepad is a game-changer—it lets you say 'Building on Sarah's point...' effortlessly.
Question 4 (Exam-Style Task — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A candidate brings a 600-word typed essay into their Level 2 Formal Presentation and reads it aloud word-for-word without looking up. Explain which two SLC assessment criteria this candidate is at high risk of failing and how they should prepare their notes instead.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify Criterion L2.3 (Communicate clearly and effectively in speech) and L2.5 (Use spoken techniques such as pitch, pace, eye contact, and natural delivery). [M1]
2. Explain the fix: Convert the essay into 4 small index cue cards with 3–4 bold bullet-point keywords per card. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: At-risk criteria: L2.3 (effective spoken communication) and L2.5 (using spoken techniques and audience engagement), because reading verbatim produces a flat monotone and zero eye contact.
Fix: Condense the script onto four numbered index cue cards containing only short bullet-point keywords and statistics.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Turning to look at the examiner to see if they are nodding breaks the natural group dynamic and makes you look self-conscious.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Assessors are grading your SPEAKING skills, not your ability to read an essay aloud!
Section C: Pearson Edexcel Exam-Style Analysis & Evaluation Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Model 2 (Level 2): Self-Auditing a Candidate's Performance Against the Witness Sheet) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
During a 12-minute Level 2 group discussion, Candidate Jordan spoke four times, cited two statistics, and did not interrupt anyone, yet the assessor flagged a borderline concern on Criterion L2.2. Explain why Jordan was at risk on L2.2 and how to fix it in the final two minutes.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Check the exact requirement of Criterion L2.2: Criterion L2.2 requires the candidate to 'make requests and ask detailed questions using appropriate language'. [A1]
2. Diagnose why Jordan's performance was incomplete: Although Jordan made strong statements and cited statistics (meeting L2.3, L2.4, and L2.6), Jordan never asked a question to another participant! [A1]
3. Deliver the immediate fix before the timer ends: In the final two minutes, Jordan must ask an open probing or collaborative question to a peer (e.g. 'Elena, how would we measure the success of this trial after three months?'). [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Diagnosis: Jordan met L2.3 and L2.4 by making evidenced statements, but failed to ask any detailed questions (L2.2). Fix: Before the discussion ends, Jordan must ask at least two open probing questions to peers or during the presentation Q&A.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Pretend the assessor is not in the room—keep 100% of your eye contact on your fellow group participants.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Never finish a discussion having only made statements! You MUST ask at least two open questions so the assessor can tick box L1.2 / L2.2.
Question 6 (Level 2 Mastery Challenge — 3 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Your SLC assessment is taking place online via Microsoft Teams tomorrow morning. List three practical technical and presentation checks you should perform 15 minutes before the call begins.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Check 1 (Camera & Lighting): Position camera at eye level in a well-lit, quiet room so your face and non-verbal nodding are clearly visible on the recording. [M1]
2. Check 2 (Audio & Screen-Share): Test microphone clarity and verify that your 3–4 presentation slides share cleanly. [M1]
3. Check 3 (Cue Cards & Notepad): Place physical cue cards and a blank notepad + pen next to your keyboard so your eyes stay near the webcam. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 1. Position the webcam at eye level in a quiet, well-lit room so the assessor can see your eye contact and non-verbal listening cues.
2. Test microphone audio and practise screen-sharing your presentation slides in advance.
3. Place physical bullet-point cue cards and a 2-column listening notepad on your desk so you do not read off the screen.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Failing to proofread spelling, punctuation, and grammar after completing the draft.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: On video calls, sticking your cue card right beside your webcam lens makes it look like you are maintaining direct eye contact with everyone!
