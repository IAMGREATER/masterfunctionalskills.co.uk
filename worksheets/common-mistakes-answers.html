Top 10 High-Frequency English Exam Mistakes & Fixes Pearson Edexcel: Pearson Edexcel Functional Skills English (READING)
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Foundational Concept Check Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Eliminating Comma Splices & Informal Slang) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Fix the comma splice and informal slang in this draft complaint sentence:
'The delivery van turned up mega late on Tuesday, it totally messed up our ward stock count.'
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Replace comma splice with a subordinating conjunction ('which'/'because') or semicolon [A1]
2. Upgrade informal slang ('turned up mega late', 'totally messed up') to formal register ('arrived significantly late', 'disrupted') [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: The delivery van arrived significantly late on Tuesday, which severely disrupted our ward stock count.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Upgrading the vocabulary while leaving the comma splice between the two main clauses.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Verify no comma separates two standalone sentences without a conjunction.
Question 2 (Core Skill Application — 3 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Correct the three SPaG errors in this sentence:
'The doctors surgery was closed on wednesday, all emergency patients was directed to A&E.'
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Apostrophe of possession: 'doctors surgery' must be 'doctor's surgery' (or 'doctors' surgery') [1 Mark]. [M1]
2. Capital letter for day of the week: 'wednesday' must be 'Wednesday' [1 Mark]. [M1]
3. Comma splice and verb agreement: Replace the comma with a full stop (or '; therefore,') and change 'was directed' to 'were directed' [1 Mark]. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'The doctor's surgery was closed on Wednesday. All emergency patients were directed to A&E.'
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: This classic error is penalised in every exam series. Remember the 'S' rule: never put two S's together (Sir + Sincerely = Wrong!).
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Always check plural subjects ('patients') to ensure they are paired with plural verbs ('were', not 'was').
Section B: Functional Workplace Application Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Diagnosing a Flawed Reading Exam Answer (Level 1/2)) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
An exam source text states: 'Over 92% of dental nurses reported back strain before ergonomic stools were introduced. Are you still risking your spine on outdated seating?'
The exam question asks: 'Give one language feature used in this extract and explain its effect. [2 Marks]'
A candidate writes: 'Bold font — it catches the reader's eye and makes them read on.'
Explain two reasons why this candidate scored 0 marks, and write a full-mark model answer.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Diagnose Mistake 1 (Presentational vs Language): 'Bold font' is a presentational/layout device, not a language feature. The candidate needed to choose the statistic ('92%') or the rhetorical question ('Are you still risking your spine...?'). [A1]
2. Diagnose Mistake 2 (Generic Effect Phrase) & Write Model Answer: 'Catches the reader's eye and makes them read on' is a vague cliché that earns 0 marks. Model Answer: Rhetorical question ('Are you still risking your spine on outdated seating?') — directly challenges the reader to reflect on their own health and creates urgency to upgrade their seating. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Mistakes: 1. Named a layout feature (bold font) instead of a language feature. 2. Gave a vague cliché ('makes them read on'). | Full-Mark Answer: Rhetorical question ('Are you still risking your spine...?') — prompts dental staff to worry about their own posture and persuades them to switch to ergonomic stools.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Named recipient (Dear Mr Smith) = Yours sincerely. Unnamed recipient (Dear Sir/Madam) = Yours faithfully.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Whenever a question asks for a 'language feature', banned answers include bullet points, bold text, subheadings, pictures, and text boxes.
Question 4 (Exam-Style Task — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Identify and correct three errors in this workplace email sentence:
'Their is no excuse for arriving late, staff could of checked the train timetable on they're phones.'
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Homophone 1: 'Their is' should be 'There is' [1 Mark]. [M1]
2. Comma splice & modal verb: Replace comma after 'late' with a full stop or 'because', and change 'could of' to 'could have' [1 Mark]. [M1]
3. Homophone 2: 'they're phones' (they are) should be possessive 'their phones' [1 Mark]. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'There is no excuse for arriving late; staff could have checked the train timetable on their phones.'
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: When a Level 2 task specifies 'Write 250–300 words', writing only 140 words severely restricts your ability to develop detailed points and caps your composition mark.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Test 'they're' by expanding it to 'they are' — 'on they are phones' makes no sense, proving you need 'their'.
Section C: Pearson Edexcel Exam-Style Analysis & Evaluation Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Repairing Comma Splices, Homophones & Letter Sign-Offs (Level 2)) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Read this closing extract from a candidate's formal letter to an unnamed Council Environmental Health Officer (opened with 'Dear Sir/Madam'):
'The fire exits in warehouse B was blocked by wooden pallets, this is a serious hazard, the manager should of cleared there walkways immediately. Yours sincerely, J. Patel.'
Identify and correct four distinct errors in grammar, punctuation, spelling, or letter convention. [4 Marks]
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Fix Subject-Verb Agreement: 'The fire exits... was blocked' -> plural subject ('exits') requires plural verb 'were blocked'. [A1]
2. Eliminate Comma Splices: Replace the commas joining independent clauses with full stops or semicolons: '...wooden pallets. This is a serious hazard; therefore, the manager...' [A1]
3. Fix Non-Standard Modal & Homophone: 'should of' -> 'should have'; 'there walkways' -> possessive 'their walkways'. [A1]
4. Fix Formal Letter Sign-Off Convention: Because the letter opened with 'Dear Sir/Madam', the sign-off must be 'Yours faithfully', not 'Yours sincerely'. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'The fire exits in Warehouse B were blocked by wooden pallets. This is a serious hazard; the manager should have cleared their walkways immediately. Yours faithfully, J. Patel.'
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Aim squarely for the target word band by writing 4 well-developed paragraphs (Introduction, 2 Main Body Paragraphs, Conclusion).
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: If a group of words can stand alone as a complete sentence, never join it to the next complete sentence with just a comma.
Question 6 (Level 2 Mastery Challenge — 2 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Read this short text:
'Warning: Forklift trucks operate in Aisle 6. Wear high-visibility jackets at all times.'
A student is asked to identify one language feature and writes: 'The word Warning is in bold.' Explain why this is wrong and name one correct language feature from the extract.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Why it is wrong: 'Bold' is a presentational/layout device, not a language feature [1 Mark]. [M1]
2. Correct language feature: Imperative command verb ('Wear high-visibility jackets...') or direct, cautionary vocabulary ('Warning') [1 Mark]. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Bold font is a presentational feature, not a language feature. A correct language feature is the imperative verb command ('Wear high-visibility jackets at all times').
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Failing to proofread spelling, punctuation, and grammar after completing the draft.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Imperative verbs ('Wear', 'Keep', 'Do not enter') are the most common language feature in safety notices.
