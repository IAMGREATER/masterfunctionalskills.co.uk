Welcome to the Decimal Place Value, Operations & Currency Precision blueprint. Decimals represent fractional parts of a whole grouped by powers of ten: tenths (0.1), hundredths (0.01), and thousandths (0.001). In Functional Skills exams, keeping decimal points strictly aligned during addition and subtraction is essential for full marks.

Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • E3.5 / L1.10 – Read, write, order, and compare decimals up to two decimal places in practical contexts such as money and metric length.
  • E3.5 / L1.6 – Round decimal currency and measurements to the nearest whole unit or tenth.

Golden Rules for Decimal Basics: Tenths, Hundredths & Money

Golden Rule 1: The Decimal Point Separates Whole Units from Tenths & Hundredths

In £4.75, the 4 is whole pounds, the 7 is tenths (7 × 10p = 70p), and the 5 is hundredths (5 × 1p = 5p). Single-digit pence from 1p to 9p MUST have a zero in the tenths column (e.g. 6p = £0.06, NEVER £0.6!).

Golden Rule / Method: Pounds . Tenths (10p) Hundredths (1p) • 6p = £0.06
Golden Rule 2: Pad with Trailing Zeros to Compare Decimals (0.5 = 0.50 > 0.08)

To compare 0.5 and 0.08, make both numbers have 2 decimal places by writing 0.5 as 0.50. Now it is obvious that 0.50 (50 hundredths) is much larger than 0.08 (8 hundredths).

Golden Rule / Method: 0.5 = 0.50 (50p) > 0.08 (8p)

Core Skill 1: Pounds & Pence Conversion Rules (Avoiding the Mixed-Unit Trap)

Writing currency properly with £ or p—never both at the same time.

  • Pence to Pounds (÷ 100): 345p = £3.45; 80p = £0.80; 7p = £0.07.
  • Never Mix £ and p: Write £0.85 or 85p — NEVER write "£0.85p"!
  • Always Two Decimal Places for Pounds: If your calculator shows 4.5 for money, write £4.50.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Ordering Decimal Measurements (Metres, Kilograms & Litres)

Comparing whole units first, then tenths, then hundredths.

To order 1.4 m, 1.09 m, 1.45 m, 1.38 m from shortest to longest:

  1. Pad 1.4 m with a trailing zero so all have 2 decimal places: 1.40, 1.09, 1.45, 1.38.
  2. Compare the hundredths after the dot (09, 38, 40, 45).
  3. Ordered list: 1.09 m, 1.38 m, 1.4 m, 1.45 m.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Rounding Decimals to the Nearest Whole Number or 1 Decimal Place

Using the decider digit to round prices and weights.

  • To Nearest Whole Pound (£1): Look at the tenths digit. £14.62 → 6 is 5 or more → rounds UP to £15.00.
  • To 1 Decimal Place (Nearest 10p): Look at the hundredths digit. £14.62 → 2 is less than 5 → rounds DOWN to £14.60.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Benchmark Exam Model 1 2 Marks (Entry Level 3 / Level 1)

Vocational Context: Activities Petty Cash Management

A care home activity worker buys biscuits for £1.45, tea bags for £2.80, and milk for 95p.
(a) Calculate the total cost in pounds (£).
(b) How much change will she receive from a £10.00 note?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Convert 95p to £0.95 and add all three items
£1.45 + £2.80 + £0.95 = £5.20
Criteria: M1: Converting 95p to £0.95 and adding (£5.20)
Step 2 Subtract £5.20 from £10.00
Calculate Change Received
£10.00 − £5.20 = £4.80 change
Always include trailing zero pence (.80) in UK currency answers.
Criteria: A1: Accurate change (£4.80)
Final Accredited Answer: (a) Total = £5.20 | (b) Change = £4.80
Sonya’s Examiner Tip Examiner Method
Always convert pence (95p) into pounds (£0.95) BEFORE adding to pound amounts!
Benchmark Exam Model 2 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1/2)

Vocational Context: Workplace Catering Promotion

A community clinic canteen sells items individually:
• Sandwich: £2.65 | Fruit Pot: £1.35 | Bottled Water: 85p
• A "Healthy Shift Meal Deal" includes all three items for £4.20.
If a nurse buys this meal deal every shift for 5 shifts, how much money does she save in total compared to buying the items separately?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Convert 85p to £0.85 and sum the separate item prices
Individual items: £2.65 + £1.35 + £0.85 = £4.85 per day
Criteria: M1: Separate daily total (£4.85)
Step 2 Calculate daily saving with the £4.20 Meal Deal
Daily saving: £4.85 − £4.20 = £0.65 (65p)
Criteria: M1: Daily saving (£0.65)
Step 3 Multiply daily saving by 5 shifts
Total weekly saving: 5 × £0.65 = £3.25
Criteria: A1: Accurate 5-shift saving (£3.25)
Final Accredited Answer: £3.25 saved over 5 shifts
Subi’s Assessor Pro-Tip Assessor Guidance
Convert 85p to £0.85 first so you never add 85 whole pounds by mistake!

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Adding Pence Directly to Pounds (e.g. £2.65 + 85 = £87.65)
Failing to convert 85p into £0.85 before adding to £2.65.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Convert every pence figure into £0.XX before starting your calculation.
Examiner Pitfall: Writing 5p as £0.5 Instead of £0.05
£0.5 is £0.50 (50 pence)! Single-digit pence require a zero after the decimal point.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Remember: 10p = £0.10, while 1p = £0.01.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Arrange these four parcel weights in ascending order (lightest to heaviest): 2.4 kg, 2.08 kg, 2.45 kg, 2.39 kg.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Pad to 2 decimal places: 2.40, 2.08, 2.45, 2.39.
2. Order from smallest to largest: 2.08 kg, 2.39 kg, 2.4 kg, 2.45 kg.
Final Correct Answer: 2.08 kg, 2.39 kg, 2.4 kg, 2.45 kg
Examiner Tip: Writing 2.4 as 2.40 makes it easy to see that 2.39 is lighter than 2.40.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
Round £27.46:
(a) To the nearest whole pound (£1).
(b) To 1 decimal place (nearest 10p).
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Nearest £1: tenths digit is 4 (< 5), so it rounds down to £27.00.
2. Nearest 10p (1 dp): hundredths digit is 6 (≥ 5), so 4 tenths rounds up to 5 tenths → £27.50.
Final Correct Answer: (a) £27.00 (or £27) | (b) £27.50 (or 27.5)
Examiner Tip: When rounding to the nearest £1, look only at the tenths digit (4), not the 6!
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A receptionist buys 3 packs of pens at £1.75 each and 1 stapler for £3.40. How much change does he get from a £10.00 note?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Pens = 3 × £1.75 = £5.25.
2. Total spent = £5.25 + £3.40 = £8.65.
3. Change = £10.00 − £8.65 = £1.35.
Final Correct Answer: £1.35
Examiner Tip: Check: £8.65 + £1.35 = £10.00.
