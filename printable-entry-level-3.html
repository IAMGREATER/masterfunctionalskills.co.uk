1. Place Value up to 1,000
Number System
Hundreds • Tens • Ones (H-T-O)
Every digit in a 3-digit number has a value based on its column: Hundreds (×100), Tens (×10), and Ones (×1). Zero (0) holds an empty column in place.
★ Golden Rule: Read 3-digit numbers from left to right: 407 is Four hundred and seven!
⚠️ Watch Out: Never drop the middle zero: 'Six hundred and five' is 605, NOT 65!

2. Column Addition & Subtraction
Number System
Align Right • Start at Ones • Regroup
Always line up Hundreds, Tens, and Ones on the right. Start at the Ones column. In addition, carry 10 to the next column; in subtraction, borrow from the left if the top digit is smaller.
★ Golden Rule: Always start adding or subtracting from the ONES column on the far right!
⚠️ Watch Out: In 342 - 158, do NOT do 8 - 2 = 6! You must borrow 1 ten from the 4 so 12 - 8 = 4.

3. Multiplying 2-Digit Numbers
Number System
Partition into Tens & Ones (Grid Method)
To multiply a 2-digit number by a single digit (e.g. 24 × 6), split 24 into 20 and 4. Multiply each part by 6 (20×6 = 120, 4×6 = 24), then add the answers together.
★ Golden Rule: When multiplying by 10, move digits one place left and place a 0 at the end!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't forget the zero when multiplying the Tens part: 30 × 4 = 120, NOT 12!

4. Division & Interpreting Remainders
Number System
Bus Stop Division • Round Up or Down in Context
Divide from left to right. Any leftover amount is the remainder (r). In real-life problems, decide whether to round UP (e.g. minibuses/boxes needed) or round DOWN (what you can afford).
★ Golden Rule: Round UP when booking seats or buying tins so nothing is left short!
⚠️ Watch Out: If asked for 'full packs', ignore remainder and round DOWN.

5. Rounding to Nearest 10 & 100
Number System
5 or More Rounds Up • 4 or Less Stays Same
To round to the nearest 10, look at the Ones digit. To round to the nearest 100, look at the Tens digit. If that neighbour digit is 5, 6, 7, 8, or 9, round UP; if 0–4, stay the same.
★ Golden Rule: Use rounded numbers to ESTIMATE and check if your calculator answer is sensible!
⚠️ Watch Out: When rounding 674 to the nearest 100, look at the Tens digit (7), NOT the Ones digit (4)!

6. Fractions: Thirds, Quarters, Fifths & Tenths
Number System
Divide by the Bottom • Multiply by the Top
A fraction splits a whole into equal parts. To find a fraction of an amount (e.g. 3/4 of £20): Step 1: Divide the amount by the bottom number (20 ÷ 4 = 5). Step 2: Multiply by the top number (5 × 3 = £15).
★ Golden Rule: Divide by the bottom (denominator), then times by the top (numerator)!
⚠️ Watch Out: 1/3 is BIGGER than 1/5 — a smaller bottom number means larger slices!

7. Decimals up to 2 Decimal Places
Number System
Line Up the Decimal Points Vertically
Decimals show tenths (0.1) and hundredths (0.01), just like pounds and pence (£3.45). When adding or subtracting decimals, always line up the decimal points in a straight vertical column.
★ Golden Rule: Add a trailing zero (0.5 = 0.50) so both numbers have 2 decimals!
⚠️ Watch Out: 0.4 is larger than 0.25 because 0.40 has 4 tenths while 0.25 has 2.

8. Money: Pounds, Pence & Change
Measures & Money
£1 = 100p • Never Mix £ and p Signs
Convert pence to pounds by dividing by 100 (e.g. 245p = £2.45). Write money with either a £ sign and 2 decimal places (£0.65) OR a p sign (65p), never both (£0.65p is wrong).
★ Golden Rule: Always write two digits after the dot for pounds: £4.5 on a calculator must be written as £4.50!
⚠️ Watch Out: Before adding 85p and £2.40, convert 85p into £0.85 so both are in pounds!

9. Time: Roman Numerals, 12-Hour & 24-Hour
Measures & Money
Add 12 to PM Hours • 1 Hour = 60 Minutes
On clock faces: IV = 4, VI = 6, IX = 9, XII = 12. For 24-hour time, add 12 to afternoon (pm) hours from 1:00 pm onwards (e.g. 3:15 pm → 15:15). Count time intervals across the top of the hour (:00).
★ Golden Rule: There are 60 minutes in 1 hour — never use column subtraction as if 1 hour had 100 minutes!
⚠️ Watch Out: 12:30 pm stays 12:30 in 24-hour time; 12:30 am (midnight) becomes 00:30.

10. Metric Units: Length, Weight & Capacity
Measures & Money
1 m = 100 cm • 1 kg = 1,000 g • 1 L = 1,000 ml
Length: 1 cm = 10 mm, 1 m = 100 cm, 1 km = 1,000 m. Weight: 1 kg = 1,000 g. Capacity: 1 Litre = 1,000 ml. Always convert to the SAME unit before adding or comparing.
★ Golden Rule: 'Kilo' (km, kg) and 'Milli' (ml, mm in a m) mean 1,000; 'Centi' (cm) means 100!
⚠️ Watch Out: 1 metre is 100 cm (NOT 1,000 cm!). Half a kilogram (0.5 kg) is 500 g.

11. Reading Scales & Dials
Measures & Money
Find the Gap Divided by the Number of Steps
To read an unlabelled tick mark on a scale, thermometer, or weighing dial: (1) Subtract two neighbouring numbered marks to find the gap, then (2) Divide by the number of spaces between them.
★ Golden Rule: Count the SPACES (jumps) between numbered marks, not the lines!
⚠️ Watch Out: If there are 5 spaces between 0 g and 100 g, each mark is 100 ÷ 5 = 20 g (not 10 g!).

12. 2D & 3D Shapes, Right Angles & Symmetry
Shape & Space
Faces, Edges & Vertices • 90° Right Angles
A right angle (90°) is a square corner like a book edge. A line of symmetry folds a 2D shape into two matching halves. 3D shapes have flat/curved Faces, Edges, and Vertices (corners).
★ Golden Rule: A cuboid (box) has 6 rectangular faces, 12 edges, and 8 vertices (corners)!
⚠️ Watch Out: A rectangle has 2 lines of symmetry (vertical & horizontal), NOT 4 — folding diagonally does not match!

13. Compass Directions & Turns
Shape & Space
Never Eat Shredded Wheat (N • E • S • W)
The 8 compass points go clockwise: North (N), North-East (NE), East (E), South-East (SE), South (S), South-West (SW), West (W), North-West (NW). A quarter turn is 1 right angle (90°).
★ Golden Rule: Clockwise follows clock hands; Anti-clockwise turns the opposite way!
⚠️ Watch Out: Check the facing direction before calculating quarter or half turns.

14. Bar Charts, Pictograms & Tally Tables
Handling Data
Always Check the Pictogram Key First!
In a pictogram, one symbol often stands for 2, 4, or 10 items — always read the Key first! In a bar chart, read across from the top of the bar to the vertical frequency axis.
★ Golden Rule: If 1 circle = 4 items, then half a circle = 2 items and a quarter circle = 1 item!
⚠️ Watch Out: Never count pictogram symbols as '1 each' without checking the Key box!

15. Main Purpose of a Text
Reading Basics
To Inform • To Instruct • To Persuade
Every text has a main purpose: To Inform (gives facts, dates, or news), To Instruct (step-by-step recipes or safety rules using command verbs), or To Persuade (adverts encouraging you to buy or join).
★ Golden Rule: When naming purpose, state both the verb AND topic (e.g. To warn about)!
⚠️ Watch Out: Never just write 'To inform' — write 'To inform staff about fire rules'.

17. Using a Dictionary & Alphabetical Order
Reading Basics
Compare the 1st, 2nd, then 3rd Letter
To put words in alphabetical (A–Z) order or look them up in a dictionary/index: compare the first letter. If the first letters are the same, compare the second letter (or third letter).
★ Golden Rule: Check the 3rd and 4th letters when words start with the same letters!
⚠️ Watch Out: 'Staff' comes before 'Store' because 'a' precedes 'o' in the alphabet.

To • Subject Line • Greeting • Paragraphs • Sign-Off

Everyday Writing
Start a New Paragraph for Each New Topic

★ Golden Rule: Leave a clear blank line between paragraphs to show structure clearly!
⚠️ Watch Out: Writing one giant block of text without breaks loses layout marks.

20. Sentence Punctuation & Capital Letters
SPaG Foundations
M.I.N.T.S. Capitals • Full Stops • Question Marks
Start every sentence with a Capital Letter and end with a Full Stop (.), Question Mark (?), or Exclamation Mark (!). Always capitalise Months/Days, the word 'I', Names of people, and Places (Oxford, Tuesday).
★ Golden Rule: Every question must end with a question mark (?), not a full stop!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't join two complete sentences with a comma — use a full stop.

21. Commas in Lists & Apostrophes
SPaG Foundations
Commas Separate List Items • Apostrophes for Missing Letters
Use commas to separate items in a list ('We need pens, paper, folders and tape'). Use an apostrophe (') to show where letters are missing in contractions (don't = do not, can't = cannot, I'm = I am).
★ Golden Rule: Put the apostrophe in the exact spot where the missing letter was taken out!
⚠️ Watch Out: Never put an apostrophe in simple plural words: 'Three coffees and two cakes' has NO apostrophe!

22. Subject-Verb Agreement (Was/Were & Is/Are)
SPaG Foundations
Singular Takes 'Is/Was' • Plural Takes 'Are/Were'
Match the verb to the subject: One person/thing (I, he, she, it, the bus) takes 'is' or 'was'. More than one (we, they, the buses) and 'you' ALWAYS take 'are' or 'were' ('We were happy', NEVER 'We was').
★ Golden Rule: Always write 'We were', 'They were', and 'You were' (never 'We was')!
⚠️ Watch Out: 'There is' is for one item; 'There are' is for multiple items.

23. Plurals (-s, -es, -ies) & Common Spellings
SPaG Foundations
Ch/Sh/S/X Add -es • Consonant + Y Changes to -ies
Most nouns add -s for plurals. Words ending in -ch, -sh, -s, -x, or -z add -es (box → boxes, bus → buses). Words ending in a consonant + y drop the y and add -ies (baby → babies, city → cities).
★ Golden Rule: Consonant + y becomes -ies (party → parties); vowel + y adds -s!
⚠️ Watch Out: Irregular plurals change completely: child → children, person → people.

Creating & Editing
Columns (A, B, C) • Rows (1, 2, 3) • Always Start with =
