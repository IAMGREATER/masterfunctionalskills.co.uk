Specification & Learning Outcomes

Regulated Standards

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills criteria:

  • L1.13: Read, write, order and compare percentages in whole numbers.
  • L1.14: Calculate percentages of quantities, including simple percentage increases and decreases by 5% and multiples thereof with and without a calculator.
  • L2.5: Work out percentages of amounts and express one amount as a percentage of another.
  • L2.6: Calculate percentage change (any size increase and decrease) and original value after percentage change (Reverse Percentages).
  • Format all monetary outputs to two decimal places (e.g. £96.00) and verify answers against realistic workplace contexts.

Three Non-Negotiable Golden Rules for Percentage Mastery

Golden Rule 1: Master the Non-Calculator Building Blocks (10%, 5%, 1%, 50%)

In non-calculator exam papers, never attempt messy manual fraction multiplication. Every single percentage can be built rapidly from four simple mental foundation blocks:

10%
÷ 10
Shift decimal 1 place left
5%
Half of 10%
Calculate 10%, then ÷ 2
1%
÷ 100
Shift decimal 2 places left
50%
÷ 2 (Half)
25% is half of 50% (÷ 4)
Worked Non-Calculator Building Demonstration: Find 35% of £560.00
10% = £56.00 → 30% = 3 × £56 = £168.00 + 5% = £28.00 = £196.00
Golden Rule 2: Single-Step Multiplier Mastery (Calculator Paper)

In Section B (Calculator), never perform two separate steps (calculating the percentage and then adding/subtracting). Convert the final percentage into a single decimal multiplier:

Percentage INCREASE (Multiplier > 1.00)
Add the increase to 100%, then divide by 100:
• +5% Increase: 100% + 5% = 105% → Multiplier = 1.05
• +17.5% Increase: 100% + 17.5% = 117.5% → Multiplier = 1.175
• +20% VAT: 100% + 20% = 120% → Multiplier = 1.20
Percentage DECREASE (Multiplier < 1.00)
Subtract the discount from 100%, then divide by 100:
• −15% Discount: 100% − 15% = 85% → Multiplier = 0.85
• −25% Sale: 100% − 25% = 75% → Multiplier = 0.75
• −4% Reduction: 100% − 4% = 96% → Multiplier = 0.96
Golden Rule 3: The Universal Law of Reverse Percentages

When an exam question provides the final value after a price increase or promotional discount and asks for the original starting value:

Original Starting Amount = Final Amount ÷ Multiplier
Fatal Exam Pitfall: NEVER calculate the percentage of the final price and add/subtract it. The percentage was calculated on the unknown original price, NOT the final price!

Core Skill 1: Calculating a Percentage of an Amount Level 1 & Level 2 Core
Vocational Scenario: Medical Consumable Budget Allocation
A community healthcare clinic receives a monthly consumable budget of £640.00. Management specifies that exactly 15% of this budget must be reserved for surgical personal protective equipment (PPE).
Calculate how much money must be reserved for PPE.
Method A: Non-Calculator Building Blocks
Build 15% using 10% and 5% blocks:
Step 1: Find 10% (÷ 10)
£640.00 ÷ 10 = £64.00
Step 2: Find 5% (Half of 10%)
£64.00 ÷ 2 = £32.00
Step 3: Combine Blocks (10% + 5%)
£64.00 + £32.00 = £96.00
[M1] Decomposing into 10% and 5%
Method B: Calculator Decimal Multiplier
Single multiplication for Section B (Calculator):
Convert 15% to Decimal:
15 ÷ 100 = 0.15
Single-Step Evaluation:
£640.00 × 0.15 = £96.00
[M1] Multiplier calculation
Visual Bar Model: Whole Budget (£640.00 = 100%)
10%
5%
Remaining 85% (£544.00)
10% (£64.00) + 5% (£32.00) = £96.00 PPE Allocation
Final Accredited Output: £96.00 reserved for PPE.
Core Skill 2: Expressing One Quantity as a Percentage of Another Audit & Compliance Target
Vocational Scenario: Care Centre Mandatory Training Compliance
Meadow View Care Centre employs 80 healthcare assistants. A quarterly compliance inspection confirms that exactly 68 workers have completed their mandatory Infection Prevention and Control certification.
What percentage of the workforce has completed the certification?
Step 1: Write as a Fraction
Part over Whole:
68 (Certified)80 (Total Staff)
[M1] Formulating fraction 68/80
Step 2: Convert to Decimal
Divide Numerator by Denominator:
68 ÷ 80 = 0.85
(or simplify: 1720 = 85100)
Step 3: Convert to Percentage
Multiply by 100:
= 85%
[A1] Accurate final percentage 85%
Final Accredited Output: 85% of the workforce has completed certification.
Core Skill 3: Percentage Increase (Multiplier & Breakdown) Level 2 Priority
Vocational Scenario: Annual Salary Cost-of-Living Uplift
A senior healthcare assistant earns an annual salary of £22,400.00. Following an annual performance and cost-of-living review, all staff receive a 4.5% salary increase.
Calculate their new annual salary.
Method 1: The Multiplier (Fastest)
Step 1: Add percentage to 100% baseline:
100% + 4.5% = 104.5%
Step 2: Convert to decimal multiplier:
104.5 ÷ 100 = 1.045
Step 3: Single-step multiplication:
£22,400 × 1.045 = £23,408.00
[M1] Multiplier 1.045
Method 2: Non-Calculator Breakdown
Step 1: Find 1% (÷ 100):
£22,400 ÷ 100 = £224.00
Step 2: Calculate 4% and 0.5%:
4% = 4 × £224 = £896.00
0.5% = £224 ÷ 2 = £112.00
Step 3: Sum increase & add to base:
Increase = £896 + £112 = £1,008.00
Total = £22,400 + £1,008 = £23,408.00
[M1] Finding 4.5% increase (£1,008)
Final Accredited Output: New annual salary is £23,408.00.
Core Skill 4: Percentage Decrease (Multiplier & Breakdown) Level 2 Priority
Vocational Scenario: Logistics Fleet Fuel Reduction
A regional patient transport fleet currently consumes 5,200 litres of fuel per quarter. Route scheduling optimization and driver training decrease quarterly fuel consumption by 15%.
Calculate how many litres of fuel the fleet will consume per quarter now.
Method 1: The Decrease Multiplier
Step 1: Subtract discount from 100%:
100% − 15% = 85%
Step 2: Convert to decimal multiplier:
85 ÷ 100 = 0.85
Step 3: Single-step calculation:
5,200 L × 0.85 = 4,420 litres
[M1] Decrease multiplier 0.85
Method 2: Non-Calculator Chunking
Step 1: Calculate 10% and 5%:
10% = 5,200 ÷ 10 = 520 L
5% = 520 ÷ 2 = 260 L
Step 2: Calculate total 15% reduction:
15% = 520 + 260 = 780 L
Step 3: Subtract from original fuel:
5,200 − 780 = 4,420 litres
[M1] Subtraction of 15% (780 L)
Final Accredited Output: 4,420 litres of fuel per quarter.
Core Skill 5: Reverse Percentages (Finding the Original Value) High-Distinction Level 2
Vocational Scenario: Medical Equipment Supplier VAT Invoice
A regional healthcare facility purchases an ultrasonic diagnostic scanner. The total supplier invoice including 20% VAT is £648.00.
Calculate the original pre-VAT price of the scanner before the value-added tax was applied.
Visual 120% Bar Model: Understanding the Total
Original Pre-VAT Base = 100% (£540.00)
+20% VAT (£108)
Total Invoice: 100% + 20% = 120% = £648.00
Correct Reverse Method: Divide by 1.20
Step 1: Multiplier is 120 ÷ 100 = 1.20
Step 2: Divide final price by multiplier:
£648.00 ÷ 1.20 = £540.00
Step 3: Verification Check:
£540 × 1.20 = £648.00 ✓
[M1] £648 ÷ 1.20 [A1] £540.00
Fatal Candidate Fallacy: Subtracting 20%
Many learners mistakenly take 20% of the final £648.00:
• 20% of £648.00 = £129.60
• £648.00 − £129.60 = £518.40 ✗ DEAD WRONG!
Why this fails: The 20% tax was charged on the unknown original price (£540), NOT on £648! 20% of £540 is £108, not £129.60.
Final Accredited Output: Pre-VAT price of the scanner is £540.00.

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 5 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2 Multi-Step Problem)

Vocational Context: Commercial Procurement & Capital Equipment

A healthcare facility needs to purchase 15 new specialist profiling beds. The standard catalog price is £420.00 per bed.
Two commercial medical suppliers submit competitive quotes for the order:
Supplier Alpha: Offers a straight 15% trade discount on the total list price of the entire order.
Supplier Beta: Offers a flat £50 cash rebate for every whole £500 spent on the order.
Which supplier is cheaper, and by exactly how much? Show complete step-by-step working to justify your conclusion.
Step 1: Calculate Total Undiscounted List Price
15 beds × £420.00 = £6,300.00
[M1] Process to calculate total base cost (£6,300.00)
Step 2: Supplier Alpha (15% Discount)
Multiplier method (100% − 15% = 85% → 0.85):
£6,300.00 × 0.85 = £5,355.00
Total Discount Saved: £6,300 − £5,355 = £945.00
[M1] Correct 15% discount evaluation (£5,355.00)
Step 3: Supplier Beta (£50 Rebate per £500)
Find number of whole £500 blocks in £6,300:
£6,300 ÷ 500 = 12.6 → 12 whole blocks
Calculate total cash rebate:
12 × £50.00 = £600.00 rebate
£6,300.00 − £600.00 = £5,700.00
[M1] Calculating 12 whole blocks & £5,700.00
Step 4: Compare Quotes & Calculate Financial Difference
£5,700.00 (Beta) − £5,355.00 (Alpha) = £345.00 Saving
[M1] Subtraction of both supplier quotes [A1] Fully accurate conclusion: Supplier Alpha and £345.00
Final Accredited Answer:
Supplier Alpha is cheaper by £345.00 (£5,355.00 compared to £5,700.00).
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Notice that Supplier Beta specifies whole £500 blocks. Candidates who used decimal 12.6 × £50 = £630 forfeit marks. Always round down to the integer for whole block promotional conditions.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Trap 1: The "Reverse Percentage Fallacy"
When an item costs £120.00 including 20% VAT, candidates often take 20% of £120 (£24.00) and subtract it to get £96.00. This is fundamentally wrong!
✗ £120 − 20% (£24) = £96.00 (WRONG!) ✓ £120 ÷ 1.20 = £100.00 (CORRECT!)
How to Avoid This Pitfall: £120 represents 120% of the original price. Always divide by the multiplier: Original = £120 ÷ 1.20 = £100.00. Verify by adding 20% back on (20% of £100 = £20 → £120).
Trap 2: Single-Digit Percentage Multiplier Slip
A very frequent exam slip is writing 1.4 for a 4% increase, or 0.3 for a 3% discount:
• 4% increase is 1 + 0.04 = 1.04 (1.4 is a massive 40% increase!)
• 3% discount is 1 − 0.03 = 0.97 (0.3 is a massive 70% decrease!)
How to Avoid This Pitfall: For single-digit percentages (1% to 9%), always write a zero immediately following the decimal point: 5% = 0.05, 6% increase = 1.06.
Trap 3: Stopping After Calculating the Reduction Amount
When asked for the final sale price after a 12% discount on a £350 item, learners frequently calculate 12% (£42.00) and stop right there, writing £42.00 on the answer line!
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Re-read the question stem before writing your final answer. If it asks for the final price, subtract the discount: £350 − £42 = £308.00 (or use multiplier £350 × 0.88 = £308.00).

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 3 Marks
A community medical centre receives a facilities maintenance grant of £560.00. The management committee votes to allocate exactly 35% of this grant towards installing accessible wheelchair ramps.
Calculate the exact amount of money allocated to wheelchair ramp installation.
Pearson Edexcel Marking Scheme Steps:
Method A (Non-Calculator):
10% = £560 ÷ 10 = £56.00
30% = 3 × £56 = £168.00
5% = £56 ÷ 2 = £28.00
35% = £168 + £28 = £196.00
[M1] 10% or 25% base [M1] Summing to 35%
Method B (Calculator):
35% = 0.35
£560.00 × 0.35 = £196.00
[M2] Multiplier operation
Final Answer: £196.00 allocated to wheelchair ramps
[A1] Fully accurate monetary value £196.00
Exam Practice Question 2 4 Marks
A regional ambulance dispatch centre conducted a quarterly customer satisfaction survey. Out of 350 patient family responses received, exactly 287 rated the service communication as "Good" or "Outstanding".
The clinical service quality target is for at least 80% of callers to rate communication as Good or Outstanding.
Has the call centre met the service target? You must show all your calculations to justify your answer.
Pearson Edexcel Marking Scheme Steps:
Step-by-Step Calculation:
1. Formulate fraction: 287350
2. Divide: 287 ÷ 350 = 0.82
3. Convert to percentage: 0.82 × 100 = 82%
4. Compare with quality target: 82% > 80%
[M1] 287 ÷ 350 [M1] Conversion to 82% [E1] Comparing 82% > 80%
Final Answer: Yes, the target is met (82% achieved vs 80% target).
[A1] Correct conclusion "Yes" supported by complete 82% evidence
Exam Practice Question 3 4 Marks
A mobile health clinic purchases a backup diesel generator on a promotional discount. The promotional sale price is £408.00 after a 15% discount was taken off the original recommended retail price.
Calculate the original price of the generator before the discount was applied.
Pearson Edexcel Marking Scheme Steps:
Reverse Percentage Method:
1. Recognise £408 represents: 100% − 15% = 85%
2. Decimal multiplier: 85 ÷ 100 = 0.85
3. Reverse division: £408.00 ÷ 0.85 = £480.00
4. Verification: 15% of £480 = £72 → £480 − £72 = £408 ✓
[P1] Identifying 85% [M1] £408 ÷ 0.85 [A1] £480.00
Final Answer: Original Pre-Discount Price = £480.00
