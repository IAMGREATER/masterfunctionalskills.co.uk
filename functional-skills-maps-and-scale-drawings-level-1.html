Welcome to the Maps, Scale Drawings & Grid Navigation blueprint. In functional exams, scale questions are either architectural plans (e.g. 1 : 50 where 1 cm = 0.5 m) or road maps (e.g. 1 : 25,000 where 1 cm = 0.25 km). The key to full marks is disciplined unit conversions: multiply by the scale to find real cm, then divide by 100 for metres or 100,000 for kilometres!

Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • L1.19 – Work out actual dimensions from scale drawings and calculate scale distances to draw plans.
  • L2.27 – Interpret ratio scales such as 1 : 50, 1 : 200, and 1 : 25,000 and convert between cm, m, and km.

Golden Rules for Maps, Scale Ratios & Floor Plans

Golden Rule 1: Drawing to Real Life → MULTIPLY by Scale Factor

A ratio scale of 1 : 50 means 1 cm on the plan represents 50 cm (0.5 m) in reality. To find the real-world size: multiply the drawing measurement by the scale number, then convert cm to metres (÷ 100) or kilometres (÷ 100,000).

Golden Rule / Method: Real Distance = Map Length × Scale Factor (Convert 100 cm = 1 m)
Golden Rule 2: Real Life to Drawing → DIVIDE by Scale Factor

To draw a real-world object onto a scale plan: first convert the real dimension into centimetres (× 100), then divide by the scale number.

Golden Rule / Method: Plan Length (cm) = Real Distance (in cm) ÷ Scale Factor

Core Skill 2: Calculating Real-World Area from a Scale Plan (Convert Sides First!)

Why you must convert scaled cm into real metres BEFORE multiplying Length × Width.

Crucial Rule: Never calculate area in cm² and then multiply by the linear scale factor! Instead, always convert the length and width into real metres FIRST:

  1. On a 1 : 50 plan, a room measures 10 cm by 8 cm.
  2. Convert Length: 10 cm × 50 = 500 cm = 5.0 m.
  3. Convert Width: 8 cm × 50 = 400 cm = 4.0 m.
  4. Calculate Real Area: 5.0 m × 4.0 m = 20.0 m².
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Checking Furniture Clearance & Placement Rules on Scaled Grids

Verifying minimum walkway gaps and wall clearances on exam floor plans.

  • Convert Everything to Grid Squares: If 1 grid square = 0.5 m by 0.5 m, then a bed measuring 2.0 m by 1.0 m occupies 4 squares by 2 squares.
  • Check Clearance Constraints: If safety rules require "at least 1.0 m clearance from the door", block out 2 full grid squares in front of the door before placing the bed.
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1)

Vocational Context: Architectural Ward Redesign

A hospital architect produces a ward floor plan using a scale of 1 : 50.
(a) On the drawing, Bay 3 measures 14 cm long by 9 cm wide. What are the actual real-world dimensions in metres?
(b) A clinical consultation desk is 2.5 metres long in reality. What length should it be drawn on the scale plan in cm?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Multiply map measurements by scale factor (50)
Real length = 14 cm × 50 = 700 cm.
Real width = 9 cm × 50 = 450 cm.
Criteria: M1: Scale multiplication
Step 2 Convert real dimensions from centimetres to metres (÷ 100)
700 cm ÷ 100 = 7.0 metres.
450 cm ÷ 100 = 4.5 metres.
Criteria: A1: Accurate real dimensions (7.0 m × 4.5 m)
Step 3 Calculate drawing length for the 2.5 m consultation desk
Real length = 2.5 m = 250 cm.
Drawing length = 250 cm ÷ 50 = 5 cm.
Criteria: A1: Accurate drawing length (5 cm)
Final Accredited Answer: (a) Real dimensions = 7.0 m long by 4.5 m wide | (b) Drawing length = 5 cm
Sonya’s Examiner Tip Examiner Method
Sanity check: Real room dimensions should be in metres (7 m × 4.5 m); paper drawings should be in centimetres (5 cm).

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Forgetting to Convert Centimetres to Metres or Kilometres
Writing that a hospital room is "700 m" or "700 cm" when the answer line specifies metres forfeits the accuracy mark.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Remember: 100 cm = 1 m, and 100,000 cm = 1 km.
Examiner Pitfall: Scaling Area by the Linear Factor Instead of Converting Sides First
Multiplying 14 cm × 9 cm = 126 cm² and then multiplying by 50 gives the wrong area (area scales by 50², not 50).
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always convert length and width into real metres BEFORE multiplying them together for area!

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
On an Ordnance Survey map with scale 1 : 50,000, the straight-line distance between two medical clinics measures 7.4 cm. What is the actual distance in kilometres?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Real distance in cm = 7.4 × 50,000 = 370,000 cm.
2. Convert cm to km (÷ 100,000): 370,000 ÷ 100,000 = 3.7 km (or 1 cm = 0.5 km → 7.4 × 0.5 = 3.7 km).
Final Correct Answer: 3.7 km
Examiner Tip: On a 1 : 50,000 map, 1 cm = 500 m = 0.5 km.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
A landscape gardener draws a rectangular lawn measuring 16 m long by 11 m wide using a scale of 1 : 200. Calculate the length and width of the lawn on the scale drawing in cm.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Convert real dimensions to cm: 16 m = 1,600 cm; 11 m = 1,100 cm.
2. Divide by 200: Length = 1,600 ÷ 200 = 8 cm; Width = 1,100 ÷ 200 = 5.5 cm.
Final Correct Answer: 8 cm long by 5.5 cm wide
Examiner Tip: Convert metres to cm (× 100) before dividing by the scale factor (200).
Exam Practice Question 3 4 Marks
On a floor plan drawn to a scale of 1 : 50, a rectangular office measures 12 cm by 8 cm. Carpet tiles cost £15.00 per m². Calculate the total cost to carpet the real office.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Real length = 12 × 50 = 600 cm = 6.0 m.
2. Real width = 8 × 50 = 400 cm = 4.0 m.
3. Real area = 6.0 m × 4.0 m = 24.0 m².
4. Total carpet cost = 24.0 × £15.00 = £360.00.
Final Correct Answer: £360.00
Examiner Tip: Convert 12 cm and 8 cm into real metres (6 m and 4 m) FIRST, then multiply for area (24 m²).
