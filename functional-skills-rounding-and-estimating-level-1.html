Official Level 1 Specification & Scope (L1.N.12)

Regulated Ofqual Functional Skills Mathematics Level 1 learning outcomes (Open Awards, Pearson Edexcel, City & Guilds, NCFE & AQA):

  • Approximate whole numbers and decimals by rounding to the nearest whole number, 10, 100, or 1,000, and to 1 or 2 decimal places.
  • Use front-end rounded numbers to estimate answers and check whether exact calculations are sensible.
  • Round money answers accurately to the nearest penny (2 decimal places).

Core Level 1 Golden Rules: Rounding and Estimating Checks (Level 1)

Golden Rule 1: 5 or More? Raise the Score!

Draw a cut-off line right after your target column and look ONLY at the next single digit: 0–4 stays the same; 5–9 rounds up by 1.

Level 1 Key Rule: 14.762 to 1 d.p. → look at 6 → 14.8
Golden Rule 2: Round BEFORE Calculating When Estimating

If a question says 'Use estimation to check...', round each number first (e.g. £19.85 → £20, 4.9 m → 5 m) and show £20 × 5 = £100.

Level 1 Key Rule: Never round AFTER doing the exact sum!
Golden Rule 3: Keep Placeholder Zeros on Whole-Number Rounding

Rounding 4,682 to the nearest 100 gives 4,700 (not 47!).

Level 1 Key Rule: 4,682 → 4,700
Core Skill 1: Decimal Places vs Significant Figures Comparison Directory Core Convention

In Level 1 exams, candidates frequently confuse Decimal Places (dp) with Significant Figures (sf). Understand the golden difference:

Decimal Places (dp)

Start counting digits only after the decimal point, moving left to right. Ignore all numbers to the left of the decimal point.

Significant Figures (sf)

Start counting from the first non-zero digit on the far left (whether before or after the decimal point). Leading zeros NEVER count!

Core Skill 2: Estimating Complex Calculations by Rounding to 1 Significant Figure Exam Check Benchmark

In Non-Calculator exams, complex multi-step fractions can be estimated in seconds by replacing each messy value with its 1 significant figure round:

Estimate: 58.7 × 19.4 0.48
Stage 1: Round to 1 s.f.
58.7 → 60  |  19.4 → 20  |  0.48 → 0.5
[M1] Rounding Mark
Stage 2: Solve Numerator
Numerator = 60 × 20 = 1,200
6 × 2 = 12, add two zeros
Stage 3: Divide by 0.5
1,200 ÷ 0.5 = 2,400 ✓
[A1] ESTIMATE = 2,400
Non-Calculator Division Secret: Dividing any number by 0.5 is exactly identical to multiplying it by 2 (doubling it)!
1,200 ÷ 0.5 = 1,200 × 2 = 2,400.
Core Skill 3: Identifying Overestimates vs Underestimates Explanation Mark [B1]

Pearson Edexcel and City & Guilds frequently attach an explanation mark [B1]: "Is your estimate an overestimate or an underestimate? Give a reason." Here is the complete decision logic:

Rounding Direction Mathematical Impact Accredited Status Exam Reason Template
Both Values Rounded UP
e.g. 194 → 200, £4.85 → £5		 Estimated product is LARGER than actual value (£1,000 > £940.90) OVERESTIMATE "Overestimate because both values were rounded up."
Both Values Rounded DOWN
e.g. 312 → 300, £2.15 → £2		 Estimated product is SMALLER than actual value (£600 < £670.80) UNDERESTIMATE "Underestimate because both values were rounded down."
One UP, One DOWN
e.g. 194 → 200 (UP), £5.20 → £5 (DOWN)		 The rounding errors partially cancel each other out COMPENSATING "Close estimate as one rounded up and one rounded down."

Common Level 1 Examiner Traps to Avoid

Level 1 Examiner Trap: Double-Rounding Step by Step
Rounding 3.448 to 1 d.p. by turning 3.448 → 3.45 → 3.5! Look ONLY at the second decimal digit (4), giving 3.4.
Level 1 Examiner Trap: Working Out the Exact Answer First and Rounding It
Examiners award 0 marks for 'estimating' if you calculate 29 × 19.75 = 572.75 and round 572.75 to 600.
