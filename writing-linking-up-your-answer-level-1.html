Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.25 – Organise writing into logical paragraphs, establishing clear transitions and cohesive devices.
  • L2.26 – Master complex discourse markers to contrast, elaborate, Sequence, and conclude professional arguments.

Golden Rules for Paragraphing, Transitions & Cohesion

Golden Rule 1: Build Every Body Paragraph Using the PEEL Formula

Every body paragraph should contain 3 to 4 sentences following PEEL: Point (clear topic sentence) → Evidence (realistic statistic, cost, or example) → Explain (impact on the workplace/community) → Link (bridge to the overall goal).

Golden Rule / Technique: Point (Topic Sentence) → Evidence (Fact/Example) → Explain (Impact) → Link
Golden Rule 2: Upgrade 'Also', 'And', and 'But' to Level 2 Discourse Markers

Examiners look specifically for advanced cohesive transitions: use 'Furthermore' or 'Moreover' to add points, 'Conversely' or 'Nevertheless' to contrast, and 'Consequently' or 'Therefore' to show results.

Golden Rule / Technique: Addition: Furthermore / Moreover | Contrast: Conversely / However | Cause/Effect: Consequently / Therefore

Core Skill 1: The Level 2 Discourse Marker Toolkit (Categorised by Function)

Memorise two transitions from each category and weave them into every Writing exam response.

Cohesive Function High-Scoring Transitions Punctuation Rule & Example
Adding & Reinforcing Furthermore, Moreover, In addition, Equally important Follow with a comma: "Furthermore, recent staff surveys confirm that..."
Contrasting & Qualifying However, Conversely, Nevertheless, Whilst, On the other hand Use after a full stop or semicolon: "...costs £600; however, it saves £2,000 annually."
Cause & Consequence Consequently, Therefore, As a direct result, Accordingly Follow with a comma: "Consequently, waiting times dropped by 40%."
Concluding & Summarising In conclusion, Ultimately, In light of these findings Opens final paragraph: "In light of these findings, I recommend..."
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: The TiP ToP Rule: When to Start a New Paragraph

Never write a wall of text—start a new paragraph every time you shift Time, Place, Topic, or Person.

  • Ti — Time Change: Moving from past background ("Last winter, our heating failed...") to future proposals ("From next month, we propose...").
  • P — Place Change: Moving from discussing the reception desk to discussing the staff car park.
  • To — Topic / Bullet Point Change: Moving from Prompt Bullet 1 (health benefits) to Prompt Bullet 2 (financial costs).
  • P — Person / Perspective Change: Moving from the management view to addressing staff concerns or counter-arguments.
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Before & After: Transforming Choppy Sentences into Cohesive PEEL Prose

See how discourse markers and pronoun referencing glue disconnected sentences together.

Disconnected, Choppy Sentences (Repetitive & Basic):

"The library roof is leaking. Rain comes in when it storms. Ten computers got ruined last month. We need to fix the roof soon. People cannot use the study room."

Cohesive PEEL Paragraph with Discourse Markers (Level 2 Standard):

"Urgent structural repairs are required on the library roof to prevent further damage to public IT equipment. During last month's heavy storms, rainwater penetrated the ceiling panels and destroyed ten desktop computers worth £4,500. Consequently, the entire adult study suite has been closed to residents for three weeks. Therefore, authorising emergency roofing maintenance this month will protect council assets and restore vital community learning access."
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 Level 1 Paragraphing & Cohesion Benchmark (3 Marks)

Vocational Context: Customer Service Feedback Letter

This draft extract is written as one unbroken block of short sentences. Divide it into two logical paragraphs and insert appropriate linking words (However, Furthermore, Therefore):
'I visited your leisure centre on Tuesday evening for the beginner swimming class. The receptionist was very welcoming and showed me to the changing rooms. The changing room floors were covered in muddy footprints. Two of the three hairdryers were broken and had exposed wires. You need to inspect the changing rooms daily.'
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Identify the topic shift from positive reception to changing room hazards
Start Paragraph 1 with the visit and positive reception; start Paragraph 2 where the complaint about the changing rooms begins.
Criteria: Logical Paragraph Split (L1.25)
Step 2 Insert a contrasting marker ('However,') at the start of Paragraph 2
Use 'However,' to pivot from the polite receptionist to the unhygienic changing rooms.
Criteria: Cohesive Transition
Step 3 Insert 'Furthermore,' and 'Therefore,' to link the electrical hazard and action
Connect the muddy floor to the dangerous hairdryers and final recommendation.
Criteria: Smooth Narrative Flow
Final Accredited Response: I visited your leisure centre on Tuesday evening to attend the beginner adult swimming class. Upon arrival, the front-desk receptionist was extremely welcoming and kindly directed me to the poolside facilities.

However, I was disappointed to find that the changing room floors were covered in muddy footprints. Furthermore, two of the three wall-mounted hairdryers were out of order with exposed electrical wiring, presenting a serious safety hazard. Therefore, I ask that centre management conduct daily hygiene and electrical safety inspections.
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Starting Paragraph 2 with 'However,' creates a smooth bridge between your praise in Paragraph 1 and your complaint in Paragraph 2.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Starting Every Paragraph Mechanically with 'Firstly', 'Secondly', 'Thirdly'
While better than no markers at all, starting every paragraph with 'Firstly... Secondly... Thirdly...' feels robotic at Level 2.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Link paragraphs conceptually using thematic bridges: 'In addition to these clinical safety benefits, the financial implications must also be considered.'
Examiner Pitfall: Using a Discourse Marker That Contradicts the Meaning
Writing 'The course was cheap. However, everyone enjoyed it' makes no sense because 'cheap' and 'enjoyed it' are both positive points, not a contrast!
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Only use 'However' or 'Conversely' when the second idea genuinely opposes or contrasts with the first.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Combine these two choppy sentences into one cohesive Level 2 sentence using a semicolon and an appropriate contrasting discourse marker (e.g. nevertheless, however):
'The night-shift care team experienced two unexpected staff sickness absences. They completed all resident medication rounds safely and on schedule.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Place a semicolon after the first independent clause ('...sickness absences;').
2. Insert 'nevertheless,' or 'however,' followed by a comma and the second clause.
Final Model Answer: 'The night-shift care team experienced two unexpected staff sickness absences; nevertheless, they completed all resident medication rounds safely and on schedule.'
Examiner Tip: Using 'semicolon + discourse marker + comma' (; nevertheless, / ; however, / ; therefore,) is one of the highest-scoring punctuation structures in Level 2 English.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Choose the most logical discourse marker from the list (Consequently / Conversely / Furthermore) to fill each gap below:
1. Replacing halogen bulbs with LED panels will cut electricity usage by 45%. __________, the new panels have a ten-year lifespan that reduces maintenance callouts.
2. The north car park lighting timer failed at 17:00 yesterday. __________, staff leaving the evening shift had to walk to their vehicles in complete darkness.
3. Online training modules allow staff to study flexibly at home. __________, practical manual-handling skills can only be assessed safely in person.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Sentence 1 adds a second positive benefit → Furthermore.
2. Sentence 2 shows the direct result/effect of the timer failing → Consequently.
3. Sentence 3 contrasts online study with in-person assessment → Conversely.
Final Model Answer: 1. Furthermore | 2. Consequently | 3. Conversely
Examiner Tip: Test the relationship between the two sentences first: Addition = Furthermore; Result = Consequently; Contrast = Conversely.
Exam Practice Question 3 4 Marks
Write a complete 4-sentence PEEL body paragraph for a formal letter to your College Principal arguing why the college library should remain open until 20:00 on Tuesdays and Thursdays for adult evening-class learners.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Point: Topic sentence stating that extending library hours until 20:00 is essential for adult learners.
2. Evidence: Cite that over 120 adult apprentices work full-time daytime shifts and have no quiet study space or Wi-Fi at home.
3. Explain (with 'Furthermore'): Explain how access to textbooks and computers after work improves exam pass rates.
4. Link (with 'Consequently'): Conclude that keeping one librarian on duty for two extra hours will significantly boost student retention.
Final Model Answer: 'Extending college library opening hours until 20:00 on Tuesdays and Thursdays is essential to support our adult evening-class learners. Currently, the library closes at 17:00, which means over 120 adult apprentices who work full-time daytime shifts are completely locked out of textbook and computer resources. Furthermore, many learners live in shared accommodation without quiet study spaces or reliable broadband to complete their coursework. Consequently, funding just six additional hours of library staffing per week will directly improve functional skills pass rates and student retention.'
Examiner Tip: Every sentence in this paragraph has a clear job: Point → Evidence → Explanation → Link.
