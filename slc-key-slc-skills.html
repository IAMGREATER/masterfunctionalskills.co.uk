Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.1 / L2.1 – Identify relevant information and lines of argument in spoken explanations and presentations.
  • L1.2 / L2.2 – Make requests and ask detailed probing questions using appropriate register and language.
  • L1.3 / L2.3 – Communicate information, ideas, and opinions clearly and effectively, providing further elaboration.
  • L1.5 / L2.7 – Respect the turn-taking rights of others during discussions, using appropriate spoken conventions.

Golden Rules for Key Speaking, Listening & Communicating Skills

Golden Rule 1: Active Listening Constitutes Half Your Grade

Assessors grade you not just when you speak, but on how effectively you listen. Nodding, taking brief keyword notes, referencing a peer's point by name, and asking clarifying questions prove observable active engagement.

Golden Rule / Technique: Examiner Focus: Do you build on what others say, or merely wait for your turn to talk?
Golden Rule 2: Use the P-E-E-R Spoken Structure in Every Contribution

Whenever contributing to a group discussion, state your Point, provide Evidence/Example, Explain the vocational impact, and Redirect to the team ('Point → Evidence → Explanation → Redirect').

Golden Rule / Technique: Structure: Point → Evidence → Explanation → Redirect Question

Core Skill 1: The Two Compulsory SLC Exam Components (Level 1 vs Level 2)

Understanding the exact format, duration, and assessor requirements of the Pearson Edexcel Speaking and Listening assessment.

Assessment Component Level 1 Format Level 2 Format
Task 1: Formal Presentation / Talk 3–4 minutes short talk/explanation on a familiar topic + audience Q&A. 4–5 minutes extended formal presentation on a researched topic + challenging Q&A.
Task 2: Formal Group Discussion 10 minutes formal discussion (3–5 peers) reaching shared agreement. 12–15 minutes unscripted group discussion evaluating complex options and reaching consensus.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Active Listening vs Passive Hearing on the Witness Sheet

How to demonstrate observable listening behaviours that assessors can tick off on camera.

  • Verbal Signposts of Listening: Explicitly name the previous speaker: "Following on from Marcus's point regarding staffing levels..." or "To add to what Priya mentioned about infection control..."
  • Non-Verbal Attentiveness: Maintain steady eye contact with whoever is speaking, orient your posture toward them, and jot down 2–3 keyword notes on your pad.
  • Clarification Prompts: Ask targeted follow-up questions: "Could you explain how that change would affect the weekend shift handover?"
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Before & After: Upgrading One-Word Answers into Full P-E-E-R Contributions

See why short conversational replies fail while structured P-E-E-R contributions pass effortlessly.

Weak Contribution (Fails Elaboration & Evidence Criteria):

"Yeah, I agree with Sarah. I think having a staff gym is a really good idea and everyone would like it."

Strong P-E-E-R Contribution (Passes L2.3, L2.4, and L2.7):

"Building on Sarah's suggestion, converting the unused basement room into a staff fitness area would significantly boost employee wellbeing. In last month's staff survey, 65% of warehouse operatives cited back strain as their primary health concern. Providing stretching mats and resistance machines on site would reduce sickness absence. David, from a budget perspective, could our wellbeing fund cover the initial equipment?"
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 Level 1 Pass Benchmark (L1.2, L1.3, L1.5)

Vocational Context: Retail Store Team Meeting

During a Level 1 group discussion about raising £500 for a local food bank, a colleague says:
'We could do a sponsored silence in the shop on Saturday.'
Deliver a polite spoken response that explains why that idea is impractical for retail staff, suggests a better alternative, and asks a peer for their view.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Acknowledge the colleague politely
'That is a creative idea, Chloe, and it shows great enthusiasm for the food bank appeal.'
Criteria: Respectful Turn-Taking (L1.5)
Step 2 Explain the practical issue and offer an alternative
'However, because Saturday is our busiest trading day, staff need to speak with customers at the tills. Instead, we could hold a charity bake sale and raffle at the store entrance.'
Criteria: Clear Point & Explanation (L1.3)
Step 3 Redirect with an open question
'Ben, what prizes do you think we could donate for a Saturday customer raffle?'
Criteria: Asking Questions (L1.2)
Final Accredited Response: 'That is a creative idea, Chloe. However, because Saturday is our busiest trading day, till staff must be able to speak clearly with customers. Instead, we could host a charity bake sale and raffle by the main entrance, which raised £380 last December. Ben, what prizes do you think our store could donate for the raffle?'
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Notice how the candidate never says 'That is a silly idea'—they validate the speaker first, explain the operational constraint, and propose a practical alternative.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 Level 2 Pass Criteria (L2.3, L2.4, L2.7)

Vocational Context: NHS Healthcare Shift Handover

Observe the dialogue during a morning hospital ward meeting:
Colleague A (Marcus): 'We had three fall incidents in Bay 2 last week. Call bells were ignored because the agency nurses didn't know the buzzer panel layout.'
Deliver a model response demonstrating L2.3, L2.4, and L2.7.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Acknowledge and validate the colleague's observation
'Thank you for raising that, Marcus. Patient safety in Bay 2 is an urgent operational priority.'
Criteria: Active Listening & Bridge (L2.7)
Step 2 Provide constructive analysis and supporting evidence
'According to our clinical audit last month, 40% of agency staff receive less than ten minutes of ward orientation before starting their shift.'
Criteria: Supporting Evidence (L2.4)
Step 3 Propose a structured vocational solution and invite feedback
'I propose we attach a laminated colour-coded buzzer map next to every nursing terminal and include a mandatory two-minute call-bell check at handover. Elena, from an occupational therapy perspective, would that resolve the response delay?'
Criteria: Elaboration & Peer Invitation (L2.3, L2.7)
Final Accredited Response: 'Thank you for raising that, Marcus; patient safety in Bay 2 is an urgent operational priority. According to our audit last month, 40% of agency staff receive less than ten minutes of ward orientation. I propose we place a laminated colour-coded buzzer guide at every nursing station and run a two-minute induction check at handover. Elena, from an occupational therapy view, would that resolve the delay?'
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Assessors look for candidates who bring points together and invite quieter members in. Linking Marcus's observation to Elena's expertise ticks three criteria in 25 seconds.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: The Silent Participant Trap
Some candidates believe that sitting quietly and nodding is enough to pass the listening component. Assessors cannot award marks for unobservable internal listening.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: You must verbally reference other participants' contributions and ask at least two relevant follow-up questions during each discussion.
Examiner Pitfall: Dominating the Airtime and Interrupting
Speaking for 75% of the meeting or cutting off quieter peers causes you to fail criterion L2.7 (respecting turn-taking rights) even if your ideas are strong.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Keep each speaking turn to 30–45 seconds, then deliberately hand over the floor with an open question.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 3 Marks
You are participating in a workplace meeting discussing whether your department should adopt a 4-day working week (four 9.5-hour shifts). A colleague argues:
'It will leave our customer service helpdesk unstaffed on Fridays.'
Write a 45-word spoken response that acknowledges their concern, introduces counter-evidence, and invites another colleague (David) to contribute.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Acknowledge: Validate the concern regarding Friday customer service coverage.
2. Counter-evidence: Explain how staggered Monday–Thursday and Tuesday–Friday rotas maintained 100% cover at another branch.
3. Redirect: Ask David how the logistics team manages rotating shifts.
Final Model Answer: 'That is a very valid operational concern regarding Friday helpdesk coverage. However, when our Manchester branch trialled a four-day week last quarter, staggering staff across Monday-to-Thursday and Tuesday-to-Friday rotas maintained 100% telephone cover every weekday. David, how does the logistics team currently manage rotating Friday cover?'
Examiner Tip: Always pair your rebuttal with a concrete example ('when our Manchester branch trialled...') and finish with a named handover question.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Upgrade this weak, 8-word spoken comment into a full Level 2 P-E-E-R contribution for a council meeting on youth crime:
'I think we should open the youth club on Friday nights.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Point: State the proposal to reopen Oakfield Youth Centre on Friday and Saturday evenings.
2. Evidence: Cite that local antisocial behaviour callouts peak between 19:00 and 22:00 on Fridays.
3. Explain & Redirect: Explain how structured sports and music workshops divert teenagers positively, and ask a peer about volunteer staffing.
Final Model Answer: 'I strongly believe we should reopen Oakfield Youth Centre on Friday evenings between 18:00 and 21:30. Neighbourhood policing figures show that 60% of local antisocial behaviour reports occur on Friday nights when teenagers have nowhere safe to socialise. Providing structured boxing and music workshops would channel their energy positively. Samira, could local sports coaches help staff those Friday sessions?'
Examiner Tip: Adding a time window ('18:00 to 21:30') and a realistic statistic ('60% of reports') transforms a basic opinion into a Level 2 argument.
Exam Practice Question 3 4 Marks
During a group discussion, Participant A has spoken for two minutes non-stop while Participant C (Maya) has not spoken at all. Write the exact spoken intervention you would use to politely bring Participant A to a close and invite Maya into the discussion.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Wait for Participant A to take a breath and briefly summarise their core point so they feel heard.
2. Use a polite transition to shift the floor.
3. Invite Maya by name with an accessible open question linked to the topic.
Final Model Answer: 'Thank you, Liam—your point about the upfront software cost is really important for our budget summary. Before we finalise that section, we haven't heard from Maya yet. Maya, from your experience on the reception desk, how easy would this booking software be for staff to use daily?'
Examiner Tip: Summarizing a dominant speaker's point ('Thank you, Liam—your point about X is really important') is the most diplomatic way to close their turn without offending them.
