1. Order of Operations (BIDMAS)
Number System
B - I - D/M - A/S
Operations must be completed in order: 1. Brackets (), 2. Indices (², ³, √), 3. Division & Multiplication (left-to-right), 4. Addition & Subtraction (left-to-right).
★ Golden Rule: Always solve brackets and powers first before multiplying or adding!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't just calculate left-to-right! 5 + 3 × 2 is 11, NOT 16.

2. Negative Numbers in Context
Number System
Rules of Signs & Difference
Difference between positive & negative = Add their absolute values (|a| + |b|). Multiplying/Dividing: Same signs = POSITIVE (+). Different signs = NEGATIVE (-).
★ Golden Rule: Subtracting a negative is the same as ADDING! 5 - (-3) = 5 + 3 = 8.
⚠️ Watch Out: -5°C is COLDER than -2°C. The larger the numeral after the minus sign, the lower the value.

3. Fractions, Decimals & % Equivalence
Number System
Conversion Benchmarks
To compare values, convert them all to percentages or decimals: Fraction to %: (Numerator ÷ Denominator) × 100. Decimal to %: Multiply by 100.
★ Golden Rule: Eighths are common exam traps: 1/8 = 12.5% (0.125), not 18%!
⚠️ Watch Out: 0.4 is 40%, NOT 4%! 0.04 is 4%. Count your decimal places carefully.

4. Fraction of an Amount (Non-Calc)
Number System
Divide by Bottom, Times by Top
To find a fraction (a/b) of any amount: 1. Divide total by denominator (b) to find 1 part. 2. Multiply result by numerator (a) to get required parts.
★ Golden Rule: Divide by the denominator, multiply by the numerator!
⚠️ Watch Out: If a question asks for the REMAINING amount, remember to subtract your answer from the original total.

5. Adding & Subtracting Mixed Fractions
Number System
Improper & Common Denominator
1. Convert mixed numbers to improper fractions (Whole × Denom + Num). 2. Find lowest common denominator (LCM). 3. Add or subtract numerators only. 4. Simplify.
★ Golden Rule: NEVER add denominators together! 1/4 + 2/3 is NEVER 3/7.
⚠️ Watch Out: When subtracting, always convert to top-heavy improper fractions first to prevent negative fractional parts.

6. Multiplying & Dividing Fractions
Number System
Multiply Straight / Keep-Change-Flip
Multiplication: Multiply tops, multiply bottoms (cross-cancel if possible). Division: KEEP the first fraction, CHANGE ÷ to ×, FLIP the second fraction (reciprocal).
★ Golden Rule: Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal!
⚠️ Watch Out: Never flip the FIRST fraction — only flip the SECOND fraction after changing the sign.

7. Percentage Increase & Decrease (Multiplier)
Number System
Single Multiplier Method
Increase: Add % to 100%, divide by 100. (e.g. +17.5% = × 1.175). Decrease: Subtract % from 100%, divide by 100. (e.g. -15% = × 0.85).
★ Golden Rule: Using a single multiplier is faster and eliminates rounding errors on calculator papers!
⚠️ Watch Out: Be careful with single digit percentages: +5% is × 1.05, NOT × 1.5 (which is +50%)!

8. Percentage Change Formula
Number System
(Change ÷ Original) × 100
Percentage Change = (Actual Difference ÷ Original Starting Value) × 100. Works for percentage profit, loss, discount, and inflation.
★ Golden Rule: Denominator is ALWAYS the original starting value before the change occurred!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't divide by the new price! Dividing 1,200 by 6,800 gives 17.6%, which is incorrect.

9. Reverse Percentages (Original Value)
Number System
Divide by Multiplier
To find the original price before VAT or sale: 1. Identify the new percentage (e.g. Price + 20% VAT = 120% = 1.20). 2. Divide final price by multiplier (Price ÷ 1.20).
★ Golden Rule: To go BACKWARDS to the original, DIVIDE by the multiplier!
⚠️ Watch Out: Never calculate the percentage of the sale/final price and subtract! 20% of £144 is £28.80 (£115.20 = WRONG).

Number System
Fixed Amount vs Interest on Interest

★ Golden Rule: Compound formula: Total = Principal × (1 + r/100)ⁿ
⚠️ Watch Out: Check whether the question asks for the TOTAL ACCUMULATED AMOUNT or just the INTEREST EARNED!

11. Sharing in a 3-Part Ratio
Number System
Add Parts, Divide, Multiply
1. Add all parts together to find total parts (e.g. 2 + 3 + 5 = 10 parts). 2. Divide total amount by total parts to find value of 1 part. 3. Multiply 1 part by each share.
★ Golden Rule: Check your work: Add the final shares together — they must equal the starting total!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't divide the total by just one number! Always sum ALL parts first.

12. Reverse Ratios (Difference / One Share)
Number System
Find 1 Part from the Difference
When told 'Person A gets £X more than Person B': 1. Find difference in ratio parts. 2. Divide given money by difference in parts = 1 part. 3. Multiply by required share.
★ Golden Rule: Match the given amount to its specific parts, NEVER the total parts!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't divide £40 by 8! The £40 represents the DIFFERENCE (2 parts), not the total.

13. Direct & Inverse Proportion
Number System
Direct (Both Up) vs Inverse (One Up, One Down)
Direct: As X doubles, Y doubles (Scale factor or Unitary method). Inverse: As workers increase, time decreases! Workers × Time = Constant Total Work.
★ Golden Rule: More workers take LESS time! Multiply workers by hours to get total person-hours.
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't scale inverse proportion by multiplying both! If 3 workers take 8 hrs, 6 workers do NOT take 16 hrs!

Number System
Cost per Unit (Price ÷ Quantity)
To compare value for money, find the cost for 1 unit (100g, 1ml, or 1 item): Cost per 100g = (Price in pence ÷ Grams) × 100. The lowest cost per unit is the best value.
★ Golden Rule: Convert pounds to pence first to avoid difficult decimal calculations!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't just pick the cheaper price tag — a smaller pack might cost more per 100g.

15. Rounding to Significant Figures
Number System
Count from First Non-Zero Digit
1st sig fig is the first digit that is NOT zero reading left-to-right. Look at next digit: 5 or more rounds UP, 4 or less rounds DOWN. Fill trailing spaces with placeholder zeroes.
★ Golden Rule: Leading zeros in decimals NEVER count as significant figures!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't turn 48,290 into 5! You must keep the placeholder zeros (50,000).

17. Powers, Roots & Indices
Number System
Square, Cube & Roots
x² = x × x. x³ = x × x × x. Square root (√x) is inverse of squaring. Cube root (∛x) is the number multiplied by itself 3 times to give x.
★ Golden Rule: 5³ is NOT 5 × 3 = 15! 5³ is 5 × 5 × 5 = 125.
⚠️ Watch Out: Square root of a number between 0 and 1 gets BIGGER! √0.25 = 0.5.

18. Formula Substitution
Number System
Replace Letters with Numbers
1. Replace each variable with given numbers (use brackets around negatives!). 2. Follow BIDMAS: Multiply adjacent terms before adding. 3. Check units.
★ Golden Rule: Letters side-by-side mean MULTIPLY: 4ab means 4 × a × b!
⚠️ Watch Out: Squaring a negative number gives a positive: (-4)² = +16, NOT -16.

20. Circumference of a Circle
Shape & Space
Circumference = 3.142 × Diameter
Circumference is the distance around the outside edge of a circle. Diameter (d) goes straight across through center. Radius (r) is from center to edge (d = 2r).
★ Golden Rule: Distance around outside: Circumference = 3.142 × Diameter!
⚠️ Watch Out: If given radius (r), you MUST double it before multiplying by 3.142 (Diameter = 2 × r)!

21. Area of Triangle & Parallelogram
Shape & Space
Triangle: 1/2 × b × h | Parallelogram: b × h
Both use PERPENDICULAR vertical height at 90° to the base. Triangle is halved because two identical triangles make a parallelogram/rectangle.
★ Golden Rule: Always use the perpendicular vertical height, NEVER the slanted side!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't divide by 2 for a parallelogram — only halve for a triangle!

22. Area of a Trapezium
Shape & Space
Area = 1/2(a + b)h
1. Add parallel top and bottom sides (a + b). 2. Divide by 2 to get average parallel width. 3. Multiply by perpendicular vertical height (h).
★ Golden Rule: Half the sum of parallel sides times vertical height!
⚠️ Watch Out: Examiners include slanted side lengths as a trap. Cross out the slant immediately.

23. Area of a Circle & Semicircle
Shape & Space
Circle: π r² = 3.142 × r² | Semicircle: (3.142 × r²) ÷ 2
The ² symbol means squared: r² means radius multiplied by itself (r × r). Calculate r² (r × r) first, then multiply by 3.142 (Area = 3.142 × r²). If given diameter, halve it first to get radius. Semicircle area is half the full circle: (3.142 × r²) ÷ 2.
★ Golden Rule: Circle Area = 3.142 × r² (where r² means Radius × Radius)!
⚠️ Watch Out: Do NOT multiply diameter by itself! Always halve diameter first to find radius.

38. Mean from a Frequency Table
Handling Data
Mean = Σfx ÷ Σf
1. Multiply value (x) by frequency (f) to make an fx column. 2. Sum the fx column (Total score Σfx). 3. Sum the frequency column (Total count Σf). 4. Divide: Σfx ÷ Σf.
★ Golden Rule: Divide by total frequency (Σf), NEVER by the number of rows in the table!
⚠️ Watch Out: Don't just average the 'x' column — that ignores how many times each value occurred.

Inside Range (Reliable) vs Beyond (Unreliable)
Interpolation: Estimating a value INSIDE the given data range (Reliable). Extrapolation: Extending line of best fit BEYOND the data points (Unreliable / Risky).
★ Golden Rule: Predictions outside the original data range are UNRELIABLE because trends may flatten or reverse!
⚠️ Watch Out: Examiners will ask 'Why might this prediction be unreliable?' — answer: 'It is extrapolated beyond the data range.'

48. Pie Charts: Angles & Frequencies
Handling Data
Angle = (Value ÷ Total) × 360°
Total degrees in any circle = 360°. Degrees per item = 360° ÷ Total Frequency. To find angle for a category: Multiply its frequency by degrees per item.
★ Golden Rule: Sum of all pie chart sector angles MUST equal exactly 360°!
⚠️ Watch Out: A pie chart only shows PROPORTIONS, not raw quantities, unless the total sample size is given!

49. Dual & Grouped Bar Charts
Handling Data
Side-by-Side Comparison with Key
Dual bar charts place two bars side-by-side for each category to compare groups (e.g. Male vs Female, 2023 vs 2024). Always check the legend key and vertical axis scale.
★ Golden Rule: Read the key carefully to avoid mixing up years or cohorts!
⚠️ Watch Out: Check the vertical axis scale increments — each grid square may represent 2, 5, 10, or 20 units.

50. Misleading Graphs & Traps
Handling Data
Non-Zero Baselines & Uneven Scales
Common tricks used to deceive: 1. Broken vertical axis (not starting at 0) exaggerates tiny differences. 2. Unequal scale increments. 3. 3D distortion making front slices look bigger.
★ Golden Rule: Always check if the vertical axis starts at ZERO (0)!
⚠️ Watch Out: A graph that looks dramatic often only represents a 1% or 2% actual difference.
