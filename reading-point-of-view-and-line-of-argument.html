Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.9 / L2.11 – Identify the writer's main point of view, attitude, and line of argument across extended texts.
  • L2.14 – Recognise bias and determine whether arguments are balanced or one-sided, evaluating the strength of supporting evidence.

Golden Rules for Point of View, Bias & Line of Argument

Golden Rule 1: Trace the Line of Argument (Claim -> Evidence -> Conclusion)

A line of argument is the logical chain a writer builds: 1. Opening Claim (what they believe is wrong/right), 2. Supporting Reasons & Evidence (why they believe it), and 3. Final Call to Action (what must happen next).

Golden Rule / Technique: Line of Argument = Opening Stance -> Supporting Reasons -> Final Conclusion
Golden Rule 2: Balanced Arguments Acknowledge Counter-Arguments

A balanced text considers both advantages and disadvantages before reaching a measured verdict. A biased text suppresses counter-arguments, uses loaded language, or stems from a vested financial/personal interest.

Golden Rule / Technique: Balanced = Weighs Pros & Cons | Biased = One-sided + Loaded Vocabulary + Vested Interest

Core Skill 1: Checklist for Detecting Bias vs Balance in Workplace Texts

Key diagnostic questions to evaluate argument objectivity in Level 2 exams.

Diagnostic Test Balanced / Objective Argument Biased / One-Sided Argument
Counter-Arguments Explicitly acknowledges opposing views ("While critics point out the £2m cost, supporters note..."). Completely ignores any drawbacks or dismisses critics with insults ("Only fools oppose this...").
Word Choice (Diction) Measured, neutral, analytical verbs and adjectives. Emotionally loaded pejoratives or extreme superlatives ("disastrous", "greedy", "miraculous").
Author Identity Independent auditor, public regulator, or neutral journalist. Company CEO selling a product, lobbyist, or angry individual with a personal grudge.

Core Skill 2: Tracking Discourse Markers in a Line of Argument

How connectives reveal the direction of the writer's reasoning.

When asked to trace a writer's line of argument, follow their structural signposts from paragraph to paragraph:

  • Building the Case (Cumulative Signposts): "Firstly...", "Furthermore...", "In addition to financial savings...", "More importantly..." → Adds weight to the author's primary stance.
  • Addressing the Counter-Argument (Concession Signposts): "Admittedly...", "While some residents worry that...", "Although initial training takes two days..." → Shows the writer considering an objection before knocking it down.
  • Reaching the Verdict (Causal Signposts): "Consequently...", "Therefore...", "For these reasons, the board must..." → Signals the writer's ultimate conclusion and recommendation.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Distinguishing the Author's View from Quoted Views

Avoid the classic trap of confusing a critic's quote with the writer's own opinion.

Watch Out for Reported Speech! An article might write: "Some landlords claim that licensing fees will force them to raise rents. However, council data proves that 91% of landlords easily absorb the £80 annual fee."
If asked for the writer's point of view, do NOT say the writer thinks fees will raise rents! That is the landlords' claim, which the writer immediately disproves after the word "However".

Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 2 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Reading Level 1)

Vocational Context: Local Library Consultation Letter

Read this extract from a resident's letter to the council:
'Closing Oakwood Library on Saturdays is a terrible mistake. Saturday is the only day working parents can bring their children to borrow books, and elderly residents rely on the Saturday computer club to pay their bills online.'
(a) What is the writer's point of view on closing the library on Saturdays? [1 Mark]
(b) Give two reasons the writer uses to support their argument. [1 Mark]
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 State the writer's stance clearly
The writer is strongly against closing Oakwood Library on Saturdays (calls it 'a terrible mistake').
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Point of View]
Step 2 Extract the two supporting reasons
1. Working parents can only bring their children to borrow books on Saturdays. 2. Elderly residents rely on the Saturday computer club to pay bills online.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Supporting Reasons]
Final Accredited Response: (a) Strongly opposes closing Oakwood Library on Saturdays. | (b) 1. Working parents can only take children on Saturdays. 2. Elderly residents need the Saturday computer club to pay bills.
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Always state clearly whether the writer is 'in favour of' (supports) or 'against' (opposes) the proposal.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Reading Level 2)

Vocational Context: Digital Healthcare Procurement

Read the editorial written by the CEO of 'InstantDoctor Ltd':
'Traditional GP surgeries are inefficient relics of the past. Our revolutionary InstantDoctor app guarantees video consultations in 3 minutes, solving Britain's healthcare crisis overnight.'
(a) Identify the author's point of view and main line of argument. [1 Mark]
(b) Give two pieces of evidence showing that this text is biased. [2 Marks]
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 State the author's point of view and argument
The author argues that traditional GP surgeries are outdated and should be replaced by their 3-minute digital consultation app.
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Viewpoint]
Step 2 Identify Evidence 1 of bias (Loaded Language & Hyperbole)
Uses dismissive, loaded language to insult GP surgeries ('inefficient relics of the past') and unverified hyperbole ('solving Britain's healthcare crisis overnight').
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Language Evidence]
Step 3 Identify Evidence 2 of bias (Vested Interest & One-Sidedness)
Written by the CEO of InstantDoctor Ltd, who profits financially from app sales and omits any limitations (such as patients needing physical examinations).
Criteria: 1 Mark [A1 Vested Interest]
Final Accredited Response: (a) Argues that digital telehealth apps are far superior to traditional GP clinics and should replace them. | (b) Bias evidence: 1. Dismisses GP surgeries with loaded language ('inefficient relics of the past') and exaggerated claims ('solving Britain's healthcare crisis overnight'). 2. Written by the company CEO who has a commercial motive and ignores patients needing physical check-ups.
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
In Level 2 papers, identifying both the loaded vocabulary AND the author's commercial vested interest guarantees full marks.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Confusing a Counter-Argument Quote with the Writer's Own Stance
When a writer mentions an opposing view in order to argue against it ('While some claim X, the reality is Y'), candidates sometimes mistakenly report X as the writer's opinion.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Look for pivot words like 'However', 'In reality', or 'Despite these claims' — the writer's true stance always follows the pivot!
Examiner Pitfall: Confusing Personal Passion with Objective Proof
Just because an author writes with strong conviction and dramatic adjectives does not mean their line of argument is factually proven.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Ask: Where is the independent third-party evidence, sample size, and balanced consideration of drawbacks?

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A trade union newsletter argues: 'The proposed shift roster overhaul is an unmitigated disaster designed solely to exploit loyal warehouse staff.'
Identify the author's stance and explain why the phrasing demonstrates bias.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Stance: Strongly opposed to the proposed shift roster overhaul [1 Mark].
2. Bias Reason: Uses extreme, emotionally loaded language ('unmitigated disaster', 'exploit') and assumes malicious intent without presenting any of management's operational reasons [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Stance: Strongly opposes the new shift roster. | Bias: Uses loaded emotional words ('unmitigated disaster', 'exploit') and presents a one-sided view without management's perspective.
Examiner Tip: Quote the exact loaded phrase ('unmitigated disaster') to substantiate your bias finding.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Read this extract from a town planning report:
'Some high-street retailers fear that pedestrianising Market Street will reduce passing car trade. However, evidence from 14 comparable UK market towns demonstrates that pedestrian zones increase footfall by 28% and reduce empty shop units by half. Therefore, the council should approve the pedestrian zone immediately.'
Trace the writer's line of argument across the three sentences. [3 Marks]
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Sentence 1 (Counter-argument): Acknowledges the opposing concern from retailers that banning cars might reduce passing trade [1 Mark].
2. Sentence 2 (Rebuttal with Evidence): Rebuts that fear using statistical evidence from 14 UK towns showing a 28% rise in footfall and 50% fewer empty shops [1 Mark].
3. Sentence 3 (Conclusion): Concludes that the council should approve the pedestrianisation scheme immediately [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: 1. Acknowledges retailers' fear of losing car trade. 2. Counters this with data from 14 UK towns showing a 28% footfall increase. 3. Concludes that the council should approve pedestrianisation immediately.
Examiner Tip: Notice the classic three-part argument structure: Counter-claim -> Statistical Rebuttal ('However...') -> Final Recommendation ('Therefore...').
Exam Practice Question 3 2 Marks
Read this extract from a nutrition blog written by the founder of 'KetoShake Supplements':
'Standard breakfast cereals are toxic bowls of pure poison. Drinking two KetoShake pouches every morning is the only way to stay healthy.'
Give two reasons why a reader should treat this argument with caution.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Reason 1 (Vested commercial interest): The writer is the founder of KetoShake Supplements and is trying to sell their own product [1 Mark].
2. Reason 2 (Extreme hyperbole / lack of scientific proof): Uses unverified, alarmist exaggeration ('toxic bowls of pure poison', 'the only way to stay healthy') without independent nutritional evidence [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: 1. The author owns KetoShake Supplements and has a financial motive to boost sales. | 2. Uses extreme, unscientific exaggeration ('toxic bowls of pure poison', 'the only way').
Examiner Tip: Always check the author introduction above the excerpt for commercial conflicts of interest.
