Counting, Reading & Writing Numbers up to 1,000

Everyday example for Counting, Reading & Writing Numbers up to 1,000.
★How to Do It • Key Rule
Numbers up to 1,000 have three place value columns: Hundreds (H), Tens (T), and Units (U).

In 468: 4 hundreds (400), 6 tens (60), 8 units (8). Words: four hundred and sixty-eight.
✎Step-by-Step Example
Write the number five hundred and twenty-four in digits.
  1. 5 hundreds = 500
  2. 2 tens = 20
  3. 4 units = 4
  4. Combined digits = 524
✓Top Tip
Always write digits clearly in the correct column.
!Remember!
Don't write 50024 — remember each column takes one digit!

Comparing & Ordering Numbers to 1,000

Everyday example for Comparing & Ordering Numbers to 1,000.
★How to Do It • Key Rule
Compare numbers by looking at the largest place value column first (Hundreds, then Tens, then Units).

> means greater than (e.g. 520 > 480). < means less than (e.g. 310 < 390). = means equal to.
✎Step-by-Step Example
Put these parcel weights in order from lightest to heaviest: 482 g, 419 g, 503 g, 475 g.
  1. Compare hundreds: 419, 475, 482 all start with 4; 503 starts with 5
  2. Compare tens: 419 (1 ten), 475 (7 tens), 482 (8 tens)
  3. Correct order: 419 g, 475 g, 482 g, 503 g
✓Top Tip
The wide end of the > symbol always points towards the larger number.
!Remember!
Always check whether question asks for smallest-to-largest or largest-to-smallest!

Addition: Column Method with Regrouping

Everyday example for Addition: Column Method with Regrouping.
★How to Do It • Key Rule
Column addition adds numbers in vertical columns from right to left (Units first, then Tens, then Hundreds).

If a column adds up to 10 or more, carry the 1 into the next column on the left.
✎Step-by-Step Example
A shop assistant sells 248 items on Friday and 375 items on Saturday. Find the total items sold.
  1. Units: 8 + 5 = 13 (write 3, carry 1)
  2. Tens: 4 + 7 + 1 = 12 (write 2, carry 1)
  3. Hundreds: 2 + 3 + 1 = 6
  4. Total = 623 items
✓Top Tip
Always line up your columns from the right-hand side.
!Remember!
Don't forget to add in any carried digits!

Subtraction: Column Method with Borrowing

Everyday example for Subtraction: Column Method with Borrowing.
★How to Do It • Key Rule
Column subtraction subtracts the bottom digit from the top digit in each column from right to left.

If the top digit is smaller than the bottom digit, borrow 1 from the column to the left.
✎Step-by-Step Example
A warehouse has 642 boxes in stock. 278 boxes are dispatched on a lorry. How many boxes remain?
  1. Units: 2 - 8 cannot do; borrow 1 from tens -> 12 - 8 = 4
  2. Tens: 3 - 7 cannot do; borrow 1 from hundreds -> 13 - 7 = 6
  3. Hundreds: 5 - 2 = 3
  4. Remaining = 364 boxes
✓Top Tip
Never swap the digits around: if the top is smaller, you MUST borrow!
!Remember!
Check your answer: 364 + 278 = 642.

Multiplication Facts: 2, 3, 4, 5 & 10

Everyday example for Multiplication Facts: 2, 3, 4, 5 & 10.
★How to Do It • Key Rule
Multiplication is repeated addition. Learn your times tables to calculate quickly in everyday life.

2x table doubles numbers; 5x table ends in 0 or 5; 10x table adds a place-value zero.
✎Step-by-Step Example
A pharmacy orders 8 packs of face masks. Each pack contains 5 masks. How many masks altogether?
  1. Calculation: 8 packs x 5 masks
  2. Use 5 times table: 5, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40
  3. Total masks = 40 masks
✓Top Tip
Multiplication can be done in any order: 8 x 5 is the same as 5 x 8.
!Remember!
Multiplying by 10 shifts digits one place to the left.

Multiplying 2-Digit Numbers by 1-Digit

Everyday example for Multiplying 2-Digit Numbers by 1-Digit.
★How to Do It • Key Rule
To multiply a 2-digit number by a single digit: Multiply the units first, then multiply the tens.

Partitioning method: 24 x 3 = (20 x 3) + (4 x 3) = 60 + 12 = 72.
✎Step-by-Step Example
A baker makes 3 batches of muffins. Each batch has 24 muffins. How many muffins in total?
  1. Multiply units: 4 x 3 = 12 (write 2, carry 1)
  2. Multiply tens: 2 x 3 = 6; add carry: 6 + 1 = 7
  3. Total muffins = 72 muffins
✓Top Tip
Break large numbers into tens and units to multiply mentally.
!Remember!
Check: 25 x 3 = 75, so 72 is very sensible.

Division Facts & Sharing with Remainders

Everyday example for Division Facts & Sharing with Remainders.
★How to Do It • Key Rule
Division shares an amount into equal groups: Total / Number of groups = Items per group.

Any leftover amount is called the remainder.
✎Step-by-Step Example
Share 29 safety badges equally between 4 team members. How many does each get, and how many are left over?
  1. 4 x 7 = 28
  2. 29 - 28 = 1 remainder
  3. Each person gets 7 badges with 1 left over
✓Top Tip
Multiplication is the opposite of division: 28 / 4 = 7 because 7 x 4 = 28.
!Remember!
Remainder must always be smaller than the number you divided by.

Fractions of Shapes & Amounts: 1/2, 1/4, 1/3, 1/10

Everyday example for Fractions of Shapes & Amounts: 1/2, 1/4, 1/3, 1/10.
★How to Do It • Key Rule
A fraction represents equal parts of a whole. 1/2 = half (divide by 2); 1/4 = quarter (divide by 4).

1/3 = third (divide by 3); 1/10 = tenth (divide by 10).
✎Step-by-Step Example
Find 1/4 of £32 for a customer discount.
  1. Divide by 4: £32 / 4
  2. Calculation: 4 x 8 = 32
  3. Discount = £8.00
✓Top Tip
To find half, divide by 2. To find a quarter, halve and halve again.
!Remember!
All parts of a fraction MUST be equal in size.

Entry Level 3 Key Rules & Conversions

Addition & Subtraction:
• Column addition: add units first, then tens, then hundreds (carry over into next column).
• Column subtraction: if top digit is smaller, borrow 1 from the column to the left.

Multiplication & Division:
• Learn 2, 3, 4, 5, and 10 times tables.
• Multiplying by 10 shifts digits one place to the left (with a trailing zero).
• Division shares into equal groups; leftover amount is the remainder.

Fractions & Money:
• 1/2 = half (divide by 2)   1/4 = quarter (halve and halve again)
• 100 pence = 1 pound (£)   Change = Amount given − Total cost

Everyday Measures:
• 1 metre = 100 cm   1 cm = 10 mm
• 1 kilogram = 1,000 grams   1 litre = 1,000 millilitres
• Perimeter = add all 4 sides of a rectangle

Mathematical Glossary & Vocabulary

Add
Combining two or more numbers together to find the total sum (+).
Bar Chart
A chart with rectangular bars showing amounts for different categories.
Borrowing
Taking 1 ten or 1 hundred from the left column in subtraction when the top digit is smaller.
Capacity
The amount of liquid a container can hold, measured in millilitres (ml) or litres (l).
Centimetre (cm)
A metric unit of length: 100 cm = 1 metre.
Change
The money returned to you when you pay with more than the bill amount.
Digit
Any single number symbol from 0 to 9 used to write numbers.
Division
Sharing an amount into equal groups or finding how many groups fit into a total (÷).
Gram (g)
A metric unit of mass/weight: 1,000 grams = 1 kilogram.
Half
One of two equal parts of a whole (1/2).
Kilogram (kg)
A metric unit of heavy weight: roughly the weight of a bag of sugar.
Litre (l)
A metric unit of liquid capacity: 1 litre = 1,000 ml.
Perimeter
The total distance all the way around the outside edge of a shape.
Quarter
One of four equal parts of a whole (1/4).
Remainder
The amount leftover when a number cannot be divided equally.
Subtract
Taking one number away from another to find the difference (-).
Tally
Counting in groups of five using vertical marks and a diagonal strike.
