Golden Rules for 3D Shape Basics: Cubes, Spheres & Cones

Golden Rule 1: Use 3D Names for Solids (Never Call a Cube a Square!)

2D shapes are flat (Square, Rectangle, Circle, Triangle). 3D shapes have depth and volume (Cube, Cuboid, Sphere/Cylinder, Pyramid/Cone). Calling a cuboid box a "rectangle" loses the mark!

Golden Rule / Method: Flat 2D: Square, Rectangle, Circle • Solid 3D: Cube, Cuboid, Cylinder, Sphere
Golden Rule 2: Flat Faces Allow Stacking; Curved Surfaces Roll

Cubes and cuboids have 6 flat faces so they stack stably on warehouse pallets. Cylinders stack upright on their 2 flat circular ends, while spheres have 1 continuous curved surface and 0 flat faces.

Golden Rule / Method: Cylinder = 2 Flat Circular Ends + 1 Curved Wall • Sphere = 1 Curved Surface

Core Skill 2: Counting Faces, Edges & Vertices on Basic Solids

Quick visual counting rules for cubes, cuboids, cylinders, and cones.

  • Cube & Cuboid: 6 flat faces, 12 straight edges, 8 corner vertices.
  • Square-Based Pyramid: 1 square base + 4 triangular sides = 5 faces, 8 edges, 5 vertices.
  • Cylinder: 2 flat circular faces + 1 curved surface, 2 circular edges, 0 vertices.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: 2D Footprints & Warehouse Pallet Stacking

How 3D cartons fit onto 2D shelf and pallet footprints.

To find how many boxes fit on a shelf:

  • Divide the shelf length by the box length, divide the shelf width by the box width, and divide the shelf height by the box height.
  • Multiply the three whole numbers together: Boxes Along × Boxes Deep × Layers High.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Entry Level 3 / Level 1)

Vocational Context: Clinical Stores Inventory

Give the correct mathematical 3D name for each of these three healthcare items:
(a) A roll of sterile surgical tape.
(b) A box of disposable nitrile gloves with rectangular sides.
(c) A pointed paper cup for a water dispenser.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Identify shape of the surgical tape roll
Circular ends with straight sides = CYLINDER.
Criteria: A1: Cylinder
Step 2 Identify shape of the rectangular glove box
Six rectangular faces = CUBOID.
Criteria: A1: Cuboid
Step 3 Identify shape of the pointed paper cup
One circular opening tapering to a single point = CONE.
Criteria: A1: Cone
Final Accredited Answer: (a) Cylinder | (b) Cuboid | (c) Cone
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Always write "Cuboid" rather than "box" or "rectangle".
Benchmark Exam Model 2 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1/2)

Vocational Context: Pharmacy Dispensary Shelving

A pharmacy shelf space measures 60 cm wide, 30 cm deep, and 24 cm high. A technician stacks cuboid medicine cartons that each measure 12 cm wide, 10 cm deep, and 8 cm high (kept upright in the same orientation).
Calculate the maximum number of medicine cartons that will fit onto the shelf.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate how many cartons fit along the width and depth
Along width = 60 cm ÷ 12 cm = 5 cartons.
Along depth = 30 cm ÷ 10 cm = 3 cartons.
Criteria: M1: Base layer counts (5 and 3)
Step 2 Calculate how many layers fit vertically in the 24 cm height
Layers high = 24 cm ÷ 8 cm = 3 layers.
Criteria: M1: Vertical layer count (3)
Step 3 Multiply all three counts together
5 × 3 × 3 = 45 cartons.
Criteria: A1: Accurate total cartons (45)
Final Accredited Answer: 45 medicine cartons
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Divide each dimension separately (5 × 3 × 3 = 45) so you are sure the boxes fit without cutting them!

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Writing 2D Names (Square, Circle, Rectangle) for 3D Objects
Calling a tin can a "circle" or a cardboard box a "rectangle" scores 0 marks.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Memorise: Circle → Cylinder/Sphere; Square → Cube; Rectangle → Cuboid.
Examiner Pitfall: Confusing Cube and Cuboid
A cube has 6 identical square faces (all edges equal). If the length, width, and height are not all equal, it is a cuboid.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Check the dimensions: 10×10×10 is a Cube; 10×10×20 is a Cuboid.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A container is shaped as a cylinder. State:
(a) How many flat faces and how many curved surfaces it has.
(b) What 2D shape its flat faces are.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. A cylinder has 2 flat faces (top and bottom) and 1 curved surface.
2. The 2 flat faces are circles.
Final Correct Answer: (a) 2 flat faces, 1 curved surface | (b) Circles
Examiner Tip: Think of a tin of beans: 2 flat circular ends and 1 curved label.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
Which 3D solid has 5 faces made up of 1 square base and 4 triangles that meet at a top point?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. A 4-sided square base with 4 triangular sides meeting at an apex is a Square-Based Pyramid.
Final Correct Answer: Square-based pyramid
Examiner Tip: Include "square-based" to distinguish it from a triangular pyramid.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A warehouse crate holds cube-shaped gift boxes of side length 10 cm. The internal crate measures 50 cm long, 40 cm wide, and 30 cm high. How many cube gift boxes can be packed inside the crate?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Along length = 50 ÷ 10 = 5.
2. Along width = 40 ÷ 10 = 4.
3. Layers high = 30 ÷ 10 = 3.
4. Total boxes = 5 × 4 × 3 = 60 boxes.
Final Correct Answer: 60 boxes
Examiner Tip: 5 along × 4 wide = 20 per layer; 3 layers × 20 = 60 boxes.
