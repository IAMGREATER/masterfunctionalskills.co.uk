Official Level 1 Specification & Scope (L1.N.3)

Regulated Ofqual Functional Skills Mathematics Level 1 learning outcomes (Open Awards, Pearson Edexcel, City & Guilds, NCFE & AQA):

  • Multiply whole numbers and decimals by 10, 100, and 1,000 accurately without a calculator.
  • Multiply 2-digit and 3-digit whole numbers by 1-digit and 2-digit multipliers using column or grid methods.
  • Know and apply square numbers up to 10 × 10 (L1.N.6) in practical area and pack calculations.

Core Level 1 Golden Rules: Multiplication and Scaling by 10, 100 and 1,000 (Level 1)

Golden Rule 1: Slide Digits Left for ×10, ×100, ×1,000

Never say 'just add a zero' on decimals! Slide digits 1 place left (×10), 2 places left (×100), or 3 places left (×1,000).

Level 1 Key Rule: 3.45 × 100 = 345 (not 3.4500!)
Golden Rule 2: Place the Magic Zero on Row 2

In 2-digit column multiplication (e.g. 46 × 24), always write a 0 in the units column before multiplying by the tens digit (20).

Level 1 Key Rule: Row 1: 46 × 4 = 184 | Row 2: 46 × 20 = 920
Golden Rule 3: Know Squares Up to 10²

Level 1 explicitly tests squares like 6² = 36, 8² = 64, and 10² = 100—remember 8² means 8 × 8, NEVER 8 × 2!

Level 1 Key Rule: 7² = 7 × 7 = 49

"If you forget the green placeholder 0 on Row 2, you calculate 843 + 562 = 1,405 instead of 6,463! That single forgotten zero costs 2 out of 3 marks in Pearson Edexcel Non-Calculator exams. Write the 0 before you multiply a single digit of the tens row!"

Core Skill 3: Multiplying Decimals Without a Calculator

Strip the decimal points, multiply whole numbers, and count back total decimal places.

THE 3-STAGE DECIMAL MULTIPLICATION PROTOCOL
Worked Example: 3.6 × 0.25
STAGE 1: Count Total Decimal Digits
3.6 (1 d.p.) × 0.25 (2 d.p.)
1 + 2 = 3 Total Decimal Places

Ignore points temporarily and treat as whole integers: 36 × 25.

STAGE 2: Multiply Pure Integers
36 × 25
• 36 × 5 = 180
• 36 × 20 = 720
• 180 + 720 = 900

Whole number integer product is 900.

STAGE 3: Count 3 Places In From Right
9 0 0 → 0 . 9 0 0
= 0.9 ✓

Trailing zeros after a decimal can be dropped: 0.900 = 0.9.

Accredited Full-Mark Answers [A1]
  • 0.9 (Simplified standard format ✓)
  • 0.900 (Accepted by Pearson Edexcel mark scheme ✓)
  • 0.90 (Accepted in currency or measurement contexts ✓)
Common Zero-Mark Mistakes [0 / 1]
  • 9.0 × (Learner only counted 2 decimal places instead of 3)
  • 0.09 × (Learner counted 4 decimal places)
  • 900 × (Learner forgot to restore decimal points entirely)
Sonya's Decimal Strategy: Never Multiply with Decimals in Place

"Never try to do column multiplication while keeping decimals in the rows! Trying to calculate with floating points on paper leads to confusion and misaligned digits. Strip them completely, calculate whole numbers, and hop the point back at the finish line."

An events hall sets out 28 circular tables, each seating 12 guests, plus 100 extra folding chairs costing £1.35 each to hire. Calculate the total guest seating at the tables and the cost of hiring the 100 extra chairs.

Common Level 1 Examiner Traps to Avoid

Level 1 Examiner Trap: Forgetting the Tens Placeholder Zero
Calculating 34 × 23 as (34 × 3 = 102) + (34 × 2 = 68) = 170 instead of 102 + 680 = 782.
Level 1 Examiner Trap: Treating Squaring as Doubling
Writing 6² = 12 instead of 6 × 6 = 36.
