Official Level 1 Specification & Scope (L1.N.3 / L1.N.4)

Regulated Ofqual Functional Skills Mathematics Level 1 learning outcomes (Open Awards, Pearson Edexcel, City & Guilds, NCFE & AQA):

  • Divide whole numbers and decimals by 10, 100, and 1,000 by shifting digits right.
  • Divide multi-digit numbers by single-digit and simple two-digit divisors using bus-stop short division.
  • Interpret remainders sensibly in real life: rounding UP for coaches/boxes, or rounding DOWN for full items affordable.
Included at Level 1
Dividing by 10, 100, 1,000; short division; rounding remainders up or down in context.
Saved for Level 2 (Not Tested Here)
Dividing by arbitrary decimals (e.g. 42.6 ÷ 0.15) without a calculator. View Level 2 Guide →

Core Level 1 Golden Rules: Division, Remainders and Dividing by 10, 100, 1,000 (Level 1)

Golden Rule 1: Slide Digits Right for ÷10, ÷100, ÷1,000

Dividing makes positive numbers smaller: shift digits 1 place right (÷10), 2 places right (÷100), or 3 places right (÷1,000).

Level 1 Key Rule: 450 ÷ 100 = 4.5
Golden Rule 2: Round UP When Nothing Can Be Left Behind

For minibuses, coaches, boxes, tins of paint, or packs of tiles, any remainder requires 1 extra whole unit.

Level 1 Key Rule: 75 people ÷ 16-seat minibus = 4 r 11 → 5 minibuses
Golden Rule 3: Round DOWN for 'How Many Full Items Can You Buy?'

When dividing a fixed budget or length of ribbon into complete pieces, ignore the leftover remainder.

Level 1 Key Rule: £50 ÷ £8 ticket = 6 full tickets (£2 left over)

STEP 1 4 into 5 (Hundreds)
4 into 5 = 1 time, remainder 1
Write 1 on top. Carry red remainder ¹ onto next digit to make 11.
STEP 2 4 into 11 (Tens)
4 into 11 = 2 times (4 × 2 = 8), remainder 3
Write 2 on top. Carry red remainder ³ onto next digit to make 38.
STEP 3 4 into 38 (Units)
4 into 38 = 9 times (4 × 9 = 36), remainder 2
Write 9 on top. Since we have a remainder of 2 in currency, add decimal point and trailing zeros .00.
STEP 4 Decimal Extension: 4 into 20 (Tenths)
4 into 20 = 5 times (exact!), 4 into 0 = 0
Write 5 and 0 after the decimal point. Final answer: £129.50 ✓
Sonya's Visual Quick-Check: Never Write 'Remainder 2' on Currency!

"In Functional Skills exams, writing '£129 r 2' automatically costs you the final accuracy mark [A1]! Whenever a division question involves money, add the decimal point and trailing zeros (.00) immediately under the bus stop to finish in pounds and pence (£129.50)."

Core Skill 2: Dividing by 2-Digit Numbers Using Chunking & Multiples Ladders

Failsafe method for non-calculator division by numbers like 14, 16, 18, or 24.

WORKED METHOD: CHUNKING WITH MULTIPLES LADDER
Worked Calculation: 432 ÷ 18 (2-Digit Divisor)
Step A: Multiples Ladder in the Margin
1 × 18 = 18
2 × 18 = 36
4 × 18 = 72
10 × 18 = 180
20 × 18 = 360 (Double 10 × 18)
Step B: Subtract Big Known Chunks
Total: 432
− 360 (Chunk of 20 × 18)
Remaining: 72
− 72 (Chunk of 4 × 18)
Remaining: 0 ✓
STEP 1 Build Quick Margin Multiples
1×, 2×, 4×, 10×, 20×
Always double (18 → 36 → 72) and multiply by 10 (180 → 360). This takes under 15 seconds.
STEP 2 Subtract Chunk of 20
432 − 360 = 72 remaining
20 × 18 = 360. Subtracting 360 leaves only 72 left to divide.
STEP 3 Spot Final Chunk of 4
72 = 4 × 18
Our multiples ladder directly shows that 72 is exactly 4 × 18. Remainder is 0.
STEP 4 Combine the Two Chunks
20 + 4 = 24
Add the chunk counts together. Final quotient: 24 ✓
Subi's Assessor Golden Rule: Chunking Outsmarts Long Division

"Don't waste time trying to guess 18-times tables on the fly! The doubling multiples ladder (×1, ×2, ×4, ×10, ×20) lets you subtract huge friendly chunks (like 360) in seconds. It guarantees full method marks [M1]!"

Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Contextual Remainder Decision Matrix (Round Up vs Round Down)

How to score the final accuracy mark on real-world vocational division problems.

Why Pearson Edexcel Tests Contextual Rounding

The mark scheme explicitly separates the [M1] Method Mark (for performing raw division, e.g. getting 8.875) from the [A1] Accuracy Mark (for applying human vocational reasoning to state 9 buses).

The Mathematical Rounding Trap (5/4 Rule)

Do NOT use pure math rounding rules! For example, 12.25 tins would round down to 12 in pure math, but in the real world 12 tins leaves 5 m² unpainted. Always ask: "Does this leave anything unfinished or anyone stranded?"

Sonya's Practical Exam Rule: Always State Both Raw Value and Rounded Decision

"Always write down the unrounded division result first (e.g. 142 ÷ 16 = 8.875 or 8 r 14) to bank your method mark [M1], followed by your rounded contextual sentence ('Therefore, 9 minibuses are required') to secure your accuracy mark [A1]!"

Common Level 1 Examiner Traps to Avoid

Level 1 Examiner Trap: Rounding Down Instead of Up on Transport/Packaging
Seeing 14.4 on a calculator and rounding down to 14 coaches—leaving 40% of a coachload stranded!
Level 1 Examiner Trap: Shifting the Wrong Way on ÷100
Writing 68 ÷ 100 = 6,800 instead of 0.68.

Level 1 Practice 1: Metric Cable Conversion 2 Marks
A spool holds 3,450 cm of cable. Divide by 100 to write this length in metres.
Level 1 Worked Solution & Mark Breakdown:
Divide by 100 (shift 2 places right) (M1): 3,450 ÷ 100
Exact decimal value (A1): 34.5 m
Final Answer: 34.5 m
Level 1 Practice 2: Staff Training Rooms 2 Marks
86 staff members attend safety training. Each room holds 15 people. How many rooms are required?
Level 1 Worked Solution & Mark Breakdown:
86 ÷ 15 = 5 remainder 11 (M1): 15 × 5 = 75 (11 left)
Round up to cover all 86 staff (A1): 6 rooms
Final Answer: 6 rooms
