Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.3 – Identify and structure clear lines of argument and narratives in spoken explanations and formal presentations.
  • L2.3 / L2.5 – Follow and develop complex lines of argument, using spoken signposting to guide the audience and transition smoothly to Q&A.

Golden Rules for Structuring Arguments & Formal Presentations

Golden Rule 1: The Three-Part Spoken Signposting Architecture

Unlike readers who can re-read a paragraph, listeners rely entirely on your verbal signposts. Tell your audience what you are going to cover (Roadmap), guide them through 3 numbered points ('First...', 'Moving to my second point...'), and summarise your conclusion.

Golden Rule / Technique: Hook & Roadmap (45s) → 3 Signposted Arguments (3m) → Conclusion & Q&A Invite (30s)
Golden Rule 2: Speak From Bullet-Point Cue Cards—Never Read an A4 Essay Verbatim

Reading word-for-word from a sheet of paper flattens your voice, destroys eye contact, and violates spoken communication criteria. Write 3 or 4 bold keywords per index card and speak naturally to the room.

Golden Rule / Technique: 4 Cue Cards (1. Intro/Hook | 2. Point 1 & 2 | 3. Point 3 & Counter | 4. Conclusion)

Core Skill 1: Minute-by-Minute Blueprint for a 4-Minute Level 2 Presentation

How to pace your presentation so you hit every marking criterion without running over time.

Time Segment Structural Stage Exact Spoken Signpost Phrase
0:00 – 0:45 Welcome, Hook & Roadmap "Good morning everyone. Did you know that...? Today I will examine three areas: first..., second..., and finally..."
0:45 – 1:45 Argument 1: The Core Problem "Starting with my first point, let's look at the current impact on..."
1:45 – 2:45 Argument 2: Proposed Solution & Evidence "Turning now to my second point, the evidence from our pilot scheme shows..."
2:45 – 3:30 Argument 3: Counter-Argument & Cost "Finally, while some may question the upfront cost, the return on investment..."
3:30 – 4:00 Summary & Q&A Handover "In conclusion, implementing these three steps will... Thank you for listening; I welcome any questions."
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Using Visual Aids & PowerPoint Slides Effectively

How to use 3–4 simple slides or a handout to boost your presentation without hiding behind the screen.

  • The 6x6 Slide Rule: Put no more than 6 bullet points per slide and no more than 6 words per bullet. Your slides are visual prompts for the audience, NOT your script!
  • Face the Audience, Not the Whiteboard: Never turn your back on your peers to read off the projector wall. Glance at your laptop or cue card and maintain eye contact with your listeners.
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Structuring Spoken Narratives & Workplace Case Accounts (STAR Method)

When recounting an incident or case study in speech, use Situation → Task → Action → Result.

To keep spoken anecdotes focused and chronological, use STAR:

• Situation & Task: "Last winter, our pharmacy dispensary faced a 40-minute backlog during peak afternoon discharges."
• Action: "We introduced a dedicated express tray for single-item prescriptions."
• Result: "Within two weeks, average patient waiting times fell from forty minutes to under twelve minutes."
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Benchmark Exam Model 1 Level 1 Presentation Structure Benchmark (L1.1, L1.3)

Vocational Context: Community Health & Education Talk

Create a 3-part spoken talk outline (Introduction, 3 Main Points, Conclusion) explaining why all secondary school pupils should learn basic first aid and CPR.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Opening Hook & Purpose
'Good morning everyone. If someone collapsed with a cardiac arrest in front of you today, would you know how to perform CPR? My talk today explains why every secondary school pupil should receive two hours of mandatory first-aid training each year.'
Criteria: Engaging Hook & Clear Topic (L1.3)
Step 2 Three Sequenced Main Points
'First, immediate CPR in the first three minutes doubles a person's chance of survival before anambulance arrives. Second, teenagers often look after younger siblings and can treat choking or burns safely. Third, St John Ambulance provides free school training kits, so the cost to schools is minimal.'
Criteria: Clear 3-Point Sequence
Step 3 Concluding Summary & Q&A Invite
'To sum up, teaching CPR in schools is simple, low-cost, and saves hundreds of lives every year. Thank you for listening—what questions do you have?'
Criteria: Strong Conclusion & Q&A Transition
Final Accredited Response: Complete 3-minute talk structure featuring a rhetorical question hook, three clearly signposted arguments (survival rates, home safety, low cost), and a confident invitation for audience questions.
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Opening your talk with a direct question ('If someone collapsed in front of you today, would you know what to do?') grabs every listener's attention in the first 10 seconds.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Reading Verbatim from an A4 Sheet of Paper
Staring down at a typed essay and reading it aloud monotone will fail the spoken delivery criteria even if the written essay is well written.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Write your talk onto 4 small cue cards using large bullet-point keywords only.
Examiner Pitfall: Stopping Abruptly With 'Yeah, That's Everything'
Ending a formal presentation by muttering 'So yeah, that's all I've got' leaves the audience hanging and ruins your final impression.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always memorise your final two sentences: 'In conclusion, [1-sentence summary]. Thank you for listening, and I welcome any questions.'

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 3 Marks
Draft an attention-grabbing opening hook and roadmap (35–45 words) for a 4-minute Level 2 presentation proposing that your company should replace its diesel delivery vans with electric vehicles.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Open with a striking statistic or question about diesel fuel costs and urban clean-air charges.
2. State your proposal clearly.
3. Give a 3-point roadmap (fuel savings, clean-air zone compliance, and overnight depot charging).
Final Model Answer: 'Good morning everyone. Last year alone, rising diesel prices and city Clean Air Zone penalties cost our delivery fleet over £38,000. Today, I will demonstrate why transitioning to electric delivery vans is essential, covering three key areas: annual fuel savings, zero-emission zone compliance, and overnight depot charging.'
Examiner Tip: Hook Stat (£38,000 cost) + Clear Thesis + 3-Part Roadmap = the perfect 30-second presentation opening.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Convert this long, written paragraph into 4 concise Cue Card bullet points (maximum 6 words per bullet) so a candidate can speak naturally without reading:
'If we introduce a four-day working week at the call centre, staff morale will improve dramatically and fewer people will resign. Last year we spent £15,000 recruiting and training replacement call handlers because staff were burned out. By rotating Friday and Monday rest days, we can keep phone lines open five days a week while giving staff a three-day weekend.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Extract Keyword Bullet 1: Morale & staff retention.
2. Extract Keyword Bullet 2: £15,000 recruitment cost last year.
3. Extract Keyword Bullet 3: Rotate Monday/Friday rest days.
4. Extract Keyword Bullet 4: 100% 5-day phone cover maintained.
Final Model Answer: Cue Card 2 — Staff Retention & Cover:
• Boosts morale & stops burnout
• Saves £15,000 annual recruitment cost
• Rotate Monday / Friday rest days
• Maintains 100% 5-day phone cover
Examiner Tip: Each bullet contains just the trigger statistic or concept—allowing you to look up at your audience and form the sentences naturally.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
Write a 35-word concluding summary and Q&A invitation for a presentation on why your care home should adopt an electronic medication administration record (eMAR) tablet system.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Start with a clear concluding signpost ('In conclusion,' or 'To sum up,').
2. Summarise the three core benefits (eliminates paper transcription errors, saves nursing time, and strengthens CQC safety ratings).
3. Thank the audience and invite questions.
Final Model Answer: 'In conclusion, adopting the eMAR tablet system eliminates handwritten medication errors, saves nurses forty minutes per shift for direct resident care, and strengthens our CQC safety rating. Thank you for listening—I now welcome your questions.'
Examiner Tip: Using the Rule of Three in your final summary sentence makes your presentation end on a crisp, memorable note.
