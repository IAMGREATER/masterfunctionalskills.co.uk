Part 1: Quick-Check Answer Key
Quick answers for practice questions
Q1 Question 1: Exam Dos and Don'ts
(a) Using bullet points in a formal report → Recommended
(b) Using slang or text abbreviations like 'u' or 'thx' → Avoid (severely penalised)
(c) Writing one single giant paragraph without breaks → Avoid (fails organisation criteria)
(d) Using a range of simple, compound, and complex sentences → Recommended
(e) Leaving the last 5 minutes to check full stops and capitals → Recommended
Q2 Question 2: Word Count and Timing Targets
(a) Target word count for Level 2 Task 1 → 200 to 250 words
(b) Target word count for Level 2 Task 2 → 250 to 350 words
(c) Number of distinct punctuation marks to demonstrate at Level 2 → At least 4-5 (., !, ?, commas, apostrophes, semicolons/colons)
Q3 Question 3: Question 2: Pearson Edexcel Writing Criteria Breakdown
(a) Using consistent past and present verb tenses throughout → SPaG (Grammar)
(b) Including the recipient's address and 'Yours sincerely' in a formal letter → CFD (Form & Conventions)
(c) Spelling specialist technical and complex words accurately → SPaG (Spelling)
(d) Providing two distinct arguments supported by realistic workplace examples → CFD (Content & Detail)
(e) Using semicolons and colons accurately to link clauses and lists → SPaG (Punctuation)
(f) Structuring ideas into logically sequenced paragraphs with topic sentences → CFD (Organization & Cohesion)
(g) Adapting vocabulary and register appropriately for a formal executive director → CFD (Audience & Purpose)
(h) Ensuring subject-verb agreement with singular and plural collective nouns → SPaG (Grammar)
Q4 Question 4: Question 3: Meeting Word Count Expectations and Avoiding Traps
(a) Candidate writes 90 words when the guidance specifies 200-250 words. → Severe penalty under CFD for lack of development, detail, and substance
(b) Candidate writes 550 words for a 200-word task, running out of time for Task 2. → Catastrophic time management failure; leaves second task incomplete, losing up to 60% of total marks
(c) Candidate copies the entire exam scenario prompt word-for-word into their answer. → Copied words are discounted from word count; no credit given for copied text
(d) Candidate forgets to write a headline for a newspaper article task. → Loss of form and layout convention marks under CFD criteria
(e) Candidate writes their entire 250-word response in one unbroken block with no paragraphs. → Loss of structural organization marks under CFD; penalised for lack of cohesion
(f) Candidate misspells basic high-frequency homophones (there/their, to/too, its/it's). → Deductions under SPaG criteria; high-frequency spelling errors restrict higher band marks
(g) Candidate ignores the bullet points listed in the exam scenario prompt. → Fails to meet core assessment criteria; severe mark loss for incomplete content
(h) Candidate writes in faint pencil that does not scan clearly on digital exam scanners. → Risk of unreadable digital script; examiners cannot award marks to illegible text
Q5 Question 5: Question 4: Polishing Exam Scripts for Top Band Marks
(a) Standard: 'I want to complain about the food because it was cold and bad.' → Level 2: 'I am writing to express my profound dissatisfaction regarding the catering service, as the meal served was cold and utterly unpalatable.'
(b) Standard: 'We should get more computers because people need them.' → Level 2: 'Investing in additional computer terminals would significantly streamline administrative workflows and reduce digital bottlenecks.'
(c) Standard: 'The staff are nice and help everyone a lot.' → Level 2: 'The nursing team demonstrates exemplary professionalism, consistently providing compassionate and attentive patient care.'
(d) Standard: 'There are bad things about working from home too.' → Level 2: 'Conversely, remote working presents notable challenges, including professional isolation and reduced spontaneous collaboration.'
(e) Standard: 'Fix the leaking pipe now or the floor gets wet.' → Level 2: 'Immediate remedial maintenance is imperative to prevent structural water damage and hazardous slip risks.'
(f) Standard: 'I think apprenticeships are good for young people.' → Level 2: 'Apprenticeships offer invaluable vocational pathways, equipping emerging talent with industry-accredited practical expertise.'
(g) Standard: 'Write back to me soon.' → Level 2: 'I look forward to receiving your prompt response regarding this urgent matter.'
(h) Standard: 'The room was dirty and nobody cleaned it.' → Level 2: 'The clinical environment fell far below hygiene standards, showing obvious neglect of routine sanitisation schedules.'
Part 2: Real-Life Problem Solutions
Step-by-step methods and mark breakdown
Question 1 Worked Solution Pearson Edexcel SPaG Self-Audit
[3 Marks Available]
In Pearson Edexcel Level 2 Writing, examiners award marks across three distinct SPaG criteria:
1. Spelling accuracy of specialist and everyday vocabulary
2. Punctuation range and precision
3. Grammatical syntax and subject-verb agreement

Describe three specific strategies you will use during the final 5 minutes of your exam to maximise your SPaG score.
Strategy 1: Scan every sentence ending for missing full stops and verify the following word starts with a capital letter
[W1]
Strategy 2: Check homophones (there/their/they're, your/you're, its/it's) and common double consonants (e.g. accommodate, necessary)
[W1]
Strategy 3: Check subject-verb agreement on collective nouns and ensure no comma splices exist
[A1]
Correct Final Answer: 1. Verify sentence boundary punctuation (full stops & capital letters). 2. Targeted homophone and silent letter spelling check. 3. Check for comma splices and subject-verb agreement.
Quick Check Method: Actionable, exam-ready review strategies.
Common Mistake to Avoid: Merely saying 'I will re-read it' without specific targeted error checks.
