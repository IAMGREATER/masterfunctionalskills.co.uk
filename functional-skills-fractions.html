Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • L1.9 / L2.8 – Find fractions of whole number quantities or measurements.
  • L2.9 – Add, subtract, multiply, and divide proper and mixed fractions.

Golden Rules for Fractions: Operations & Fractions of Amounts

Golden Rule 1: Fraction of an Amount: Divide by Bottom, Multiply by Top

To find 3/8 of £480: divide £480 by 8 (denominator = £60), then multiply by 3 (numerator = £180).

Golden Rule / Method: Amount ÷ Denominator × Numerator
Golden Rule 2: Dividing Fractions: Keep, Change, Flip (KCF)

Keep the first fraction, Change division (÷) to multiplication (×), and Flip the second fraction upside down (its reciprocal).

Golden Rule / Method: Keep • Change • Flip (KCF)

Core Skill 1: Skill 1: Finding a Fraction of an Amount & Expressing One Number as a Fraction of Another

Essential Level 1 and Level 2 workplace fraction calculations.

  • Fraction of an Amount (Divide by Bottom, Times by Top): To find 5/9 of £630 → £630 ÷ 9 = £70, then £70 × 5 = £350.
  • Finding the Original Whole (Reverse Fraction): If 3/5 of a budget is £240, divide by top (£240 ÷ 3 = £80 for 1/5) and multiply by bottom (£80 × 5 = £400 total).
  • Simplifying Ratios/Fractions: Divide top and bottom by their Highest Common Factor (e.g. 45/60 ÷ 15/15 = 3/4).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Skill 2: Adding & Subtracting Fractions with Common Denominators

Finding the Lowest Common Multiple (LCM) before adding or subtracting.

To calculate 2 3/4 - 1 2/5:

  1. Convert Mixed Numbers to Improper Fractions: 2 3/4 = 11/4 and 1 2/5 = 7/5.
  2. Find Common Denominator (LCM of 4 and 5 is 20): 11/4 = 55/20 and 7/5 = 28/20.
  3. Subtract Numerators Only: (55 - 28)/20 = 27/20.
  4. Convert Back to Mixed Number: 27 ÷ 20 = 1 remainder 7 → 1 7/20.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Skill 3: Multiplying, Dividing (KCF) & Improper Fractions

Fast rules for multiplying numerators/denominators and Keep-Change-Flip.

Operation Method Rule Worked Example
Multiplication Top × Top over Bottom × Bottom 3/4 × 2/5 = 6/20 = 3/10
Division (KCF) Keep 1st, Change ÷ to ×, Flip 2nd 3/4 ÷ 2/5 = 3/4 × 5/2 = 15/8 = 1 7/8
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1/2)

Vocational Context: Residential Care Staff Scheduling

A residential care provider employs 72 care workers. On night duty, 3/8 of the workforce are deployed. Of the remaining staff, 2/3 work the day shift, and the rest are on scheduled leave.
How many staff members are on scheduled leave?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate staff on night duty (3/8 of 72)
72 ÷ 8 = 9
9 × 3 = 27 staff on night shift
Criteria: M1: Fraction of amount calculation
Step 2 Calculate remaining staff and day shift workers
Remaining staff = 72 - 27 = 45 staff.
Day shift = 2/3 of 45: (45 ÷ 3) × 2 = 15 × 2 = 30 staff.
Criteria: M1: Subtraction and second fraction step
Step 3 Calculate staff on scheduled leave
45 - 30 = 15 staff on scheduled leave.
Criteria: A1: Accurate final staff count
Final Accredited Answer: 15 care workers on scheduled leave
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Always read carefully whether the second fraction applies to the 'original total' or the 'remaining staff'. Here it was of the remaining staff.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2 Problem Solving)

Vocational Context: Healthcare Estates Planning

A hospital redevelops a 240-space staff car park:
• 1/6 of spaces are reserved for emergency doctors.
• 3/8 of the total spaces are allocated to nursing staff.
• 1/5 of the total spaces are converted into electric vehicle (EV) bays.
• All remaining spaces are available for general visitors.

The estates director requires at least 65 spaces for general visitors. Does this plan meet the requirement?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate spaces for emergency doctors and nursing staff
Doctors (1/6 of 240) = 240 ÷ 6 = 40 spaces.
Nurses (3/8 of 240) = (240 ÷ 8) × 3 = 30 × 3 = 90 spaces.
Criteria: M1: First two fractional allocations
Step 2 Calculate spaces for EV bays
EV bays (1/5 of 240) = 240 ÷ 5 = 48 spaces.
Criteria: M1: Third fractional allocation
Step 3 Subtract allocated spaces from 240 total
Allocated = 40 + 90 + 48 = 178 spaces.
General visitor spaces = 240 - 178 = 62 spaces.
Criteria: M1: Remaining visitor space calculation
Step 4 Compare against 65-space requirement
62 spaces < 65 spaces (3 spaces short). No, the plan does not meet the director's requirement.
Criteria: A1: Accurate conclusion supported by 62 spaces
Final Accredited Answer: No (Only 62 visitor spaces remain, which is 3 spaces short of the 65-space target).
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Because all three fractions are taken from the total 240 spaces, calculate each fraction directly from 240 and subtract their sum.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Adding Denominators Together
Adding 1/4 + 1/4 = 2/8 is wrong! When denominators are identical, only add numerators: 1/4 + 1/4 = 2/4 = 1/2.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Never add or subtract denominators; only add or subtract numerators over a common denominator.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Calculate 1 3/5 + 2 1/4, giving your answer as a mixed number in its simplest form.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Common denominator for 5 and 4 is 20: 3/5 = 12/20; 1/4 = 5/20.
2. Add whole numbers: 1 + 2 = 3.
3. Add fractions: 12/20 + 5/20 = 17/20.
4. Combine: 3 17/20.
Final Correct Answer: 3 17/20
Examiner Tip: Keep whole numbers and fractions separate when adding to avoid large improper numerators.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
Work out 2 1/4 ÷ 3/5 without a calculator. Give your answer as a mixed number in its simplest form.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Convert mixed number to improper fraction: 2 1/4 = 9/4.
2. Apply Keep-Change-Flip (KCF): 9/4 × 5/3 = 45/12.
3. Simplify by dividing by 3: 15/4 = 3 3/4.
Final Correct Answer: 3 3/4
Examiner Tip: Always convert mixed numbers into improper fractions BEFORE multiplying or dividing!
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A charity grant of £1,800 is split between three youth projects. Project A receives 4/9 of the grant, Project B receives 1/3 of the grant, and Project C receives the rest. How much money does Project C receive?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Project A = (1,800 ÷ 9) × 4 = 200 × 4 = £800.
2. Project B = 1,800 ÷ 3 = £600.
3. Project C = 1,800 - (800 + 600) = £400.
Final Correct Answer: £400
Examiner Tip: Check: 4/9 + 3/9 = 7/9, leaving 2/9 for Project C (2/9 of £1,800 = £400).
