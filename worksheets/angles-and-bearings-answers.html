Angles, Bearings & Turn Navigation Pearson Edexcel: Pearson Edexcel L1 & L2 Mathematics
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Core Skills (Warm-Up) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Three-Figure Compass Bearings) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
An air ambulance helicopter flies due South-East (SE) from Base Hospital A to Clinic B.
Write down the three-figure bearing of Clinic B from Base Hospital A, and calculate the back-bearing from Clinic B to Base Hospital A.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Outward bearing for South-East (halfway between 090° East and 180° South): 135° [M1]
2. Back-bearing: 135° + 180° = 315° (North-West) [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Outward: 135°, Back-bearing: 315°
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Omitting the leading zero on bearings under 100° or adding 90° instead of 180° for the reverse bearing.
🔄 Quick Check: 315° - 180° = 135°.
Question 2 (Level 1 Foundation — 2 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A straight hospital corridor is met by an angled fire-exit passage. One angle at the wall is 128°. Calculate the acute angle next to it on the straight wall.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Angles on a straight line sum to 180°. [M1]
2. 180° − 128° = 52°. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 52°
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Writing 65° instead of 065° loses the bearing accuracy mark.
🔄 Quick Check: 180° − 128° = 52°.
Section B: Everyday & Workplace Maths Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Air Ambulance Flight Navigation & Return Bearing (Level 1/2 Foundation)) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
An emergency transfer helicopter flies from Helipad H on a bearing of 074° to an accident site A.
(a) State whether 074° is an acute, right, obtuse, or reflex angle.
(b) Calculate the 3-figure back bearing required to fly from accident site A back to Helipad H.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Classify 074°: Since 074° is between 0° and 90°, it is an ACUTE angle. [A1]
2. Add 180° for the reverse bearing: 074° + 180° = 254°. [M1 / A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) Acute angle | (b) 254°
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Always write 3 digits for every bearing.
🔄 Quick Check: Always write bearings with 3 figures (074° and 254°).
Question 4 (Workplace Application — 2 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A delivery drone flies from Depot D to Drop-Zone Z on a bearing of 242°. Calculate the 3-figure back bearing from Drop-Zone Z to Depot D.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Since 242° > 180°, subtract 180°: 242° − 180° = 62°. [M1]
2. Pad to 3 digits: 062°. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 062°
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Measuring the angle at A instead of at B when asked for "the bearing of A from B".
🔄 Quick Check: Remember the leading zero: 062°!
Section C: Exam-Style Problem Solving Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Offshore Wind Farm Service Vessel Bearing & Scale Plot (Level 2 Multi-Step)) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A maintenance vessel sails from Harbour P to Turbine T. The angle measured anti-clockwise (West) from North is 65°, and the real-world distance is 28 km.
(a) Calculate the 3-figure clockwise bearing of Turbine T from Harbour P.
(b) Calculate the 3-figure back bearing of Harbour P from Turbine T.
(c) On a sea chart with scale 1 : 400,000 (where 1 cm = 4 km), how many cm from P should Turbine T be plotted?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Subtract 65° from 360° for the clockwise bearing from P: 360° − 65° = 295°. [M1 / A1]
2. Subtract 180° for the back bearing from T to P: 295° − 180° = 115°. [A1]
3. Divide 28 km by 4 km/cm for the chart distance: 28 km ÷ 4 km = 7.0 cm. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) 295° | (b) 115° | (c) 7.0 cm
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Circle the letter after the word "FROM"—that is where your North line and protractor must sit!
🔄 Quick Check: Whenever an angle is given to the West (left) of North, subtract it from 360° to get the clockwise bearing.
Question 6 (Level 2 Multi-Step Problem Solving — 3 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Three cables meet at a central mast point. The angles between the first two pairs of cables are 115° and 140°. Calculate the third angle around the point.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Sum of known angles = 115° + 140° = 255°. [M1]
2. Third angle = 360° − 255° = 105°. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 105°
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Stating a conclusion without showing the full numerical comparison values.
🔄 Quick Check: Angles around a full point always add up to 360°.
