English Level 2 Writing Examples & Model Responses Pearson Edexcel: Pearson Edexcel Functional Skills English (WRITING)
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Foundational Concept Check Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Crafting an Engaging Level 2 Article/Blog Headline & Strapline) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
You are writing a Level 2 online blog post for apprentice nurses titled How to Survive Your First Night Shift.
Write an engaging headline (using alliteration or a rhetorical question) and a one-sentence opening hook addressing the reader directly.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Engaging headline using rhetorical device: e.g. 'Beat the 3 A.M. Burnout: The Apprentice Nurse's Guide to Night Shifts' [A1]
2. Direct-address opening hook: e.g. 'Have you ever stared at the ward clock at 3 a.m. wondering how you will stay awake until handover?' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Headline: Beat the 3 A.M. Burnout: Mastering Your First Night Shift | Hook: Have you ever stared at the ward clock at 3 a.m. wondering how to keep your energy up until morning handover?
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Using Dear Sir/Madam in a blog or article (letters only!).
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Includes a catchy headline + direct address ('you') hook.
Question 2 (Core Skill Application — 3 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
You are asked to write a formal letter to the Director of Estates, Mrs Helen Vance, proposing improvements to the staff canteen. Write: (a) the correct salutation, (b) an appropriate formal opening sentence, and (c) the correct complimentary close.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Salutation: 'Dear Mrs Vance,' (named recipient). [M1]
2. Opening sentence: 'I am writing on behalf of the clinical staff team to propose three practical improvements to the hospital canteen facilities.' [M1]
3. Complimentary close: 'Yours sincerely,' followed by your full name. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) Dear Mrs Vance, | (b) I am writing on behalf of the clinical staff team to propose three practical improvements to the hospital canteen facilities. | (c) Yours sincerely, [Full Name]
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Candidates frequently write an article or blog post as plain continuous paragraphs without a headline or subheadings, losing essential format and organisational marks.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Because the recipient's name (Mrs Vance) is given in the prompt, you must use 'Yours sincerely', never 'Yours faithfully'.
Section B: Functional Workplace Application Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Model 1: Community Wellbeing Blog / Article — Launching a Workplace Cycle-to-Work Scheme) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Your hospital trust is launching a new Cycle-to-Work and secure bike storage scheme. Write a blog post (250–300 words) for the staff intranet persuading employees to join the scheme, explaining the health and financial benefits, and addressing safety concerns.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Headline, Byline & Engaging Opening Hook: Headline: Ditch the Traffic Jam: Why Our New Cycle-to-Work Scheme Is a Win for Your Wallet and Wellbeing | Posted by Samira Khan, Staff Wellbeing Coordinator | 14 October 2026 | Opening: Are you tired of sitting in gridlocked hospital car park queues every morning while paying rising fuel prices? Next month, our Trust is launching an upgraded Cycle-to-Work programme designed for every staff member. [A1]
2. Subheading 1: Financial & Physical Health Benefits: Save Up to 40% While Boosting Your Energy | Through salary sacrifice, staff can save up to 40% on a brand-new commuter bike or electric bicycle, spreading the cost interest-free over twelve months. Furthermore, cycling just twenty minutes each way burns calories, reduces cardiovascular risk, and ensures you arrive on the ward feeling energised rather than stressed. [A1]
3. Subheading 2: Addressing Safety & Storage + Interactive Call to Action: Safe, Lit Routes and Card-Access Bike Hubs | We know that bike security and wet weather have previously deterred staff; consequently, the Estates team has installed heated shower rooms and CCTV-monitored cycle pods outside the Main Wing. | Ready to Ride? Click the Staff Benefits tab today to claim your voucher, or drop your questions in the comments box below! [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Full-mark Level 2 blog response featuring a bold headline, date/author byline, two thematic subheadings, rhetorical question hook, semicolon connecting independent clauses, statistical detail (40% savings), and interactive blog comment call-to-action.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Always write a clear, bold Headline at the top of every article/blog, and include at least two short subheadings to divide your main sections.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Notice how a blog post differs from a formal report: it uses direct address ('you', 'our Trust'), an engaging conversational yet polished tone, subheadings, and invites reader comments at the end.
Question 4 (Exam-Style Task — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Write an engaging Headline, Byline, and 2-sentence opening hook (including a rhetorical question) for a community blog post encouraging local residents to volunteer at a weekend riverbank clean-up.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Write a catchy, action-oriented headline. [M1]
2. Add a blog byline (Author + Date). [M1]
3. Open with a rhetorical question about the local river and invite residents to Saturday's clean-up. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Headline: Let’s Restore Our Riverbank: Join Saturday’s Two-Hour Community Clean-Up!
Posted by Liam O'Connor | 14 October 2026 | Riverside Neighbourhood Blog

Have you walked along the River Cherwell footpath recently and felt disheartened by the plastic bottles and discarded shopping trolleys choking our wildlife habitats? This Saturday from 10:00 to 12:00, fifty local volunteers are teaming up to transform our riverbank—and we need your help to make a lasting difference.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Writing 'don't', 'can't', 'it's', or 'kids' in a formal letter to a Council Director undermines your register mark.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Combining a direct rhetorical question with a dash ('—and we need your help') creates an authentic, lively blog voice.
Section C: Pearson Edexcel Exam-Style Analysis & Evaluation Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Model 2: Formal Complaint & Proposal Letter to Local Council Highways Department) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Write a formal letter (250–300 words) to Mr David Vance, Head of Highways at Westshire Council, complaining about dangerous speeding outside Oakfield Primary School and proposing specific traffic-calming measures.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Full Letter Address Block, Date & Named Salutation: Sender address + Date (top right); Mr David Vance, Head of Highways, Westshire Council (left); Salutation: Dear Mr Vance, + Subject line: Re: Urgent Request for Traffic-Calming Measures on Oakfield Lane. [A1]
2. Body Paragraphs (Evidence + Counter-Argument + Solutions): Detail morning peak speeding (vehicles exceeding 40 mph in a 20 mph zone) and last week's near-miss on the zebra crossing. Propose three measures: digital speed-indicator signs, raised speed cushions, and peak-hour school crossing patrols. [A1]
3. Formal Close & Matching Sign-Off: Urge a site inspection before winter dark mornings arrive and close with Yours sincerely, + full name. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark:
28 Oakfield Lane, Westshire, WS4 2LP
14 October 2026

Mr David Vance, Head of Highways
Westshire County Council, Civic Way, WS1 1AA

Dear Mr Vance,

Re: Urgent Request for Traffic-Calming Measures Outside Oakfield Primary School

I am writing on behalf of the Oakfield Residents' Association to express our grave concern regarding persistent vehicle speeding along Oakfield Lane between 08:15 and 09:00.

Although Oakfield Lane is designated as a 20 mph school zone, commuter vehicles routinely use the street as a shortcut, frequently exceeding 40 mph. Last Thursday morning, a delivery van failed to stop at the pedestrian crossing, narrowly missing two seven-year-old pupils; consequently, parents now fear a fatal collision is inevitable unless physical deterrents are installed.

Whilst we recognise that council highways budgets are tightly constrained this financial year, the cost of preventative safety infrastructure is minimal compared to the human cost of a serious accident. We therefore urge your department to implement three urgent measures: installing solar-powered digital speed-indicator signs, constructing raised speed cushions on the approach to the school gates, and funding a dedicated school crossing patrol officer.

I invite a highways engineer to observe morning drop-off traffic next week and look forward to your prompt written response.

Yours sincerely,
Rebecca Sterling
Chair, Oakfield Residents' Association
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: In formal tasks, write 'do not', 'cannot', 'it is', and 'pupils/children'.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Study how this letter hits every single Level 2 top-band requirement: two addresses, date, 'Re:' line, fronted 'Although' clause, semicolon before 'consequently', counter-argument starting with 'Whilst', colon introducing a 3-item list, and 'Yours sincerely'.
Question 6 (Level 2 Mastery Challenge — 4 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Upgrade this weak Band 1 report conclusion into a formal, high-scoring Band 3 Level 2 report Recommendations section:
'So basically I think the office should buy recycling bins for paper and plastic and tell staff to turn off their computers at night so we save money.'
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Add a numbered subheading ('3.0 Strategic Recommendations'). [M1]
2. Replace casual phrasing ('So basically I think') with an authoritative formal lead-in ending with a colon. [M1]
3. Format the two recommendations as parallel bullet points with bold lead-ins and measurable impacts. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 3.0 Strategic Recommendations
In light of the audit findings, senior management should implement the following two sustainability measures immediately:
• Dual-Stream Recycling Stations: Install colour-coded paper and plastic recycling bins next to every printer hub to divert 70% of office waste from landfill.
• Mandatory Overnight Shutdown Policy: Instruct all staff to power down desktop terminals and monitors at 17:30 daily, reducing annual electricity expenditure by approximately £1,400.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Failing to proofread spelling, punctuation, and grammar after completing the draft.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Replacing 'So basically I think' with 'In light of the audit findings, senior management should implement...' transforms the authority of your writing.
