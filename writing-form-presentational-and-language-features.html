Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.23 / L2.23 – Write in a variety of forms, utilising appropriate format conventions, layouts, and structural features.
  • L2.24 – Demonstrate full mastery of professional conventions across formal letters, emails, articles, blogs, and reports.

Golden Rules for Format & Conventions: Letters, Emails, Articles & Reports

Golden Rule 1: Master the Non-Negotiable Letter Sign-Off Rule

If you address a named person (e.g. 'Dear Mr Phillips' or 'Dear Mrs Patel'), you MUST sign off 'Yours sincerely,'. If you do not know the recipient's name (e.g. 'Dear Sir or Madam,' or 'Dear Editor,'), you MUST sign off 'Yours faithfully,'.

Golden Rule / Technique: Named Recipient = Yours sincerely | Sir or Madam = Yours faithfully (Remember: never two S's together!)
Golden Rule 2: Articles Need Headlines; Reports Need Numbered Sections; Emails Need Subject Lines

Examiners deduct format marks immediately if an article lacks a headline, a report lacks subheadings, or an email lacks To/Subject lines.

Golden Rule / Technique: Article = Headline + Subheadings | Report = Title + Numbered Headings | Email = To + Subject + Sign-off

Core Skill 3: Language Features Matched to Format Types

Choosing the right rhetorical and grammatical tools for articles vs reports.

  • Articles & Blogs: Use direct address ("Have you ever wondered...?"), inclusive pronouns ("our neighbourhood"), short punchy subheadings, and a lively, motivating tone.
  • Formal Reports: Avoid personal emotion or rhetorical questions. Use objective third-person phrasing ("The audit revealed that 68% of respondents...") and numbered recommendations.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Forgetting the Subject Line in Email Tasks
Writing an email response without 'To:' and 'Subject:' lines at the top automatically forfeits format marks in Pearson Edexcel and City & Guilds exams.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Even if the onscreen exam box doesn't have pre-printed To/Subject boxes, always type 'To:' and 'Subject:' on the first two lines of your email.
Examiner Pitfall: Signing Off an Article or Report with 'Yours sincerely'
Ending a magazine article or a workplace report with 'Yours sincerely, John' shows the examiner you have confused letter conventions with article/report conventions.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Only letters and formal emails use 'Yours sincerely / faithfully'. Articles end with a call to action; reports end with 'Report compiled by: [Name, Role]'.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
State the exact salutation and complimentary close (sign-off) required for each formal letter below:
1. A letter addressed to Ms Samantha Davies, Operations Director.
2. A letter addressed to The Customer Services Manager (name unknown).
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Named recipient (Ms Davies) takes 'Dear Ms Davies,' and 'Yours sincerely,'.
2. Unnamed recipient takes 'Dear Sir or Madam,' and 'Yours faithfully,'.
Final Model Answer: 1. Salutation: Dear Ms Davies, | Sign-off: Yours sincerely,
2. Salutation: Dear Sir or Madam, | Sign-off: Yours faithfully,
Examiner Tip: Memory rule: Never put two S's together (Sir/Madam never pairs with Sincerely!).
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
You are asked to write an email to all warehouse staff reminding them that the annual fire drill takes place tomorrow at 10:30 am and that everyone must assemble at Point B. Write the exact email header block (To, Subject, Salutation) and the opening sentence.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Write 'To: All Warehouse Staff (staff@warehouselogistics.co.uk)'.
2. Write a specific, informative 'Subject:' line including the time.
3. Include a professional greeting and clear opening sentence.
Final Model Answer: To: allstaff@warehouselogistics.co.uk
Subject: Mandatory Annual Fire Drill – Tomorrow at 10:30 am (Assembly Point B)

Dear Colleagues,

Please be reminded that our mandatory annual warehouse fire evacuation drill will take place tomorrow morning at 10:30 am prompt, and all staff must evacuate immediately to Assembly Point B in the north car park.
Examiner Tip: A strong Subject line summarizes the entire purpose of the email in 6 to 10 words.
Exam Practice Question 3 4 Marks
A student wrote a workplace report on canteen energy waste as one giant paragraph starting with 'Dear Manager, I walked around the kitchen and saw the ovens left on...'.
Rewrite the opening of this report using correct formal report conventions (Report Title, To/From/Date block, and Section 1.0 Introduction).
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Remove the letter greeting ('Dear Manager') and casual first-person storytelling.
2. Add a formal Report Title and To/From/Date metadata block.
3. Add numbered subheading '1.0 Introduction' and write an objective opening statement.
Final Model Answer: REPORT ON REDUCING ENERGY WASTE IN THE STAFF CANTEEN
To: Facilities Director
From: Environmental Compliance Officer
Date: 14 October 2026

1.0 Introduction & Audit Scope
This report presents the findings of a five-day energy audit conducted in the main staff canteen kitchen between 5 and 9 October 2026, identifying significant electricity wastage caused by commercial ovens and chillers remaining switched on outside operating hours.
Examiner Tip: Reports never start with 'Dear...'. Always use a bold Title, To/From/Date lines, and numbered section headings.
