Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills Mathematics outcomes:

  • L1.10 / L2.10 – Read, write, order and compare decimals up to three decimal places.
  • L1.11 / L2.10 – Perform the four operations with decimals up to two decimal places in practical contexts.

Three Non-Negotiable Golden Rules for Decimals & Place Value

Golden Rule 1: Always Format Currency to Exactly 2 Decimal Places

In Pearson Edexcel and City & Guilds exams, omitting the trailing zero on pounds loses the final accuracy mark [A1]:

✗ £14.5 or £14.500 (LOSES MARKS)
✓ £14.50 (MANDATORY ACCREDITED FORMAT)
Golden Rule 2: Dividing by Decimals: Scale Both Numbers Equally

Never divide by a decimal divisor using bus-stop directly. Multiply both numbers by 10, 100, or 1,000 to transform the divisor into a whole number before dividing:

£18 ÷ 0.25 → (× 100) → £1,800 ÷ 25 = 72
Golden Rule 3: Pad Whole Numbers with Zeros Before Subtraction

When subtracting a decimal from a whole number (e.g. paying with a £20 note), write £20.00 with two zeros so you can perform proper place-value exchanges:

£20 − £6.45 → Write as: £20.00 − £6.45 = £13.55

Core Skill 2: Multiplying Decimals (The 3-Stage Decimal Place Protocol) Core L1 / L2 Method

To multiply decimals without calculator errors, follow the universal 3-stage protocol: Strip → Multiply → Restore.

Stage 1: Strip Decimals to Whole Numbers
Target: Calculate 2.45 × 1.2
Temporarily ignore the decimal points:
245 × 12
Break down by tens and units:
• 245 × 10 = 2,450
• 245 × 2 = 490
• 2,450 + 490 = 2,940
Stage 2: Count Original Decimal Places
Count digits behind the decimal points in the question:
2.45 2 decimal places
1.2 1 decimal place
Total places to restore: 3 decimal places
Stage 3: Restore the Decimal Point
Count 3 positions left from the end of 2940:
2 . 9 4 0 → 2.94
(Trailing zero can be dropped unless working with currency £2.94)
Final Output: 2.45 × 1.2 = 2.94
Core Skill 3: Dividing by a Decimal (Equivalent Fraction Scaling) Non-Calculator Master Strategy

Division by a decimal is never performed directly. Write the division as a fraction and scale both the top and bottom by 10, 100, or 1,000 so the divisor (bottom number) becomes a whole number.

Original Division Scaling Multiplier Whole-Number Equivalent Quotient
14.4 ÷ 0.6 × 10 (1 d.p.) 14.4 × 100.6 × 10 = 1446 24
4.5 ÷ 0.15 × 100 (2 d.p.) 4.5 × 1000.15 × 100 = 45015 30
3.6 ÷ 0.009 × 1,000 (3 d.p.) 3.6 × 1,0000.009 × 1,000 = 3,6009 400
Why Does This Work?
Multiplying both numerator and denominator by the same power of 10 creates an equivalent fraction of identical mathematical value. The answer to 450 ÷ 15 is exactly identical to 4.5 ÷ 0.15 — zero remainder, zero confusion!

Vocational Context: Clinical Pharmacy Fluid Dispensing

A clinical pharmacy team receives a bulk container holding 4.5 litres of sterile saline solution. The pharmacist dispenses the solution into unit-dose ampoules containing exactly 0.15 litres each.
Calculate how many complete dose ampoules the pharmacist can fill from the container.
Step 1: Clear the Decimal Divisor
The divisor (0.15) has 2 decimal places. Scale both numbers by 100:
4.5 × 1000.15 × 100 = 45015
[M1] Division setup & scaling method
Step 2: Integer Division
Divide 450 by 15 (use 15 times table or chunking):
15 × 3 = 45 → 15 × 30 = 450
450 ÷ 15 = 30
[M1] Process to calculate quotient
Final Accredited Answer: The pharmacist can fill exactly 30 complete dose ampoules.
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Notice how shifting the decimal point by two places turns an intimidating decimal division into straightforward mental arithmetic (450 ÷ 15 = 30).

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Trap 1: Dropping the Trailing Zero on Currency
Writing £3.4 or £17.6 instead of £3.40 or £17.60 forfeits the accuracy mark on Pearson Edexcel mark schemes.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always review money answers before completing your exam and verify that exactly two digits follow the decimal point.
Trap 2: Subtracting Decimals from Integers Without Padding Zeros
When asked to calculate £20 − £6.45, learners often mistakenly subtract 20 − 6 = 14 and append .45 to get £14.45!
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Write £20.00 with two zeros. Exchange 1 unit into tenths and hundredths: £20.00 − £6.45 = £13.55.
Trap 3: Unequal Power-of-10 Shifting in Decimal Division
In 18.9 ÷ 0.07, multiplying 0.07 by 100 to get 7, but only multiplying 18.9 by 10 to get 189, gives 189 ÷ 7 = 27 (off by a factor of 10!).
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Treat the division as an equivalent fraction. Whatever power of 10 you multiply the divisor by, you MUST multiply the dividend by the exact same power: 18.9 × 100 = 1,890 → 1,890 ÷ 7 = 270.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A courier service charges £6.50 for the first kilogram of package weight, plus £1.85 for each additional kilogram.
Calculate the total cost to deliver a 7 kg package.
Pearson Edexcel Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Additional weight: 7 kg − 1 kg = 6 kg
2. Cost of additional weight: 6 × £1.85 = £11.10
3. Total cost: £6.50 + £11.10 = £17.60
[M1] 6 × 1.85 + 6.50
Final Answer: £17.60 (formatted to 2 decimal places)
[A1] Fully accurate £17.60
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
Without using a calculator, evaluate: 18.9 ÷ 0.07.
Pearson Edexcel Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Scale both numbers by 100: 18.9 × 100 = 1,890 and 0.07 × 100 = 7
2. Integer division: 1,890 ÷ 7 = 270
3. Check via multiplication: 270 × 0.07 = 18.90 ✓
[M1] Scaling to 1,890 ÷ 7
Final Answer: 270
[A1] Accurate integer 270
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A laboratory technician has a 2.5 litre stock bottle of clinical buffer solution. She uses 0.375 litres for Assay A, 0.85 litres for Assay B, and fills 4 sample vials with 0.125 litres each.
How many litres of buffer solution remain in the stock bottle?
Pearson Edexcel Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Sample vials: 4 × 0.125 = 0.500 litres
2. Total buffer used: 0.375 + 0.850 + 0.500 = 1.725 litres
3. Remaining in bottle: 2.500 − 1.725 = 0.775 litres
[M1] 4 × 0.125 = 0.5 [M1] Subtraction from 2.500
Final Answer: 0.775 litres remaining
[A1] Fully accurate 0.775 litres
