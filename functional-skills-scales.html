Golden Rules for Reading Gauges, Dials & Measuring Scales

Golden Rule 1: Value of 1 Small Division = (Gap Between Numbers) ÷ (Number of Spaces)

Never guess what each small tick mark is worth! Pick two adjacent numbered marks, subtract to find the gap, and divide by the number of SPACES (jumps) between them.

Golden Rule / Method: 1 Division = (Higher Number − Lower Number) ÷ Number of Spaces
Golden Rule 2: Check the Units (kg vs g, L vs ml) Before Calculating

A scale marked 1, 2, 3 kg with 10 subdivisions has ticks worth 0.1 kg (= 100 g). Always check whether the question asks for the answer in kg or in grams!

Golden Rule / Method: 0.1 kg = 100 g • 0.1 L = 100 ml • Count SPACES, Not Tick Lines

Core Skill 1: The 4-Step Method for Unlabelled Scale Divisions

Counting spaces (jumps) rather than tick marks to avoid off-by-one errors.

  1. Find two consecutive numbered marks: e.g. 40 and 60.
  2. Subtract to find the gap: 60 − 40 = 20 units.
  3. Count the SPACES (jumps) from 40 to 60: e.g. 4 spaces (which has 3 unnumbered tick lines inside).
  4. Divide Gap by Spaces: 20 ÷ 4 = 5 units per division (40, 45, 50, 55, 60).

Core Skill 3: Fractional Fuel Tank & Battery Gauges (1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, Full)

Converting needle positions on vehicle fuel dials into litres.

Vehicle fuel gauges are usually split into 8 equal subdivisions (each tick = 1/8 of a tank):

  • If a full tank holds 64 litres, then 1 tick mark (1/8) = 64 ÷ 8 = 8 litres.
  • If the needle rises from 1/8 full to 7/8 full, you added 6/8 (3/4) of a tank = 6 × 8 L = 48 litres.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1)

Vocational Context: Respiratory Ward Medical Gas Safety

A ward oxygen cylinder pressure gauge has major numbered marks at 100 bar and 150 bar, with 10 equal spaces between them.
(a) What pressure does each small tick mark represent?
(b) The pointer sits 7 small marks past 100 bar. What is the cylinder pressure, and is it above the minimum safe operating pressure of 130 bar?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate value of 1 small division
(150 − 100) ÷ 10 spaces = 50 ÷ 10 = 5 bar per mark.
Criteria: M1 / A1: 5 bar per division
Step 2 Calculate pressure at 7 marks past 100 bar
100 + (7 × 5) = 135 bar.
Criteria: M1: Accurate reading (135 bar)
Step 3 Compare 135 bar with 130 bar safe minimum
Yes, 135 bar is above the 130 bar safe minimum.
Criteria: A1: Valid comparison conclusion
Final Accredited Answer: (a) 5 bar per mark | (b) 135 bar — Yes, above the 130 bar minimum
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Count by 5s from 100 up to 150 to double-check your scale before reading the pointer.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2)

Vocational Context: Fleet Fuel Management

An ambulance has a fuel tank capacity of 80 litres. Its fuel gauge is divided into 8 equal subdivisions from Empty (0) to Full (F).
• Before refuelling, the pointer is at 1 subdivision above Empty.
• The driver pumps diesel until the pointer reaches 7 subdivisions above Empty.
• Diesel costs 145.9p per litre.
Calculate the total cost of the diesel added, rounded to the nearest penny.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate litres per subdivision (80 L ÷ 8)
80 litres ÷ 8 subdivisions = 10 litres per subdivision.
Criteria: M1: 10 litres per mark
Step 2 Calculate litres added from mark 1 to mark 7
Subdivisions added = 7 − 1 = 6 subdivisions.
Litres added = 6 × 10 L = 60 litres.
Criteria: M1: 60 litres added
Step 3 Convert 145.9p to £1.459 and multiply by 60 litres
60 × £1.459 = £87.54.
Criteria: M1 / A1: Accurate fuel cost (£87.54)
Final Accredited Answer: £87.54 (60 litres added)
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Remember to convert 145.9p into £1.459 (divide by 100) so your final answer is in pounds and pence.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Counting the Internal Tick Lines Instead of the Spaces
Between 0 and 100, if there are 3 internal lines, there are 4 SPACES (25 each). Dividing 100 by 3 gives 33.3 instead of 25!
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Count the JUMPS from the first numbered line to the second numbered line.
Examiner Pitfall: Assuming Every Small Mark Equals 1
Reading 3 marks past 20 as "23" when each mark actually represents 2 units (26) or 5 units (35).
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always write "1 mark = ..." before writing down your reading.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A kitchen weighing scale has numbered marks at 200 g and 400 g, with 4 equal spaces between them. A parcel moves the pointer to 3 marks past 200 g. What is the weight of the parcel?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. 1 space = (400 − 200) ÷ 4 = 200 ÷ 4 = 50 g.
2. Weight = 200 g + (3 × 50 g) = 350 g.
Final Correct Answer: 350 g
Examiner Tip: Check: 200, 250, 300, 350, 400.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
A clinical syringe is marked at 1.0 ml and 2.0 ml with 5 equal subdivisions between them. A nurse draws liquid up to 4 subdivisions past 1.0 ml. How many ml of liquid are in the syringe?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. 1 subdivision = (2.0 − 1.0) ÷ 5 = 0.2 ml.
2. Reading = 1.0 + (4 × 0.2) = 1.8 ml.
Final Correct Answer: 1.8 ml
Examiner Tip: With 5 spaces per whole unit, each tick mark is 0.2.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A boiler pressure dial has major marks at 1.0 bar and 2.0 bar with 4 equal spaces between them. Normal safe pressure is between 1.2 bar and 1.8 bar. If the needle points exactly at 3 marks past 1.0 bar, what is the pressure in bar and is the boiler within the safe range?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. 1 space = (2.0 − 1.0) ÷ 4 = 0.25 bar.
2. Reading = 1.0 + (3 × 0.25) = 1.75 bar.
3. Since 1.75 bar is between 1.2 and 1.8 bar, YES, it is within the safe range.
Final Correct Answer: 1.75 bar; Yes, within the safe range (1.2 to 1.8 bar)
Examiner Tip: 1.0 ÷ 4 = 0.25 bar per mark.
