Part 1: Quick-Check Answer Key
Quick answers for practice questions
Q1 Question 1: Assessment Criteria True or False
(a) You must prepare a 10-page PowerPoint slide deck for the presentation. → False (cue cards or brief notes are sufficient; slides are optional)
(b) You can pass even if you never ask any questions during discussions. → False (asking relevant questions is a mandatory criterion)
(c) Discussions must be carried out in a formal, respectful register. → True (Standard English and professional register required)
(d) You are permitted to glance at bullet-pointed cue cards while presenting. → True (cue cards are encouraged; reading a verbatim script is penalised)
Q2 Question 2: Mandatory Role Checklists
(a) Deliver a continuous 3-5 minute spoken talk → Task 2 (Presentation)
(b) Negotiate consensus and invite others to contribute → Task 1 (Discussion)
(c) Answer questions from peers and assessor → Task 2 (Presentation Q&A)
(d) Use appropriate non-verbal body language and maintain eye contact → Both
Q3 Question 3: Question 2: Pearson Edexcel SLC Exam Format and Structure
(a) What are the two mandatory components of the SLC assessment? → 1. Formal Presentation with Q&A; 2. Formal Group Discussion
(b) How long must an individual Level 2 presentation typically last? → 3 to 4 minutes (plus 2 to 3 minutes answering audience questions)
(c) Can you read your entire presentation word-for-word from a printed script? → No (script reading loses presentation marks; use bulleted prompt cue cards)
(d) How many people typically participate in the formal group discussion? → 3 to 5 candidates (small collaborative group)
(e) What must you do during another candidate's presentation to secure listening marks? → Listen actively, take brief notes, and ask a relevant, probing or clarifying question during Q&A
(f) Can you pass the exam if you speak fluently in the presentation but remain completely silent in the discussion? → No (must meet all criteria across both components; silence in discussion results in fail)
(g) Are presentation visual aids (slides, charts, handouts) mandatory? → Yes or highly recommended (Level 2 requires effective use of supporting materials or detailed visuals)
(h) Who assesses and records the official Functional Skills SLC grade? → An accredited assessor/tutor using Pearson Edexcel observation record sheets, subject to Standards Verification
Q4 Question 4: Question 3: Designing Presentation Cue Cards for Confident Delivery
(a) Writing an entire 400-word speech in tiny handwriting on a single cue card → Bad Practice (X - candidate will get lost, squint, and read like a robot)
(b) Writing 3-5 bold bullet points per card with keywords and statistics highlighted → Best Practice (B - prompts memory while preserving natural eye contact)
(c) Numbering cards (1, 2, 3) in the top-right corner in case they are dropped → Best Practice (B - enables instant reordering if dropped or shuffled)
(d) Including reminder prompts in brackets like '[PAUSE - LOOK UP - SMILE]' → Best Practice (B - regulates pacing and encourages engaging non-verbal cues)
(e) Writing on thin flimsy paper that flutters and rattles when hands shake → Bad Practice (X - amplifies nervous hand tremors; use sturdy index cards)
(f) Color-coding cards: Blue for intro, Green for evidence, Red for conclusion → Best Practice (B - visual signposting aids rapid mental orientation)
(g) Holding the cards directly in front of your face while speaking → Bad Practice (X - blocks voice projection and breaks eye contact with audience)
(h) Practicing out loud three times using only the cue cards before exam day → Best Practice (B - cements flow, timing, and confident independent delivery)
Q5 Question 5: Question 4: Mastering the Group Discussion Component
(a) Candidate dominates 80% of the conversation and frequently talks over other speakers → Poor / Penalised (fails collaborative group discussion criteria)
(b) Candidate speaks three times, each time validating a peer's point, adding new evidence, and asking an open question → Top Band (exemplary collaboration, active listening, and progression)
(c) Candidate agrees with every single statement by saying 'Yeah, I agree' with no elaboration → Low Band (fails to develop arguments or contribute substantive content)
(d) Candidate notices a quiet peer and says: 'Priya, what has your experience been with this issue?' → Top Band (demonstrates facilitation and inclusive leadership)
(e) Candidate gets visibly angry and tells a peer their opinion is stupid → Penalised (violates respectful professional discourse criteria)
(f) Candidate introduces a relevant news statistic to resolve a disagreement between two peers → Top Band (synthesizes evidence to move the debate forward constructively)
(g) Candidate brings the group back to the topic: 'We have five minutes left; let's decide on the recommendation.' → Top Band (effective timekeeping and steering toward outcome)
(h) Candidate looks only at the tutor rather than maintaining eye contact with peers in the circle → Poor (must interact with group members, not address the assessor)
Part 2: Real-Life Problem Solutions
Step-by-step methods and mark breakdown
Question 1 Worked Solution Pearson Edexcel SLC Candidate Performance Audit
[3 Marks Available]
During an SLC Task 1 formal discussion on workplace training, candidate Jamal speaks passionately for 6 uninterrupted minutes, citing numerous personal stories. When other candidates attempt to speak, Jamal talks over them. He asks no questions of others.

(a) Identify two specific Pearson Edexcel assessment criteria Jamal failed to demonstrate.
(b) State two concrete pieces of advice you would give Jamal before his reassessment.
Criteria failed: 1. Respecting turn-taking / not dominating discussion. 2. Listening actively and asking relevant questions of others
[S1]
Advice 1: Pause after making a 1-minute point and explicitly invite a peer to share their perspective
[S1]
Advice 2: Prepare 2-3 open questions in advance to demonstrate active listening and inquiry
[A1]
Correct Final Answer: (a) Failed criteria: 1. Facilitating turn-taking without interrupting/dominating. 2. Asking relevant questions to extend discussion. (b) Advice: 1. Keep individual contributions under 90 seconds, then pass the floor to others. 2. Actively ask open questions ('How does your department handle this, Sarah?').
Quick Check Method: Identifies assessment criteria failures and provides actionable coaching advice.
Common Mistake to Avoid: Praising Jamal for speaking for 6 minutes (dominating is a major failure).
