Section A: Core Skills (The Basics) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Level 1 Using Subheadings to Navigate a Document) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A community leisure centre guide is divided into four bold sections:
Section 1: Pool & Sauna Opening Hours
Section 2: Peak & Off-Peak Membership Tariffs
Section 3: Crèche & Junior Swim Rules
Section 4: Car Parking & Bus Routes

State which section a parent should read to check the minimum age for children in the pool, and explain how subheadings help readers navigate the guide.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify correct section: Section 3: Crèche & Junior Swim Rules [A1]
2. Explain benefit: Subheadings divide the guide by topic so readers can scan and jump straight to the section they need without reading the whole text [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Section 3: Crèche & Junior Swim Rules. Subheadings allow readers to scan and go straight to their required topic without reading every word.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Writing just 'Section 3' without explaining how subheadings aid reader navigation.
🔄 Quick Check: Does Section 3 cover children's swimming rules? Yes.
Question 2 (Level 1 Numbered Lists vs Bullet Points) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A laboratory chemical spill card uses a bulleted list for 'Kit Inside the Spill Bucket (gloves, goggles, absorbent granules)' and a numbered list (1, 2, 3, 4) for 'Emergency Clean-Up Procedure'.

Explain why the writer used a numbered list for the clean-up procedure instead of ordinary bullet points.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Chronological sequence: Numbers (1 to 4) show the exact step-by-step chronological order that must be followed [A1]
2. Safety compliance: Ensures staff do not skip a step or perform emergency actions in the wrong order (unlike the non-sequential kit list) [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Numbers establish a strict chronological sequence (Step 1 before Step 2) so staff follow the emergency clean-up steps in the exact safe order.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Saying 'it makes it easier to read' without mentioning chronological order or step-by-step sequence.
🔄 Quick Check: Bullet points = any order; Numbered list = strict chronological sequence.
Section B: Real-Life & Workplace Practice Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Level 1/2 Evaluating a Data Table vs Continuous Prose) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A courier company prints its parcel delivery prices in a three-column table:
Column 1: Parcel Weight (Under 2kg / 2–10kg / Over 10kg) | Column 2: Standard 48-Hr Cost | Column 3: Express Next-Day Cost.

Explain three advantages of presenting these delivery tariffs in a table rather than a continuous paragraph of text.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Rapid cross-referencing: Aligns weight bands with 48-Hr and Next-Day prices in rows and columns for instant lookup [A1]
2. Easy comparison: Allows customers to compare Standard vs Express prices side-by-side at a glance [A1]
3. Prevents reading errors: Eliminates visual clutter so staff do not misread figures buried in dense sentences [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 1. Aligns weights and prices in rows/columns for fast lookup. 2. Makes comparing 48-Hr vs Next-Day costs easy. 3. Reduces risk of misreading numbers in dense prose.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Repeating the same point three times instead of giving three distinct functional benefits (speed, comparison, accuracy).
🔄 Quick Check: Check you mentioned row/column alignment, side-by-side comparison, and error reduction.
Question 4 (Level 2 Purpose of a Dedicated Sidebar Panel) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A two-page Healthcare guide on managing asthma in schools is laid out with three main columns explaining inhaler types and triggers, plus a red-bordered vertical sidebar down the right-hand margin titled: '999 RED FLAG SYMPTOMS: WHEN TO CALL AN AMBULANCE'.

Analyse why the designer placed the 999 symptoms in a separate red-bordered sidebar and how this helps school staff.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Structural separation: Physically isolates life-threatening emergency signs from the general background paragraphs on triggers [A1]
2. Visual prominence: The red border and right-margin position draw the eye immediately to critical danger warnings [A1]
3. Practical workplace benefit: Enables a teacher dealing with an acute asthma attack to check 999 criteria in seconds without hunting through three columns [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Isolates critical 999 danger signs from general background text; the red border draws immediate visual attention so teachers can verify ambulance criteria instantly during an attack.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Summarising asthma symptoms instead of analysing the structural position and red border of the sidebar.
🔄 Quick Check: Does your answer explain both visual separation and speed of access in an emergency?
Section C: Exam-Style Tasks Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Level 2 Analysing Flowchart & Q&A Structural Devices) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
An IT troubleshooting webpage for homeworkers combines two layout devices:
• Top Half: A decision flowchart with Yes/No arrows ('Is the router power light green? → YES: Reboot VPN / NO: Check wall socket').
• Bottom Half: An FAQ (Frequently Asked Questions) section with bold question subheadings.

Explain how both layout devices work together to help an employee whose internet has just disconnected.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Flowchart visual guidance: Uses Yes/No directional arrows to guide the worker step-by-step through diagnosing their router fault logically [M1]
2. Flowchart efficiency: Saves time by steering the user only to the action that matches their light status (green vs off) [A1]
3. FAQ navigation: Uses bold common questions as signposts so workers with specific follow-up queries can scan straight to the right answer [M1]
4. Combined benefit: Makes complex technical troubleshooting accessible and stress-free for non-IT staff working alone at home [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: The Yes/No flowchart guides workers logically through step-by-step fault diagnosis based on their router light, while the bold FAQ headings allow them to scan straight to specific follow-up answers.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Analysing only the flowchart and ignoring the FAQ layout.
🔄 Quick Check: Verify both the Yes/No decision path (flowchart) and question scanning (FAQ) are explained.
Question 6 (Level 2 Comparing Layout & Structure Across Two Documents) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Compare the layout of two texts about food allergens:
Text A (Kitchen Wall Poster): Uses a single large page with a 14-icon allergen grid, bold red warning boxes, and bulleted rules.
Text B (Food Standards Journal Article): Uses four pages of dense, two-column continuous prose paragraphs with numbered academic footnotes (¹ ²) and a bibliography at the end.

Compare how the layout of Text A and Text B is tailored to their different audiences and workplace uses.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Text A layout & audience utility: Uses a 14-icon grid, red warning boxes, and bullets so busy chefs can check allergens at a glance during fast kitchen service [M1]
2. Text B layout & audience utility: Uses dense multi-column prose, academic footnotes, and a bibliography suited for researchers or policy managers reading in depth [M1]
3. Direct structural contrast: Whereas Text A prioritises instant visual scanning on a wall, Text B prioritises detailed scholarly citation and extended reading [A1]
4. Explain footnotes in Text B: Footnotes/bibliography verify scientific sources without interrupting the formal academic narrative [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Text A uses an icon grid, red warning boxes, and bullets for instant wall-scanning by busy chefs. Conversely, Text B uses dense two-column prose and footnotes for in-depth study and source verification by researchers.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Describing the topic of food allergies instead of comparing the structural layout features (icon grid/bullets vs prose columns/footnotes).
🔄 Quick Check: Have you linked Text A's layout to busy chefs and Text B's layout to researchers/in-depth study?
