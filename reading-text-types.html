Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.16 / L2.17 – Identify different text types, purposes, and audiences across informational, instructional, and transactional materials.
  • L2.16 – Recognise structural and formatting conventions distinguishing reports, articles, forums, manuals, and formal correspondence.

Golden Rules for Text Types, Genre Conventions & Audience

Golden Rule 1: Identify Genre from Structural Conventions + Purpose

Every text type has a visual fingerprint: Letters have addresses, date, and 'Dear.../Yours...'; Reports have numbered sections (1.0 Introduction, 2.0 Findings, 3.0 Recommendations); Forums have usernames, timestamps, and 'Reply' buttons.

Golden Rule / Technique: Visual Conventions (Format) + Communicative Goal (Purpose) = Text Type
Golden Rule 2: Always Define Target Audience Specifically

Never write 'anyone' or 'the general public' for an audience question if the text is aimed at a specific group (e.g. 'parents of primary school children', 'HGV drivers at the depot', or 'first-time homebuyers').

Golden Rule / Technique: Be specific: Who needs this exact information, and what role or interest do they have?

How to recognize and describe every document genre tested in Level 1 and Level 2.

Text Genre Structural Conventions Primary Purpose & Register
Formal Business Letter Sender/recipient addresses, date, Reference line, Dear... / Yours sincerely/faithfully. Transactional: to complain, apply, request, or notify officially in a formal register.
Formal Report Title, To/From/Date, numbered subheadings (Findings, Conclusion, Recommendations), data tables. To investigate an issue objectively, present audit facts, and propose action points.
Magazine / Newspaper Article Catchy headline, strapline, author byline, columns, pull quotes, captioned photo. To engage, inform, persuade, or debate a topical issue with a lively voice.
Information Leaflet / Guide Q&A subheadings, bullet points, shaded advice boxes, helpline contact details. To inform, advise, and guide readers clearly on health, community, or workplace services.
Online Discussion Forum Thread title, multiple user handles/avatars, timestamps, Like / Reply buttons. To share multiple contrasting personal opinions, tips, and lived experiences informally.
Instruction Manual / SOP Equipment list, warning symbols, numbered sequential steps starting with imperative verbs. To instruct the reader step-by-step on how to perform a procedure safely and accurately.

Core Skill 2: Distinguishing Dual-Purpose Texts (Level 2)

Why many real-world texts combine two purposes at once (e.g. to inform AND persuade).

At Level 1, a text usually has one clear purpose. At Level 2, examiners regularly select texts with a dual purpose:

  • To Inform AND Persuade: A council leaflet that gives factual dates and maps for a new park-and-ride bus service (informing) while urging commuters to ditch their cars to cut town pollution (persuading).
  • To Review/Evaluate AND Advise: A workplace software review that evaluates the pros and cons of two payroll systems (evaluating) and recommends which one small businesses should buy (advising).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Pinpointing the Exact Target Audience from Text Clues

How to deduce who a text was written for using pronouns, jargon, and topic focus.

3 Clues That Reveal Target Audience:
1. Direct Address Context: "If your child is starting Year 7 this September..." → Target Audience: Parents/guardians of Year 6 children moving up to secondary school.
2. Level of Jargon: Unexplained clinical terms ("administer 5mg IV bolus") → Qualified medical staff, NOT patients.
3. Publication Source: Printed in 'Logistics Manager Monthly' → Fleet managers and warehouse directors.

Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Writing a Single Word Like 'To Inform' Without Topic Context
In Pearson Edexcel Level 2 exams, writing 'To inform' or 'To persuade' on its own scores 0 marks! The mark scheme requires you to state WHAT the text is informing or persuading the reader about.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always use the full formula: 'To [inform/persuade/instruct] the reader about [specific topic of the text].'
Examiner Pitfall: Confusing an Online Forum with an Article or Email
If a text shows 3 or 4 different usernames (e.g. '@Dave_Builds', '@Sarah_N') posting separate replies under a question thread, it is an online discussion forum, not a single-author article.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Check for multiple user handles, avatars, and 'Reply' timestamps.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A webpage displays a thread titled 'Best waterproof work boots for winter site work?' followed by posts from @BricklayerDan (Posted 10 mins ago) and @SparkyJen (Posted 4 mins ago).
Identify the text type and explain why someone would read it.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Text type: Online discussion forum / message board thread [1 Mark].
2. Reason for reading: To read real-world recommendations, personal opinions, and reviews from fellow tradespeople about durable winter work boots [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Text type: Online discussion forum thread. | Purpose for reader: To gather firsthand tips and reviews from other tradespeople on the best waterproof work boots.
Examiner Tip: Multiple user handles ('@BricklayerDan', '@SparkyJen') and timestamps ('Posted 4 mins ago') always signal an online forum.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
Read the header of this workplace document:
MEMORANDUM | To: All Outpatient Receptionists | From: Practice Manager | Date: 12 October | Subject: Updated Statutory Prescription Exemption Checks
Identify the target audience and the main purpose of this document.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Target audience: Outpatient receptionists at the medical practice [1 Mark].
2. Main purpose: To inform and instruct reception staff on the updated procedures for checking patient prescription exemptions [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Target Audience: Outpatient receptionists. | Main Purpose: To inform/instruct staff on the updated procedure for checking statutory prescription exemptions.
Examiner Tip: In memos and emails, the 'To:' line gives you the exact target audience and the 'Subject:' line gives you the exact topic!
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
Read this extract from a leaflet distributed in university halls:
'Struggling to stretch your student loan till December? Download the FreeBudget App today! You'll get instant spending alerts, plus 20% student discounts at 50 local takeaways and supermarkets.'
State the specific target audience [1 Mark] and explain how the text has a dual purpose [2 Marks].
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Target audience: University students living in halls / managing a student loan [1 Mark].
2. Purpose 1 (Inform): Informs students about the features of the FreeBudget App (spending alerts and 20% local discounts) [1 Mark].
3. Purpose 2 (Persuade): Persuades/urges them to download and use the app ('Download the FreeBudget App today!') [1 Mark].
Final Model Answer: Target Audience: University students managing a student loan. | Dual Purpose: 1. To inform students about the app's budgeting alerts and 20% discounts. 2. To persuade them to download the FreeBudget App immediately.
Examiner Tip: Naming both 'to inform about...' and 'to persuade to...' secures full marks on Level 2 purpose questions.
