UK GDPR, Privacy and Digital Footprints — Answer Key

5. Being Safe and Responsible Online • Standard Code: L1.5.3
Style 1: Solutions (6 Drills)
Task 1 — Model Solution [2 Marks]

What UK legislation protects individual privacy and regulates how personal data is processed by organisations?

A. Data Protection Act 2018 (UK GDPR) (CORRECT)
B. Computer Games Act 2005
C. Broadband Speed Regulation 2012
D. Printer Maintenance Code 1999
Tutor Diagnostic Explanation:

The Data Protection Act 2018 sets out UK data protection and GDPR standards.

Task 2 — Model Solution [2 Marks]

What is the 'Right of Access' (Subject Access Request) under UK GDPR?

A. An individual's legal right to ask an organisation for a free copy of the personal data held about them (CORRECT)
B. The right to use anyone's Wi-Fi without permission
C. The right to borrow computers from the library
D. The right to change your date of birth legally online
Tutor Diagnostic Explanation:

Subject Access Requests allow people to see what personal data organisations store about them.

Task 3 — Model Solution [2 Marks]

What happens when you close an 'InPrivate' or 'Incognito' browser window?

A. Your browsing history, cookies, and temporary form data from that session are deleted from that device (CORRECT)
B. Your computer automatically shuts down
C. All your bookmarks are permanently lost
D. Your internet subscription is cancelled
Tutor Diagnostic Explanation:

Private browsing cleans local traces (cookies, history, cache) upon window closure.

Task 4 — Model Solution [2 Marks]

Under the UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR), which of the following is defined as 'Special Category' (sensitive) personal data?

A. Health/medical records, biometric data, and ethnic origin (CORRECT)
B. A company office business address
C. A public corporate telephone switchboard number
D. A list of office stationary items
Tutor Diagnostic Explanation:

Special Category data includes health records, biometric templates, religious beliefs, and trade union membership, and requires much stricter legal protections under UK GDPR.

Task 5 — Model Solution [2 Marks]

An organisation discovers that an unencrypted spreadsheet containing 500 patient names and medical conditions was accidentally emailed to an external marketing agency. What is the legal requirement under UK GDPR?

A. Notify the Data Protection Officer (DPO) and report the breach to the ICO within 72 hours if there is a risk to individuals' rights (CORRECT)
B. Keep it secret and hope nobody notices
C. Delete all emails from the company servers and deny the breach occurred
D. Wait 6 months before informing anyone
Tutor Diagnostic Explanation:

UK GDPR mandates that serious personal data breaches presenting a risk to individuals must be reported to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) within 72 hours of becoming aware.

Task 6 — Model Solution [2 Marks]

What is the UK GDPR principle of 'Data Minimisation'?

A. Organisations must only collect personal data that is strictly adequate, relevant, and limited to what is necessary for the specified purpose (CORRECT)
B. Documents must be typed using 6 pt font size to make them minimal
C. All customer records must be deleted after 1 hour
D. Organisations are forbidden from storing data on computers
Tutor Diagnostic Explanation:

Data Minimisation prevents organisations from collecting excessive or unnecessary personal data 'just in case' — they may only gather what is strictly required for the service.
