Section A: Core Skills (The Basics) Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Using Signposting Markers to Structure a Formal Presentation) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
You are delivering a 4-minute Level 2 presentation proposing Staggered Shift Start Times.
Write two signposting sentences: one to introduce your first main argument, and one to transition into your final conclusion.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Opening signpost: e.g. 'First of all, let us examine how staggered start times will reduce peak car-park congestion by 40%.' [A1]
2. Closing signpost: e.g. 'To sum up the evidence presented today, staggered shifts benefit both staff wellbeing and patient punctuality.' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Opening: 'First of all, let us examine how staggered shifts cut peak parking congestion by 40%.' | Closing: 'To sum up the evidence today, staggered start times improve both staff morale and clinic punctuality.'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Jumping between slides or topics with no verbal transition markers.
🔄 Quick Check: Uses explicit discourse markers ('First of all...', 'To sum up...').
Question 2 (Core Skill Application — 3 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Draft an attention-grabbing opening hook and roadmap (35–45 words) for a 4-minute Level 2 presentation proposing that your company should replace its diesel delivery vans with electric vehicles.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Open with a striking statistic or question about diesel fuel costs and urban clean-air charges. [M1]
2. State your proposal clearly. [M1]
3. Give a 3-point roadmap (fuel savings, clean-air zone compliance, and overnight depot charging). [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'Good morning everyone. Last year alone, rising diesel prices and city Clean Air Zone penalties cost our delivery fleet over £38,000. Today, I will demonstrate why transitioning to electric delivery vans is essential, covering three key areas: annual fuel savings, zero-emission zone compliance, and overnight depot charging.'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Staring down at a typed essay and reading it aloud monotone will fail the spoken delivery criteria even if the written essay is well written.
🔄 Quick Check: Hook Stat (£38,000 cost) + Clear Thesis + 3-Part Roadmap = the perfect 30-second presentation opening.
Section B: Real-Life & Workplace Practice Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Model 1 (Level 1): 3-Minute Talk Structure — Benefits of First-Aid Training in Schools) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Create a 3-part spoken talk outline (Introduction, 3 Main Points, Conclusion) explaining why all secondary school pupils should learn basic first aid and CPR.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Opening Hook & Purpose: 'Good morning everyone. If someone collapsed with a cardiac arrest in front of you today, would you know how to perform CPR? My talk today explains why every secondary school pupil should receive two hours of mandatory first-aid training each year.' [A1]
2. Three Sequenced Main Points: 'First, immediate CPR in the first three minutes doubles a person's chance of survival before anambulance arrives. Second, teenagers often look after younger siblings and can treat choking or burns safely. Third, St John Ambulance provides free school training kits, so the cost to schools is minimal.' [A1]
3. Concluding Summary & Q&A Invite: 'To sum up, teaching CPR in schools is simple, low-cost, and saves hundreds of lives every year. Thank you for listening—what questions do you have?' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Complete 3-minute talk structure featuring a rhetorical question hook, three clearly signposted arguments (survival rates, home safety, low cost), and a confident invitation for audience questions.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Write your talk onto 4 small cue cards using large bullet-point keywords only.
🔄 Quick Check: Opening your talk with a direct question ('If someone collapsed in front of you today, would you know what to do?') grabs every listener's attention in the first 10 seconds.
Question 4 (Exam-Style Task — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Convert this long, written paragraph into 4 concise Cue Card bullet points (maximum 6 words per bullet) so a candidate can speak naturally without reading:
'If we introduce a four-day working week at the call centre, staff morale will improve dramatically and fewer people will resign. Last year we spent £15,000 recruiting and training replacement call handlers because staff were burned out. By rotating Friday and Monday rest days, we can keep phone lines open five days a week while giving staff a three-day weekend.'
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Extract Keyword Bullet 1: Morale & staff retention. [M1]
2. Extract Keyword Bullet 2: £15,000 recruitment cost last year. [M1]
3. Extract Keyword Bullet 3: Rotate Monday/Friday rest days. [M1]
4. Extract Keyword Bullet 4: 100% 5-day phone cover maintained. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Cue Card 2 — Staff Retention & Cover:
• Boosts morale & stops burnout
• Saves £15,000 annual recruitment cost
• Rotate Monday / Friday rest days
• Maintains 100% 5-day phone cover
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Ending a formal presentation by muttering 'So yeah, that's all I've got' leaves the audience hanging and ruins your final impression.
🔄 Quick Check: Each bullet contains just the trigger statistic or concept—allowing you to look up at your audience and form the sentences naturally.
Section C: Exam-Style Tasks Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Model 2 (Level 2): 4-Minute Presentation — Implementing Mental Health First Aiders in NHS Trusts) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Deliver the structured opening, signposted argument transitions, and conclusion of a 4-minute Level 2 presentation advocating for accredited Mental Health First Aiders across acute hospital wards.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Hook, Authority & 3-Part Roadmap: 'Good morning colleagues. Did you know that anxiety and work-related stress accounted for 54% of all working days lost across the NHS last year? My name is Alex, and today I will present the business case for training one accredited Mental Health First Aider on every ward, covering three areas: early burnout prevention, confidential peer signposting, and the financial return on investment.' [A1]
2. Signposted Arguments & Counter-Argument: 'Starting with early intervention, ward staff frequently hide emotional exhaustion until they reach crisis point... Turning to my second point, peer champions wear a green lanyard so colleagues can access confidential listening support on shift... Finally, while training forty champions costs £8,000, preventing just twenty agency-covered sick days recovers that entire cost within six months.' [A1]
3. Memorable Conclusion & Seamless Q&A Handover: 'In conclusion, caring for our staff is the foundation of safe patient care. By approving this £8,000 pilot today, we protect both our workforce and our ward budgets. Thank you for your attention; I now welcome any questions on our proposed rollout schedule.' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Full 4-minute presentation blueprint combining a striking 54% NHS sickness statistic hook, explicit verbal signposting ('Starting with...', 'Turning to...', 'Finally...'), ROI cost rebuttal, and a composed handover to audience Q&A.
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Always memorise your final two sentences: 'In conclusion, [1-sentence summary]. Thank you for listening, and I welcome any questions.'
🔄 Quick Check: Notice how the speaker gives the audience a clear 3-point roadmap in the first 30 seconds and then uses verbal signposts to tick off each stage.
Question 6 (Level 2 Mastery Challenge — 3 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Write a 35-word concluding summary and Q&A invitation for a presentation on why your care home should adopt an electronic medication administration record (eMAR) tablet system.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Start with a clear concluding signpost ('In conclusion,' or 'To sum up,'). [M1]
2. Summarise the three core benefits (eliminates paper transcription errors, saves nursing time, and strengthens CQC safety ratings). [M1]
3. Thank the audience and invite questions. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'In conclusion, adopting the eMAR tablet system eliminates handwritten medication errors, saves nurses forty minutes per shift for direct resident care, and strengthens our CQC safety rating. Thank you for listening—I now welcome your questions.'
⚠️ Common Mistake to Avoid: Failing to proofread spelling, punctuation, and grammar after completing the draft.
🔄 Quick Check: Using the Rule of Three in your final summary sentence makes your presentation end on a crisp, memorable note.
