Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.1 / L2.1 – Identify relevant information and lines of argument in spoken explanations, briefings, and presentations.
  • L2.5 – Follow and analyse spoken lines of argument, synthesising key facts to formulate relevant questions and responses.

Golden Rules for Listening & Extracting Key Information

Golden Rule 1: Use the 2-Column Listening Note Grid During Peer Talks

Draw a line down the middle of your notepad before your peers start speaking. Label the left column 'Key Facts / Figures' and the right column 'My Follow-Up Question'. Jot down 3 keywords per speaker—never try to write full sentences.

Golden Rule / Technique: Left Column: Dates, Costs & Core Claims | Right Column: 1 Clarifying or Probing Question
Golden Rule 2: Listen for Verbal Signposts and Stress Markers

Speakers naturally flag their most important points using verbal signposts: 'Crucially...', 'Our primary recommendation is...', 'The mandatory deadline is...'. Perk up and note whatever follows these phrases.

Golden Rule / Technique: Listen for: 'Crucially' • 'The main risk is' • 'By Friday' • 'Three key reasons'

Core Skill 1: Filtering Actionable Signal from Conversational Noise

How to strip away anecdotes and pleasantries to isolate mandatory workplace instructions.

Spoken Briefing Extract Conversational Filler (Ignore) Extracted Key Information (Note Down)
"Well, as you know the weather has been awful all week, but anyway, from Monday 3 November, all medication trolleys must be double-locked to the wall bracket between 13:00 and 14:00." Weather remarks, 'well, as you know', 'but anyway'. Rule: Double-lock med trolleys to wall 13:00–14:00 (starts Mon 3 Nov).
"I personally think the new navy tunics look very smart, though the supplier phoned to say medium-sized tunics are delayed until Friday 20th." Personal opinion on how smart navy looks. Supply Update: Size Medium tunics delayed until Fri 20th.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Identifying a Speaker's Underlying Line of Argument & Bias

At Level 2 (Criterion L2.1 & L2.5), you must track not just isolated facts, but the speaker's overall argument.

  • Ask 3 Listening Questions:
    1. What is the speaker's main proposal? (e.g. Moving to cashless payments only).
    2. What evidence are they relying on? (e.g. Faster till queues and zero cash-handling bank fees).
    3. What perspective have they overlooked? (e.g. Elderly or vulnerable customers who rely on cash budgets).
  • Spotting what a speaker overlooked gives you the perfect probing question for the Q&A!
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Shorthand Note-Taking Symbols for Fast Spoken Briefings

Keep your eyes on the speaker 85% of the time by using quick symbols.

  • Use ↑ / ↓ for increases and decreases (e.g. "Staff turnover ↓ 18%").
  • Use → for cause and effect (e.g. "Broken boiler → Ward 2 moved to Ground Floor").
  • Use ? in the margin next to any point you want to ask about in the Q&A.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Benchmark Exam Model 2 Level 2 Listening & Analysis Benchmark (L2.1, L2.5)

Vocational Context: NHS Acute Ward Morning Briefing

Read the transcript of the Ward Manager's briefing:
'Good morning team. First, thank you for covering the busy weekend shifts. A crucial reminder that our CQC inspection window opens next Monday, 17 November. All environmental cleaning audits must be uploaded to the digital portal by 16:00 daily without exception. Second, pharmacy reported that three controlled-drug entries lacked a second signature last Thursday; therefore, anyone administering Schedule 2 medications must log two practitioner signatures at the bedside immediately. Finally, the staff summer barbecue has been moved to 18 July.'
1. Extract the two mandatory clinical compliance directives.
2. Formulate one relevant follow-up question to clarify implementation.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Filter out social notices and pleasantries
Ignore weekend thanks and the July barbecue date.
Criteria: Isolates Regulatory Content
Step 2 Extract Compliance Directives 1 & 2 with exact times
Directive 1: Upload environmental cleaning audits to the digital portal by 16:00 daily (ahead of CQC window on Mon 17 Nov).
Directive 2: Log two practitioner countersignatures immediately at the bedside for all Schedule 2 controlled drugs.
Criteria: Exact Extraction (L2.1)

Ask what backup procedure applies if the digital audit portal is offline at 16:00 or when only one registered nurse is on the bay during night break rotations.

Directive 2: Mandatory immediate bedside dual-signature logging for all Schedule 2 controlled drugs.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Staring at Your Notepad Trying to Write Full Sentences
If you try to write down every word a speaker says, you will miss their next two points AND lose marks for lack of eye contact.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Write max 3–4 words per line (e.g. 'CQC 17 Nov | Audit 16:00 | CD 2 sigs') while keeping your head up.
Examiner Pitfall: Asking a Question the Speaker Already Answered!
Asking 'How much will it cost?' when the speaker clearly stated 'The total cost is £800' proves to the assessor that you were NOT listening.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Refer to the figure they gave and probe deeper: 'You mentioned the total cost is £800—does that include installation?'

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A warehouse supervisor announces:
'We've had two near-misses in Loading Bay 4 this morning due to hydraulic oil spills. All operatives must wear steel-toecap boots and high-visibility vests at all times, and Loading Bay 4 is closed for steam cleaning until 14:30.'
Extract (a) the mandatory PPE rule and (b) the operational restriction.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Identify the mandatory PPE requirement.
2. Identify the specific location and time of the closure.
Final Model Answer: (a) Mandatory PPE Rule: All operatives must wear steel-toecap boots and high-visibility vests at all times.
(b) Operational Restriction: Loading Bay 4 is closed for steam cleaning until 14:30.
Examiner Tip: Be exact with locations ('Loading Bay 4') and times ('until 14:30') when summarising safety briefings.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Read this spoken pitch from a colleague in a meeting:
'Switching all customer support entirely to an AI chatbot will save our company £30,000 a year in call-centre wages and give instant 24/7 replies.'
1. Identify the speaker's core line of argument.
2. Identify one critical customer-service risk the speaker has ignored.
3. Write an open question exposing that risk.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Core argument: Replace human call handlers with an AI chatbot to save £30,000/year and offer 24/7 responses.
2. Ignored risk: Complex complaints, elderly/vulnerable customers who struggle with chatbots, and customer frustration.
3. Open question probing how complex or distressed customer calls will be handled.
Final Model Answer: 1. Line of Argument: Automating customer support via an AI chatbot saves £30,000 annually while providing 24/7 instant replies.
2. Overlooked Risk: Elderly or vulnerable customers and complex complaints that automated bots cannot resolve.
3. Probing Question: 'While the £30,000 saving is attractive, how would an automated chatbot support elderly customers without internet access or handle complex complaints that require human empathy?'
Examiner Tip: Referencing the speaker's exact figure ('While the £30,000 saving is attractive...') before probing the flaw proves Level 2 active listening.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
Convert this 55-word spoken briefing extract into 3 concise shorthand notepad lines (using arrows/symbols) that take less than 10 seconds to jot down:
'Starting next Monday 9 November, our main staff car park will close for five days so contractors can install six electric vehicle chargers. During next week, all staff must park at the Leisure Centre overflow car park on Mill Road and show their staff ID badge to the barrier attendant.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Line 1: Date & closure (Mon 9 Nov, 5 days: Main car park CLOSED → 6 EV chargers).
2. Line 2: Alternative parking (Park at Leisure Centre overflow, Mill Rd).
3. Line 3: Requirement (Show Staff ID badge at barrier).
Final Model Answer: • Mon 9 Nov (5 days): Main car park CLOSED → 6 EV chargers installed
• Temp parking: Leisure Centre overflow (Mill Rd)
• Action: Must show Staff ID badge at barrier
Examiner Tip: Short keyword notes let you capture 100% of the facts while keeping your eyes on the speaker.
