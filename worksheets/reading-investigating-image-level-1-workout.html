Fluency Drills
Linking Images to Written Text
  • Images in functional texts are never purely decorative; they reinforce, clarify, or extend the written message.
  • Identify image type: Photograph, diagram, flowchart, map, infographic, or warning icon.
  • Analyze purpose: Does it instruct (how to wear PPE), persuade (happy patient recovery photo), or inform (step-by-step assembly)?
  • Explain the relationship between the image and the surrounding text.
  • Look at captions: Captions provide critical explanatory context.
Calculator Strategy: When asked why an image was included, answer: 'The image illustrates [detail] which helps the reader to understand [concept] by visually showing [action].'

(c)
A line graph showing monthly staff sickness absence rates over five years
(d)
A photograph of a completed sterile tray setup before minor surgery
(e)
An exploded diagram of an industrial vacuum cleaner showing filter placement
(f)
A bar chart comparing customer ratings across four regional car dealerships
(g)
A visual flow chart mapping patient triage steps from arrival to doctor consultation
(h)
A high-resolution photograph of correct PPE donning order on a locker room poster
Question 4 Question 4: Purpose & Impact of Promotional & Editorial Images
Analyze the emotional impact or rhetorical purpose of the visual:
(a)
A photograph of an apprentice proudly holding their qualification certificate
(b)
A stark image of a severely damaged car wheel rim next to an article on potholes
(c)
A smiling nurse gently holding an elderly resident's hand in a care home leaflet
(d)
A close-up image of fresh, brightly coloured organic vegetables on a supermarket website
(e)
An infographic displaying five trees saved for every tonne of office paper recycled
(f)
A dramatic photo of empty shelves in a supermarket during a supply chain disruption
(g)
A graphic of a padlock next to an online banking login button
(h)
A cartoon character with a bandage smiling next to a children's dental clinic notice
Question 5 Question 5: Critiquing Images & Detecting Misleading Graphics
Explain how the visual choice might mislead, confuse, or distort the reader's understanding:
(a)
A bar chart with a vertical y-axis that starts at 80% rather than 0%
(b)
A 3D pie chart tilted towards the viewer where the front slice appears much larger
(c)
Using a stock photo of an ultra-modern glass laboratory for an underfunded rural clinic
(d)
A line graph showing sales growth where the time intervals on the x-axis are uneven
(e)
Pictograms where larger quantities use larger icons rather than more icons of the same size
(f)
An image of diverse happy workers placed on a brochure for a company with no diversity
(g)
A product photo on packaging showing serving suggestions with ingredients not in the box
(h)
Using red shading for positive profits and green shading for financial losses on a report
