Section A Mark Scheme — Knowledge & Understanding

10 Marks
Question 1 • Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
CORRECT ANSWER: B 1 Mark [B1]

Correct Statement: It provides an additional layer of defence by requiring two or more verification factors to confirm identity.

Pearson Examiner Rationale: MFA significantly impedes cyber attackers because knowing a password alone is insufficient; access requires physical control over a secondary factor (SMS OTP or Authenticator app token).
Why distractors are wrong: Option A confuses MFA with network bandwidth; Option C confuses authentication with backup; Option D confuses MFA with automatic OS software patch schedules.
Question 2 • Display Screen Equipment & Ergonomics
CORRECT ANSWER: A 1 Mark [B1]

Correct Statement: Positioning the monitor directly in front of the user with the top of the screen at eye level and reducing screen glare.

Pearson Examiner Rationale: In accordance with HSE Display Screen Equipment regulations, placing the screen at eye level directly ahead maintains neutral neck posture, while glare reduction prevents eye fatigue and headaches.
Why distractors are wrong: Option B causes extreme neck extension; Option C promotes wrist flexion and lack of lumbar support; Option D induces severe eye strain.
Question 3 • Refining Online Searches & Search Operators
CORRECT ANSWER: B 1 Mark [B1]

Correct Statement: "sports facility guidelines" site:gov.uk filetype:pdf

Pearson Examiner Rationale: Quotation marks enforce exact phrase matching, site:gov.uk confines the search to authoritative central government portals, and filetype:pdf restricts results specifically to downloadable guidance documents.
Why distractors are wrong: Options A and C use imprecise keywords yielding irrelevant blogs; Option D exposes the user to commercial advert tracking.
Question 4 • Digital Footprint & Child Safeguarding
CORRECT ANSWER: B 1 Mark [B1]

Correct Statement: It breaches data privacy and child safeguarding regulations by exposing personal identifiable information publicly.

Pearson Examiner Rationale: Children's data and imagery are subject to heightened protections under UK GDPR and Keeping Children Safe in Education (KCSIE). Explicit parental consent is required before posting identifiable images on public social channels.
Why distractors are wrong: Options A, C, and D describe irrelevant technical processes that do not relate to safeguarding or legal liability.
Question 5 • Cybersecurity & Ransomware Threats
CORRECT ANSWER: B 1 Mark [B1]

Correct Statement: Ransomware

Pearson Examiner Rationale: Ransomware is malicious software designed to block access to a computer system or encrypt data files until a sum of money (ransom) is paid.
Why distractors are wrong: Spyware covertly monitors user habits; Adware displays unwanted banners; Phishing is a social engineering email attack vector, not encryption malware.
Question 6 • Secure Web Connections & SSL/TLS
CORRECT ANSWER: A 1 Mark [B1]

Correct Statement: The web address starts with https:// accompanied by a closed padlock icon in the browser address bar.

Pearson Examiner Rationale: The 's' in HTTPS denotes Transport Layer Security (TLS), encrypting payment data between the client browser and merchant gateway to prevent packet sniffing.
Why distractors are wrong: Banners, domain suffixes (.org), and loading speeds have zero bearing on cryptographic encryption protocols.
Question 7 • Digital Storage Units & Capacity Hierarchy
CORRECT ANSWER: B 1 Mark [B1]

Correct Statement: Kilobyte (KB) → Megabyte (MB) → Gigabyte (GB) → Terabyte (TB)

Pearson Examiner Rationale: Standard binary prefix progression: 1 KB (~1,000 bytes) < 1 MB (~1,000 KB) < 1 GB (~1,000 MB) < 1 TB (~1,000 GB).
Why distractors are wrong: All other options place larger units before smaller ones in violation of ascending order.
Question 8 • Data Protection & Subject Access Requests
CORRECT ANSWER: B 1 Mark [B1]

Correct Statement: Right of access (Subject Access Request)

Pearson Examiner Rationale: Under Article 15 of UK GDPR, the Right of Access allows data subjects to obtain a copy of their personal data and supplementary information from data controllers.
Why distractors are wrong: Options A, C, and D are fabricated terms unrelated to statutory individual data protection rights.
Question 9 • File Compression & Archiving
CORRECT ANSWER: B 1 Mark [B1]

Correct Statement: To reduce total file size for faster transmission and avoid email attachment size limits.

Pearson Examiner Rationale: Standard email servers reject messages with attachments larger than 20–25 MB. Lossless ZIP compression consolidates multiple photos into a single compact archive package.
Why distractors are wrong: Compressing does not delete originals, does not lock mobile screens, and does not manipulate image colour tones.
Question 10 • Safe Workplace Communication & Sensitive Data
CORRECT ANSWER: B 1 Mark [B1]

Correct Statement: Encrypting or password-protecting the file and communicating the strong password via a separate, secure secondary channel.

Pearson Examiner Rationale: Confidential payroll and banking records must be protected in transit. Sending the decryption key via out-of-band communication (e.g. phone call or separate encrypted chat) prevents interceptors from reading data even if email accounts are breached.
Why distractors are wrong: Options A, C, and D represent catastrophic data breaches under GDPR punishable by ICO fines.

Task E • File Organization & Network Troubleshooting 6 Marks Total

Model Troubleshooting Document Summary:

Document Title: Tablet Connection Troubleshooting.docx
File Path: Candidate_Workspace / Network Support / Pool Deck Tablet / Tablet Connection Troubleshooting.docx

1. Possible Root Causes:
• DHCP IP address pool exhausted on router, preventing new lease allocation.
• RF physical obstruction/attenuation: wet tiled walls and water barriers blocking 5 GHz Wi-Fi signal.
• Incompatible Wi-Fi frequency band (tablet attempting to connect to 5GHz instead of longer-range 2.4GHz).
• Incorrect WPA3 enterprise encryption passphrase entered.

2. Step-by-Step Resolution Steps:
Step 1: Check tablet Wi-Fi settings, toggle 'Airplane Mode' on for 10 seconds, then reconnect.
Step 2: Forget network and re-enter WPA3 network key with attention to case sensitivity.
Step 3: Move within line-of-sight of access point to rule out signal attenuation.
Step 4: Check router DHCP leases table and expand IP subnet scope if saturated.
Step 5: Verify HTTPS connection to gateway; update tablet OS network driver.

3. Source & Date: Verified from official guidance at https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/guidance/wi-fi-security-for-small-business (Updated October 2026).
