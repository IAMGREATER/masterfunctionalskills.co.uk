Section A: Foundational Concept Check Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Open vs Closed Questions in Consultation) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
In a clinical feedback meeting, a supervisor wants to understand why staff feel rushed during morning handovers.
1. Convert this closed question into an open question: 'Do you find the morning handover too short?'
2. Explain why open questions are more effective in workplace discussions.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Convert to open: 'How could we improve the morning handover process?' OR 'What factors make the morning handover feel rushed?' [A1]
2. Explanation: Open questions prompt detailed, reflective answers and constructive insights, whereas closed questions limit responses to a single word ('yes'/'no') [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Open: 'What challenges do you experience during morning handover?'; Explanation: Prompts qualitative insight rather than a yes/no answer.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Rewriting as another closed question (e.g. 'Is the handover 15 minutes long?').
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Open questions begin with How, What, Why; closed questions prompt Yes/No.
Question 2 (Active Listening & Non-Verbal Communication) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
During a formal committee discussion, describe two positive non-verbal cues that demonstrate active listening without interrupting the speaker.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Cue 1: Maintaining steady, respectful eye contact and nodding to signal comprehension [A1]
2. Cue 2: Leaning slightly forward with open posture (arms uncrossed) and taking brief notes [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 1. Eye contact and nodding; 2. Open posture and taking relevant notes.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Describing verbal interruptions like saying 'I agree' repeatedly.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Both cues show engagement through body language without speaking.
Section B: Functional Workplace Application Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Constructive Disagreement in a Team Meeting) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A colleague proposes: 'We should cancel all staff training days this quarter to cover sick leave.'
You strongly disagree with this proposal.

Write down a professional, diplomatic response that expresses disagreement respectfully while suggesting an alternative solution.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Acknowledge colleague's concern courteously ('I understand we are under acute staffing pressure...') [P1]
2. State disagreement professionally without personal attack ('However, cancelling training will harm long-term compliance and staff retention...') [M1]
3. Propose constructive alternative ('Could we instead look at short modular e-learning sessions or staggered bank staff cover?') [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Model: 'I understand staffing levels are critical right now; however, cancelling mandatory training could compromise patient safety and compliance. Could we instead deliver the training in shorter, 30-minute modules or utilize bank staff to cover key hours?'
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Aggressive language ('That is a terrible idea and will never work').
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Acknowledges concern + provides evidence-based objection + offers alternative.
Question 4 (Adapting Register for Varied Audiences) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
An apprentice healthcare assistant explains infection control procedures to two different audiences:
• Audience A: A team of consultant surgeons and clinical leads.
• Audience B: An anxious elderly patient and their family.

Describe how your vocabulary, sentence structure, and tone must adapt between Audience A and Audience B.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Audience A: Formal clinical register, specialist medical terminology (aseptic technique, pathogen transmission), concise evidence-based data [M1]
2. Audience B: Warm, empathetic, accessible plain English; avoiding frightening jargon; checking for understanding with reassuring body language [M1]
3. Contrast clearly articulated [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Audience A uses formal clinical terminology and data; Audience B uses compassionate plain English, reassuring tone, and checks for comfort.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Using patronizing or overly simplistic language with professionals, or jargon-heavy terms with patients.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Adapts complexity and empathy to match audience needs.
Section C: Pearson Edexcel Exam-Style Analysis & Evaluation Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Synthesizing Arguments to Reach Consensus) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
In a 15-minute group discussion, four team members debate whether to introduce 12-hour shifts or keep 8-hour shifts.
• Member A argues 12-hour shifts give more consecutive days off.
• Member B argues 12-hour shifts cause extreme clinical fatigue in the final 3 hours.
• Member C worries about childcare pickup times.

As meeting facilitator, write down a concluding summary statement that synthesizes all three viewpoints and proposes a collaborative compromise.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Summarize key advantages highlighted (consecutive rest days from Member A) [P1]
2. Acknowledge safety and wellbeing risks (fatigue from Member B, childcare from Member C) [P1]
3. Propose concrete compromise / trial period (e.g. 3-month pilot of hybrid rota with fatigue audits and flexible start options) [M1]
4. Invite consensus confirmation from all participants [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Model: 'We have heard compelling points today: while 12-hour shifts provide valuable consecutive rest days, we cannot ignore the valid concerns regarding end-of-shift clinical fatigue and childcare logistics. Would the team agree to a 3-month pilot allowing staff to choose between 8-hour and 12-hour schedules, backed by monthly fatigue reviews?'
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Picking one side and ignoring the other participants' contributions.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Incorporates all 3 participants' points into a fair collaborative solution.
Question 6 (SLC Assessment Criteria & Self-Evaluation) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
An apprentice prepares for their Pearson Edexcel Level 2 SLC formal presentation on 'Improving Environmental Sustainability in Health Clinics'.
Review the four Pearson Edexcel SLC grading criteria below:
1. Structure & Logical Sequencing
2. Language Register & Vocabulary
3. Effective Use of Presentation Aids
4. Handling Audience Questions

Describe one specific action the apprentice must take during their preparation or delivery to achieve top marks in each of the four criteria.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Structure: Use clear signposting (introduction with agenda, 3 logical subtopics, concise conclusion with call to action) [A1]
2. Register: Maintain formal professional vocabulary, clear diction, and appropriate pace without slang [A1]
3. Visual Aids: Use clean, uncluttered visual slides or handouts with relevant charts/statistics, referencing them directly during speech [A1]
4. Questions: Listen attentively to audience questions, thank the questioner, clarify if needed, and provide evidence-backed answers [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 1. Clear signposting; 2. Formal professional diction; 3. Well-referenced visual charts; 4. Active listening and evidence-based Q&A replies.
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Vague answers like 'do well' or 'speak loudly'.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Directly maps to the official Pearson Edexcel SLC Level 2 assessment grid.
