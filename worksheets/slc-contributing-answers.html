Contributing to Group Discussions Pearson Edexcel: Pearson Edexcel Functional Skills English (SLC)
Total Marks Available: 18 Marks (Pass Threshold: ~11/18)
Examiner Standard: Full Method (M) and Process (P) marks awarded even if final arithmetic errs.
Section A: Foundational Concept Check Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Using the Acknowledge-Build-Pivot Technique in Group Discussions) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
In a group discussion about reducing hospital plastic waste, Speaker A says:
'We should ban all disposable plastic water cups in the waiting rooms immediately.'
Write a constructive spoken contribution that acknowledges Speaker A's point, adds a practical concern, and proposes a solution.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Acknowledge Speaker A's environmental aim respectfully ('I agree with your goal of cutting plastic waste...') [A1]
2. Build/Pivot with a practical solution ('...however, patients still need drinking water, so we should replace plastic cups with biodegradable paper cones.') [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'I completely agree that we need to cut single-use plastic in our waiting areas; however, elderly patients still need access to water while waiting, so we could replace plastic cups with recyclable paper cones.'
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Interrupting or dismissing Speaker A rudely ('That's a terrible idea') instead of building constructively.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Follows Agree/Acknowledge → Practical Constraint → Constructive Alternative.
Question 2 (Core Skill Application — 2 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
Write a 2-sentence verbal bridge that connects Colleague A's point ('We need more car park security lights') to your own point about installing CCTV cameras at the bicycle shed.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Sentence 1: Reference Colleague A by name and validate their point about car park lighting. [M1]
2. Sentence 2: Link lighting to your complementary proposal for motion-activated CCTV over the cycle shelter. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'I completely agree with Hassan's point that upgrading the car park floodlights is essential for staff safety during winter evenings. Building on that security improvement, we should also install a motion-activated CCTV camera overlooking the staff bicycle shelter so that cyclists and drivers are equally protected.'
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Cutting across a speaker while they are still developing their point directly violates Criterion L1.5 / L2.7 and can force a full group resit.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Use the phrase 'Building on [Name]'s point about X, we should also...' at least twice in your exam discussion.
Section B: Functional Workplace Application Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Model 1 (Level 1): Refocusing a Drifting Group Discussion) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
Your group is supposed to agree on how to spend a £500 grant to improve the staff breakroom, but two colleagues have spent three minutes arguing about football results. Deliver a polite spoken intervention that steers the group back to the task and invites a decision.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Polite interjection at a natural pause: 'Sorry to step in, everyone—it sounds like it was a great match on Saturday!' [A1]
2. Remind the group of the remaining time and core task: 'Conscious that we only have five minutes left to allocate our £500 breakroom grant, could we refocus on the equipment list?' [A1]
3. Propose a concrete item and invite a peer's vote: 'We have £300 left after the new microwave—shall we spend that on comfortable armchairs or a filtered water cooler? Jade, what would you prefer?' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'Sorry to step in, everyone—it sounds like a great match! Conscious that we only have five minutes left to allocate our £500 breakroom grant, could we refocus on our final items? After buying the commercial microwave for £200, we have £300 remaining. Jade, do you think we should spend that on new armchairs or a chilled water dispenser?'
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Wait until the speaker finishes their sentence before using a verbal bridge ('Building on that point...').
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Citing the clock ('Conscious that we only have five minutes left...') is the most natural, polite excuse to bring an off-topic group back on track.
Question 4 (Exam-Style Task — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
During a group discussion on improving staff induction, two participants start interrupting each other heatedly about whether induction should last one day or one week. Write a diplomatic 40-word intervention that calms the exchange, summarises both points, and proposes a middle ground.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Step in calmly and validate both perspectives (getting staff onto the floor quickly vs thorough safety training). [M1]
2. Propose a hybrid solution (2 days intensive core induction + 3 days shadowed floor buddy shifts). [M1]
3. Ask for agreement. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'Both of you are raising really important points: we need new starters productive on the shop floor quickly, yet we cannot rush mandatory safety training. Could we compromise on a two-day classroom induction covering core safety, followed by three days of paid floor shadowing with a senior buddy? Does that balance both priorities?'
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Many groups chat for 15 minutes and stop when the timer rings without ever agreeing on the final recommendations requested in the brief.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Stepping in to de-escalate two arguing peers and offering a practical compromise guarantees top facilitation marks.
Section C: Pearson Edexcel Exam-Style Analysis & Evaluation Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Model 2 (Level 2): Hospital Waste Reduction Committee Consensus Facilitation) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
Four candidates are discussing how to reduce single-use plastic cups across hospital wards. The conversation has stalled after Liam argued reusable mugs pose a hot-drink scald risk to confused patients, while Sophie argued the hospital wastes 5,000 plastic cups a month. Intervene to resolve the impasse and guide the group to consensus.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Synthesise both competing concerns fairly: 'I think both points are vital: Liam is completely right that patient scald prevention on clinical bays comes first, while Sophie has shown our plastic waste exceeds 5,000 cups every month.' [A1]
2. Propose a targeted, phased compromise: 'Could we bridge both concerns by introducing reusable ceramic mugs strictly in staff breakrooms and administrative offices first—which accounts for 60% of cup usage—while keeping lidded biodegradable paper cups on patient bays?' [A1]
3. Invite consensus confirmation and assign next step: 'Liam and Sophie, would that split approach protect patient safety while still cutting over 3,000 plastic cups a month?' [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'I think both points are vital: Liam is right that patient scald prevention on clinical wards comes first, while Sophie has shown our plastic waste exceeds 5,000 cups monthly. Could we bridge both concerns by introducing reusable mugs across all staff breakrooms and offices first—eliminating 3,000 plastic cups a month—while retaining safe lidded biodegradable cups for patient bays? Would everyone agree to recommend that two-tier policy?'
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: When 2 minutes remain, step in as the Synthesiser: 'In our final two minutes, let's confirm the three recommendations we all agree on...'
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Assessor witness sheets contain specific tick-boxes for 'helps move discussion forward' and 'synthesises viewpoints to reach consensus'.
Question 6 (Level 2 Mastery Challenge — 4 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
There are 90 seconds left in your 15-minute Level 2 group discussion on reducing staff sickness absence. Write a 50-word concluding synthesis statement that summarises the group's three agreed measures (ergonomic chairs, flu vaccination vouchers, and flexible return-to-work interviews) and checks for unanimous agreement.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Signal that the meeting is concluding ('As we are in our final minute, let me summarise our agreed action plan...'). [M1]
2. List the three agreed points clearly. [M1]
3. Ask the group to confirm unanimous agreement. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 'As we are in our final ninety seconds, let me summarise the three recommendations we have agreed on today: first, replacing broken office chairs with ergonomic lumbar seating; second, offering free winter flu vaccination vouchers to all staff; and third, introducing supportive return-to-work wellbeing check-ins. Are we all happy to put those three measures forward as our final group decision?'
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Failing to proofread spelling, punctuation, and grammar after completing the draft.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Delivering the final 60-second summary statement is the single smartest move you can make in a Functional Skills group discussion.
