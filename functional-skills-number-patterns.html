  • E3.6 / L1.1 – Recognise, describe, and continue linear sequences of whole numbers, decimals, and negative numbers.
  • L1.5 / L2.5 – Apply term-to-term rules and simple position-to-term formulas to solve workplace scheduling and stock problems.

Golden Rules for Number Patterns & Linear Sequences

Golden Rule 1: Find the Step Difference Between TWO Pairs to Confirm the Rule

Subtract the 1st term from the 2nd term, and ALWAYS check that the gap from the 2nd to the 3rd term is identical (e.g. +7, −14, +0.25). Write the step rule (+ or −) above the arrows!

Golden Rule / Method: Step Rule = 2nd Term − 1st Term (Check with 3rd − 2nd)
Golden Rule 2: Jumping Multiple Steps = Start Term + (Number of Jumps × Step)

To go from the 1st term to the 10th term, there are 9 JUMPS (not 10!). So 10th Term = 1st Term + (9 × Step Difference).

Golden Rule / Method: Nth Term = 1st Term + ((N − 1) × Step)

Core Skill 1: Increasing, Decreasing, Decimal & Negative Sequences

Spotting constant addition and subtraction patterns across zero.

  • Increasing Whole Numbers: 14, 21, 28, 35, ... (Rule: Add 7 → Next = 42).
  • Decreasing Whole Numbers: 90, 78, 66, 54, ... (Rule: Subtract 12 → Next = 42).
  • Decimal Sequence: 1.50, 1.75, 2.00, 2.25, ... (Rule: Add 0.25 → Next = 2.50).
  • Crossing Zero into Negatives: 8, 5, 2, −1, −4, ... (Rule: Subtract 3 → Next = −7).
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Finding Missing Middle Terms in a Sequence

Dividing the total jump by the number of steps when a middle box is blank.

If a sequence has a missing middle number: 18, ___, 34, 42:

  • From 18 to 34 is 2 jumps covering 34 − 18 = 16.
  • 1 jump = 16 ÷ 2 = +8 (confirmed by 34 + 8 = 42).
  • Missing term = 18 + 8 = 26.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Fixed Call-Out + Per-Unit Cost Sequences

Using linear sequences to model equipment hire and modular seating.

Example: 1 table seats 6 people; 2 joined tables seat 10 people; 3 joined tables seat 14 people (Rule: +4 people per extra table, or Seats = 4n + 2).

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 2 Marks (Entry Level 3 / Level 1)

Vocational Context: Clinical Pharmacy Stock Control

A pharmacy stock counter records the remaining boxes of bandages at the end of each day:
Monday: 85 • Tuesday: 71 • Wednesday: 57 • Thursday: 43
If usage continues at the same daily rate, how many boxes will remain at the end of Friday and Saturday?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Find the daily step rule
85 − 71 = 14 boxes used per day (Rule: subtract 14).
Criteria: M1: Step rule (−14)
Step 2 Apply rule for Friday and Saturday
Friday = 43 − 14 = 29 boxes.
Saturday = 29 − 14 = 15 boxes.
Criteria: A1: Accurate terms (29 and 15)
Final Accredited Answer: Friday: 29 boxes | Saturday: 15 boxes
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Check the difference between at least two pairs (85 − 71 = 14 and 71 − 57 = 14) before continuing.
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1/2)

Vocational Context: Hospital Training Room Layout

A training centre arranges rectangular tables end-to-end in a single long row:
• 1 table seats 6 delegates (2 on each side plus 1 at each end).
• 2 tables joined end-to-end seat 10 delegates.
• 3 tables joined end-to-end seat 14 delegates.
(a) How many delegates can sit at a row of 8 joined tables?
(b) How many tables must be joined in a single row to seat 50 delegates?
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Identify the sequence rule (+4 delegates for each extra table)
Sequence: 6, 10, 14, 18... Each additional table adds 4 seats (Rule: 4n + 2).
Criteria: M1: Identifying +4 step rule
Step 2 Calculate seats for 8 tables
1st table = 6, plus 7 extra tables × 4 = 6 + 28 = 34 delegates (or 4 × 8 + 2 = 34).
Criteria: A1: 34 delegates
Step 3 Work backwards from 50 delegates to find number of tables
Subtract the 2 end seats: 50 − 2 = 48 side seats.
Divide by 4 seats per table: 48 ÷ 4 = 12 tables.
Criteria: M1 / A1: 12 tables required
Final Accredited Answer: (a) 34 delegates | (b) 12 tables
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Notice that the 2 end seats stay constant at 2, while every table contributes 4 side seats (4n + 2 = 50 → 4n = 48 → n = 12).

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Adding Instead of Subtracting on a Decreasing Sequence
Spotting the gap of 14 on 85, 71, 57, 43 but adding 14 to 43 (= 57) instead of subtracting 14 (= 29).
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always write a plus (+) or minus (−) sign in front of the gap number.
Examiner Pitfall: Doubling the 4th Term to Find the 8th Term When There Is a Fixed Offset
For 4 tables = 18 seats, doubling 18 to get 36 seats for 8 tables is wrong because it double-counts the 2 end seats (actual answer is 34)!
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Count the number of JUMPS (+4 per table) or use the 4n + 2 rule.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Find the next two numbers in this decimal sequence: 0.4, 0.7, 1.0, 1.3, ___, ___
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Step rule = 0.7 − 0.4 = +0.3.
2. Next terms: 1.3 + 0.3 = 1.6, and 1.6 + 0.3 = 1.9.
Final Correct Answer: 1.6 and 1.9
Examiner Tip: Think of the sequence in tenths: 4, 7, 10, 13, 16, 19 tenths.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
Continue this decreasing temperature sequence for the next two terms: 7°C, 3°C, −1°C, ___°C, ___°C
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Step rule = subtract 4°C each time.
2. −1 − 4 = −5°C.
3. −5 − 4 = −9°C.
Final Correct Answer: −5°C and −9°C
Examiner Tip: Going colder from −1°C by 4°C moves further below zero to −5°C.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A saver opens a Christmas club account with £45 in Week 1 and adds £15 every week after that (Week 2 = £60, Week 3 = £75). How much money will be in the account in Week 12?
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. From Week 1 to Week 12 is 11 weekly jumps of £15.
2. Added amount = 11 × £15 = £165.
3. Total in Week 12 = £45 + £165 = £210.
Final Correct Answer: £210
Examiner Tip: From Week 1 to Week 12 is 11 jumps (12 − 1 = 11), so £45 + (11 × £15) = £210.
