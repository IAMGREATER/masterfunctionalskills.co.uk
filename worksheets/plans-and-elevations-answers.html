Section A: Procedural Fluency Check Solutions Questions 1 & 2 • 4 Marks
Question 1 (Interpreting 2D Orthographic Views of 3D Solids) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
A modular storage unit has a rectangular plan view (bird's-eye view) measuring 3 m by 1.5 m, and a rectangular front elevation measuring 3 m wide by 2 m high.
Name the 3D shape of the storage unit and calculate its volume in m³.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Identify 3D solid from rectangular orthographic projections: Cuboid [M1]
2. Volume = 3 m × 1.5 m × 2 m = 9 m³ [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Cuboid; 9 m³
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Multiplying 3 m twice (3 × 3 × 1.5 × 2) instead of extracting L=3, W=1.5, H=2.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Base area = 3 × 1.5 = 4.5 m²; 4.5 × 2 m height = 9 m³.
Question 2 (Level 1 Foundation — 2 Marks) Model Solution [2 Marks Total]
An upright cylindrical water storage tank has a diameter of 3.2 m and a height of 4.5 m.
(a) What 2D shape and measurement is its Plan View?
(b) What 2D shape and dimensions is its Front Elevation?
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Plan view from above = Circle of diameter 3.2 m (radius 1.6 m). [M1]
2. Front elevation from the side = Rectangle measuring 3.2 m wide by 4.5 m high. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) Circle (diameter 3.2 m) | (b) Rectangle (3.2 m wide × 4.5 m high)
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Drawing the vertical height on a plan view is a classic mistake—looking straight down from the sky only reveals length and width.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Looking horizontally at a cylinder from the front or side produces a flat rectangle.
Section B: Workplace & Functional Scenarios Solutions Questions 3 & 4 • 6 Marks
Question 3 (Hospital Emergency Generator Enclosure Orthographic Projections (Level 1 Foundation)) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
A backup diesel generator housing is a cuboid measuring 4.0 m long (front), 2.5 m deep (side), and 2.0 m high.
(a) State the 2D shape and dimensions of the Plan View.
(b) State the 2D shape and dimensions of the Front Elevation.
(c) State the 2D shape and dimensions of the Side Elevation.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Determine Plan View (Length × Depth): Viewed from above: a rectangle measuring 4.0 m by 2.5 m. [A1]
2. Determine Front Elevation (Length × Height): Viewed from the front: a rectangle measuring 4.0 m wide by 2.0 m high. [A1]
3. Determine Side Elevation (Depth × Height): Viewed from the side: a rectangle measuring 2.5 m deep by 2.0 m high. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: (a) Plan: 4.0 m × 2.5 m rectangle | (b) Front: 4.0 m × 2.0 m rectangle | (c) Side: 2.5 m × 2.0 m rectangle
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Think: Plan = Floorplan (Length and Width only).
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Plan view NEVER includes height; front view NEVER includes depth.
Question 4 (Workplace Application — 3 Marks) Model Solution [3 Marks Total]
An architect draws the Side Elevation of a warehouse measuring 15 m deep by 6.5 m high on centimetre squared paper using a scale of 1 cm represents 2.5 m. Calculate the width and height of the rectangle drawn on the grid in cm.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Grid width = 15 m ÷ 2.5 m = 6 cm. [M1]
2. Grid height = 6.5 m ÷ 2.5 m = 2.6 cm. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: 6 cm wide by 2.6 cm high
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Drawing only the outer outline of an L-shaped block in the front elevation without the horizontal step line loses 1 mark.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Divide each real-world dimension by 2.5 m to find the grid length in cm.
Section C: Pearson Edexcel Problem Solving & Justification Solutions Questions 5 & 6 • 8 Marks
Question 5 (Gable-Roofed Modular Pharmacy Store Front Elevation Area & Cladding (Level 2 Multi-Step)) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
An architectural drawing shows the Front Elevation of a modular pharmacy building on a grid where 1 square = 0.5 m.
• On the grid, the front wall is a rectangle 12 squares wide by 6 squares high, topped by a triangular roof gable that rises an additional 4 squares to a central peak.
• Insulated cladding panels for this front elevation cost £45.00 per m².
Calculate the total cost to clad the entire front elevation.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Convert grid squares into real-world metres (× 0.5 m): Width = 12 × 0.5 m = 6.0 m. | Wall Height = 6 × 0.5 m = 3.0 m. | Triangle Gable Height = 4 × 0.5 m = 2.0 m. [M1]
2. Calculate area of rectangular wall + triangular gable: Rectangle Area = 6.0 m × 3.0 m = 18.0 m². | Triangle Area = (6.0 m × 2.0 m) ÷ 2 = 6.0 m². | Total Front Elevation Area = 18.0 + 6.0 = 24.0 m². [M1]
3. Multiply total area by £45.00 per m²: 24.0 m² × £45.00 = £1,080.00. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: £1,080.00 (Total front elevation area = 24.0 m²)
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Any change in depth or height visible from your viewing direction must be drawn as a solid straight line.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: Convert grid squares into metres BEFORE calculating area so you do not get caught out by square grid units!
Question 6 (Level 2 Multi-Step Problem Solving — 3 Marks) Model Solution [4 Marks Total]
A concrete loading ramp is a right-angled triangular prism of width 2.0 m, horizontal base depth 4.8 m, vertical back height 1.4 m, and sloping ramp length 5.0 m. State the exact dimensions of the Plan View when viewed directly from above.
Step-by-Step Marking Criteria:
1. Looking straight down from above, the sloping ramp projects onto its horizontal base footprint. [M1]
2. Plan view is a rectangle of width 2.0 m by horizontal depth 4.8 m. [A1]
Award Final Answer Mark: Rectangle measuring 2.0 m by 4.8 m
⚠️ Examiner Trap Alert: Stating a conclusion without showing the full numerical comparison values.
🔄 Reverse Sanity Check: From directly above, a sloped ramp appears as its horizontal footprint (4.8 m), not its 5.0 m slant length!
