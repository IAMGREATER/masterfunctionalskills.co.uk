Regulated Ofqual Functional Skills English Level 1 learning outcomes (Open Awards, Pearson Edexcel, City & Guilds, NCFE & AQA):

  • L1.SLC.2 – Make requests and ask relevant questions to obtain specific information in different contexts.

Core Level 1 Golden Rules: Asking Relevant Questions to Obtain Specific Information (Level 1 SLC)

Golden Rule 1: Listen for One Specific Detail to Ask About

While another candidate gives their 3–4 minute talk, jot down ONE topic detail they mentioned so you can ask a genuine follow-up question at the end.

Level 1 Key Rule: 'You mentioned training takes six weeks—can trainees complete that alongside part-time work?'
Golden Rule 2: Know When to Use Open vs Closed Questions

Closed questions ('How much does a pass cost?') check exact facts; Open questions ('How did you overcome the hardest part?') invite detailed explanations.

Level 1 Key Rule: Aim to ask at least 2 clear questions across the assessment!
Golden Rule 3: Invite Quieter Group Members In

Asking 'Priya, what do you think about the Tuesday timetable?' demonstrates leadership, turn-taking, and question skills all at once!

Level 1 Key Rule: Helps the whole group pass smoothly.

Core Skill 1: The 4-Type Questioning Taxonomy for Functional Skills Exams

Deploy all four question types across your group discussion and presentation Q&A.

Question Type Functional Purpose High-Scoring Exam Example
1. Open Exploratory Opens up a new topic or invites broad views. "What are the main challenges our team faces during the Friday evening rush?"
2. Clarifying Fact-Check Pins down exact numbers, dates, or definitions. "Could you clarify whether that £1,500 quote includes annual maintenance?"
3. Probing / Analytical Tests feasibility, risks, or deeper evidence (Level 1). "How will we maintain customer service standards if we reduce reception staffing by 20%?"
4. Collaborative / Inviting Brings a quiet peer into the conversation by name. "Priya, having worked on the night shift, how realistic does this timetable seem to you?"
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Converting Weak Closed Questions into High-Scoring Open Probes

Practise upgrading single-word Yes/No questions into analytical questions.

  • Closed: "Is the new software hard to learn?"
    Open Upgrade: "What training support will staff need to master the new software confidently?"
  • Closed: "Did the trial work well?"
    Open Upgrade: "What measurable improvements did you see during the four-week pilot scheme?"
  • Closed: "Do you think we should ban cars from the town centre?"
    Open Upgrade: "How might pedestrianising the High Street affect elderly residents and disabled badge holders?"
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Polite Indirect Requests & Diplomatic Question Framing

How to ask challenging questions without sounding hostile or interrogative.

Instead of firing blunt questions ("Why didn't you check the cost?"), soften and elevate your question using a polite lead-in:

• "Thank you for a very clear presentation, Alex. Could you expand a little on how the scheme will be funded in Year 2?"
• "That is an interesting proposal. Have we considered what safeguards would be needed if a supplier delivery is delayed?"
Level 1 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Level 1 Step-by-Step Worked Exam Model

Level 1 Benchmark Exam Model 2 Marks (SLC Q&A Criterion (L1.SLC.2))

Vocational Context: Q&A After a Peer's Short Talk on Volunteering at an Animal Shelter

A fellow learner finishes a 3-minute talk explaining what they do on Sunday mornings at a local animal rescue centre. Write one specific factual question and one open follow-up question you could ask them.
Step
Level 1 Method & Working
Marking Focus
Step 1 Specific information question
Ask for a concrete practical detail (age, training, hours)
→ 'Do new volunteers need any previous experience or training before their first Sunday shift?'
L1.SLC.2 — Obtains specific information
Step 2 Open exploratory question
Start with 'What / How' to invite fuller detail
→ 'What has been the most rewarding part of volunteering there for you so far?'
L1.SLC.2 — Relevant open follow-up
Final Exam Answer: 1. 'Do new volunteers receive training before starting?' | 2. 'What has been the most rewarding part of volunteering there?'
Sonya’s Level 1 Exam Tip: Your assessor ticks off L1.SLC.2 on their observation sheet the moment you ask clear, relevant questions—don't wait until the last 30 seconds!

Common Level 1 Examiner Traps to Avoid

Level 1 Examiner Trap: Asking Something the Speaker Already Answered Clearly
Asking 'What day do you volunteer?' right after they said 'every Sunday morning' shows you weren't listening!
Level 1 Examiner Trap: Sitting Silently Through Every Peer Q&A
You MUST ask questions yourself to pass L1.SLC.2—answering others' questions alone does not cover asking.

Interactive Level 1 Exam Practice Questions

Level 1 Practice 1: Talk on Home Batch-Cooking 2 Marks
A classmate gives a short talk on saving money by batch-cooking Sunday meals. Write two relevant questions you could ask at the end of their talk.
Level 1 Worked Solution & Mark Breakdown:
Question 1 (1 Mark): Specific factual question, e.g. 'Roughly how much money do you save on food shopping each week?'
Question 2 (1 Mark): Practical/open question, e.g. 'Which meals freeze and reheat best for busy workdays?'
Final Answer: 1. 'Roughly how much does batch-cooking save you each week?' | 2. 'Which meals freeze best without losing texture?'
Level 1 Practice 2: Polite Workplace Request 1 Marks
Turn 'Give me the Friday rota now' into a polite workplace request suitable for L1.SLC.2.
Level 1 Worked Solution & Mark Breakdown:
Polite request (1 Mark): 'Could you please send me a copy of Friday's shift rota when you have a moment?'
Final Answer: 'Could you please share a copy of Friday's shift rota with me before lunchtime?'
