  • L1.21 – Understand and use properties of regular and irregular polygons, including symmetry, side lengths, and angles.

Golden Rules for 2D Shapes & Polygons

Golden Rule 1: Regular Polygons Have Equal Sides AND Equal Angles

A polygon is 'regular' ONLY if all its sides are of equal length AND all its interior angles are equal (e.g. an equilateral triangle or a square). A rectangle is NOT regular because its length and width differ.

Golden Rule / Method: Regular = All Sides Equal + All Angles Equal
Golden Rule 2: Parallel vs Perpendicular Lines

Parallel lines run in the same direction and never meet (marked with matching arrows >>). Perpendicular lines meet at an exact right angle of 90° (marked with a small corner square).

Golden Rule / Method: Parallel = Never Meet • Perpendicular = Meet at 90°

Core Skill 2: Classification of Triangles by Sides and Angles

Distinguishing equilateral, isosceles, scalene, and right-angled triangles.

  • Equilateral Triangle: 3 equal sides, 3 equal 60° angles, 3 lines of symmetry.
  • Isosceles Triangle: 2 equal sides (marked with tick lines), 2 equal base angles, 1 line of symmetry.
  • Scalene Triangle: All 3 sides and all 3 angles are different; 0 lines of symmetry.
  • Right-Angled Triangle: Contains one 90° perpendicular corner; can be isosceles (90°, 45°, 45°) or scalene.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Polygon Naming & Regular Symmetry Rule (3 to 10 Sides)

Memorizing polygon prefixes and the n-fold symmetry law for regular polygons.

For any regular polygon with n sides, it has exactly n lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order n:

  • 5 Sides: Pentagon (Regular = 5 lines of symmetry, interior angle 108°)
  • 6 Sides: Hexagon (Regular = 6 lines of symmetry, interior angle 120°)
  • 8 Sides: Octagon (Regular = 8 lines of symmetry, interior angle 135°)
  • 10 Sides: Decagon (Regular = 10 lines of symmetry, interior angle 144°)
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1)

Vocational Context: Facility Signage Wayfinding Design

An emergency stop signboard is manufactured in the shape of a regular octagon.
(a) How many sides does an octagon have?
(b) How many lines of reflective symmetry does a regular octagon possess?
(c) State the order of rotational symmetry of a regular octagon.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 State the number of sides of an octagon
An octagon has 8 straight sides.
Criteria: A1: Correct polygon side count (8)
Step 2 Determine lines of reflective symmetry
A regular polygon with n sides has n lines of symmetry. An 8-sided regular octagon has 8 lines of symmetry.
Criteria: A1: Correct lines of symmetry (8)
Step 3 Determine rotational symmetry order
A regular octagon matches its outline 8 times in a 360° turn. Order = 8.
Criteria: A1: Correct rotational symmetry order (8)
Final Accredited Answer: (a) 8 sides | (b) 8 lines of symmetry | (c) Rotational symmetry of order 8
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
For ANY regular polygon, number of sides = lines of symmetry = order of rotational symmetry!
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2)

Vocational Context: Landscape Architecture & Tessellation

A courtyard designer arranges regular hexagonal paving slabs of side length 25 cm around a central square feature.
• Twenty-four outer edges of the hexagons form the external boundary of the patio.
• Steel edging strips are sold in 1.5 m lengths at £9.20 per strip.
Calculate the total cost of the steel edging strips required for the 24 outer hexagon edges.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate total boundary perimeter in cm and convert to metres
24 edges × 25 cm = 600 cm.
600 cm ÷ 100 = 6.0 metres.
Criteria: M1: Boundary length in metres (6.0 m)
Step 2 Calculate number of 1.5 m steel strips required
6.0 m ÷ 1.5 m = 4 strips.
Criteria: M1: Division by strip length (4)
Step 3 Calculate total cost of steel edging
4 × £9.20 = £36.80.
Criteria: A1: Accurate total cost (£36.80)
Final Accredited Answer: £36.80 (4 strips needed for 6.0 m boundary)
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Because the hexagons are regular, every single side measures exactly 25 cm.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Confusing a Parallelogram with Having 2 Lines of Symmetry
A standard non-rectangular parallelogram has ZERO lines of reflective symmetry, even though it has rotational symmetry of order 2.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Fold it mentally: the corners do not align when folded horizontally, vertically, or diagonally.
Examiner Pitfall: Claiming a Rectangle Has 4 Lines of Symmetry
Only a square has 4 lines of symmetry. A rectangle has only 2 (vertical and horizontal midlines; folding along a diagonal does not line up the corners).
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Remember: Rectangle = 2 lines; Square = 4 lines.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A ceramic wall tile has 6 equal sides and 6 equal interior angles, each side measuring 8 cm.
(a) What is the full mathematical name of this shape?
(b) Calculate the perimeter of one tile.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. A 6-sided polygon with equal sides and equal angles is a Regular Hexagon.
2. Perimeter = 6 × 8 cm = 48 cm.
Final Correct Answer: (a) Regular Hexagon | (b) 48 cm
Examiner Tip: Include the word "Regular" whenever all sides and angles are equal.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
A quadrilateral has one pair of parallel sides of lengths 12 cm and 18 cm, and two equal non-parallel sloping sides of 7 cm each.
(a) Name this specific type of quadrilateral.
(b) How many lines of symmetry does it have?
(c) Calculate its perimeter.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. One pair of parallel sides and equal sloping sides = Isosceles Trapezium.
2. An isosceles trapezium has 1 vertical line of symmetry.
3. Perimeter = 12 + 18 + 7 + 7 = 44 cm.
Final Correct Answer: (a) Isosceles Trapezium | (b) 1 line of symmetry | (c) 44 cm
Examiner Tip: A standard trapezium has 0 lines of symmetry, but an isosceles trapezium (equal slanted sides) has 1.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A rectangular metal frame measures 1.2 m long by 0.8 m wide, with one diagonal brace running from corner to corner of length 1.44 m.
(a) How many right angles (90°) are in the outer rectangular frame?
(b) Calculate the total length of steel bar needed to make the outer frame plus the single diagonal brace.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. A rectangle has 4 right angles (90°).
2. Outer frame perimeter = 2 × (1.2 + 0.8) = 4.0 m.
3. Add diagonal brace: 4.0 m + 1.44 m = 5.44 m.
Final Correct Answer: (a) 4 right angles | (b) 5.44 m
Examiner Tip: Read carefully whether internal braces are included in the total steel length.
