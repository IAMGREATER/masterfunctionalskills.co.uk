Cross-Text Synthesis Strategy
  • Find points of similarity: Both writers agree that X is important...
  • Find points of divergence: However, Writer A suggests Y, whereas Writer B advocates Z...
  • Compare presentation: Writer A uses a formal report layout with tables, while Writer B uses a personal blog with informal anecdotes.
  • Use balanced comparative language: 'In comparison', 'Whereas', 'On the contrary', 'Both texts demonstrate...'
Calculator Strategy: Structure your comparison paragraph using point-by-point synthesis rather than writing two separate unconnected blocks.
Part 1: Quick Practice
Quick calculation drills to build confidence
Question 1 Comparative Connectives Practice
Fill in the connective that best highlights the comparison (Both, Conversely, In contrast, Similarly):
(a)
(Both / Conversely) authors agree that regular physical exercise improves cardiovascular health.
(b)
Text A supports a mandatory sugar tax. (In contrast / Similarly), Text B argues for voluntary industry reform.
(c)
Text 1 warns of cyber threats; (similarly / conversely), Text 2 highlights data protection vulnerabilities.
(d)
Author X writes from personal experience. Author Y, (conversely / likewise), relies strictly on statistical surveys.
Question 2 Matching Contrasting Arguments
Pair the argument in Text 1 with its direct counter-argument in Text 2:
(a)
Text 1: 'Automated check-in kiosks save money and reduce reception queues.'
(b)
Text 1: '12-hour shifts give staff longer blocks of consecutive rest days.'
