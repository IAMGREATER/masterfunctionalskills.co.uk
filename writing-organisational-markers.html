Specification & Learning Outcomes

Candidates must demonstrate mastery of the following regulated Functional Skills English outcomes:

  • L1.24 / L2.24 – Use organisational markers and structural features to signpost information clearly for the reader.
  • L2.25 – Structure complex technical reports and procedural guides using systematic numerical and thematic headings.

Golden Rules for Organisational Markers & Headings

Golden Rule 1: Organisational Markers Are Visual Signposts for Busy Readers

A busy ward manager, safety inspector, or examiner should be able to grasp the entire architecture of your report or guide in 5 seconds just by scanning your bold subheadings and bullet lead-ins.

Golden Rule / Technique: Use informative subheadings (e.g. '2.0 Cost Savings of LED Lighting', not just 'Part 2').
Golden Rule 2: Numbered Lists for Chronological Steps; Bullet Points for Unordered Lists

If steps MUST happen in strict time order (e.g. fire evacuation, medication administration), use numbers (1, 2, 3). If items are an unordered set of points or features (e.g. required PPE items), use bullet points (•).

Golden Rule / Technique: 1, 2, 3 = Mandatory chronological sequence | • Bullets = Unordered features or recommendations

Core Skill 1: The Professional Report Heading Hierarchy

How to structure Level 2 business, safety, and audit reports using numbered sections.

  • Main Document Title: Capitalised or bold at the very top (e.g. REPORT: WAREHOUSE FORKLIFT SAFETY AUDIT).
  • 1.0 Executive Summary / Purpose: 2–3 sentences stating why the investigation was conducted.
  • 2.0 Key Operational Findings: Divided into clear thematic points (e.g. 2.1 Pedestrian Walkway Signage, 2.2 Lighting Levels).
  • 3.0 Risk & Cost Evaluation: Analyses the impact of leaving the issue unresolved.
  • 4.0 Actionable Recommendations: A bulleted or numbered list specifying who must do what by when.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 2: Writing Grammatically Parallel Bullet Points with Bold Lead-Ins

How to use bullet lists in Functional Skills exams without losing SPaG marks for sentence fragments.

Weak Shopping-List Bullets (Loses SPaG Marks for Fragments):

• new locks on doors
• staff need training on Tuesdays
• buying 4 CCTV cameras

High-Scoring Parallel Bullets with Bold Lead-Ins (Full SPaG & Format Marks):

To upgrade depot security immediately, the estates team should implement three measures:
• Keypad Door Locks: Install digital combination locks on all external stockroom doors by 30 November.
• Mandatory Staff Briefings: Deliver a 20-minute security refresher during Tuesday morning shift handovers.
• Perimeter CCTV Coverage: Mount four motion-activated infrared cameras overlooking Loading Bay 1.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Where to Use Organisational Markers (And Where NOT To)

Matching organisational features to the right exam text format.

Format Recommended Organisational Markers What to Avoid
Formal Report / Info Sheet Main Title, numbered subheadings (1.0, 2.0), bulleted recommendations, bold key terms. Writing unbroken walls of prose without headings.
Article / Blog Post Catchy Headline, 2–3 thematic subheadings, optional short bullet box of top tips. Robotic numbered headings like '1.0 Introduction'.
Formal Letter Subject line (Re: ...) after salutation, clear indented or spaced paragraphs. Do not put article subheadings inside a short formal letter.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

'When you hear the continuous siren leave the building right away by the green fire exits. Don't stop to pick up bags or coats and never use the lift. Walk to Car Park A and wait for the Fire Warden to call your name.'

If the continuous fire siren sounds, all staff and visitors must follow these three steps immediately:

3. Remain in your department group until the Fire Warden has completed the roll call.

Vocational Context: NHS Infection Control Standard Operating Procedure

Format a guidance section for hospital domestic and auxiliary staff detailing the 3-tier waste bag colour-coding system (Yellow, Orange, Black) and sharps disposal rule using professional organisational markers.

Criteria

Section 2.0: Clinical Waste Segregation Protocols followed by a complete lead-in sentence ending with a colon.

Step 2 Use bulleted bold lead-ins with complete sentences for the 3 bag colours
Detail Yellow (infectious incineration), Orange (clinical autoclaving), and Black (domestic general waste).

Final Accredited Response: 2.0 Clinical Waste Segregation Protocols
To maintain strict CQC infection-control compliance, all ward staff must segregate waste according to the three colour-coded categories below:
• Yellow Bags (Highly Infectious Waste): Use exclusively for dressings, swabs, and PPE contaminated with infectious bodily fluids requiring high-temperature incineration.
• Orange Bags (Standard Clinical Waste): Use for non-infectious clinical items such as disposable aprons, wipes, and incontinence pads treated via autoclaving.
• Black Bags (Domestic General Refuse): Use strictly for non-clinical waste including paper towels, food packaging, and clean cardboard.

[CRITICAL SAFETY DIRECTIVE]: Needles, lancets, and scalpel blades must be deposited immediately into rigid yellow BS-approved sharps bins; never place sharps inside plastic waste bags.
Marking Scheme Guidance & Assessment Criteria
Notice how every bullet point starts with a bold category label followed by a colon and a complete grammatical sentence. This earns top marks for both layout and SPaG.

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Turning Your Entire Exam Answer into Bullet Point Fragments
If 80% of your response consists of 3-word bullet points without full sentences or paragraphs, examiners cannot award Level 2 grammar or cohesion marks.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Limit bullet points to one specific section (such as Recommendations), and always write full, grammatically complete sentences inside each bullet.
Examiner Pitfall: Using Subheadings That Don't Match the Paragraph Content
Writing the subheading 'Cost Savings' and then spending the paragraph talking about staff parking permits confuses the reader and loses organisation marks.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Ensure every sentence beneath a subheading directly addresses the topic promised in that heading.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
Rewrite these unformatted notes into a structured workplace safety section with a bold heading and two grammatically complete bullet points:
'Workers need steel toe cap boots on the warehouse floor and also high-vis jackets must be worn at all times near forklift trucks.'
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Create a clear heading (e.g. Mandatory Warehouse PPE Regulations).
2. Write an introductory lead-in clause followed by two parallel, complete-sentence bullet points.
Final Model Answer: Mandatory Warehouse PPE Regulations
All operatives entering the main distribution floor must comply with the following personal protective equipment rules:
• Safety Footwear: Steel toe-cap boots must be worn at all times across all warehouse storage and packing zones.
• High-Visibility Clothing: Fastened high-visibility vests or jackets are mandatory whenever working near active forklift transit lanes.
Examiner Tip: Adding bold lead-ins ('Safety Footwear:', 'High-Visibility Clothing:') makes bullet points look like an authentic workplace policy document.
Exam Practice Question 2 3 Marks
Explain whether you should use a numbered list (1, 2, 3) or unordered bullet points (•) for each workplace document below, and give the reason why:
Document A: The four stages of sterilising surgical instruments in an autoclave.
Document B: The four employee benefits offered in a company recruitment leaflet.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Analyse Document A: Sterilising instruments must follow a strict chronological sequence to be safe → Numbered list.
2. Analyse Document B: Employee benefits are a collection of features with no required time order → Bullet points.
Final Model Answer: Document A: Use a numbered list (1, 2, 3, 4) because sterilisation steps must be performed in a strict chronological sequence.
Document B: Use unordered bullet points (•) because employee benefits are separate features that do not happen in a sequence.
Examiner Tip: Numbers = strict sequence where order matters; Bullets = non-sequential collection.
Exam Practice Question 3 4 Marks
You are compiling the final section of a Level 2 Report titled 'Reducing Patient Waiting Times at Oakfield GP Surgery'. Draft Section 3.0: Recommendations containing a brief lead-in sentence and three bulleted, actionable recommendations with bold lead-ins.
Marking Scheme Criteria:
1. Write heading: '3.0 Strategic Recommendations'.
2. Write a formal introductory sentence ending with a colon.
3. Draft three full-sentence bullet points covering online triage, evening nurse clinics, and SMS cancellations.
Final Model Answer: 3.0 Strategic Recommendations
To reduce average appointment waiting times by 30% over the next quarter, the Practice Partnership should implement the following three measures:
• Digital Triage Portal: Launch an online symptom-checker form from 1 November so routine prescription renewals do not block morning telephone lines.
• Extended Nurse Practitioner Hours: Introduce two evening minor-illness clinics on Tuesdays and Thursdays until 19:30 for working patients.
• Automated SMS Cancellation Links: Send automated text reminders 24 hours prior to appointments, allowing patients to cancel unused slots with a single reply.
Examiner Tip: Notice how each recommendation states the specific initiative, how it works, and the operational benefit.
