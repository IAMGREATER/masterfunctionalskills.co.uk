  • L1.7 – Estimate and check answers using rounding.

Golden Rules for Multiplication & Scaling

Golden Rule 1: The Total Decimal Places Rule

When multiplying decimals, count the total number of decimal places in BOTH numbers. Multiply as whole numbers, then put that exact total number of decimal places back into the answer.

Golden Rule / Method: 2.4 (1 dp) × 0.15 (2 dp) = 0.360 (3 dp) = 0.36
Golden Rule 2: Grid Method Eliminates Carrying Errors

If column multiplication confuses you with carried digits, use the grid (box) method to partition numbers by tens and units before summing.

Golden Rule / Method: Partition: (30 + 4) × (20 + 7) = 600 + 210 + 80 + 28 = 918

Core Skill 2: Skill 2: Standard Column Multiplication & The Tens Row Zero

How to avoid the classic missing-zero error in two-line vertical multiplication.

To compute 138 × 24 vertically:

  1. Row 1 (Multiply by 4 units): 138 × 4 = 552.
  2. Row 2 (Multiply by 2 tens = 20): Write down a 0 in the units column FIRST, then compute 138 × 2 = 276 → 2,760.
  3. Add both rows: 552 + 2,760 = 3,312.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Core Skill 3: Skill 3: Multiplying Decimals Without a Calculator

Strip the decimal points, multiply whole numbers, and count back total decimal places.

Example: Work out 3.6 × 0.25

  • Count decimal digits in question: 3.6 (1 d.p.) + 0.25 (2 d.p.) = 3 decimal places total.
  • Multiply as whole integers: 36 × 25 = 900.
  • Count 3 places back from the right of 900: 0.900 = 0.9.
Level 1 Level 2 Pearson Edexcel City & Guilds NCFE

Pearson Edexcel Step-by-Step Worked Models

Benchmark Exam Model 1 3 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 1/2 Non-Calculator)

Vocational Context: NHS Healthcare Staff Uniforms

A care home orders 48 tunic sets at a cost of £26.50 each.
Calculate the total cost of the order without using a calculator.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Partition or convert decimal to whole number multiplication
Multiply £26 × 48 and £0.50 × 48 separately (or 265 × 48 ÷ 10).
Criteria: M1: Valid non-calculator multiplication method
Step 2 Compute the partial products
48 × 20 = 960; 48 × 6 = 288; 48 × 0.50 = 24.
Sum = 960 + 288 + 24 = 1,272.
Criteria: M1: Accurate partial products
Step 3 Format as currency to 2 decimal places
Total Cost = £1,272.00
Criteria: A1: Accurate final currency answer
Final Accredited Answer: Total Cost = £1,272.00
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Multiplying by £0.50 is simply halving the number (half of 48 is £24)!
Benchmark Exam Model 2 4 Marks (Pearson Edexcel Level 2 Problem Solving)

Vocational Context: Hospitality Staffing Budget

An events manager has a weekend staffing budget of £2,100.
She employs:
• 12 catering assistants working 8.5 hours each at £12.40 per hour.
• 4 shift supervisors working 9.5 hours each at £16.50 per hour.

Is the £2,100 budget sufficient to cover the total weekend wage bill? Show full working.
Step
Description / Working
Criteria
Step 1 Calculate total hours and pay for catering assistants
Total assistant hours = 12 × 8.5 = 102 hours.
Assistant pay = 102 × £12.40 = £1,264.80.
Criteria: M1: Assistant hours and wage calculation
Step 2 Calculate total hours and pay for shift supervisors
Total supervisor hours = 4 × 9.5 = 38 hours.
Supervisor pay = 38 × £16.50 = £627.00.
Criteria: M1: Supervisor hours and wage calculation
Step 3 Sum total weekend wage bill
£1,264.80 + £627.00 = £1,891.80.
Criteria: M1: Total payroll summation
Step 4 Compare with £2,100 budget and conclude
£1,891.80 < £2,100. Yes, the budget is sufficient with £208.20 remaining.
Criteria: A1: Correct decision with £1,891.80 and £208.20 surplus
Final Accredited Answer: Yes, sufficient (Total wage bill is £1,891.80, which is £208.20 under the £2,100 budget).
Method Checklist & Marking Scheme Guidance
Multiply the number of workers by the hours per worker first (12 × 8.5 = 102 whole hours) to eliminate a decimal before multiplying by the hourly rate!

Critical Examiner Traps & Common Pitfalls

Examiner Pitfall: Forgetting the Zero in the Tens Row
In long column multiplication, when multiplying by the tens digit, you MUST put a 0 in the units column before multiplying.
How to Avoid This Pitfall: Always drop a '0' as your placeholder before multiplying by the tens digit.

Interactive Exam Practice Questions

Exam Practice Question 1 2 Marks
A logistics operative works 37.5 hours per week at an hourly rate of £14.00. Calculate their gross weekly pay without a calculator.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. 37.5 × 14 = (37.5 × 10) + (37.5 × 4)
2. 375 + 150 = £525.00.
Final Correct Answer: £525.00
Examiner Tip: Multiplying by 14 can be simplified into multiplying by 10 then by 4 and summing.
Exam Practice Question 2 2 Marks
Without using a calculator, work out 4.8 × 0.35.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Count decimal places: 4.8 (1 dp) + 0.35 (2 dp) = 3 dp total.
2. Multiply whole numbers: 48 × 35 = (48 × 30) + (48 × 5) = 1,440 + 240 = 1,680.
3. Restore 3 decimal places: 1.680 = 1.68.
Final Correct Answer: 1.68
Examiner Tip: 1,680 with 3 decimal places becomes 1.680, which simplifies to 1.68.
Exam Practice Question 3 3 Marks
A conference centre sets up 28 circular tables, each seating 12 guests, and 15 rectangular tables, each seating 8 guests. Each guest meal costs the organisers £15.00. Calculate the total catering cost if every seat is filled.
Marking Scheme Steps:
1. Circular seats = 28 × 12 = 336 guests; Rectangular seats = 15 × 8 = 120 guests.
2. Total guests = 336 + 120 = 456 guests.
3. Total cost = 456 × £15 = (456 × 10) + (456 × 5) = £4,560 + £2,280 = £6,840.00.
Final Correct Answer: £6,840.00
Examiner Tip: To multiply by 15 quickly without a calculator, multiply by 10 and add half of that result.
